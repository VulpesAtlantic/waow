Chapter 1: 4C

In this life and all others, Rio Vidal would choose to be a first responder. 

Seriously, she wouldn’t change a thing. 

The paychecks are a little empty, but the team she’s surrounded herself with makes the loneliness inside her bones ache a little less. The rush of adrenaline she feels every time their engine siren echoes through the busy city streets of Manhattan keeps her grounded but enthralls her all the same. 

Growing up in this city, that sound was the one constant in her tumultuous childhood of bouncing through different foster homes for some of the most impressionable years of her life. At six, her parents died, and she and her thirteen year old brother, Benny, were passed around like damaged goods inside New York City’s broken foster system. 

Her brother had promised her to save them both and to give them a life worth something more than the hand that was so desperately trying to keep them down. So at eighteen, he’d signed on at the first fire engine company he could find within a miles walk of their sixth or seventh foster home and saved up every single paycheck thereafter with the intention of getting Rio in his custody. 

And he did just that. 

He raised her with his engine company of six crewmates. He had somehow found a village and by extension, so did Rio. Nearly all of her meals were made by a man named Big Joe, including her school lunches that were always packed with a bite sized bag of sour skittles (her favorite) and she learned how to multiply with the help of a woman named Judy who had blonde hair and a warm smile. 

It’s the only home she’s ever come to know. Apartments have come and gone, but her bed at the station has always remained the same. It just has more room for her now that her brother isn’t curled into her as she sleeps. The floor still creaks beneath her feet when she gets out of bed and the window to her left still has the crack in it from a ball one of the guys had thrown a little too hard. 

Sometimes she finds herself just laying in the bed on her back, looking up at the ceiling that has held so many memories, both before her time and during. If she closes her eyes, she can hear Big Joe calling her to breakfast and her brother reminding her to brush her teeth. It’s funny how something can feel so close, but yet so distant as if she never really lived the memory at all. 

“Morning Vidal,” a deep, raspy voice calls from the bed next to hers. It’s not Big Joe or her brother, but it’s still a voice that feels like home. The voice belongs to Lieutenant Bucky Barnes, a thirty two year old nuisance to the company despite his entire role being based around keeping everyone in line. Benny loved him. But every now and then, Matt strays from the path, and more often than not, Rio is right there beside him. She’s been there for enough of his bad ideas to know which ones he’ll regret in the morning, and he’s returned the favor more times than either of them would care to count.

It’s not the kind of friendship that needs much explaining. They’ve accumulated it slowly, in stolen hours outside the station and stories neither of them would ever tell the rest of the crew. Somewhere between the nights out, the rides home, the whispered commentary from across crowded bars, and the occasional rescue from their own poor judgment, Matt became one of Rio’s people.

“So I guess I need to start putting in applications for a new wingwoman,” he jokes, nudging her softly with his shoulder.

“Not you too,” she murmurs, taking another swig.

“That’s the only joke I’ll make about it.” He promises, crossing his heart for extra measure, pulling a snicker from the woman beside him. “Besides, I know Bucky is giving you enough hell for all of us.”

“Like you wouldn’t fucking believe,” she sighs, tipping her head back to rest against the cool metal, closing her eyes gently.

“He’s just excited,” Matt muses. “He doesn’t see you with women like I do.”

Rio cracks one eye open. “You can’t see shit.”

He belly laughs. “You know what I mean.”

“I don’t think I do.”

“It means I know you.”

The simplicity of it makes her quiet.

Matt takes another drink, letting his shoulder settle against hers. “I might be blind, but I know what you sound like when you’re bored. I know what you sound like when you’re trying to get someone into bed. And I know what you sound like when you actually give a shit.”

Rio looks away, suddenly finding the floor of the bay much more interesting than Matt’s face.

“And?” she asks, quieter this time.

“And,” he says, with a small shrug, “you give a shit.”

Rio opens her mouth to argue, but nothing comes out. Because the fact of the matter is, she does give a shit. She really, really does. 

She gives so much shit, actually.

Enough that she cares if Agatha sleeps in the hallway of their apartment building. Enough that she invites Agatha to their Sunday dinner and cares what her family thinks of her. Enough that she wants to fucking go out with her friends and make them like her. 

Rio swallows thickly. “Fuck.”

Matt chuckles beside her. “Yeah. That’s about what I thought.” 

He doesn’t prod anymore after that. He doesn’t ask more of Rio than she’s willing to give. He just sits next to her, quiet and steadying, drinking his beer while she stares at hers like it might somehow solve everything. 

They sit like that for a few more minutes, drinking their beers and taking in the quiet of each other's presence until there’s footsteps approaching them from behind the truck.

Bucky appears a few seconds later, a beer in his hand and a soft look in his eye. “There you are.”

He doesn’t give her time to react. He sits beside her, the truck dipping softly under his weight. He brushes his shoulder against hers, leaving her squished between Matt and himself, but she really can’t find it in her to be bothered.

They quietly sit there just as she and Matt had been doing moments before, sipping their beers. She tells herself it’s a comfortable silence, but it starts eating at her. She finds it hard to believe there hasn’t been a quip or a comment from Bucky. Not even a teasing look.

She can’t take it anymore.

“Aren’t you gonna say anything?” she finally asks, looking at Bucky curiously.

He turns to her, shrugging. “Should I be?”

“You don’t have anything to say?” she tries again, not buying it for a second.

Matt chuckles next to her.

“About what?”

“You know what.”

He sits for a second, just looking at her with the kind of eyes she’s seen a million times before. The ones that say just how much he loves her without ever having to say the words. The ones that dry out her throat and prickle tears in her eyes.

His mouth twitches into a smile before he settles on, “You seem happy.”

And her expression falters completely.

“I am,” she says honestly.

“Then I’m happy,” he says as he bumps his shoulder against hers. “That’s all I need to know, kid.”

Rio looks down at her beer again, but this time she smiles. She still doesn’t know what the hell she’s going to do about Agatha.

But for the first time tonight, the thought doesn’t scare her quite as much.
