As Agatha’s hand moved from Maurice, so did Jennifer move now towards her.

“We must fashion you a disguise. Nothing outlandish. Simply…” Agatha watched on as Jennifer struggled for the word. “Ordinary.”

Agatha looked over herself and in her naïveté knew not why her attire would be considered anything other than “ordinary”.

Lilia made sure each garment was washed as often as possible. On occasion, Agatha would find new chemises or hose left for her with her daily bread.

Looking down, Agatha saw only an ordinary cream chemise hanging below the waist and her well-worn brown hose. Though she supposed she knew no fashion nor finery, and could well be wearing clothes more suited to another style from another time.

“Fear not, I will return. And you shall leave a new woman.”

Agatha looked back up and saw that Jennifer was already making haste to leave, she assumed because she was afeared that Agatha would change her mind.

But fickle, nor easily changeable, Agatha was not.

For the rest of the afternoon, Agatha tended the bells. As she oiled and whispered to them, she did all she could to focus on her old friends rather than the memory of the woman whose ring she now wore.

Her thoughts, too, often betrayed her as they meandered into a territory she would rather they did not.

Another unhelpful distraction from her work was the unsettling of her stomach whenever she pictured dark eyes and a kind smile.

She knew what that feeling meant.

It meant sin.

But she was custodian of the bells and so she must care for them as diligently as she was able.

Jen called for her when she was behind Emmanuel. She showed herself during a period where Agatha was most intent on her duty, just as she had blocked all unholy thoughts from her sin-sensitive brain.

“Here, we do not have much time if we wish to make the most of Madame’s absence. Change. I will help.”

Agatha looked down from her perch and raised her brows at Jennifer’s offering.

“I have never worn shoes.”

Jennifer held one of the brown boots she’d brought with her and Agatha eyed it suspiciously for it would raise at least to her knees and had a complicated plait of strings upon the front.

“These will be comfortable enough, you have my word. I shall help.”

“I do not wish to be bound in them,” Agatha uttered, though she was now climbing down from her seat above Emmanuel.

Jennifer remained silent as she watched and, not for the first time, thought about how useful a strong, lithe, and capable Agatha would be in her legion of guards.

“Do not you hear the irony of your words?”

Agatha was now on solid ground, though the wood squeaked where she stepped.

“Might I forgo the shoes?”

Jennifer sighed and placed the bundle of borrowed clothing on the floor and the boots with them.

“To traipse to le huitième with your feet bare?”

“I shall be careful.”

“Enough, Agatha. You must change for we must go. Come now.”

Agatha retired her protestations and simply took the clothes with her behind Gabriel.

When she emerged she did so with an expression of such forlorn that Jennifer sighed.

“Your doublet looks well on you.” She moved closer and dropped down to lace her brown boots. “You cut a fine figure, Agatha.”

“I shall be caught and made a fool.”

“Your mother is well-.”

“Not by mother. By people.”

Jen looked up to Agatha and paused in her work. “You look as ordinary as I. Though perhaps a little less dashing.”

Jennifer, who smiled infrequently but grinned when she did, encouraged even a small smile from Agatha, who then remained silent as she allowed her boots to be bound.

There was no looking glass in the belfry so Agatha relied on Jennifer’s continued praises to conjure a picture of herself. A brown doublet, cream tunic below it, and somber black hose made up the ordinary outfit.

The best garments Jennifer could find in Agatha’s relative size and build. Mild infringements to their general good condition were occasional loose threads and scuff marks on the leather.

But they were the most handsome clothes Agatha had ever worn.

Agatha insisted upon seeing to every bell before they left. How could she leave her friends without saying goodbye?

Jennifer waited impatient and imperious by the hatch that would take them to the outside world but Agatha did not rush.

For goodbye was never made easier with any haste.

Though soon she joined Jen in climbing down to the great cathedral below.

It was not until her feet hit the flagstone of the floor that Agatha realised truly what she had done. A great wave of panic washed over her and she scrambled to return to her home.

Jen, taller, but no stronger than Agatha, made to wrestle her into her right mind.

It eventually worked but in the tussle Agatha cut a knuckle against the stone wall behind her where she’d ripped her hand away from her friend.

They halted and both stared at the ruby droplet against her pale skin.

“May that be the last drop of blood spilled this night.”

Agatha, breathing heavily, her heart like the thunder of Emmanuel bringing in the dawn, nodded as her friend straightened and released her hold on her.

“Come now, Agatha. We must make haste.”

But Agatha, biddable after her pitiful sense of panic, did not need to be told again.

Thinking only of blood and thunder, she kept in step with Jennifer as they skirted around the edges of the cathedral.

Notre Dame, and all her munificent splendour, was lost on the bell-ringer who refused to bear witness to it.

Frightened of enjoying it too much, of not enough. Truly frightened of anything at all, she felt it best to bow her head in an act of ashamed humility.

Or give it a more fascinating name, call it humiliation.

Agatha’s torture did not end outside the heavy oak front doors. The streets of Paris became a rabbit warren of danger and mocking at every turn.

But she did not falter.

She did not even break step with Jennifer.

She instead marched on, an ordinary civilian with her ordinary soldier. Jennifer occasionally pointed out landmarks or notable venues but Agatha paid it all no mind until the words: “Here, at last, the heart of le huitième.”

Agatha raised her gaze and realised that she was not looking at what she had expected to find. A fool’s blindness, to expect what you know could never be.

In Agatha’s case: a gypsy girl with eyes only for her.

Instead she looked upon a small square with a round cut of grass at the centre and a juvenile tree growing from it.

It looked not unlike any other square from a picture book but pictures do not come with sounds and this square came with many.

Agatha, all blood and thunder, followed Jen to the source of the sound and found herself bracing for unknown impact as the door to the tavern swung open for them.

A cacophony of noise unlike anything the bell-ringer had ever heard erupted the moment the door to the little tavern was opened.

Even her largest, most clangorous bells did not a noise like this make.

But Agatha had no time to get used to it for now she was a part of it, being driven by Jen’s firm hand at the base of her back.

“Courage, Agatha. A little frivolity never hurt anyone.”

Almost missing Jennifer’s words entirely for the incessant thrum of sound and the insistence that her heart now live in her ears, Agatha only did as she was bade.

She moved with Jennifer’s steady command but found her path constantly blocked by the busy revellers who frequented places such as this.

Courage, Agatha knew, was not needed in a place like this. Not if you were an ordinary citizen. But Agatha was far from ordinary. She was a beast, released on a leash for the night, with the promise of being returned to her chains.

But the two elopers soon made it through to find a lone table in the back corner. And Jennifer, her attentions on things other than Agatha, did not see her companion’s struggle.

For Agatha could not breathe with how frightened she felt. She could on longer distinguish between the faces of a crowd so large and so alien to her.

She breached the confines of her doublet, defied the loose ties of her tunic, and pressed her damp palm against the skin of her chest.

Jennifer may not have noticed, too busy was she in searching for wine or old friends, but somebody else had.

“Pray tell me, what is the matter?”

Agatha, swallowing air like heavy broth, did not hear the words spoken for her alone but she felt the hand that found her shoulder.

“Friend? You came here with Jennifer, yes?”

Agatha turned her head, the room span with her, and for a moment all was lost.

Then dark eyes could be distinguished in the blur. Dark hair too. A frown, a frown of concern rather than disgust or anger.

And Agatha swallowed her vital broth and almost choked as she asked: “Perchance do you know of a Rio?”

Agatha was still struggling with her vision, still unsure if the woman smiling at her now was even here, but a squeeze to her shoulder told her not everything was in her head.

“Agatha, I see you are already making friends.”

A cup of rich red wine was placed down heavily enough for Agatha to turn to it and she saw Jennifer’s triumphant return as well as the movement of the woman from before to her friend’s side.

Even in her state of abject terror, Agatha was soon able to divine the reason now for their excursion.

The shorter woman had placed a kiss to Jennifer’s cheek and Jennifer’s rare grin had appeared for the second time that night.

“Allow me to find her for you, for I know she is near.”

The unknown woman had turned again to Agatha and yet Agatha could not respond because everything was still slow and her breathing still laboured.

“Might I enquire as to-?”

“You may not.” The other woman cut Jen off with a smile.

Agatha, breathing raggedly, her fingers twitching against her warm chest, took the time now to observe her.

She was draped in dark skirts that were less voluminous than the skirts that paraded around them.

The woman moved, turning more into Jennifer, and Agatha realised that the skirts were not skirts at all, but hose of a kind she’d never seen before. The fabric reminded Agatha of billowing sails from picture books of old.

She was dressed for movement, for dance.

As Agatha continued to stare, she found herself a voyeur to a scene meant for no one but the players themselves.

The shorter woman with the sails had found her port in the storm of noise. She was leaning into Jennifer, enjoying her close attentions and her hand as it traversed the bow of her back.

This woman had been the destination. The tavern simply the chosen space to shelter her until Jen’s arms could provide their warmth.

Agatha watched, her fingers now twitching not for the panic that they felt but for the longing to be touching another the way the stranger and her friend now embraced.

Breath was still thick, it congealed in her throat and had to be swallowed before it could slip down to her lungs.

But she was alive still. The flotilla around her, the skirts and sounds were part of her heart’s raucous thunder.

“I shall send word,” the stranger announced, turning from the taller woman. “She shall be found.”

Agatha did not acknowledge this offer and saw not when the stranger leant around to speak to another. For she had decided instead to try to soften her swallowed breath with the wine in her cup.

It helped not.

A movement by her side told her that Jennifer had taken ownership of the small stool to her right.

“Be glad, friend. For you are safe. La Lune is a haven for people who are ordinary but who wish to live less ordinary lives.”

Agatha’s thumb moved an inch across her warm skin but she continued to stare into the wood grain of the rickety table before her.

“I fear I cannot breathe.”

“You are speaking, therefore your breath is of no consequence to me, nor should it be to you.”

Agatha swallowed but with a nod of her head she agreed she was indeed breathing, as painful and thick as it felt.

“My friend here, her name is Alice. La Alice.” Jennifer indicated the smaller woman now floating through the tavern, her sails rippling with every movement.

“A gypsy,” Agatha whispered, now seeing that which she had not before. La Lune was indeed a place for those less ordinary. As her eyes adjusted she saw claret, violet, and blues the like only the sky had shown her.

The ordinary people of Paris in their unordinary garments. Gypsies, locals, people from lands far beyond the city’s reach or Agatha’s mind could fathom.

“She is a jewel, is she not?”

But Agatha could no longer see Alice through the bodies that thrummed with the thunder of her bells.

Blood and thunder, coursing through her body but too the bodies of those around them. They were all storms unto themselves.

And Agatha’s was raging.

She tapped her chest, seeking comfort she knew not how to give herself.

She longed for Scratchy’s stoicism, for Emmanuel’s largess, for Gabriel’s protection.

She longed for the safety of her chains.

Jennifer soon turned her attentions to others, people who could give her the conversation Agatha was not capable of.

No one paid Agatha much mind as she sat with her hand clamped against her chest and her eyes closed to the world around her.

There she remained, as still as the apostles adorning Notre Dame. Until such time as Jennifer had jostled her and instructed her to look now upon the approach of Alice and her charge.

Agatha did as she was bade and saw before her an angel in the sea of bodies.

“Rio,” she breathed, a prayer that allowed her breath to flow easier. She expelled it and the gentleness of the name upon the air seemed to gather in strength as it reached the voluminous skirts of the gypsy girl who danced on her way to her.

Her arm was entangled in Alice’s until it suddenly wasn’t, until it was around Agatha’s shoulders and Agatha knew not what to do but breathe.

So she breathed.

“My Agatha.”

Lavender, something ephemeral, like the blossom on the trees in spring, like the surge of the Seine during the rainy season, it filled her lungs and let her breathe easy for the first time since stepping foot outside the belfry.

Rio pulled away and Agatha was inches away from dark eyes and a smile so warm she could have melted winter into spring.

“You found me.”

“I merely spoke your name.”

“I told you if you called I would come.”

Agatha knew not what to do with her limbs. They became other to her. They remained in place as Rio held her loosely about her neck, as she moved to perch upon the stool empty to her left, as she stole her cup of wine and drank what remained.

Blood and thunder. The red of the wine and the thrum of her heart in her chest as fingers trailed across the back of her neck.

She knew not why the first utterance of her lips had been to call for this woman. But she’d whispered her name and had made her manifest before her.

Agatha watched, drunk on she knew not what but certainly not the wine, as Rio spoke with Alice, as Alice spoke with Jen, as they all spoke together, and none to her.

But Rio’s hand remained on her. Unobtrusive, unashamed, simply resting on the back of her neck, trailing down to her shoulder, slipping its way to her upper arm.

She couldn’t have known it. Surely the gypsy girl had no idea of it. But it anchored her. It grounded Agatha in a way that her own hand across her body could not have.

It kept her present when the movement and the noise of La Lune threatened to sweep her into oblivion.

More wine was placed before her. And soon shared by Agatha and Rio beside her. It was done quietly, almost silently, and Agatha smiled each time Rio’s fingers brushed her own to take the cup.

Because she’d never shared before.

Because Scratchy had never thanked her with a touch or a nod or a gentle “Merci”.

Because Scratchy had never thanked her at all.

Soon Rio’s hand was on her own, tucked below the small table that shook with each roar of laughter from the patrons of the tavern.

Rio’s hand was warm against her own and Agatha’s eyes soon closed again as she felt her fingers slip into the gaps that had never before been filled.

She might have felt shame about the callouses on her own hands but happiness has a strange way of softening disgrace.

In the darkness of La Lune, Agatha held onto the hand that hung with her own between their two bodies.

“It’s romaní,” Rio whispered, in answer to a question she had not asked, so close to Agatha’s ear that she shivered. “Gitana. Calé, sometimes.”

Agatha turned her head inwards and gasped for the brush of a cheek against her own.

“Gypsy in your dialect is permissible. Unless,” and now Rio had moved back, was tilting her head, was smiling slowly, as if it needed time to grow, “Unless you mean harm by it, my Agatha.”

Agatha blinked, aware that for the first time she was being asked for an opinion that was outside of her opinion of herself.

“I-.”

“Be not afraid,” Jennifer’s voice now, leaning around Rio to look at Agatha who was entranced still by the dark-eyed woman before her. “For Agatha means no harm to anyone. Nor does she speak any harm. Unless to myself, of course.”

Agatha blinked, catching a flash of amusement in Rio’s gaze that woke her from her stupor.

“Harm spoken for harm’s sake is welcomed, Jennifer,” she uttered to her friend, “Only when you have deserved it.”

Beside her, the sound of bells. Rio was laughing.

Agatha looked around to the gypsy and raised her brows as she watched her in her amusement.

“I do not wish harm upon you. Nor any of your kind.”

The truth of her own heart remained inside herself, for she could not bring herself to say it.

I am one of you. Your blood and my blood are one in the same.

“I can not imagine,” Rio said, her laughter softening until it was blown out like that of a candle’s flame, “that you would wish harm on anyone.”

“Be assured,” Jennifer again, “For I have seen Agatha in a rage that which you would not wish on your worst enemy.”

“I do not believe it,” Alice announced, her smile wider than Agatha had previously seen it.

“No,” Rio said, a smile as wide as Alice’s, but infinitely more appealing to Agatha. “Nor I.”

Though a compliment was intended, Agatha took the slight her mind created.

For she wished to be fearsome, wished to be fierce in her demeanour against the enemy. Any enemy. Particularly now the enemy of the woman holding her hand.

“I would fight for you,” Agatha uttered, her voice stronger than even she desired it to be. “If I must, if even I needn’t.”

Rio’s hand in her own tightened its hold and Agatha felt a lump in her throat. They must have looked at one another for longer even than Jen or Alice could bear as soon they were alone even amongst the crowds.

Agatha knew not what to do, nor where to look, nor what to say.

So, finding it best to stick to what she knew, to what she could feel and see, she raised her free hand and said.

“Your ring. I must return it.”

“A gift freely given should be gratefully received.”

“It is not that I do not-.”

“Then privy thee wear it, without a thought to return it.”

Agatha swallowed whatever rebuttal she felt bound to deliver and slipped the ring back over her knuckle.

Rio’s gaze fell to the ring, to the bird that now sat in Agatha’s hand, and she reached to touch it, making the bell-ringer’s breath hitch at the contact.

“I believe that every bird dreams of flying, whether they know the feeling or not.”

The pad of Rio’s fingers traced down from the gold band to course a line over Agatha’s hand.

“You live where the birds nest, you accompany them in their rest, and yet you fly with them only in dreams.”

A slow stirring of shame disquieted Agatha’s countenance, even as she remained transfixed on the gypsy, on the gentleness of her touch.

“If only you knew you had wings, my Agatha. I should wish you would but use them.”

The journey of Rio’s fingers continued and morphed as they came into contact with the scars that glistened in the candle-lit room.

“Who keeps you? Who harms you?”

Agatha knew she could not sin against this woman and so she did not.

“My aunt,” she told truthfully, “who bids I call her mother.”

“For what crime does she hold you captive? These wounds of the flesh, they run deeper, I can see they too have wounded your spirit.”

The gypsy girl’s fingers were now tracing a line from Agatha’s elbow to her wrist. It had been a malevolent gash that had left her bells uncared for over a miserable fortnight.

Agatha looked down to it now and recollected the days in which Lilia had nursed her through the pain of it. Physical, to be sure, but the guilt too had plagued her, that she was not strong enough to care for her friends.

“My mother,” Agatha whispered, “Does what she knows to be right.”

“And you? Tell me. What do you know to be right?”

As Agatha looked up she felt something other than the stirring of her loins in the loose hose she’d borrowed. Something other than the constriction of her heart. Something other than the soft trail of fingers against her skin.

She felt a connection, unlike any other she had felt before.

In the belfry, each bell is tethered to a beam. It is held with as much structural integrity as man can provide and god can divine.

Yet this, this meeting of two others in a tavern in le huitième, far outmastered any tether to any grosse cloche.

Rio’s eyes were wider than Agatha’s own; more open, more encouraging. But Agatha met her where she was.

She focused on the gypsy’s gaze with her own soft expression. A helpless one, though she wasn’t beyond reaching.

Instead her hand, her right, moved to enclose around the fingers that still journeyed along her skin and seemed to touch the deepest parts of her.

“I know only this, that I am-.”

A great shout above all others in the noisy tavern made Agatha turn. They all turned. They all stood. Agatha leading the charge, Jen next, and Rio and Alice shortly after.

A hand found the back of Agatha’s doublet and Agatha felt its grip holding her in place as she moved to see the noise.

“Guards! The guards!”

The shouts filtered through the small space as quickly and efficiently as the fear that spread through Agatha’s already tense body, and the hand on her back slipped below leather until it found linen.

Agatha looked to Jennifer, and saw in her eyes only fear of her own company. Fear of being found here with this company.

She looked to Alice and saw what she knew to be hurt and then turned to Rio and saw what she would later know to be defiance.

“Come with me.”

The words took her by surprise, the hand that flew to her cheek somehow less so.

Around them, thunder became lightening as tables upturned and ordinary folk became desperate folk.

“Rio. We must make haste!”

Agatha, with her body turning all of its own to Rio, could offer no alternative to Alice’s cry. For the briefest of moments she thought that Rio may ask her once more.

But the moment was transient.

Rio slipped from her like a thunder clap from the sky. Agatha stood still for only a moment until a shoulder collided with her own and she was forced back into the room.

La Lune had turned from a den of relative peace to a place of danger which echoed with the threat of an arquebus that sounded outside the door.

An arm threatened to halt the two escaping Romani but Agatha upturned the table in their direction.

Crimson wine spilled as the shouts grew louder and Agatha had no time to think, nor time to further defend, as an arm collided with her side and she was dragged to the other side of the moon.

“No!” She found her voice. “Rio!”

But the girl was nowhere to be seen. Not a flash of green could be made out in the melange of shouts and colours, of violence and fear.

All fight drained from Agatha then. She allowed herself to be pulled, though her eyes were wild for their desire to see Rio again. To see her laughing in the corner, the bells chiming their mirth, free and unharmed.

Jennifer crashed with Agatha through a back door and as they ran through blind alleys La Lune continued to be besieged, all spilled blood and violent thunder.

Chapter 4: Reconciliation

Chapter Summary

Agatha sees Rio again.

The flight across Paris took longer than either the two fugitives had hoped.The streets of Paris were alive with chaos and danger lurked around every corner.

For the captain of the guard to be seen fleeing with a mysterious companion was a tale Jennifer wished would never be told. But through dark ruelles and hiding in the shadows, they could not rest until they were ensconced in the shadow of their sanctuary: Notre Dame de Paris.

Agatha, for her strength and ease of her physical flight, had discarded her borrowed boots but a mile from La Lune. She’d traversed the dangerous passage back to her skyward home with her soles bare and her soul no more covered.

Jennifer abandoned Agatha at the Cathedral’s entrance and bade her safe passage to her cradle above the city.

Lilia was waiting for her when she arrived but Agatha had no words left to speak and all she had she gave to the bells as she bade them a good night one by one.

Gabriel, her great protector in sleep, was the last thing she saw. Lilia’s hand, in that dark, confusing night, was the last thing she felt, gentle against her brow as she whispered a prayer of peace and Agatha was carried into the land of slumber.

Though the coming of the dawn brought something, someone, else to Agatha’s belfry.

In the music of the bells that signaled a new day, the arrival of a Romani girl, signaled the start of something entirely other.

Agatha was sitting with Scratchy, her back to the tower that cradled her above the whole world. Between God and man.

She was talking to him in her usual manner, telling him of ruby wine and flowing skirts.

“Unlike that which you have ever seen, Ser Scratchy! Beauty as I have never known it! If I shall but live one more day I-.”

“It is unusual, is it not? To be so verbose amongst the company of stone creatures and yet so silent amongst the living.”

Agatha jumped from her spot, dexterously turning to stand upon the edge of the world as she stared across at the creature she considered to be otherworldly.

“Rio.”

“I did not wish to frighten you.”

Agatha looked behind the woman towards the chiming of her bells who were now sharing a delight in their harmony.

“I have not been followed,” Rio reassured, raising a hand with the intention of helping Agatha down.

“I simply wished to see you.”

Agatha remained still where she stood, her hand on Scratchy’s bowed head for balance. She had not expected to ever see this woman again and so her presence unnerved her.

“Do you come to this place to tease me?”

“Do you wish to be teased?”

Rio’s fingers danced to the tune of the bells and Agatha looked to them but could not take them.

“I wish,” Agatha started but could not continue for she could not speak the truth and could not lie.

She jumped from her place on the balastrade but Rio did not drop her hand as she landed.

“Allow me to show you the way out.”

“I should hope to find it only when you are to leave with me.”

Agatha, who had taken strides now around the dark-haired beauty, stopped. She turned.

“I… do you need protection?”

“No, my Agatha, I am to leave this city tonight with my people. I desire for you to come with us.”

Rio, too, had turned. Her hand remained held out to Agatha.

“You are here to tease.”

“Let it be known, I am not. I should never tease about something I am so intent on.”

Agatha swallowed, her eyes drifting to the hand that remained held out to her.

“I cannot go with you,” Agatha sinned her soul as she spoke these words, knowing them to be the antithesis of her desires. “I belong to the bells, to this city, to God, and to… to my mother.”

Rio’s hand fell until it rested upon her skirt.

“You pay them all a great compliment. But it is not a true one.”

The gypsy stepped closer and Agatha stepped back.

“You are your own person, Agatha de Notre Dame. I wish only you belonged to yourself. I do not wish to trap you or enclose you. I wish you to be as the rush of the river and the wind through the meadow.”

Rio stepped closer once more. This time Agatha did not shy away.

She stepped until her raising hands came into contact with Agatha’s flushed cheeks and Agatha shivered as the bells tolled their last.

“Come with us. Come with me. Allow me to show you the freedoms you long should have experienced.”

A gentle brush of a warm thumb and Agatha quivered for the want of more of it. She could have given her life in that moment for another second of Rio’s attentions.

“May I stay with you a while?”

The Romani woman’s words were carried on the breeze, as melodious as Emmanuelle’s song.

Agatha spoke not, nor moved to intimate that she should like that very much. Only, Rio seemed to gain an understanding in her silence. For she dropped her hand from Agatha’s face and instead found her rough fingers in her own soft ones.

Agatha was walked back through to her belfry where Rio indicated Agatha’s great protector.

“Tell me of them. The bells. Teach me of their natures. For I know they have distinct ones or you would not be so protective of them all, so interested in their wellbeing.”

Agatha opened her mouth, her eyes trained on the profile of Rio’s jaw as she looked up to Gabriel. But the woman before her had anticipated her next words: “Do not mistake me, I still do not tease.”

Silence, the kind where thoughts speak louder than anything. Agatha lived in this silence more often than not. And now she experienced it with Rio for the first time.

She looked down to the hand that still held hers and for a moment, for that was all she would allow herself, she watched as a thumb moved back and forth across her skin.

“Gabriel,” she whispered into the quiet as she raised Rio’s hand and moved her own to cover it, holding it in such a way that Rio’s palm could soon press upon the metal of her protector.

“He watches me sleep and rings out in a low pitch that soothes my soul.”

“Beautiful,” Rio whispered, her hand now moving of its own accord with Agatha’s simply resting above it. “Un ange.”

Agatha’s lips twitched at the words whispered and she nodded as she gazed upon Gabriel and Rio turned to gaze upon her.

They remained like that, stuck in time, as Agatha took in Gabriel and Rio took in Agatha.

But the bells called out in their stoicism and soon Agatha was introducing Rio to them all. Her last, the finest in all of Notre Dame, Emmanuelle, was lost entirely to Rio’s gaze as she was now focused so on the bellringer herself.

“A bourdon,” Agatha explained, her gaze upwards and her mind on Emmanuelle. “The lowest of my bells. His tone can be heard for miles around the city. I yearn to hear him from beyond these walls.”

Rio’s fingers twitched below Agatha’s against the swell of Emmanuelle’s casing and blue eyes darted down to their joined hands.

“Come with me, Agatha. My Agatha. Live in freedom with me.”

Agatha turned from the grand bell, turned from Rio, and took strides away towards the mouth of the belfry. She sought air, though her tower was in no shortage of it.

The Romani girl had followed, and Agatha soon felt her hand curl around her upper arm.

“I desire you not hide from me.”

Agatha hid from the world with her eyes closed and her head down. In her belly she felt the warm shame of her own cowardice. There was a remedy to it, of course, but she could not swallow it.

Rio had moved now to stand before her. Agatha felt her warm had against her cheek, she felt her warm breath across her face. Before mind or body could react, she felt her soft lips against her own.

Like all creatures born in sorrow and seclusion, Agatha lived mostly in her own mind. She traversed the small portion of the world she was caged in, trapped between Heaven and earth, lost often in her most comforting daydreams.

But this, this token of affection from a raven-haired beauty, was more than she would ever allow herself to conjure even in her most desperate nights.

The kiss was broken as Agatha began to return it and Rio breathed gently against her parted lips: “Forgive me. For I have wanted to do that since the very first moment I saw you.”

Agatha gazed upon the woman, whose large eyes were now full of trepidation unlike Agatha had ever seen.

“Do you tease?”

“My Agatha. You are safe now. I shall keep you safe.”

Agatha had strength enough to smile, her lips twitching to return the soft kiss delivered unto them. But sleep, sleep crept in and stole her from her beloved.

As she rested, Rio cleaned and tended to her wounds. She would not let another witness the act for she knew Agatha was shy and would wish for only her.

After hours of careful labour, Rio tucked herself into the crook of Agatha’s arm and pulled many blankets around them to ensure her bell-ringer felt their warmth as she slept.

As a new day dawned, Agatha thought she could hear the bells welcome her to waking. She shifted upon the thin canvas that held her above the ground and felt the presence of another sharing her bed.

“Rio,” she breathed, finding it hard to say anything else though she meant: “You are here. I have searched for you. You are so beloved of me.”

Rio, who had not slept, raised carefully to look upon Agatha’s face. She saw the ghosts of her torments there and could have cried for the pain she knew Agatha must be in. Her strong, determined, gentle bell-ringer, whom she loved above all else.

It was Nicolás her thoughts too drifted to as she gazed into those blue eyes.

“My Agatha, should you wish to meet your son?”

“Rio,” Agatha whispered, though she meant: “Yes, more than I have ever wished for anything.”

Rio kissed her then, promising she would soon be back.

Agatha lay in wait and watched her go. Her body, though wrapped in healing linens and covered with honey and herbal poultices, ached and burned that which she could not express.

But as Rio appeared again carrying a little boy with fair skin and blue eyes the colour of the ocean, her pain fell away.

“Nicolás,” the first word she had been able to breathe that was not her beloved’s in days. She raised her arms, barely an inch if anything at all, and Rio delivered the baby into them.

For Agatha’s dread and fear that she should not be wanted by this child, he curled immediately to her, his little fist finding purchase upon her hair as Agatha turned her face into his own.

She breathed him in, then welcomed Rio back to her.

Agatha de Notre Dame died thereafter in the arms of her ever-loving family.

To this day the bells ring in the dawn with the melody she gave them, in remembrance of their bell-ringer, her beloved Rio, and their son, Nicolás.
