A Refusal of Endings

The house is still; a somber lull has taken the place of the usual chaos that seeps from the walls on any given Monday morning.

Agatha sits at the end of the couch, eyes fixed on the young red oak tree outside the bay window of the living room. A phantom of its caregiver dances across the lawn as if seating the soil around the small trunk to ensure steady growth. The reddish-brown buds are multiplying on the sparse limbs, whispering, “I’m alive. I’m here.”

A shaky breath abrades its way out of Agatha’s lungs as she wrings her hands in her lap, bringing them up around her shoulders, hoping that the subtle touch will hold her together; will stop the echoing of fractures in her soul.

A grating sound pierces the silence around her, dragging her from the vision of life just on the other side of the pane. With a sigh, she pushes up from her seat and moves across the room, bare feet padding on the hardwood; A silent plea resonating in her mind, “Please don’t wake him up. I’m not ready.”

As she makes her way past the foot of the stairs, she hesitates, searching for any sound from the bedroom at the landing. After holding her breath for a beat, she finds herself feeling strangely grateful, if only for a moment, that the world has stayed quiet. 

Turning the corner, she makes her way into the laundry room at the back of the house. The smell of fresh detergent and bleach scents the air around her, allowing a respite, almost resembling normalcy. 

A moment passes as she stares at the machine; she wonders if she just leaves the load in the wash, never opens the lid, would she have to face what’s inside? 

A hand reaches out, her body telling her she has to keep going. Her mind, on the other hand, is still desperately searching for ways to evade the inevitable.

She steadies herself, reaching into the tub. Fingers brush against the polyester, her throat threatening to close in on the breath caught there. A gentle pull, and the garment is released from the bundle. As it unfurls in her hands, the name Harkness-Vidal flashes. Arching across the back, nestled above the number 2. Its stitching, brilliant in indigo, dominates the white landscape of the jersey. With a steady breath, she shakes the jersey, a soft pop reverberates in the small space. With a swift motion, she turns the shirt, eyes screwed shut, hoping that when they open, the reminder will no longer be there. 

The lurch in her gut, the gnawing ache in her chest, the prick of warm tears, provide the answer.

Strung across the front of the jersey, a twisted constellation of pain, of grief. Splotches, russet in color, meet her stare. Some so small they look like a speck of dirt, others condense so tightly, they form a pool that spreads across the tainted thread with a vicious reach.

Her blue eyes dart from mark to mark, tethering them, as if somehow she can piece together the events and reverse every second of the last fifty-two hours.



*      *      *  



“You think they’re going to win today?”

Agatha turns, her hand shielding her eyes from the early morning sun as she looks up at the woman walking toward her, “Stranger things have happened” she says with a snort. 

Wanda stalls beside her, dropping the chair slung over her shoulder to the ground, “At what age do we tell our kids they’re just not cut out for something?”

Agatha grins, dropping her sunglasses down on her face, “Seven is probably too early. But if I have to keep waking up at the ass-crack of dawn to come sit out here all day and act like I’m having the time of my life, I might just have to tough-love the kid.”

“Where’s Rio?” Wanda asks as she unfolds her chair and settles in beside Agatha.

“Headed back to the house,” Agatha yawns, reaching for her coffee. “Nicky grabbed his baseball  jersey this morning instead of his soccer one and we didn’t realize it until we pulled up.”

“You trust your son to keep track of his jerseys?” Wanda chuckles, “Hell, I can’t even trust the boys to find underwear most days.”

“Well — ” Agatha rebuts, dragging her hands through her unruly mane, “Rio was supposed to set out his uniform this morning, but she forgot. So she told Nicky to grab it out of the laundry room as we were walking out the door. He just grabbed the wrong one.”

Wanda hums, “Ah, I see.”

“Speaking of boys…” Agatha muses, stretching in the chair, “where is Vis this morning?”

“He’s still in Boston. The build is going way over schedule and he’s hoping to secure additional financing at some dinner tonight.” Her brow furrows as she speaks and Agatha knows not to pick at the topic too much.

“You guys doing alright?” she asks, nudging Wanda's arm.

The woman beside her slides on her best smile and nods, “Yep” she says flatly.  

Agatha leans in closer, “Ya know… I can always tell Rio to bring some prosecco back with her, we have OJ here in the cooler.” She winks, “A few mom-mosas to get the weekend started off right. Whatdaya say?”

At this, a genuine smile crests across Wanda’s features, “Yes, I’m sure Corporal Kale over there would love that.”

“Eh” Agatha huffs, waving her hand dismissively, “You know that holistic-holier-than-thou bitch puts down at least one bottle of cheap Target wine a night.”

Wanda laughs, eyeing the statuesque woman across the field, “She’ll be on us like a fly on shit. Going on and on about us being those parents.”

Agatha smiles, a knowing glint in her eyes,  “We are those parents, Maximoff. Your sons are running around free-balling it and my son is wearing a baseball jersey on a soccer field.”

“Fuck it — ” Wanda laughs, “tell Rio to grab it.”

With a swift swipe of her thumb, Agatha unlocks her phone and begins typing out the added request to her wife. She hits send just as her son comes running over, “Mama, I need my jersey. Coach says I can’t play without it.”

“Nicky, baby, Mami is at the house right now, she’s grabbing it. Don’t worry.”

Nicky looks despondently, huffing as he toes the grass with his cleat, “But the game starts in a few minutes, Mama. I need it.”

Agatha leans forward, pulling him to her by the front of his jersey, “You need it” she mimics as he smiles. 

“Yeah,” he laughs, curling onto her lap, “I can’t wear this one.”

Agatha nuzzles his neck, enjoying the smell of her clean son before it’s replaced with the body odor of a sweaty boy covered in grime. “But I thought we were at a baseball game,” she jokes. 

His giggles fill the space around her, “No mama, today is soccer!”    

Agatha pulls back, a stunned look across her face as she glances questioningly between the woman beside her and her son, “Wanda, did you know we were at a soccer game?”

Wanda, feigning shock, leans back in her chair, hand across her chest, “We’re at a what?! I swear today was baseball. Nicholas, are you sure?”

The boy playfully rolls his eyes, slipping off his mothers lap onto the ground with a thud, “You guys…” he sighs, resigned to accept his mothers game.

“Okay” Agatha puffs, lifting from her chair. “I’ll go talk to your coach.” 

She reaches for Nicky’s hand, pulling him up and watching him scamper off with a broken “Thanks, mama!”

“Be right back” she says to Wanda as she begins to cross the field.

As she approaches the group of parents on the other side of the green, she can’t help but feel the glare coming from the woman just a few feet away from her.

“Harkness…”

She turns her head, “Kale.”

“You do know that Nicholas is wearing a baseball jersey, right?”

Agatha’s nose scrunches and her eyes narrow, “Why, Jennifer Kale, nothing gets past you, does it? What would we all do without your keen observations.”

Jennifer sneers, lifting her chin, “Agatha, you can’t just have your kid running around in whatever he wants. We have rules, uniforms.”

With a hand on her hip, Agatha makes a show with an animated glace to her left, then back at the foe before her, “You know, Kale, maybe you should spend less time worrying about what my kid is wearing and more time asking yourself why your kid is sitting under the bleachers, alone, eating grass.”

This knocks Jennifer off her perch as she glances over at her son and makes her way over to him, mumbling something about drinking grass and not eating it.

Satisfied with her win, Agatha turns her attention back to the task at hand.

“Bohner,” she shouts.

The man spins on his heels, albeit slowly, with a knowing dread in his eyes, “Agatha…” he drawls. 

With her hands splayed at her sides, she asks, “Did you really tell Nicky he couldn’t play today?”

“What the hell, Ralph” says the shorter woman beside him.

“Alice, he doesn't have a jersey, I — Agatha, he isn’t in uniform, he can’t —”

“Cut the crap, Bohner, even your assistant coach thinks that’s a bullshit move” Agatha says as she sidles up to them. “Alice, Rio’s on her way back with his jersey, can’t he just swap out shirts with whoever is on the bench until she gets back?”

Ralph stares between the two women as they eye him closely, both with expressions that he knows he will never win against. 

Pinching the bridge of his nose, he lowers his head, “How far out is she? The game starts in five.”

Agatha rolls her eyes, “Bohner, you know we only live eight minutes from here, she’ll be here just after the whistle.”

“Fine,” he barks, “he can wear Marcus’ jersey. His mom doesn't want him playing today anyway.”

Agatha smiles tightly and pushes past him, headed for the bench full of chattering children. 

“Nicky” she calls, kneeling in front of him and lifting her glasses to rest atop her crown, “Coach Ralph is going to give you Marcus’ jersey until Mami gets here with yours, okay?”

Nicky nods excitedly, “Yes! I get to play!”

Smiling, Agatha lifts and kisses the top of his head, “Alright, go get ‘em, buddy! Love you.”

Nicky is already off and running down towards Ralph as he explains to the other parents why he needs their son's jersey. Agatha takes some satisfaction in the irritation and resignation on his features as they question him. She drops her shades and turns to head back to her seat on the sane side of the field.

“I see you won that battle” Wanda muses as she takes a drink from her water bottle. 

Agatha shrugs her shoulders and drops back down beside her friend, “If they’re gonna talk shit behind my back and make me the Witch of Westview, then so be it.”

Wanda leans forward, dropping the bottle back into the cooler full of drinks and snacks at Agatha's feet, “Your phone was going off while you were over there, FYI.”

Reaching, Agatha can see she has a missed call from Rio. She swipes the screen and tucks the phone to her ear, listening to the familiar ring.

“Hey” Rio chimes, “I’m about six minutes away, I had already left the house when you texted. I flipped back around and grabbed the bottle of Lamarca we had in the fridge.”

Agatha can hear the soft hum of the car static, “That’s fine. Bohner was not going to let Nicky play. Can you believe that shit-stain?”

A soft laugh eases its way through the speaker, “Fucker. Did you get it sorted out or am I going to be in the doghouse with our son for the foreseeable future?”

“Nah, he’s going to wear Marcus’ jersey until you get here. His hypochondriac of a mother didn’t want him to play today because he apparently sneezed too hard this morning. That poor kid will never have a life.”

“Well, that’s good that he can still play. I really am sorry. In my defense, you had me pretty distracted this morning.”

Agatha feels a familiar warmth creep up her neck and across her cheeks, “Yeah well, if you had gotten out of bed when I told you to, we wouldn’t have been running late. Instead you were a brat. I have no regrets for my actions.”

Wanda glances over at the playfully sinful tone in Agatha’s voice and shakes her head, “You two are disgusting” she mumbles with a laugh.

“Oh—” Rio chimes, “So this is back to being all my fault.”

“Honey, it’s always your fault, haven’t you learned that by now”, Agatha says with a soft smile.

“Yeah, Yeah…” there’s a pause on the line, “I am sorry though. Did give us an excuse to have mimosas though, so not a total loss.”

Agatha's attention is drawn to the center of the field where Nicky and the others begin to gather, “Rio, the game’s starting, I gotta go.”

“Mmkay” fills the line, “be there in a sec. L—”

Agatha ends the call as the whistle blows, dropping the phone into the netted cup holder in the chair as she stands, cheering beside Wanda as the kids begin to play. 

 

*      *      *  

 

The memory of Saturday morning slithers around her as she traces the blood stains over and over across the jersey. How easy life had been before that morning. How full it was. Tendrils of anger licked at her heart, this isn’t fair, she thinks, gritting her teeth.

“This isn’t fair” she snarls aloud, finally feeling the dam break.

With the quick snap of her arm, the jersey leaves her hands with ferocity, slamming against the wall and crumpling to the floor. A sense of urgency worms its way in, hinting at the fear and pain coming next, so she turns quickly, rips the rest of the clothes out of the washer, throwing each piece carelessly on the floor beside the discarded uniform. Drops of loose water pepper her face as she yanks with abandon; soaking the sleeves of her sweatshirt, pooling at her feet. She doesn't stop until she hears a loud thud land in the corner adjacent to her. Until that moment, the material beneath her touch was a blur, meaningless items meant to relieve her momentary need for destruction. But that sound, she knew what was waiting behind her.

She pivots slowly, cautiously, as if the item on the floor is a feral animal she is to fear. 

Lying across the space, was a single shoe; a black Converse. The edges were worn, the black fabric faded with time, white strings lay haphazardly across the dark wood of the floorboards. The only indication that it was involved in anything but a well-worn life, are those same russet splotches that pepper the white lacing that threads through the eyelets. A twin to the jersey beside it. 

The woman reaches, pausing after a step, it’s just a shoe , she tells herself. Leaning down, her long fingers curl into the heel of the shoe and she lifts the item with a gentleness reserved for only the most precious things in her life.     

With reverence, she pulls the shoe to her chest and suddenly it becomes her most significant possession. “I need more time,” she whispers. The plea comes over and over as she backs against the wall and slides down, “I need more time. I need more time.”

She lets the tears fall freely, allowing the sobs to rack her body in the quiet of the small room. Her head falls back against the paneled wall, eyes trained toward the ceiling, “I can’t do this,” she whimpers, “I can’t do this without you.”

Time slows in this space, a gentle mercy from the devastation occurring within Agatha's mind. She isn’t sure how long she sits against the wall, clinging to a piece of her wife. She figures it’s been awhile based on the near dryness of the rumpled clothing strewn across the floor. With a stretch of her hand, a pair of jeans finds its way into her lap. The denim, dark in color, hides the evidence of trauma better than the lighter clothing. But the torn fabric, carelessly cut away, is quick to remind Agatha of what was endured.   

As her fingers run cautiously across the frayed strands, she remembers fragments of the conversation from fifteen hours ago. A woman leading her to a quiet room down a sterile hall, hiding her away from the rest of the world. Words spoken in hushed tones, rehearsed compassion seeping into each syllable as the older woman spoke. 

“Go home,” she said gently, “there’s nothing you can do right now.”

The woman held out a clear bag, the contents oddly resembling the clothing her wife had been wearing the morning before. Agatha remembered thinking to herself how peculiar it was that this stranger was handing her a bag of clothing that looked just like what Rio had on when she kissed her goodbye at the soccer field and told her she’d be right back. Tucked inside the bag was also a small envelope, it drew Agatha’s attention as the woman continued to speak, her words dying on her lips, never registering. Agatha unsealed the bag, a soft zipping sound surrounded her, and reached in to grab the envelope. With a gentle squeeze, the flap released and into Agatha’s palm fell a single platinum band. It felt foreign in her hand; heavy. She gently sat the bag down behind her, turning away from the woman, and rolled the band through her fingertips.

The words drifted through her mind as she watched the harsh light above dance across the metal… With this ring, I thee wed..

Pulling back from the memory, Agatha glances down at the hand running across the jeans, that same band now sits on her left ring finger, nestled against her own for safe keeping. It had never left Rio's hand in the last ten years and 8 months. A warm feeling surrounds Agatha when she spins it on her finger, the engraving inside the band gently grating across her skin, Ever Mine . The corners of her lips pull, just slightly, as she spins her own band along with it, knowing that beneath the warmth of her own sit the words, Ever Thine . 

Her attention pulls from her hand as she arches her neck enough to see the foot of the stairs. Thoughts drift up to the little boy at the top. Sleeping soundly in his bed beneath the large wooden sign, hand crafted by Rio during one of her many fleeting notions to learn a new skill.

 

“I have a surprise” Rio coos as she steps in from the garage. 

Agatha looks up from her place on the couch, legs stretch out before her on the table, her hand resting gently at the curve of her swollen belly, “Is it an eviction notice for this freeloader? He’s refusing to leave and I have shit to do.”

Circling the couch, Rio gently places something on the floor behind Agatha and takes a seat beside her. “Unfortunately, no, I do not have an eviction notice for you, but I can think of a few things we can do to make him feel less inclined to stay” she says suggestively, dragging her fingers down the length of her wife's arm.

“Mmm, I like that idea too,” she hums. “So what did you make me? You smell like a sawmill.”

Rio grins, reaching behind the couch and pulling a large piece of art, wooden planks nailed together, over their heads with ease, “Well, it’s not really for you, but…”

As Rio stands to spin the piece, Agatha feels pride and the overwhelming urge to cry at the sight. Before her, her wife holds a sign, the wood painted a powder blue and distressed, with the words Ever Ours carved into the piece.

Rio looks at her wife with a soft shrug, “I thought we could hang it in his room over his crib” she says quietly. 

 

Agatha had been able to shield him from this for the last two days with the help of Wanda and her husband. But now, alone in this house, this newly empty shell of a home, it’s just her.

She will have to wake him up.

She will have to hold him.

She will have to tell him why his Mami isn’t there.

She will have to be enough for him right now.

Her throat grows tight as the air is extracted from her lungs, “I’m not ready” she whispers between broken heart beats.

She sits a few moments more, no tears fall; she has nothing left to give right now. She just… is. 

As she eyes the mess she's made of the room, a sense of needing to clean up takes over. She thinks about how it makes no sense to have even done the load of laundry, how cleaning it up even now will make no difference in her life. But she accepts that it is something she can control. She can re-wash the jersey, use more bleach this time. She can pick through the remnants of the clothing and save what can be saved; what wasn’t cut or torn from her wifes body.

She can pull herself up and move.

She knows she has to, not living is no longer an option.

But in this moment, she struggles to find the how in it all. 

A soft vibration at her hip brings her back to the moment, her eyes blinking away the dryness they now hold. She reaches in the pocket of her three day old jeans and retrieves the device drawing her attention. 

The screen illuminates with a message from Wanda.

 

W: How are you holding up?

A: I just destroyed my laundry room. Currently clinging to a shoe, and wondering how I’m going to face Nicky when he wakes up. All things considered…

W: Just left the house. I’m headed to you now, grabbing us some coffee and breakfast. You need to eat. 

A: I’m not hungry

W: Tough shit, Agatha. You need to eat and I will make sure you do. 

A: Hell of a thing for a friend to say to someone in my shoes

W: Would you prefer the alternative?

A: No

 

She closes the message and opens the one listed as My Love, knowing that the automated message that shattered her life would still be there.

 

Crash Detected SOS

Rio’s iPhone initiated Emergency SOS at 8:32am because Rio’s iPhone detected a crash at this approximate location. Rio’s iPhone has initiated contact with Emergency Response Services. You are receiving this message because Rio’s iPhone has you listed as an Emergency Contact.



*      *      *  

 

“Clear the ball, Tommy!” Wanda shouts from her seat, “I swear,” she says now directly to Agatha, “that kid is terrified of confrontation.”

Agatha smiles, eyes on her son as he lines up to receive the ball. She leans forward, elbow resting on her knees as she claps, “Let’s go, Nicky! You got it, baby!”

Wanda glanced at her watch, then turned slightly, eyeing the parking lot, “I thought you said Rio was only a few minutes away when she called.”

“I did” Agatha answers dismissively, still watching the kids on the pitch. “Come on, boys, you got this!”

“Did she have to stop somewhere else?” Wanda asks inquisitively. 

Agatha leans back and throws her arm over the back of her chair, “Why are you suddenly curious about my wife's whereabouts?” She asks with the arch of her brow. 

“It’s been fifteen minutes, Agatha”

Agatha adjusts in her seat, moving to pull her phone out and check the time, “Pfft, no it hasn’t. She just cal—” 

Wanda watches as her friend turns white, the air around her leaving her breath in a shuddered exhale, then the panic crawls across her features. “Agatha, what is it?”

Agatha stays frozen, eyes trained on whatever is on her screen.

“Agatha…” Wanda asks again.

Suddenly the woman jerks to life and frantically starts swiping on the screen, screwing the phone to her ear.

Two rings… Hey, you’ve reached Rio, please leave me a message.

“No, no, no” she whispers, hitting redial.

One ring…Hey, you’ve reached Rio, please leave me a message.

“Fuck. Fuck. Fuck.” she mumbles, her hands shaking, causing the last redial to fail.

“Nononononono… pick up the phone…please” her thumb slams the screen again.

Straight to voicemail…Hey, you’ve reached Rio, please leave me a message.

As her fingers fly furiously across the screen, she sends off message after message.

 

A: Rio, are you okay?

Nothing. 

A: Answer me, ARE YOU OKAY?

Nothing. 

A: Rio, please…

Nothing. 

 

An eternity passes while she waits, Wanda now kneeling before her, cradling her hands, “Agatha, what the fuck is going on” she asks desperately. 

She’s met with the deepest blue, tears threatening to fall from the corners of her eyes.

As Agatha tries to speak, her voice cracks and she swallows back the sob lodged in her throat. The world around her goes dark, only the redhead in front of her is visible. Wanda is struck by the sheer terror she sees in Agatha’s eyes. They dart, unyielding, like a wild animal ensnared and desperately seeking a way out. 

“Ri—, Rio. I…I got th—” The finite grip on her phone loosens as she shows her friend the screen, the gray message box with the SOS message. “She’s… she’s not answering me, Wanda” she says quietly, “Wh—, why is she not answering me, Wanda” she questions, near a beg.

Wanda feels the panic start to settle into her own chest, but she steels herself against it, reaching over and ripping her keys from her bag, “Take the van. I’ll stay with Nicky.”

Agatha pauses, lost in a myriad of thoughts. Only when Wanda shoves the keys in her hand does she come back, “O—, Okay. Yeah. Okay, I’ll uh… yeah. Just make something up, don’t tell him anything” she stumbles out of her chair and turns, her vision finally clearing enough for her to see Nicky across the field, playing, blissfully unaware, with his friend, a smile on his face. “Thank you, Wanda,” she says, slipping off to the parking lot, still trying to regain her balance. 

Once she’s in Wanda’s van, she clicks on the location pin that accompanied the SOS message, “1400 Old Salem Road, Westview, NJ”. She gasps, gripping the steering wheel with her free hand, Rio is two blocks away. No updated SOS message has come through, she’s still there. 

The memory of sirens wailing just minutes ago in the distance plagues her as she remembers the words she said to Wanda when she heard them. Someone's day just got worse. Bile pools in the back of her throat and she hunches over, throwing the door open to throw up. She hastily wipes her mouth with her sleeve as she turns the key in the ignition and pulls out of the lot.  

The drive was a blur, two lefts, one right and suddenly she was approaching an intersection with emergency response vehicles at all corners. Her chest tightens and her breath stills as she throws the van into park on the side of the small road. She throws the door open, but as soon as her foot hits the ground, she stops. Her mind is screaming to keep moving, to sprint with every fiber of her being. She’s glued to this spot. The flashes of red and blue capturing her attention. Bodies swarm the intersection, EMS, police, fire, all there, all forming a wall to prevent the scene from unfurling before Agatha’s eyes.

Her eyes follow the bodies, this way, that way as they scurry, until finally they land on her wifes  hunter green Defender; upside down, thrown violently against a light pole that now leans over the vehicle like it's kneeling in prayer. The bile returns to her throat, but she swallows it back down, driving her feet forward. Slow at first, but picking up speed as she feels the pull to find her wife in the chaos. As she nears, she can hear people shouting to stop her, to grab her before she gets too close. The voices only spur her on more, driven to ensure nothing comes between her and getting to Rio. 

She is close enough to smell the gasoline and melted tire-tread when a pair of arms grabs at her waist and pulls her back. Hot tears are streaming down her face as she struggles against the hold, clawing at the man, ripping at his hands, thrashing in his arms, doing anything she can to be freed. 

“Agatha!” he shouts, “Agatha stop, please” he pleads, holding her tighter now, wrapping his hands around her forearms to stop her from hurting him, from hurting herself.

“I have to get to her, let me go!” she screams, her voice breaking over and over as she repeats it, “I have to get to her! Rio! Let me g—”

“She’s gone” he says trying to hold her, his strength waning against her resolve, “she’s not—”. 

The world claps around her and begins to fold in on itself. Bright flashes of light fill her senses, a cacophony of noise, voices, memories, and sounds flood her existence. She breaks. Her wails are from somewhere deep, a place she never knew existed. One that surely only takes root in someone when the very fabric of their being is ripped from their body. They claw from her vocal chords, snapping at the very threads that have held her together up to this point. Her body finally lets go of the fight and she falls lifeless in his arms, wailing. The officer walks her down to the ground with a tenderness, trying to not let her collapse on the asphalt.

Her fists ball, and begin pounding his chest, “No” she pleads, “No, no, no, no…” She refuses to open her eyes, refuses to accept the words she just heard.

There is not a world where Rio doesn’t exist; where Agatha does not wake up to her every morning and fall asleep with her every night. There is no universe in which Agatha ever has to go a day without hearing Rio tell her that she loves her; watch her with their son. No. That does not exist, she refuses to accept that.  

“Shhh” the man says, holding her against him tightly, “she’s not here, they just took her to Westview Gen.”

Agatha swallows, the pain is unforgiving, there is no way she has heard him right. The moment of hope, of belief that death had not come for her wife, it’s surely a fantasy her mind is creating to protect her. Right?

She forced herself to push off against the man, his badge scraping across her cheek as his face came into view.

Herb. Her neighbor, and local police sergeant. 

She feels an ungodly anger building up in her. Her brow furrows, pupils narrow tightly as her teeth grit together, “You said she was gone…” she seethes.

He shakes his head, loosening his grip on her, “I’m sorry, I— poor choice of words, I just needed you to stop, It came out wrong, I’m so sor—”

The calm of the room is disturbed when Dr. Hart walks in, “Ready to take it out?” he says with such detachment, as if it’s not the scariest moment for two of the people in the room with him.

“Oh you have no idea. I had so many dirty thoughts about you when I was asleep.”

Agatha looks shocked, “No you didn’t!”

Rio grins, her full smile coming back to life, “No. I didn’t. But you should see your face right now.”

“I hate you” Agatha groans, dropping her head gently on her wifes chest.

Rio carefully brings her hand up and cups the back of Agatha’s head, lacing her fingers through the loose ponytail she has pulled back, careful not to tangle her IV in the strands. “I love you,” she hums softly. 

They stay like that for a few minutes. Agatha can feel her soul coming back to life under the gentle ministrations of Rio’s fingers against her scalp. Her head, nestled back where it belongs, feeling the steady rise and fall of Rio’s chest; feeling the soft drumming of a heartbeat she thought she had lost against her temple. Rio had come back to her. Intact, albeit a little worse for wear.

“Thank you” Agatha speaks, low and soft, her eyes finding purchase in the soft brown ones above her, “...for coming back.”

Rio looks down at her, a swell of emotion growing, extracting warm tears from her eyes as she lifts her other hand, more painful than the first thanks to the bandaged arm and shoulder it’s attached to, and brushes her thumb across her cheek. Softly, she begins to speak, voice low and measured, “And I’d choose you; in a hundred lifetimes, in a hundred worlds, in any version of reality, I’d find you and I’d choose you.”

The quote that they had recited together in their vows all those years ago. The promise they made to one another that no matter what, they would always find one another, they would always choose one another. That they would always come back to each other. 

Agatha doesn't move, fresh tears drop feely against the hospital gown beneath her. She waits a moment more before she turns her head and places a long kiss against Rio’s chest, “Speaking of vows…” She sits back and slips the platinum band off her ring finger and gently grabs Rio’s left hand, “this belongs to you” she says softly, slipping the wedding band around the vacant finger, sealing the act with a kiss against the metal. “Try not to lose that again, okay.”

Rio smiles, her eyes growing tired, “I promise.”

Agatha brushes the hair from Rio’s face gently, then wipes the tears from both of their eyes, “You should rest, baby.”

With a nod of her head, Rio agrees. “You should too” she says, eyeing the neatly folded blankets and pillows left on the small couch in the room. 

Agatha feels the heaviness of the day and agrees, “Okay” she says with a soft kiss against her wifes lips, “you’re right.” As she stands, she turns back, “do you need anything? More blankets? Water?”

Rio reaches out and takes her hand once more, “Baby, go lay down. You’re exhausted. I’m okay. I’ll be right here when you wake up.”

The words echo in Agatha’s mind; how differently they were spoken just hours ago. 

Agatha steals one last kiss before making her way across the room to the pleather couch. She stops at the light switch, turning the overheads off and leaving them in the dim glow of the small lamps above Rio’s bed and the slits of sunlight sneaking through the dark blinds.

She unfurls the blanket and sheets and tosses them freely across the cool material beneath. With a quiet sigh, she sits, slipping her shoes off and tucking her legs in against the rough, sterile cloth. Her head finally finds purchase on the pillow beneath and she adjusts slightly, so that she can see Rio. 

Her wife is already asleep, lips parted slightly as the breath enters and exits her body. Her head is nestled against the pillow; if it weren't for the oxygen tube strung across her features, and the soft beeping of the monitor, Agatha could almost believe they were home. It was enough, for now. Enough to allow her eyes to close and her body to relax. 

 

 


Agatha is awakened by the soft murmurs of conversation. Before her eyes open, she smiles at the sound of Rio’s voice as it drifts low, and circles around her.

“Okay, so explain this to me again, if this one is past—”

“Death, reversed.” Calderu nods.

“Okay… and this one is present…”

“The lovers, upright.”

“Sure… those two are pretty easy to figure out, but what is this one? The future card.”

“The Fool, upright.” 

Rio looks down at the three cards laid out before her, “Should I be worried about The Fool?” she says with a questioning giggle.

Calderu laughs, “No hun. The Fool, upright, typically means a fresh start. Seem’s kind of on the nose for you, don’t you think.”

Pausing for a moment, Rio stares at the Death card, “I saw her, you know” she says quietly.

“Saw who?” Calderu asks with curiosity.

“Death” Rio responds, flat and even. 

The older woman stills, watching her patient for a moment, “is that so…”

Rio tapped the card lightly, “Can I tell you something strange, Lilia?”

“Of course, dear. What is it?”

Rio takes a breath, “I saw myself. Like, outside the car.” She pauses, trying to piece together the brief memory of the moment before she blacked out. “I wasn’t afraid, really. I remember moving my head to the side and seeing someone walk up beside me, I could hear the broken glass under their steps. I remember thinking how odd it was that someone would just walk over to a wreck and stand there. The person knelt down and looked at me, cocked her head, like ‘ what have we here ’ ya know?”

Calderu hums, pushing Rio to continue.

“It was me” Rio says, still staring at the card, “But like, not, me. If that makes sense. I don’t know. It wasn’t like how people describe an out of body experience; I was well aware of the way my body was hanging upside down. I just remember staring at her and knowing she was Death.” She pauses, shaking the thought free and releasing a cleansing laugh, “God, that sounds so crazy to say out loud. You must think I'm Insane.”

“Not at all,” the woman says, reaching to lay a hand on the one still pressed to the card, “I think Death takes the form of whatever or whoever we need at that time.”

“What does that say about me that I saw myself, though?”

The woman takes a breath and looks caringly at the younger woman in the bed, “I think that it's a deeply personal event that doesn’t require you to know all the answers. For whatever reason, Death was curious about you, but not ready for you. She sent you back, maybe we thank Her for that and let the rest go.”

Rio takes a deep breath, shaking off the oddity of the memory, “Yeah, you’re right.”

“I’m always right, dear”

Rio smirks, “You’re starting to sound like my wife.”

Feeling a break in the heaviness of the conversation, Agatha shifts, rolling over to face the two women, and Rio pauses to meet her gaze, “Hey Beautiful” she says softly, “You finally got some sleep.”

Agatha tosses her legs over the edge of the couch and rolls her neck as she sits upright, “How long have I been out” she mumbles through a yawn. 

“About six hours,” Rio says.

Agatha's face drops, “Six hours?!” Why didn't you wake me up?”

Rio shrugged gingerly, “You looked so peaceful. Drool hanging from your mouth, hair stuck to your head, hell, you even farted… so peaceful. I wasn’t about to disturb that.”

Agatha buried her face in her hands with a groan, “you are the absolute worst, you know that.”

“I know, but here you are, married to me and forever tethered to me through a child. Choices were made.” Rio smiles, “Oh, your phone started going off about an hour ago, so I told Lilia here to hand it over, I was not about to let it wake you up. Wanda called a few times, so I finally answered. That was a fun moment.”

At the mention of Wanda, Agatha’s eyes go large, “Fuck, I told her I would call her as soon as you woke up.”

“She understands, baby. She was just worried about you.”

Rio pauses a moment, contemplating something, “I spoke to Nicky” she breathes. 

Agatha's eyes meet hers with a look just shy of fear and a little left of whoops .

“I didn’t tell him anything” Agatha says slowly, “I wasn’t sure how to. And things were so up and down the first two days, I honestly didn’t know what to tell him if I tried.”

Rio smiled, reaching her hand out, feeling more strength in the muscles of her arm, “Come here.”

Agatha pads her way across the room, the coolness of the tile beneath her feet grounding her as she moves. Rio scoots just enough to allow her to take a seat on the bed beside her.

“I didn’t tell him the details. He said he missed me, missed you. He wanted to know where I went, why I wasn’t at the game. Lots of questions.” She held Agatha’s hand as she continued, “I told him that I got hurt, but that I was okay. That I had to see some doctors, but that he didn’t need to worry. He asked where you had been, and I told him you were taking care of me. He asked when he could see me, see you; I told him that you would be home tonight and he could come back with you in the morning. He’s excited to skip another day of school. Slacker.”

Agatha felt a pang of guilt for not being the one to talk to Nicky about this. She just hadn’t been ready; didn’t know how to keep the crippling fear she was feeling in those two days from seeping into that conversation and scaring their son.

“He’s fine, honey” Rio says calmly, “In less than five minutes he was telling me all about how him and the boys got to stay up late each night playing Minecraft and eating pizza, and how they went out to the movies with Vis and something about them finding a frog in the backyard, but losing it in the house. Wanda was not excited to hear about that when she got back on the phone.” 

A smile finally breaks across Agatha’s features, “You’ve always been better at explaining the hard stuff.”

Rio smiles and draws her attention back to Calderu, who is updating the white board across the room, “Lilia is a witch” she says simply, abruptly changing the topic.

“Not a witch, per se, but I do dabble in readings” she hums, filling in the next section of the board, “Pain level, Rio?”

“Five” she answers easily.

“Thank you” Calderu sings as she circles the number on the board.

“So what does all this mean then,” Agatha asks, gesturing toward the cards on the table in front of her wife.

“Well,” Calderu says, sauntering back toward the two women, “Death, Reversed, pretty obvious looking at your wife here. The Lovers, upright, again, pretty obvious. And The Fool, upright, meaning something new. The general narrative of this reading is that Rio met death, told her she wasn’t done loving you, and now you and your family have second chance at life to make it whatever you want it to be.”

Rio smiles up at her, “Not bad, huh?”

Agatha leans in and places a kiss against her wife lips, “You told Death to fuck off for me… that sounds about right.”

Rio smiles before pulling her in to deepen the kiss, needing to feel her wife closer. 

 

Agatha knows that the road to recovery will be long, that Rio will have some obstacles along the way, but she knows that they’ll travel it together.  

 

The End
