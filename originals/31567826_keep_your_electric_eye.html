Summary

Roughly six months after Loki joins the Guardians of the Galaxy, Thor receives a phone call in the middle of the night from Quill, asking if he might be so kind as to cross half the cosmos to come bail them all out of prison.

Honestly, Thor’s only surprised this didn’t happen sooner.

Notes

hello! this little oneshot is part of a series, but it can stand alone. just, you know, loki’s a guardian of the galaxy now. don’t question it. or do question it, in which case the previous four installments of this series serve as a 233k word explanation :)

this plot is based on a prompt from the lovely Infinite_Monkeys, a friend whose writing i highly recommend and who gave me what is arguably one of the funniest prompts i’ve ever gotten in my entire life (let’s all ignore the fact that i was given this prompt nearly three years ago HEYO whoops)

“Hello? Who is this?”

“Thor! Hey, buddy!”

Thor squints, then pulls his phone away from his ear and checks to make sure that he did, in fact, read it correctly as an unknown number, and then brings the phone back to his ear.

“Quill? Is that you?”

“Yep, it’s me, your good old pal Star-Lord. You know, man, it is so good to hear from you!”

“It’s… um, good to hear from you, too. Er— Quill, is—?”

“I’m glad you said that, man, ‘cause, uh, I may have a teensy favor to ask. Just, you know, a little itty bitty favor. If you’re not busy or anything.”

“Is something wrong?”

“What? No. Come on. Thor. Buddy. Something wrong? With us? Nah, nothing’s wrong. Nothing’s wrong at all, everything’s— uh, everything is just peachy-keen, man. Right as rain. A-OK.”

“Quill. What’s happened?”

“Don’t worry, man, everything’s fine. We just might need a little help of the, uh, monetary variety? We may have gotten into a sort of… legal bind.”

“You…? What kind of—? Hold on. Quill. Where’s Loki?”

“See, yeah, that’s the thing. They only gave us the one phone call. Like, total, one phone call, for all… what is it, eight of us? Doesn’t make much sense, but the laws here are ass backwards, man. Anyway, since I told ‘em I’m the captain, they gave that one phone call to me, so…”

“Who’s they?”

“Oh. The guys that arrested us.”

“You were arrested?”

“Ah, ah, ah. We were arrested. All of us. And I’m running out of time on this phone call, so, uh, how fast can you make it to Erlaxnia? It’s in the third quadrant, right off the Kree empire border.”

“Quill, I’m halfway across the galaxy.”

“Yeah.”

“It’s the middle of the night on our part of Earth right now.”

“Uh-huh.”

Thor sighs, scrubbing at his face to wake himself up.

“Thor? Buddy?”

“Can you give me six hours?”

“Aw, I knew we could count on you, man! Alright, I definitely gotta go now, there’s a real big mean lookin’ dude behind me waiting for his turn — hey, hey, hey, it’s a compliment! Okay, gotta go, buddy, see you soon — hey, come on, watch the — !”

Click.

Thor doesn’t like to judge. He doesn’t.

But God, Erlaxnia is a dump.

The tiny planet is a dark, chilly, grey place perpetually blanketed in what could only generously be called fog and what Thor thinks might actually be pure noxious gas. The main metropolitan hub that houses their prison is no exception, a city bogged down by poverty and disease and squalor.

And the prison itself is massive. The building’s East wall alone seems to stretch all the way across more city blocks than Thor cares to count, thick seamless steel that extends several stories into the sky, at the top of which sits a truly startling amount of razor wire that — it’s hard to tell from this distance, but Thor is fairly certain — pops with sparks of electricity at random intervals. Watchtowers sit along the wall every twenty yards or so, and atop each tower sits a rotating spotlight that cuts through the thick twilit smog, illuminating the streets surrounding the prison and, Thor doesn't doubt, illuminating everything inside their courtyards as well.

When he gets inside and finally makes it through the stifling crowd of Erlaxnians in the front lobby to the service window, a young bald man with iridescent purple skin greets him.

Well, not so much greets him. But he acknowledges him.

“Name?”

“Oh, uh—Thor,” he says, automatically relaxing his shoulders to make himself appear smaller, leaning into the ledge to bring himself closer to the young man’s eye level. Not that he’s looked up at all from the paperwork on his desk. “Thor Odinson, King of Asgard? I’m here for my brother and his friends, it seems they were—”

“Uh-huh,” the young man interrupts. In his mouth is some kind of chewing gum, and he blows a bright green bubble and pops it with his tongue, never once looking up from the papers he’s inspecting. He scribbles a signature and some initials on the topmost paper. “Names of the prisoners?”

“Loki Odinson, Crown Prince of Asgard,” Thor says first, because surely addressing him as such will help expedite this whole process. “Along with the Guardians of the Galaxy.”

The pen halts in its squiggling trail along the paper, and the young man pauses, then rolls his eyes with a sigh. “Oh. Those guys.”

“Yes?”

“Hmm.” He stacks his papers together and places them neatly in a pile on his desk, blows another bubble and pops it on his tongue. “Right.”

“Er… Yes, well, I would appreciate it if you could—”

“Hey, Mink!” the young man shouts over Thor’s shoulder. When Thor turns to look, an officer standing by the entranceway looks up, a female Kronan easily a full head taller than Korg is, which makes her at least three heads taller than Thor. “Can you take this guy down to Cell Block 12? He’s bailing those Guardian guys out.”

The officer shrugs one shoulder, and the sound of grating rocks makes Thor wince. “They ain’t gonna release ‘em yet,” she says. “Still gotta go through interrogations.”

“Interrogations?” Thor asks. “What for?”

“You want a list, mate?” Mink asks, then shrugs again. “I got nothin’ better to do, though, so I’ll take ya to see ‘em. You’re just gonna be stuck here a while ‘fore they let ya bail ‘em out, is all.”

She bends over to grab up the weapon she’d left leaning against the wall by her hip, a bazooka nearly as large as Thor himself, and hefts it up onto her shoulder. Then she makes her way toward the doorway on the opposite side of the lobby, gesturing for him to follow, and she taps a key card to the lock so that the doors open with a hydraulic whirr and the clunk of shifting steel.

Before walking away from the young man at the desk, Thor turns and offers a smile that goes entirely unnoticed. “Thank you for your help, kind sir.”

“Mm-hmm,” he says, already having returned his attention to some other bit of paperwork.

Thor sighs, tapping the window ledge by way of a goodbye, and follows the Kronan officer through the doorway into the prison’s labyrinth of halls.

“So, uh — Mink,” Thor says. “Officer Mink?”

She gives a grunt to confirm she’s listening, or perhaps to confirm that he guessed her rank correctly, as she trudges through the hall in front of him with great clunking steps. Not exactly friendly, but not distinctly unfriendly, either. He’ll take it.

“Would you happen to know the reason for my brother’s and his friends’ arrests? It wasn’t clear on the phone.”

Officer Mink gives another grunt, and this time it almost sounds like half a laugh. “Yeah, I know what they did.”

“Would you mind?”

“Sure, let’s see, eh?” Mink says. She shifts the bazooka over to her left shoulder and looks down at a device on her right forearm that displays a series of foreign letters and numbers, rapidly shifting through data that Thor can’t even begin to comprehend. “You want ‘em individually or…?”

“Oh, just as a group is fine.”

She snorts. “Have it your way, then. Let’s see here. We got, uh… breaking and entering, trespassing, grand larceny, petty theft… hijackin’ a vehicle, failure to use a turn signal, reckless driving, destruction of public property, impersonatin’ an officer, obstruction of justice, disorderly conduct, vandalism, attempted bribery of an officer… Oh. Three counts of attempted bribery of an officer, actually.”

“That’s… quite the list,” Thor admits.

“I ain’t done, mate,” Mink says. “We got illegal use of pyrogenic weapons without a permit, illegal use of cryogenic weapons without a permit, illegal use of explosives without a permit, illegal use of gravitational field distortion weapons without a permit, unlawful assembly, riot incitement, defamation of character, suspected petty treason, suspected high treason, suspected incitement of domestic terrorism, attempt to bribe the prison warden, attempt to seduce the prison warden—”

“That’s a law?”

“Not officially, nah. Guess he had it added to their offenses anyway. Oh, uh,” Mink adds, squinting down at the device on her arm. “And double parking.”

Thor, pinching the bridge of his nose, asks, “Anything else?”

“Nothing else so far, no.”

“So far?”

“Yeah,” Mink answers, and there’s another rocks-being-ground-to-pieces sound as she shrugs and then unlocks the door at the end of this hall; they’ve transversed what feels like half the prison at this point. “You think everything on that list was done before they got arrested? The seducin’ thing was right after they got here. And the riot incitement charge was, what, half an hour ago maybe?”

She opens the door, and Thor steps through to find himself facing two wide jail cells that line the far wall.

One of them, the cell on the left, is absolutely packed with people of every conceivable shape and size. Erlaxnian, Xandarian, Krylorian, Sneeper, Kree, Easik, a mess of bodies all uncomfortably pressed together and shouting over each other in all manner of languages. Somewhere in that crowd Thor thinks he can see the top of Drax’s head. Maybe. In the front left corner, Kraglin sees Thor enter the room and offers a wave — or as much of a wave as he can manage, squished as he is against the left wall. Thor smiles and waves back.

The cell to the right, though, has far fewer occupants. There are only two, each with plenty of room to stretch out and walk around should they choose, though for now they’re both sitting on the bench.

And Thor recognizes them both immediately.

“I am Groot!”

“Hello, young one,” Thor greets him, planting his hands on his hips with a smile, and he turns an amused look at the only other occupant of Groot’s cell. “And, er… other young one.”

His brother only glances up and gives him a quick little half-hearted wave before crossing his arms over his chest again.

Loki looks quite a bit different than he did the last time Thor saw him. Or, to be more precise, he looks a lot more like Loki did about… eight, nine hundred years ago? He’s nearly a foot shorter, thin and gangly, his hair about half its usual length, all tousled and obscuring part of his face. A familiar petulant pout is set into his young features as he slouches in the jail cell bench.

“Thor? Hey! Is that Thor?”

The crowd in the cell on the left starts to move, people shifting every which way, their cacophonous and irritated voices interspersed with an occasional, “Sorry!” and a, “My bad, ‘scuse me,” as Quill makes his way to the front of the cell. He appears near the front right corner and leans into the bars of the cell — or is pressed against them by the people behind him, it’s hard to tell. One of his arms hangs out through the bars for lack of a better place to go.

“Thor! Buddy,” Quill says with a breathless smile. “So glad you could make it, man.”

“Yeah,” comes another voice from within the crowd, and there’s a lot more shouting this time from the cell’s other occupants as Rocket leaps from one of their heads to the other, clawing his way up to the cell bars so he can perch above all their heads. “Good to see ya, Sparkie. Now can you please tell these guys” — he points through the bars at Officer Mink — “that your a-hole of a brother is lying about his age? He ain’t actually a minor, he’s cheatin’ to get in the better cell—”

“I am Groot.”

“He has not always been this age, Groot!” Rocket shouts. “You just can’t tell the difference!”

“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” says the teenage version of Loki, now casually inspecting his nails.

“Rocket,” comes Gamora’s voice from somewhere Thor can’t see. “All you’ll accomplish by complaining about it is getting yet another person stuffed in this cell with us. You do realize that.”

“I would much prefer him to stay over there,” says Drax, also from somewhere Thor can’t see.

Rocket yells, “If we gotta suffer, so does he!”

Kraglin makes a face like he’s about to argue, but Mantis beats him to it.

“That does not make sense,” she shouts from somewhere in the back left corner of the cell, and there's a murmur of assent from some of the other prisoners.

“Oh, it makes sense, alright!” Rocket shouts, still clinging to the bars as high as he can get, which is a few inches over all of their heads. “It’s his stupid fault we’re here in the first place!”

Thor raises an eyebrow, glancing toward Loki. “Is that true?”

Loki glances up from his nails, shrugging one shoulder. “In a way.”

“Eh. Technically,” Quill admits, leaning his cheek into one of the cell bars. He waves his hand, the one hanging out of the cell, in a so-so gesture. “Sort of. It’s, uh, kind of a long story? Really boring, too, you wouldn’t want to hear it. Trust me. Point is… uh. Point is, you’re here! And that’s awesome, man, thank you again. I know it was a long way and kind of, you know, out of nowhere.”

“It… was, yes, but it’s no trouble,” Thor says, shooting a suspicious look in Loki’s direction. A long, very boring story sounds about as likely as… well, it actually sounds impossible, with Loki involved. “Can I ask, though? Why call me, when I’m on the other side of the galaxy? Surely you could have called someone that could have gotten here sooner.”

“Uh, I mean,” Quill says, glancing toward Loki and then in the general direction of the rest of his crew. “Aside from each other, you’re kind of the only family we got, man.”

Thor blinks, raising his eyebrows. Huh.

He has to admit, there is something oddly touching about that. He would be lying if he said there wasn’t, because apparently Loki’s new friends have not only adopted Loki into their little group with hardly a bat of an eye, but now they’ve adopted Thor into their slapped- together family as well. It truly is touching, it is.

Except there are times that Thor’s not sure he can handle even one little brother, let alone… however many of them there are. He can’t be bothered to count at the moment.

And, wait, he can’t be the only other family they have, right?

Thor tilts his head. “What about Neb—?”

All of them, every single one of them, shouts over him so loudly that he actually takes a step back, blinking in surprise. Kraglin winces. Rocket keeps making a gesture with his one hand cutting across his throat. Loki drops his head into one hand. Quill is the first of them to brush off the loud outburst with a nervous laugh.

“Like I said, man, you’re the only family we got,” he repeats, eyes wide. “The only one. Just. You.”

Thor hesitates, mouth hanging open and his brows knit together.

“Right…” he says at length, drumming his fingers along his bicep. Under normal circumstances, he’d ask what in the world they were getting at, but with Officer Mink standing right beside him, he supposes he’ll simply have to wait for a proper explanation. “Well, that’s… nice?” He shakes his head. “Er — anyway, I’ve been told I’ll have to wait until you’ve all gone through interrogations before I’m allowed to post your bail.”

Absolutely none of them seems surprised by that. Quill shrugs. “Yeah, I figured. We, uh, kind of got arrested for a whole lot of things, turns out.”

“So I heard.”

Rocket shouts, “Yeah! You know they even think we’re part of some weird conspiracy to overthrow the — what is it, Prime Minister? King? What’s this planet got, again?”

“Emperor,” Gamora offers helpfully from the back of the cell.

“Right, yeah, the emperor,” Rocket says. “I mean, how crazy is that, right?”

Thor raises an eyebrow, glances in Loki’s direction, and meets his brother’s eyes for one single second. But it’s enough to know Loki understands exactly what he’s thinking.

“Crazy,” Thor agrees, skepticism heavy in his tone.

Loki rolls his eyes. “Relax, Thor. I’ve no intention to depose any monarchs today.”

And Thor absolutely hates the subtle emphasis Loki applied there — I’ve no intention to depose any monarchs today — but he doesn’t have time to question it, not yet.

“I’d hope not, mate,” Officer Mink speaks up. She’s taken to idly inspecting the inside of her bazooka, but the device on her right forearm has begun to let out a series of beeps, so she swings the weapon up to balance on her left shoulder again. She hardly spares the beeping device a glance, then directs a smile up at the two prison cells. “Interrogation time, then. Now, who wants to go first? Any volunteers?”

Mantis volunteers first.

She sits at a slate grey table, hands folded neatly in front of her — apparently unbothered by the handcuffs linking them together — with an amiable smile on her face, feet lightly tapping out a rhythm on the tile floor. The interrogation room is entirely empty; Mantis is surrounded only by three blank whiteish walls to her front, back, and left. To her right, a one-way glass window spans the entire length of the fourth wall.

Thor frowns, arms crossed over his chest. Mantis seems to be perfectly content to sit there in a silent room by herself, but…

“How long do they intend to keep her waiting?”

Officer Mink, sitting on the observation room’s only bench, has been cleaning out the muzzle of her bazooka. She looks up at Thor and shrugs. “Not sure, mate. They like to let ‘em stew, sometimes. Makes ‘em nervous. Makes ‘em more likely to talk.”

Thor taps his fingers against his bicep, turning back to the window and raising an eyebrow at Mantis. Now she’s begun humming to herself some tune that, if Thor’s not mistaken, originates from a Midgardian song. He’s heard it once or twice, and he’s definitely at least heard snippets of it in the background of Loki’s phone calls while on board the Guardians’ ship. Something about getting stuck in a rainstorm, he thinks.

He glances down at his watch.

It’s been forty minutes.

“Doesn’t seem to be working, does it?” Thor asks.

“Nah,” Mink distractedly agrees, eyeing down the barrel of her bazooka. “Guess she’s got nerves of steel, that one, don’t she?”

Before Thor can answer — before he can say yes, I believe she does, but I’m not sure that’s the reason she seems so unbothered — the door to the interrogation room finally opens. Mantis straightens up, her smile brightening now that she has someone to direct it to, her antennae lifting just a bit.

“Sorry for the wait,” says a bored Xandarian-looking officer, though she doesn’t sound the least bit sorry. She runs a hand through her frock of fine white hair, settling into the seat opposite Mantis. “You may call me Officer Rey.”

“Hello, Officer Rey,” Mantis says.

“Mm-hmm. Let’s get started, shall we?” Officer Rey doesn’t look up as she speaks, fingering through the stack of papers she’s just dropped onto the table. “Please state your name for the camera.”

“Oh, there is a camera?” Mantis asks, twisting in her seat to look around the room.

Officer Rey grunts, jabbing a thumb in the general direction of the uppermost corner of the room, just above the one-way window.

“Oh! There it is.” Mantis inclines her head to the camera like it’s another officer, and she very carefully ununciates, “Man-tis.”

“Mantis…?”

“Yes, Officer Rey?”

“No, I mean, Mantis what? Do you have a last name?”

“Oh,” Mantis says, nodding, and then answers, “No.”

“No,” Officer Rey repeats, deadpan.

Mantis shakes her head, hands still folded on the table, carefree smile still unwavering.

The officer blinks, shakes her head, and then lets out a sigh. “Okay, then. Mantis,” she says, stacking the papers neatly together again. Then she leans toward Mantis, forearms on the table, and asks, “Do you know that we have absolutely no record of your existence, Mantis? Not even in the intergalactic databanks. According to all the records we could find, your entire species is supposed to be extinct.”

“Oh, yes, that would make sense. They are.”

Officer Rey raises an eyebrow. “Is that so?”

Mantis nods.

“You… do realize how that might be difficult for me to believe, don’t you?”

“Oh? Why?”

“Because you’re sitting right there. You look pretty alive to me.”

“Yes, I am.”

“So your species is not extinct, then.”

“Oh! I see. Sorry, yes. They are extinct, except for me,” Mantis explains. “At least, I think so.”

“Uh-huh,” Officer Rey says, pulling a pen from her breast pocket and scribbling a note onto one of the papers in front of her. “Okay. Well, we can have a chat about that later, I suppose. Tell me, Mantis, do you know what you’ve been charged with today?”

“No.”

“You don’t know what you’re being charged with?”

“I do not think so, no.”

Officer Rey pinches the bridge of her nose for a second. “Alright, let’s go through the list, then, yeah?” She flips through a few of the pages until she finds the one she was looking for, pulls it neatly from the pile, and tells Mantis, “You and your little gang hijacked an officer’s vehicle, took it on a joy ride through Center City, ran over three mailboxes, ignored a traffic signal, knocked over another traffic signal, and—”

“Oh,” Mantis interrupts, nodding. “That.”

“Yes, that.”

“But that was not me,” Mantis says.

Officer Rey raises an eyebrow. “It wasn’t you.”

“I was in the back seat. It was Rocket who was driving.”

“That still makes you an accessory. You know that, right? That’s still a crime.”

“Oh, okay,” Mantis says, still smiling as though Officer Rey has just told her an interesting fun fact. “In that case, yes, it was me.”

Officer Rey stares at her for another moment, then she runs a hand over her face and leans back in her seat. “Okay. So, you’re being charged with accessory to hijacking a vehicle, disorderly conduct, reckless driving, and destruction of public property. You’re also being charged with breaking and entering. Do you know why you’re being charged with breaking and entering, Miss — uh, Mantis?”

“Because Rocket broke into a building he was not supposed to?” Mantis asks, seeming genuinely curious. “And I was there?”

“Yes,” Officer Rey answers. “So you’re telling me that one was also Rocket?”

“Mm-hmm.”

“Okay.” She jots down another note. “Tell me, Mantis, are you aware of what building you and your little gang broke into?”

“I am not.”

Officer Rey regards Mantis with eyes narrowed. She worries her tongue between her teeth. “Uh-huh,” she finally says, sounding entirely unconvinced. “You’re telling me you have no idea what building you broke into this morning.”

Mantis only shakes her head.

“Do you know why you broke into the building, at least? Was there a reason?”

“Oh! Yes, I do know that,” Mantis answers with a nod. “Because Rocket wanted to.”

“Because Rocket wanted to.”

“Yes.”

“So you’re telling me Rocket was the brains behind this entire…” Officer Rey waves a vaguely in Mantis’ direction, “… operation your little gang was running. Is that what you’re telling me?”

Mantis’ smile widens and she offers an enthusiastic nod. “Yes.”

“What? Nah, come on, Mantis don’t know what she’s talkin’ about.”

Officer Rey leans forward with her cheek propped up on her hand, elbow in the table, and she raises an eyebrow at Rocket. He’s fiddling with the cuffs linking his wrists together, claws picking into the lock, but it doesn’t look like he’s having any success in so much as loosening them. Evidently Officer Rey thinks so as well, because aside from a pointed glance, she doesn’t comment on it.

“Right,” she says, “but if you weren’t—”

“Don’t you’s got a better seat than this?” Rocket cuts her off.

“Do we—?”

“Have a better seat,” Rocket repeats, grumbling as he stands up to his full height in the rickety metal chair. The cuffs clink as he heaves himself up from the chair to stand on the table, and Officer Rey blinks, lifting her head from her hand and leaning back.

“We don’t—”

“Eh, whatever, don’t sweat it. I’ll sit right here.” He settles himself comfortably with his legs crossed in front of him, still ceaselessly picking at the handcuffs and letting out a little curse under his breath when the lock refuses to budge. “Where you guys buy your cuffs, anyway? I could use a lockin’ mechanism like this. Friggin’ impossible to pick, ain’t it?”

“Sir, if we could stay on topic.”

“Right, yeah, my bad,” Rocket says, holding the cuffs up to the light and squinting at them. “Ask away, Officer Rank.”

“Officer Rey.”

“Yeah, ’s what I said,” he tells her as he jams the claw of his thumb into the lock.

“Alright,” Officer Rey relents. “Mantis informed us that you were behind the wheel of the vehicle when you and your little friends committed —” she glances down at the paper in her hands “— six of the thirty-two offenses you’ve been charged with. Is that true?”

“Nah,” Rocket says. He yanks his thumb out of the lock with a yelp, and he mutters, “Shit, that’s impressive. Yowch.”

As he shakes his hand out, Officer Rey asks him, “So you claim you were not behind the wheel of the vehicle.”

“Nah, but that ain’t Mantis’ fault. She ain’t got too good a memory.”

“So who was, then?”

Rocket’s now taken to all but pressing his eyeball into the lock itself, trying to inspect its inner mechanisms. “Uh,” he says distractedly, “that was Quill.”

“Quill. The captain? He was behind the wheel?”

“Yeah, technically speakin’.”

“He was technically speaking behind the wheel? How is that possible?”

“Oh, no, he was totally behind the wheel. Technically speakin’ he’s the captain.”

“Right,” Officer Rey says, jotting down a few scribbled notes. “Now why don’t you tell me about this building you broke into. Mantis claims to be ignorant as to the nature of that building, and she also claims that you were the one who expressed interest in breaking into the building in the first place. Is that true?”

“Eh. Yes, and no,” Rocket answers. Just once, he tests out the effect of biting down on the chain linking his cuffs, but apparently to no avail. He sticks his tongue out and cringes. “Yick. Anyway, yeah, I was the one that wanted to break in, but I just wanted a place to test out my new bomb. I didn’t care where we did it.”

Officer Rey glances down at the papers. “Says here you’ve also been charged with vandalism, illegal use of pyrogenic weapons, explosives, gravitational field distortion weapons, and cryogenic weapons, all without a permit, and using all of those against uniformed officers. Do you deny any of the charges?”

“I didn’t have no cryogenic weapons. The rest, though, yeah, that sounds about right. They came at us first, though. That’s self defense, ya know.”

“After you illegally demolished a building.”

“Eh. Potato tomato.”

“… Right.” She pulls a paper from the pile, shakes it out, and places it on the table where Rocket can see it. “Is this the effect of the bomb you were seeking to test out?”

Rocket glances down at the photo and shrugs. “Yeah, didn’t work as good as I wanted, but it’s a start, I guess.”

“Didn’t work as—? Sir, your weapon demolished six stories of a twelve-story building. It destroyed thousands of dollars worth of merchandise and hundreds of back-up records.”

“That’s what I’m sayin’. Six stories? Should’ve gone at least ten.”

Officer Rey runs a hand over her face. She seems to do that a lot. She also looks like she’s seriously considering a change in careers. “That building’s walls were fortified with several layers of flame-retardant insulation and steel structural support.”

“Ohhh,” Rocket says, eyes widening. Then a smile breaks out over his face. “That explains it!” He claps his hands together, and the cuffs let out a high pitched ting. “The bomb does work! Sweet. How many layers of insulation are we talkin’, here?”

“That’s not of import.”

“Aw, come on—!”

“So you are also making the claim,” Officer Rey interrupts, speaking over him until his shouting dulls to a low grumbling, “that you were entirely ignorant as to the nature of that building?”

“Yeah, didn’t I say that?”

“Uh-huh. So you just happened to choose that particular building for your… er, experiments.”

“Well, no, Loki picked out that one.”

“Loki did? The teenager?”

“He ain’t a teenager. Yeesh. How easily fooled are you people?”

Again she pinches the bridge of her nose. “Okay. Are you telling me that Loki was the one who suggested testing your highly dangerous and destructive weapons in that particular building?”

“Yeah. Yeah, that’s what I’m sayin’.”

“Please speak your name into the camera.”

Loki slouches in the chair with a sigh, directing a mild glare at the camera in the uppermost corner of the interrogation room, and says, “Loki Odinson, Crown Prince of Asgard and Rightful King of Jotunnheim, Sorcerer and God of Mischief.”

“Uh-huh,” says Officer Rey. She flips through the pile of papers that had been stuffed into the folder on the table, frowns at something she’s reading, and then shoots a look up at him. “You know, we’re missing records on most of your little gang, but it says here that the Crown Prince of Asgard is supposed to be over a thousand years old. If that’s true, I gotta say, you’re looking pretty good for your age, Mr. Odinson.”

In the observation room, Thor wrinkles his nose.

“Over a thousand, eh?” Mink asks. “Yeah, she’s right, he really don’t look it, does he?”

Loki moves to cross his arms over his chest, falters when he remembers he’s wearing handcuffs, and elects instead to rest his fists on the table, idly cracking his knuckles with a sour frown on his face. He holds the officer’s gaze, though, unwavering for about… six seconds.

Then he lets out an annoyed groan and sinks even further into his seat, rolling his eyes to the point that his head leans against the chair back.

There’s a familiar ripple of seiðr that Thor’s seen countless times before, and Loki grows nearly a foot taller before the officer’s eyes. She jumps, just a bit, just enough that her chair scoots back against the tile floor with an aborted little screech. As Loki’s legs stretched out and grew to their real length, Thor thinks, his feet must have hit hers and given her quite the shock. Loki flicks some hair from his face — it’s just grown from above his ears to past his shoulders in the span of seconds — and spreads his hands as far as he can while still handcuffed.

“Ta-da,” he says, bored and unsmiling.

“You—? But you were—”

“Shorter, yes,” Loki cuts her off.

“You were… disguising yourself as a teenager?”

“Well, yes,” he admits with a shrug. “You didn’t expect me to willingly enter that ridiculously overcrowded cell, did you?”

“I don’t care why,” she tells him, her voice and her shoulders tense. “I care how.”

“Loki Odinson, Crown Prince of Asgard and Rightful King of Jotunnheim, Sorcerer and God of Mischief,” Loki repeats, and he finally straightens up in his seat to reach across the table and tap the folder in front of her. The cuffs let off a tink tink against the table. “Do keep up.”

Officer Rey stares at him for a moment, glances at the papers on the table, blinks, then says, “… Magic.”

“Mm-hmm.”

“You can alter your appearance… with magic.”

“That and much more,” Loki tells her, with the first signs of a smirk on his face since he entered the interrogation room.

“Alright, well, I’m going to need a detailed list of everything you’re capable of doing with— with magic,” she says, stumbling over the last word like she can’t quite believe she’s saying it. “Later. Once we’ve finished the interrogations.”

“Oh, I look forward to it.”

“I’m sure.” Officer Rey leans back in her seat, crosses her arms over her chest. “Okay. Let’s move on, then, shall we? Are you aware of what you’re being charged with today?”

“Riot incitement, I believe, which is ridiculous,” Loki says with a roll of his eyes, and Thor drops his head into his hand. Of course that one was Loki. “It’s hardly illegal to speak to the guards, is it? I spoke barely a word to one of them in the cafeteria before he began firing that weapon of his. He was honestly more than eager to—”

“Before your arrest,” Officer Rey interrupts. “Do you know what you did, before your arrest, to come to be detained in this facility?”

“Ah. Yes, of course, accessory to… what is it, hijacking? Breaking and entering?”

“Both, yes, along with a whole host of other infractions.” She tilts her head and regards him with narrowed eyes for a moment, and she says, “But what really concerns me is the breaking and entering, Mr. Odinson. Tell me, is there any particular reason you and your little gang chose that building to illegally demolish?”

Loki shrugs. “To test one of Rocket’s new toys, I believe.”

“Yes, I’m aware. The explosion from his bomb leveled six stories and destroyed quite a lot of property. Very expensive, very difficult to replace,” she tells him. “And when our officers arrived, you and your friends attacked them with a whole slew of illegal weapons. Pyrogenic, cryogenic, gravitational —”

“There were no cryogenic weapons used.”

“I — Excuse me?”

“I said there were no cryogenic weapons used, or even in our possession.”

For the first time, the officer bristles, appearing genuinely thrown off. “One of our officers was frozen in a block of ice —”

“Unfortunate,” Loki says, “but not, I’m afraid, the result of a cryogenic weapon, I assure you.”

“Oh, he didn’t,” Thor mutters to himself.

“Didn’t what?” Mink asks.

Thor doesn’t answer, cringing as he massages his temple. Of all the times for Loki to suddenly become comfortable with his abilities as a Jotunn, he chooses to unleash it in the middle of a fight against uniformed officers.

That, or he somehow has the Casket of Ancient Winters on him. Thor can’t believe he’s actually hoping for the former.

In the interrogation room, Officer Rey says, “You can’t honestly expect me to believe that.”

Loki shrugs again. “Believe what you wish. I’m certain when your… esteemed officers complete their search of the wreckage, they will find no such weapons on the premises.”

Officer Rey takes a slow breath, closing her eyes. “Okay. Okay. We’ll circle back to that. Now, what made you decide on that particular building? Why did your joy ride in our officer’s vehicle take you to that particular building? Why did you enter that particular building, armed to the teeth, and demolish the place?”

“I’m afraid I wouldn’t know.”

“Are you serious?”

“Very.”

“I have it on good authority that it was your decision.”

“Then your information is incorrect, Officer,” Loki tells her with an entirely perfect poker face. If Thor hadn’t seen that look a million times before, he’d even believe it. After a few seconds in which Loki refuses to drop the look, Officer Rey seems to believe it, too. She frowns, her brow creasing.

“Then whose decision was it?”

Something subtle changes in Loki’s expression, subtle enough that the Officer doesn’t seem to notice.

That was exactly the question he must have been waiting for.

He leans forward, elbows on the table, dropping his voice to conspiratorial whisper. “Between you and me, Officer Rey, I would speak to the captain. The human, Quill.”

“Oh?”

“He and his — er, lady friend,” Loki tells her. “In fact, I’d start with her. There’s something… off about that one, if you ask me.”

“His lady friend. The green one?”

“Indeed.”

“The one who’s refused to give us her name,” Officer Rey says, and Thor raises an eyebrow. He hadn’t known that.

“That’s the one.”

“I don’t suppose you know anything about her. Her name, her occupation — even her damn species?”

“I’m so sorry, Officer, but you know as much about her as I do,” says Loki, lying through his teeth with all the grace of a snake. “Made her acquaintance just as we were all arrested.”

“Please speak your name into the camera.”

Gamora doesn’t move. She doesn’t speak, either. Instead she remains exactly as she is, leaning back in her chair, gaze unyielding on Officer Rey. Though she cannot cross her arms over her chest due to the handcuffs, she’s somehow given off the air that she is nonetheless, with her fingers laced and her hands on her stomach.

The look on her face alone would terrify most. Thor has to admit even he’d be a little intimidated by it.

Officer Rey seems unaffected.

“Ma’am, I said please speak your name into the camera.”

Again, the only answer she receives is silence. She lets out a disappointed sigh and flips through some of the papers in front of her.

“Yeah, I figured as much. Any reason you seem completely unwilling to divulge any information about yourself, ma’am?”

“I will not cooperate with the lackeys of a corrupt legal system.”

“Oh, you can speak,” says Officer Rey with half a smile. “Corrupt, huh? That’s what you think?”

Gamora raises an eyebrow. Just enough to make it clear that, yes, that’s exactly what she thinks.

“You know you’ll have to tell us your name eventually. Or we’ll find it eventually, but either way, there’s really nothing to gain from delaying,” Officer Rey tells her.

“You will not.”

“Hmm, right. Because you and your little boyfriend destroyed our entire records facility yesterday, is that right?”

“A happy coincidence.”

“Is that so?”

Gamora’s only answer, this time, is a slow smile.

“Mm-hmm,” Officer Rey answers, deadpan. “Well, perhaps they’ll just have to charge someone else with high treason, then, hmm? I would guess that rodent wouldn’t last very long in the maximum security unit.”

For a second, it looks like Gamora wants to laugh. “You would be mistaken.”

“So you do know Rocket personally, then,” Officer Rey says with a self-satisfied gotcha smirk. “What is your relationship to him?”

Once again, Gamora lapses into stony silence.

“No? Alright, then, what about the Captain, Quill? What’s your relationship to him?” Officer Rey asks, leaning forward on her elbows. “You two seem rather close. Some of the others have claimed that they’ve only just met you today, but I would imagine you’ve known Quill for far longer than that, at the very least.”

Gamora gives a disinterested roll of her eyes, and then levels Officer Rey with a look that’s just short of fully patronizing. “Would you like a bit of advice? Talk to the rodent.”

“I already have.”

“Then talk to him again.”

“Ma’am, you may as well cooperate with us. We’re going to find out about you eventually, whether it’s through you, or through any of our records that made it out of the explosion, or through one of your teammates.”

Gamora smirks. “They will tell you nothing.”

“That so?”

“It is, yes.”

“You have the bearing of a man with honor.”

Quill leans back and gazes wistfully at the far wall, shaking his head with a smile.

“That’s what she said, just like, leaning on that wall with all her…” he trails off, gesturing vaguely with his hands in the general shape of what Thor swears is meant to be a woman’s curves. “You know, the legs and all, batting those eyes at me.” He lets out a low whistle. “Caught my eye right away — ‘course, to be fair, that’s what she was tryin’ to do. Catch my eye, I mean. I had something she wanted, she got me to let my guard down, and wham!” He smacks his palm down on the table. “Boot right to the stomach, and she ran off with my stuff. Stealin’ from each other! Ain’t that a meet cute, or what?”

“Sir, if we could just—”

“Been almost, what, six years now? And I’ll tell ya, I mean, have you ever been with a woman that can bench press you? ‘Cause she’s done it. Couple times. Among… among other things,” he adds distractly, still smiling as he stares down at the table, before he shakes himself out of it. “It’s something else, seriously, I totally recommend it.”

“Sir.”

“Dances like you wouldn’t believe, too—”

“Sir,” Officer Rey cuts in, finally loud enough to bring Quill’s rambling to a halt.

“Hm?”

“The question I asked,” Officer Rey says, “was not when you met. I asked you her name.”

“Oh, right, yeah,” Quill answers with a nod. “My bad. Got a little carried away, I guess. What, did she not tell you her name?”

“No, she did not.”

Quill frowns. “Well, I mean, then I can’t tell ya, can I? That’d be a dick move.”

“You know we’re going to find out either way.”

“Then you’re gonna find out eventually anyway. You don’t need me tellin’ you and ending up in the doghouse, I mean, come on,” Quill laughs, like he and the officer are in on the same joke. “Cut me a little slack, here.”

“You’re under arrest for a laundry list of felonies, Mr. Quill. I don’t need to cut you any ‘slack.’”

“Harsh,” Quill whispers.

As Officer Rey gives up on getting Gamora’s name out of him and moves on to a proper interrogation, Mink speaks up behind Thor:

“They ain’t makin’ this too easy, huh?” she asks. “Guess you’re gonna be stuck here a while.”

Thor sighs. “So it seems.”

Mink yawns. “I’m gonna head down to the cafeteria, grab a snack. Ya wanna come?”

“Oh, no, I’d better stay here,” Thor says. “Thank you.”

“Want me to grab ya something? Prisoner food ain’t great, but the staff cafeteria’s got these, uh… salty meat-flavored chip things. A-Grade, mate.”

“Thank you,” Thor says, trying his best to sound genuine. “But I’m alright.”

“Eh. Suit yourself. I’ll be back in a jiff. Lemme know if anything interesting happens, yeah?”

“Of course.”

She lugs her giant bazooka up onto her shoulder, yawns again, and taps her card key to the lock beside the door. The heavy steel door retracts up into the ceiling with a dull whirr and slams shut as soon as she’s cleared the doorway, and Thor turns back to the window, where Quill is now being escorted out of the interrogation room so they can bring someone else in.

And then—

“Finally,” comes a very familiar, very annoyed voice from behind him. “I genuinely thought she would never leave.”

Thor turns, and sure enough, there’s Loki standing in the observation room with him. Or a duplicate of Loki, anyway, with no handcuffs on and his hands on his hips. Thor raises an eyebrow and asks, “Shouldn’t you be in the holding cell?”

“I am. Obviously.”

“Right. Obviously.”

Loki steps up beside Thor and peers into the interrogation room, and he asks, “Who are they retrieving next?”

“I’m not sure.”

“What?” Loki shoots him a look. “Have you not been paying attention?”

“Mostly.”

“Mostly,” Loki mutters under his breath, rolling his eyes. “You have literally nothing to do in this room aside from watching what’s going on in that one. I’d have thought you’d at least know who they’re retrieving next.”

“If you’re really in the holding cell right now, you’ll know in a minute, won’t you?”

Loki squints, then says, “Eh?”

“You’re not in the holding cell, are you?”

“I appear to be in the holding cell.”

“But you’re not.”

“No,” Loki admits. “I’m about… three floors below where you’re standing.”

In the interrogation room, the door opens, and both brothers turn to watch as Kraglin is brought in.

“Oh, good,” Loki says. “I told them all we need to be as difficult as possible, which is not especially… well, difficult for them. This won’t be terribly interesting. He’s just going to ask for a lawyer repeatedly until they give in, which they will likely never do. That buys us some time.”

And it’s right about then — as, sure enough, Kraglin begins asking Officer Rey when his lawyer will arrive — that a particular suspicion hits Thor. Or perhaps suspicion isn’t quite the right word. Realization is likely more accurate, because the very moment the thought crosses his mind, he knows it’s true.

“I’m not here to post your bail, am I?”

“You’re not here to post our bail, no.”

Thor heaves a sigh. “Am I here to break you all out? Because if you called me here to break you all out—”

“Oh, please,” Loki scoffs. “You truly believe we’re not all fully capable of breaking ourselves out of a prison? Honestly, Thor. This place is child’s play, especially these lower security tiers.”

“You know, I’d sort of gathered that, actually,” Thor says, thinking of how utterly nonplussed Mantis had seemed after being made to wait in the interrogation room for nearly an hour, and… actually, how utterly nonplussed every single one of them seems to be.

Then something else occurs to him.

“Wait, lower security tiers?”

“As opposed to the maximum security unit, yes. That bit’s underground, near the center of the prison.”

And there we go, Thor thinks. The real reason they’re all here. He’s already resigning himself to the situation before he even asks, “Nebula’s in that part of the prison, isn’t she?”

“She is,” Loki confirms, with a sort of easy nonchalance that makes Thor wonder exactly how often the Guardians find themselves in these situations. “Got herself arrested for some of the crimes she committed while she was still working under the Mad Titan, I’m afraid.”

“And you need me to break her out.”

“Again, have you no faith in any of us? Honestly. I’ve got that part handled,” Loki says, casually leaning a shoulder against the one-way mirror, unbothered by the fact that his magic duplicate shouldn’t technically be able to do that. As an afterthought, he adds, “With Rocket’s help.”

“Uh-huh,” Thor says.

“Yes, the locks used on those particular cells are unfortunately slightly outside my area of expertise,” Loki admits. “Luckily Rocket has plenty of experience with them. Speaking of which, did he make a show of being impressed by the prison’s security like I told him to?”

“He did mention the handcuffs were impressive. Unbreakable, apparently.”

At that, Loki actually snorts. “Fantastic. He’s taken them off and relocked them something like a dozen times now.”

“Really?”

“I’m literally holding on to them as we speak while Rocket works on the lock to Nebula’s cell, yes.”

“Huh,” Thor says, because there’s no reason to be surprised by anything Loki says anymore. “A dozen times? Why so many?”

Loki shrugs. “Well, there’s a lot of time to kill while these lovely guards here are busy interrogating the others. He gets bored. He’s like a cat, if you don’t give him something to keep him occupied he’ll start tearing up the furniture— OW!”

Thor jumps, watching as Loki glares daggers at something to his left and then ripples out of existence half a second later.

It’s utterly silent in the observation room. In the interrogation room, Kraglin starts spelling out the word lawyer. Incorrectly, Thor’s pretty sure.

After ten seconds or so, Loki comes back.

“Sorry,” he says. “Accidentally said that bit out loud. Rocket overheard.”

“I imagine he takes offense to being compared to a cat,” Thor says.

“There are worse things,” Loki shrugs.

Thor asks, “So you didn’t call me here to post your bail, and you didn’t call me here to break you all out of prison, and you didn’t call me here to break Nebula out of prison. Is that right?”

“Correct,” Loki says. “You are here for a far greater purpose, brother.”

“Am I?”

“Yes, you are,” Loki tells him, and a conspiratorial grin spreads over his face. “Did you know that, with the sole exception of the maximum security unit, every single lock in this building is operated through a series of electrical circuit boards?”

Thor gives him a warning look. “Oh, no.”

“Oh, yes,” Loki says, practically giddy with it. “The whole thing is a network. Under normal circumstances it would never be a security flaw, but given enough power…”

“No. Absolutely not. You cannot be suggesting—”

“I’m requesting, actually—”

“The entire prison?”

“No, no, not the entire prison. I just said Rocket and I are taking care of the maximum security unit.”

“So, almost the entire prison.”

“Well… yes.”

“But why in the world would you want me to—?”

In the middle of his sentence there’s another dull whirr of the observation room’s door opening up, and Loki mutters a curse under his breath before vanishing into thin air again.

Officer Mink steps into the room, her trusted bazooka leaning against one shoulder, and she’s tossing back a handful of some kind of snack with her free hand. She pauses, gives Thor a look like he’s losing his mind, and then nods at the little intercom built into the wall beside the one-way window.

“You know they can’t hear ya in here, mate,” she says through a mouthful. “If you wanna talk to ‘em, you gotta press the button. Unless you’re talking to yourself, I dunno. I won’t judge.”

Thor glances over his shoulder, where it seems Officer Rey finally grew tired enough of Kraglin’s incessant demands for a lawyer, since she’s now had another officer switch him out of the room and bring Drax in.

It doesn’t seem she’s having a much better time with him.

Thor looks back to Officer Mink, and he realizes that if he wants any more answers out of his brother — and he does — he is most definitely not going to get them unless he’s alone. Luckily, judging by the fact that Loki appeared immediately after Mink left, he’s keeping an eye out for exactly when that happens.

“Officer Mink,” he asks. “Would you be so kind as to point me to the nearest bathroom?” “My God, you couldn’t have chosen somewhere a bit less… disgusting?”

“Oh, don’t be so dramatic, you’re not even actually here,” Thor says. He considers, briefly, sitting down somewhere, before he realizes there isn’t a single surface in this bathroom he’d be willing to touch. He’ll have to stand for now. “Besides, anywhere else and she might have come with me. Now, what’s this about me setting an entire prison’s worth of criminals loose?”

Loki shoots him what almost seems to be a chastising look. “They’re not criminals. Well… technically, yes, they’re criminals. Depending on who you ask.”

“Loki. Why do you want me to set everyone in this prison free?”

“Well, it’s rather a long story, isn’t it? And I don’t know if you’ve noticed, but we’re running a bit short on time. The interrogations are only going to keep them occupied for so long, and Rocket’s very nearly done in the maximum security unit, and once he’s finished we’ll need to coordinate this in a very timely manner, or— What? What’s the look for?”

“You know what the look’s for. This is insane.”

“Consider it a favor.”

“I don’t owe you a favor.”

“You’re my brother. I don’t need you to owe me a favor.”

“True, true. Hey, Loki, do you remember the last time you asked a favor of me?”

“Really? You’re bringing that up, four hundred years later? Sif’s hair grew back eventually —”

“After six months!”

Loki looks away from him for a moment, his attention diverted by something Thor can’t see. Then, evidently forgetting their argument entirely, he says, “Look. Brother. If I had more time, believe me I would explain, but for now you’re just going to have to trust me.”

Thor raises an eyebrow at him.

“Yes,” Loki adds. “I am fully aware of how ridiculous that sounds.”

“Oh, well, as long as you’re aware.”

Loki definitely hears the sarcasm in his voice and chooses to ignore it. “We’ve got…” he says, glancing elsewhere again, and a look of worry passes over his face. “Oh, dear. We’ve got about forty-five seconds before every door in the maximum security unit opens. That means you have… roughly sixty to get to the main control room.”

“What? I don’t even know where that is!”

“Obviously I’m going to lead you there, relax,” Loki says, and his magic double is already heading for the door. “Now, let’s go.”

“And if we run into any guards on our way there?”

Loki doesn’t even fully turn his head to answer, like he knows without having to check that Thor’s following close behind, already cooperating despite all his myriad complaints. Which he is, but still.

“Oh, I wouldn’t worry about that,” Loki says. “Gamora and Mantis have graciously cleared a path for us.”

The whole ordeal, which Thor has chosen to refer to in his head as The Great Unexplained and Probably Ill-Advised Erlaxnian Prison Break, seems to go more or less how Loki and the others have planned.

Which is to say, of course, that it erupts into chaos in a matter of minutes.

Loki’s magic double leads him down a curiously empty hall, and then to a stairwell where Thor sees no less than twenty guards crowded together and sleeping peacefully in a pile underneath the staircase. Mantis and Gamora’s handiwork and all a part of the plan, Thor doesn’t doubt, especially since Loki doesn’t so much as spare them a glance as they pass them by.

They ascend four flights of stairs, and at the fourth, an alarm begins blaring through the halls.

“Alert,” a robotic voice calls down from all around them, amidst a flare of red light. “Unauthorized cell access on Sub-Level 3B. Repeat: Unauthorized cell access on Sub-Level 3B.”

“That’ll be Rocket, I imagine?” Thor calls ahead, but Loki doesn’t answer, taking him through a doorway on the fifth floor and directly into the prison’s main control room.

Gamora and Mantis are already waiting for them.

“Hi, Thor!” Mantis says.

“Hi, Thor,” Gamora says with a smile in his direction, and then points an annoyed glare at Loki. “Really? This is cutting it a little close, don’t you think?”

“He’s here now, isn’t he?”

“I’m sorry,” Thor interrupts. “What exactly am I supposed to do, here?”

Loki opens his mouth to say something that Thor knows is going to be bitingly sarcastic, but Gamora beats him to it.

“I’ll explain,” she says. Then, to Loki: “You go focus on making sure Rocket and Nebula don’t do anything stupid. Half the prison’s staff is already heading to where you three are.”

Loki nods. “Meet at the front gate?”

“Meet at the front gate.”

And just like that, Loki’s magic double vanishes again. Mantis gives him a cheerful wave on his way out.

“Alright,” Gamora says, squaring her shoulders and stepping up to the control panel that lines the entire back wall of this room. She inserts her sword in between two metal panels and uses it as a crowbar to pry one of them off, revealing a mess of coiled wires underneath. “According to Rocket, if enough electricity is pumped into this control panel, it will overload the circuits and unlock every cell from here to the first floor.”

“So I’ve been told.”

Gamora nods. “He said it’ll take about two million volts to get the job done. Think you can manage it?”

And honestly, it’s either the fact that Gamora and Mantis — two people who Thor implicitly trusts to have at least semi-reasonable judgement — are both on board for this plan as well, or it’s the fact that Gamora’s just expressed uncertainty that Thor can produce as little as a few paltry million volts of electricity. Whatever the reason, Thor doesn’t question it.

He scoffs, steps forward, and plants his palm over the control panel.

Does he think he can manage two million volts?

Please.

“You might want to close your eyes,” he tells them, and in the next instant, the lightning floods through him the exact same exhilarating way it always does, and the control panel is entirely overtaken by a nearly blinding flash of white-blue light.

There’s a pop, and a long drawn-out sizzling sound, and by the time the lightning fades, the entire control room is filled with smoke.

Gamora kicks open the control room door, airing it out a bit.

“Alright, nice work, Thor,” she says, shouting over the blaring alarms and the incessant robotic voice on the intercom and, now, the gradually rising din of newly freed prisoners’ voices. “Are you both ready?”

Mantis, already bouncing on her toes in anticipation, jogs off past Gamora and into the hall.

Gamora smiles after her and then, surprisingly, retracts her sword back into its sheath. She must see the confusion on Thor’s face, because she shrugs and explains, “Trying to keep casualties to a minimum today,” before she heads off into the fray herself.

She calls back, “See you at the front gate! Try not to kill anyone!”

And, well. He has never been one to turn down a good fight. Thor sighs, cracks his knuckles, and dives out into the hall as well.

“Alright, does anyone plan on telling me exactly what all of that was about?”

Thor sits back on the middle cushion of the Guardians’ couch, in the room that Quill oddly referred to as the living room — as if the nine of them don’t live in the entirety of the Quadrant — and he watches as Rocket leaps up onto a high shelf, inserting the entire upper half of his body into a box labeled EXTRA SNACKS. Mantis hops up on the cushion to Thor’s right, and Loki to his left.

“Eh,” Rocket calls back. “Wasn’t really plannin’ on it.”

“Rocket, don’t be mean,” Gamora says, taking a seat on what seems to be a big, fluffy bag on the floor. Quill follows quickly behind, settling on the floor between her outstretched legs so that he can lean back against her.

Kraglin’s giggling, pulling can after can after can out of a small refrigerator in the corner and stacking them haphazardly in his arms, and he shouts, “Hey, Neb! You wanna tell him?”

Nebula rolls her eyes, sitting on the floor in front of where Mantis is. “I could have managed it perfectly well on my own.”

“Could you have?” Loki asks, and she — without so much as turning to look — spikes a perfectly aimed bag of chips at his head.

“Neb here got arrested by the Erlaxnians,” Kraglin says, as Loki pops open the bag of chips.

“And I could have escaped on my own. That prison was essentially a child’s playpen.”

“But she refused,” Gamora says.

“You refused to escape from the prison?” Thor asks, and Nebula shrugs.

“Yes, she did,” Loki explains.

Kraglin starts carrying the dangerously wobbling stack of cans across the room. He carefully hands the topmost can to Quill, the next one to Gamora, then to Mantis, then to Nebula, then Thor, then Loki. Finally he sets the last can down on the floor and takes a seat on a pillow in front of the couch.

Up on the shelf, Rocket finally extracts an armful of crinkling snack bags and hops down to the floor, taking the only remaining big fluffy bag-like seat.

“When she never returned to the ship after her arrest,” Loki goes on, “I sent an illusion into the prison to ask her what the hell was taking so long—”

“It was only two days—”

“— and she told me in no uncertain terms that she utterly refused to leave the prison, her heart simply would not allow it until every single poor soul in the facility was freed—”

“I did not word it like that!”

“I’m paraphrasing,” Loki shrugs, taking a sip from his drink, “but that was the gist.” “Oh, please,” Nebula rolls her eyes. On her big fluffy bag chair, Gamora’s watching her with a carefully controlled expression that Thor recognizes well; she’s trying, and failing, to hide the fact that she’s proud of her sister. Nebula continues, “That planet is in the middle of a revolution. Their criminal justice system is hardly a system, it operates solely at the whims of its idiotic Emperor, and everyone who dissents against him ends up in that prison. Or dead.”

“Nebs has been very committed to doing the right thing lately,” Quill speaks up.

Nebula shrugs. “It is a phase. It will pass.”

“Sure it will,” Loki mutters.

“Anyway,” Gamora cuts in before Nebula can get up and start physically fighting with Loki. “Nebula refused to break out of the prison until she could break everyone else out, too, so we decided to help. Peter had the idea that we should get arrested in order to get inside, and once Rocket figured out how the locks operated, calling you in for help was the clear next step. And now the wrongfully imprisoned citizens have all been freed with minimal casualties, and all record of their so-called wrongdoings have been irreversibly expunged—”

“You’re welcome,” Rocket pipes up.

“— and their biggest holding facility for political prisoners no longer has functioning locks or a functioning electrical system.”

Loki says, “So the Erlaxnians should be free to continue their revolution and overthrow the Emperor in due time.”

Quill adds, “We tipped off Nova Prime, too, just in case. Probably best if the Nova Corps keeps an eye on the situation, you know, makes sure it doesn’t get too outta hand.”

“And hey, if it does, we’ll just have to go back and help out again,” Kraglin says.

Rocket groans. “Are we at least gonna get paid for it this time?”

“Dude,” Quill says. “We’re still swimming in cash from the Urunia assignment. How the hell are you worried about money already?”

“Wouldn’t you like to know.”

“I mean… I would, yeah. The hell are you planning on buying now?” Quill asks, but before he can press Rocket for a real answer, there’s a thunk that can only be Drax hopping down from the next level up on the ship, and they hear him and Groot talking back and forth before the two of them emerge into the room.

“Another successful mission!” Drax shouts. “The battle was exhilarating, even without the use of weapons.”

He’s holding an utterly massive pot in his arms, so big that he nearly can’t wrap his arms fully around it, and it’s filled to the brim with something that smells amazing. Without so much as giving a word of explanation for what’s in the pot, he drops down onto the floor between Kraglin and Mantis, directly in front of Thor.

“Drax, dude,” Quill says. “That is a shitload of popcorn.”

“I am Groot,” Groot says, settling down beside Rocket, eyes down on his video game.

Loki nods. “He’s got a point.”

“Exactly,” Drax agrees. “We always have popcorn when we commune in the Room with the Couch and the Television.”

“That’s usually ‘cause we’re watching movies,” Quill admits. “But yeah, you’re not wrong.”

“Hell, I ain’t complaining,” Kraglin says, pulling out a handful of popcorn from the pot.

“Me neither, me neither,” Quill admits. “Hey, we might as well throw a movie on anyway, right? Thor? You good to stick around for another couple hours? No big kingly duties waiting for you back home?”

“It’s eight in the morning back home,” Thor says. “So probably not.”

Loki adds, “In any case, I’m sure Heimdall will tell you if you’re needed.”

“Sweet!” Quill shouts, jumping up off the floor and heading for the TV across the room. It’s hooked up to a fairly standard Midgardian DVD player, if Thor’s not mistaken, though the screen is also connected to a holographic projector that spans the width of the entire far wall. “So what are you feeling? We picked up a whole box of these bad boys the last time we were on Earth, so we got everything, man. Action, buddy cop, classics, those dumb romcoms Drax likes, a few horror movies, we got Alien, Predator, Alien vs. Predator, some TV shows…”

Thor gestures for Drax to hand him some of the popcorn, and he lifts the entire pot up so that Thor can take a few handfuls. Then he pulls his legs up onto the couch, finally opens up the drink Kraglin gave him, and sits back and relaxes.

“What’s a buddy cop?”

“Oh, ho, ho,” Quill says, clearly pleased with the decision even as Loki and Nebula both groan and roll their eyes. “Just you wait! I got something that’ll knock your socks off!”

“Why would he—?” Drax starts to ask, but then the box of DVDs glows green with magic and flies out of Quill’s hands, soaring across the room until it ends up in Loki’s lap.

“Trust me,” Loki says. “Do not let Quill choose the movie.”

“Hey!”

Quill throws one of Rocket’s snacks at Loki, which Loki catches and spikes right back at him, and then, predictably, the rest of them begin fighting, too. Loki throws a pillow that misses and hits Groot instead, Rocket picks up his bag-chair and hits Quill with it, Quill tries to steal the box of DVDs back, Loki jumps up off the couch to keep it out of his reach, Kraglin takes the pot of popcorn out of Drax’s arms so that Drax is free to join in, and the rest dissolves into chaos. Again.

Kraglin shimmies up into Loki’s now vacant seat, and Thor sighs, sitting back and sharing the popcorn between himself, Kraglin, and Mantis.

Oh, well. They’ll get to the movie eventually.
