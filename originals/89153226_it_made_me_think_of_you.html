Chapter 1

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for notes

Nobody could remember the exact moment that Agatha Harkness and Rio Vidal stopped being two separate people and instead became Agatha and Rio. 

There had never been a sweeping romance, or an official declaration in front of their loved ones and it didn’t help that both women vehemently denied any such thing. They were just two friends. Two good friends. Two good friends existing in a time where they couldn’t openly be anything else but good friends and for them, it worked. 

True to their character, their first meeting was anything but ordinary. 

Agatha, aged twenty-five, had been in her second year of residency at Portland General when she pulled back the fading curtains to unveil a woman, bloodied and bruised with several twigs and leaves in her hair, grinning directly up at her. 

She had a good mind to pull the curtains taut and let an intern take hold of the Cheshire Cat that had decided to stroll into her ER that day, but thank goodness that she never had, otherwise there would be no story to tell. 

“It wasn’t the tree’s fault,” the woman spoke with a nervous laugh. “I’m fine.” 

Agatha huffed, her eyes immediately seeking out the purpling skin and unnatural angle of the woman’s right wrist. “Hi fine, I’m Dr. Harkness.” 

“Wow, a dad joke.” The woman’s nerves dissipated, replaced with an emboldened laugh, a slight curl to her lips and wary eyes. “You look way too miserable to pull one of those off.” 

It wasn’t often that somebody saw through her so brazenly. It wasn’t often that anyone saw her at all. Sure, each day there were men trying their luck with her, but a woman? Women usually took one look at her and pitied that she had opted for a career rather than a picket fence, husband to feed and babygrows hanging out on the washing line. 

Allowing herself to look at the woman had been her first mistake. Her tanned skin was unlike anything she had laid eyes on before, so clear - apart from several scratches littering her forehead - it left her enraged. Perhaps she’d leave those scratches until last. No one had any business being that attractive anyway. 

Her hair was straight and rested just below her shoulders and a kind of darkness she had only ever seen in the mahogany furniture her mother had insisted on decorating her childhood home with. Agatha appreciated that she clearly didn’t bother with a bouffant or beehive that everyone and their aunt seemed to adorn nowadays, but then again, by the state of the woman, it was clear she didn’t bother with much. 

Her second mistake had been looking into the woman’s eyes. The moment she did, she was lost in deep-hazel as the scent of earth, hard-work and an air of mischief clouded her senses. She didn’t like earth, nor did she like mischief. Her life had been hard enough as it was, and she didn’t need to get distracted which was exactly what was happening to her at that moment. 

“I am miserable,” she mumbled, choosing to subvert her attention back to her clipboard. “Four hours of sleep and being in this place will do that to you. Let me look at that wrist.” 

“The name is Rio, by the way. If your sleep-deprived brain can handle that.” 

Rio. 

Agatha would have thought the name was absurd; she could hear her mother screaming absurdities about it from her grave, but it did suit Rio, and she thought that was beautiful. 

“Wait - should I be asking for another doctor? ‘Cause you’re pretty and all, but I have to get back out to work and I don’t need someone fixing this whilst hallucinating.”

Agatha reached for Rio’s wrist, her fingers gently palpating at the swelling skin until a hiss met her ears. “You underestimate me,” she smirked. “How did this happen? It appears you got into a rather heated altercation with the forest.” 

“Like I said, it's not the tree’s fault,” Rio said hastily. “The branch was rotten.” 

“And you just climb trees for fun?” 

This woman was becoming more appealing by the minute. 

Rio’s head shook, before she hissed again at Agatha’s fingers pressing into her skin. “I’m in the Parks and Urban Forestry Department.”

Agatha sucked her breath in. “Sounds riveting.” 

“It’s broken, isn’t it?” 

“That generally is what happens when bones are pointing in the opposite direction to how they’re supposed to point, yes.” 

And Rio laughed. A deep, throaty laugh that became the first thing to actually make Agatha feel awake that shift. Most people cried, or shuddered, and some even screamed out; Rio had simply laughed and it annoyed Agatha down to her very fibres. 

“Are you always this cheerful?” 

Rio shrugged her shoulders, flashing that annoying, smirk of a grin once more. “Are you always this grumpy?” 

“Yes.” 

“Would it make you less grumpy if I asked to see you again?” 

“... In what capacity?” 

Rio shrugged her shoulders. “I don’t know. Friends? Something… Nevermind.” 

“Spit it out, tree-girl.” 

“Something more… if that's your thing.” 

“What makes you think I am looking for that?” Agatha feigned shock. “You have some nerve saying that here.” 

She expected Rio’s cheeks to redden or her shoulders to sink or her mouth to begin spluttering. What she didn’t expect was to be met with that same, infuriatingly-dizzying smirk. 

“I mean, I doubt your husband would let you become a doctor. So I’m assuming you don’t have one, nor are you looking for one. And you should have freaked out more… thrown a scalpel at me or whatever.” 

“You’re just lucky I don’t have a scalpel on me right now.” 

“So?”

“No.” 

After refusing any further contact, Rio ended up in her ER three more times in the following two weeks with injuries that were in no way ER worthy, and Agatha quickly learned that this woman would not be leaving her life anytime soon. 

She would soon learn after that, that she didn’t mind it at all.

1970 - Two Years After Their First Meeting  

She knew it had been a bad idea. Not the vacation - god no, she needed this vacation like she needed air to breathe - but choosing to sit next to Rio for five and a half hours, cooped up in a pressurised metal container had absolutely been a bad idea. 

Before they had boarded, Rio had insisted on visiting the terminal’s book store and had bounded out of the store and over to Agatha with something large and heavy clutched to her chest and a beaming smile plastered on her face. It was an encyclopedia of plants that Agatha proceeded to become far too well acquainted with during their flight for her liking. 

“Bougainvillea,” Rio announced proudly, tapping the photograph with one finger. “We definitely need to see some of those. They’re so-”

“It’s a flower, Rio. I am not travelling out of the country for the first time to see a flower.” 

“It’s not a flower, it’s a vine. They’re magnificent.”

“If you say so.” 

Not two minutes later and the book was being thrust into Agatha’s face again. 

“And this is a ceiba tree. They say it carries spirits into heaven.” 

“Oh, wow,” Agatha rolled her eyes. “Perhaps it will carry me far, far away from you and your obsession with borderline-maniac obsession with anything green.” 

But the truth was, she didn’t want to be far, far away from Rio. Despite her teasing, and her feigned disinterest, this woman had become a constant in her life, ever since that time two years before when she had ended up in Agatha’s ER department with a fractured wrist and that same, beaming smile. 

They had been doing whatever it was that they were doing, for two years now, and it had been the best two years of Agatha’s life. She had a purpose after work. She had someone who cooked her dinners and gave her somewhere to go on her otherwise-lonesome Christmases. She had a space for intimacy that she had never had before - and sure, she had always ensured she got her fix from somewhere but before Rio? Before Rio, it had always been meaningless; a one night thing that meant nothing and left her feeling like nothing afterwards. 

And now, here she was on the sunny shores of Cancun with somebody who meant something to her, even if neither had ever established what they meant to one another. 

The resort gardens were green, and clean, and not five minutes into their arrival, Agatha had lost Rio to the outdoors. It had taken her fifteen minutes of searching until she came across a hunched figure, crouched to the floor before a flower that Agatha could never name. 

“Rio.”

She received no answer. 

“For god's sake, Rio. The restaurant closes in thirty minutes-”

“Shh!” 

“Excuse me?” 

“Agatha,” Rio’s voice was merely a breath escaping, every inch of her body frozen in place. “Don’t move an inch.” 

“I’m not moving.” 

That was a lie; she had, in fact, moved several steps closer to Rio, now finding herself towering over her. 

Rio reached back, her fingers blindly searching until they found Agatha’s wrist, gently tugging her forward. Her touch was warm, gentle, and the only thing that broke Agatha’s tunnel-visioned mind. 

“Look.” Rio gently eased herself up from the ground, her body backing into Agatha’s like it was second nature. She held onto Agatha’s arm, moving it gently into the direction of whatever she had spotted. “Can you see?” 

“It’s a plant.” 

“No,” Rio sighed. “Just be patient,” she whispered. “And watch.” 

Several quiet seconds passed and Agatha questioned, once again, her own sanity, until her eyes caught something that shimmered beside a cluster of scarlet flowers. It wasn’t flying - more so hovering, its wings moving too quickly for her to make sense of. Flecks of green, dark blue and something oddly iridescent were all she could make out. 

A breath she hadn’t realised she had been holding left her lungs. “... Oh.” 

Rio was smiling without looking up. “A hummingbird.” 

“They’re tiny, I thought they’d be bigger.” 

When Rio didn’t reply, Agatha found herself looking in her direction rather than the magnificent, tiny creature before her. She already had one of those to look at - the same thing that she had chosen to spend the last two years with, the same thing she had chosen to take her first vacation with and the same thing that had enabled to show her a new lens of life. 

Rio looked happy. So, so happy that it only inspired the same feeling in Agatha. 

“They fly backwards, you know.” 

“I didn’t know,” Agatha murmured, her eyes never leaving Rio’s face. “That seems exhausting.” 

Rio finally looked up at her, her eyes wild and bright. “For us, yes. Not for them. That’s why they're my favourite. It’s like… they refuse to be told what they can and can’t do.” 

“Sounds a lot like somebody else I know…” 

She felt the bump of a shoulder against her own before she realised that Rio had done so, both of them laughing under their breath until the hummingbird caught wind of their presence and promptly darted away to isolation. 

Once it had disappeared, Rio frowned. “They’re only around for a few seconds at a time.” 

“How cruel.” 

Rio shrugged her shoulders. “I think that's what makes seeing one so special.”

They went on that way - in an unestablished, established way - for several more years. 

Rio bought Agatha potted plants, and Agatha knew she had done so as an excuse to come over more often because Agatha would never be the one remembering to water them. On her days off, Agatha would double up portions on her batch cooking, knowing that Rio had an affinity with forgetting to eat if she was ever working long days in the parks. 

Each time Jen held a dinner party, Rio would be invited. Each time Alice held games night, Agatha was expected to be there alongside their host’s best friend.

One night, nearly a decade into their quasi-relationship-slash-friendship, Jen had consumed too much wine and decided it was time to state the obvious.  

“You know,” she said, her eyes narrowing at Agatha and Rio. “Normal people date.” 

Whilst Rio had nearly spat out her own drink, Agatha merely shrugged. 

“Tried that already,” she replied. “Could have worked until I discovered she picks up worms on the sidewalk and finds the nearest piece of soil to ‘rescue’ them. Oh, and she claps when the plane lands.” 

Rio scoffed. “And Agatha rams her shopping cart into the back of innocent old men when she gets impatient.”   

“Exactly, we’re incompatible. Besides, the world doesn’t exactly cheer on the gays.” 

Both Jen and Alice looked between them with disbelieving brows. 

“But you still sleep together?” 

“And go on vacations together?” 

“She can clap for the pilot to her heart's content if I’m not dating her,” Agatha’s shoulders found her neck, her finger circling the rim of her glass. “And we’re not discussing the other thing.” 

“You also finish each other’s sentences.”

“And,” Jen coughed. “You spend Christmas together.” 

“It makes sense,” Rio finally spoke up. “Her shifts are all over the place and it means we both don’t have to travel anywhere. I think you two are too far in your own heads about us.”

“Yes,” Agatha agreed. “Perhaps focus that energy into finding your own partners and you won’t have to worry about our lack thereof.” 

“Lack is a funny term for being each other's soulmate,” Alice, sitting in the corner of Jen’s condo and watching the exchange with fondness as she often did, mused. 

“We are not soulmates,” Agatha hissed, meanwhile Rio groaned, her hands finding her face in mock-disbelief despite their two friends knowing she only did it to hide her reddening cheeks. "Soulmates don't exist. It's merely made-up. A lie that helps people to get over the fact that they have chosen to be stuck with the same person for the rest of their life." 

The pair of them would continue to announce their disregard for one another for years to come. It had always been who they were, and it worked, ever since they had met, that morning in the ER department.

The seventies came and left, and with it life evolved and shifted in ways neither could have anticipated. Agatha reached the top of the food chain - or rather, became an attending physician with interns of her own to install fear into - whilst Rio was offered the Head of Urban Forestry position before she had even turned forty. Apartments grew and professional commitments strained the amount of time they could spend with one another. 

The only constant that had remained in their lives was each other, and Jen and Alice’s constant complaints about their lack of relationship with one another. 

The truth was, they had tried, but timing had never been on their side. 

When Rio had wanted more, Agatha was working seventy-five-hour weeks and barely had a chance to sleep in her own bed. When Agatha had finally felt ready, Rio had accepted a fellowship overseas for a minimum of two years. 

They had tried long distance, but neither could last without the other. 

New people came into the picture, none of them lasted because none of them could ever compare. 

There would be days, weeks and months that they came into each other’s lives and filled a hole that had been left open-wide. They would cook dinner together, fall asleep on the same couch during an old classic or end up in the same bed if one of them couldn’t be bothered to make their way home. Occasionally they would kiss, and occasionally they would do more, but it would always, always end. 

If it wasn’t Agatha pulling away - and it usually was - then Rio would and Agatha would pretend not to notice. Those same days, weeks and months spent together would turn into days, weeks and months without so much as a word from the other person and neither allowed themselves to feel the true extent of what it cost them. 

And then Rio would call, out of the blue, about a small, insignificant fact about her day that would usually never warrant the awakening of something so dormant. But Rio had always been that way - like a volcano, eruptive when she needed to be and quietly existing in her own corner of the earth when she knew it was better that way.

It was probably why they meshed so well together; Agatha had always been exactly the same. 

“Found a tree growing out of a chimney. It was awful, Agatha. Awful. I just had to tell you… I thought you’d appreciate the poor decision making. You’ve always loved anything that you can critique.” 

Each time Agatha received a call at home, she would answer in the hope of hearing that same, dark and mischievous voice. The moment she realised her ears hadn’t deceived her, and Rio was truly on the other end of the receiver, she would experience an odd, tingling sensation and fullness within her chest that sickened her to her core. 

“I feel sorry for the chimney,” she would reply. “Oh, and whilst you’re here, the leaves on my monstera are turning brown.” 

“I’ll come over with some food for it. Make sure you water it tonight, but do not over-water it, Agatha. I know what you’re like.” 

And just like that, they’d find each other again.

1991 - 23 Years After First Meeting 

It was Thursday when she got the call. A quiet Thursday, a rare day free of work which she would always look back on and regret because if she had been at work, there would have been nothing she could have done to prevent what had happened. 

A drunk driver had failed to stop at an intersection. 

It was Rio that they had smashed into. 

It was both soul-destroying and completely insignificant. 

It should have been insignificant because she was nothing to Rio. Not really. Not blood, not married, not anything meaningful to one another. Not in the eyes of the law, at least. Not even in the eyes of their own words to one another. It was just a hopeless, wishful thing that had never happened and now Agatha had to live with that for the rest of her days. 

But to receive that call, and learn that Rio would never be over for another dinner, or around for another vacation, or one more Christmas together… it changed every aspect of the life she had known for the last twenty-three years. 

When she attended the funeral, she had sat beside Rio - Rio in that damn ceiba wood coffin that she had insisted on having and Agatha had done everything in her power to actually ensure Rio would have  - long after the procession had left for the wake. 

She had sat there and thought of every moment they had endured together, and every moment they had missed out on. She had sat there and realised that there was no one else that had been keeping Agatha Harkness alive. No one apart from Rio Vidal. 

Agatha and Rio. 

There was no Agatha and Rio anymore. Just Agatha, and all the plants that Rio had bought for her, that she now had no choice but to keep alive because how else would she keep Rio alive? 

She hadn’t cried until she had gotten home and looked at those plants, sitting upon her porch amongst the salvias and cupheas and azaleas, realising that she had spent the last twenty-three years waiting for them both to become one, instead of whatever had actually happened between them, and now there would never be a world in which that could come true. 

There was a hole left open inside of her. Her heart, her lungs, her entire being. She felt it every day and could never fully recover. 

It may have been minutes, it may have been hours; she didn’t know how long she had been sitting outside in the early evening low-light until a glimmer of green broke through her burning eyes. 

She couldn’t help but laugh. 

“You’ve got to be kidding me,” she murmured under her breath as her eyes tracked the hummingbird. 

It wasn’t fast like the first time she had seen one. No - this one hovered and took its sweet time about it, as if taunting her, as if her sadness was a source of its own hydration. 

“Oh Rio,” she whispered. “I wish you could see me now.” 

And then, she was crying all over again. 

She cried about Rio for years to come, sitting on that porch after watering the plants, and each time she did, that same hummingbird would return.
