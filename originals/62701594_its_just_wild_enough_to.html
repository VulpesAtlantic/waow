Chapter 1

For what must be the hundredth time that day, Agatha stares at the advertisement in the Salem Gazette.

 

My name is Rio Vidal. I’m a thirty-year-old farmer looking for a wife. I do not drink, gamble, or fight. Women between twenty and thirty years of age are preferred. You must know how to cook, clean, and sew. You must be willing to move west and help run a homestead  You do not need to be good-looking. I will try to provide a comfortable and safe life. Serious inquiries only.  Find me at the Calderu house. 

 

Agatha knows the saying, ‘The devil you know is better than the devil you don’t,’ but her devil is Ralph Bohner, and Agatha is certain he is worse than any other devil she might meet. Ralph Bohner is twice Agatha’s age, and his last three wives all died of what the town records called  ‘unimaginable accidents’ within six months of marrying him. He is commonly known to be a drunk, and a wife killer.

 

The day Ralph first came to ask Agatha’s mother for Agatha’s hand in marriage, Agatha had falsely trusted that Evanorah would protect her and send Ralph away with a raised fist and scathing words. Agatha was astounded when Evanorah invited him into their home and offered him supper. By the time the meal had ended, Evanorah had quickly and happily agreed to let Ralph marry Agatha. Agatha knows there is no love lost between her mother and herself, but she didn’t realize her mother was so eager to have her out of the house that she would see her only child laid in a grave to make it happen.

 

As a fatherless young woman with little social standing and no money, Agatha knew her marriage prospects were bad. When she had reached twenty years of age and no one in Salem expressed any interest, Agatha resigned herself to a future of spinsterhood. She had finished school, became a teacher, and was working on saving enough money to have a small cottage of her own built as far from her mother as possible. Her mother knew this. Agatha had told her mother that she had approached the Maximoffs about buying a small plot of their land come fall. Could her mother truly not live another season under the same roof as her? Agatha swallows thickly. She takes out a pair of scissors, cuts the advertisement out from the paper, shoves it into her pocket, and gets ready to leave for Lilia Calderu’s house. 

 

Like most townspeople, Lilia Calderu avoided Evanorah Harkness and, by extension, Agatha. Agatha knows little of the widow Calderu. She knows that when her husband passed, Lilia took over his family’s tailoring business.  Evanorah found this to be particularly scandalous. She often would tell Agatha, “A woman’s place is in the home, anything else is wicked.” Agatha also knows that in early spring, the widow Calderu’s nephew, Rio Vidal, moved into the Calderu house. Presumably to help with the business. This makes Mr. Vidal’s advertisement even stranger. As the heir to a profitable business, why would he leave and risk life out in the West? And why would he search for a wife in the paper instead of asking one of the local families directly? 

 

Agatha doesn’t bother saying goodbye to her mother on her way out. She takes only the three goddess pendant her grandmother had given her on her death bed, her best jacket, and the money she’d saved from teaching. Agatha is sad to leave her students in the middle of the year, but she knows that their teacher leaving to go out west will be less traumatic for them than their teacher being murdered by her new husband. Agatha passes the schoolhouse on her way to the Calderu’s and feels a fierce longing to turn back and try her luck with Ralph Bohner, but she knows in her gut that if she stays she won’t see next Fall.

 

When Agatha reaches the Calderu house, she’s surprised by how immense and pristine it is. It has two stories and is painted a glimmering white with a fresh coat of paint. A far cry from the rotting logs that make up the two-room cottage Agatha shares with her mother. Agatha suddenly feels self-conscious, what is a girl like her doing at a house like this? She wipes dirt from her light purple woolen dress and tries to make her wild hair presentable by braiding it quickly down her back. She squares her shoulders, plasters on a smile that doesn't reach her eyes, and knocks on the door. This house contains her only hope. 

 

Lilia Calderu answers the door, and Agatha is surprised to see a smile and warm eyes. Agatha feels slightly calmer and puts her hand out to shake the older woman’s. 

 

“Good morning, Mrs. Calderu. I’m Agatha Harkness, and I’m here about the add in the paper.”

 

Lilia chuckles slightly, her eyes crinkling as she smiles, “Agatha, I know who you are. Salem is a small town and you’ve lived here you’re whole life”

 

Agatha swallows, “Has the position been filled?”

 

“No, dear. I’m so glad you’re here. Let's go find Rio, shall we?” Lilia asks, inviting Agatha into the house, and gesturing at a lovely chair for Agatha to sit in. 

 

“Rio’s in the garden, I’m sure. I’ll be right back,” Lilia tells Agatha. 

 

Agatha takes a moment to look around. She’s never seen such fine furniture. She notices an entire wall covered in shelves of books and feels dizzy at the possibility. She’s never seen so many books in one place, and the idea of possessing that much knowledge makes her breath catch. Knowledge is power, and power is agency. Agatha looks up at the sound of footsteps coming into the parlor. Lilia has come back and is followed by a man who looks entirely too young to be the thirty-year-old farmer who placed the add. 

 

“This is Rio,” Lilia tells Agatha with a quirk of her eyebrow. 

“My aunt tells me you’re Agatha,” Rio greets shyly. Agatha is surprised by how soft and high his voice is. He’s so clean-shaven, and his olive skin is so smooth that she wonders briefly if he can even grow a beard. Agatha gets lost in his deep warm brown eyes for a moment but catches herself. 

 

“You’re thirty years old?” Agatha asks skeptically and feels panic claw at her throat. Rio can’t be more than seventeen with his soft jaw, and girlish lips. Even his hair, chopped loosely around his shoulders and framing his face seems feminine. Agatha shakes her head and laughs bitterly. She addresses Lilia as if Rio weren’t even in the room.

Agatha sighs deeply. “Yes. My mother has promised me to Ralph Bohner.” She says in a small voice. Lilia humms sympathetically and Rio looks at her curiously.

Rio clears his throat, and Agatha notices again how smooth his neck is. “Rio” Lilia sighs, “Agatha is in no position to judge you. You’ll be helping her as much as she’ll be helping you. Tell her your secret.” Lilia urges, and Agatha looks between her and Rio frantically. She hates it when she is the least informed person in a room.
