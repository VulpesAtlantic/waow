Chapter 1

Agatha wouldn't claim to outright hate soccer; she simply didn't find the concept of people kicking a ball around, without using their hands, all that fascinating.

Despite this, every week her wife Rio, the town's youth soccer coach, would invite her to watch the practice.

Agatha always declined with a gentle smile, saying, "I'd love to, hun, but it just doesn't excite me."

Although Agatha noticed the flicker of disappointment in Rio’s eyes each time, she remained firm in her decision.

Even their son, Nicky, had toyed with the idea of joining the team but quickly realised that he did not share his mother's soccer talent or enthusiasm.

After just one game, Agatha withdrew him, having noticed his lack of interest.

"I just wanted to make Mami happy," the 11-year-old confessed to Agatha.

She admired Nicky's caring nature, a trait he surely inherited from Rio.

She reassured him, "Sweetheart, you make Mami and me happy every day just by being you."

Rio had recently taken over the coaching role from her former coach, who had retired.

Thrilled by this opportunity, Rio managed to juggle her landscaping job and her coaching duties seamlessly.

One evening, as Rio prepared to leave for practice, she asked, "Are you sure you two don't want to come watch?" Her navy hoodie, emblazoned with 'COACH' and the team logo, was ready.

 

Agatha, lounging on the couch with a book, glanced up and said, "No thanks, hon, go knock 'em dead."

Nicky, absorbed in his video game, flashed a sympathetic smile. Rio's shoulders slumped slightly as she headed out the door.

“Okay my loves, I will be home by 7pm”

That weekend, Rio busied herself getting ready for a big game.

Dressed in a team tank top and running shorts to beat the summer heat, she asked again if Agatha and Nicky wanted to join her, only to be met with Agatha's half-interested look.

 

After Rio left, Nicky approached Agatha, who was folding laundry.

"Mama?" he began hesitantly. Sensing something was on his mind, she looked up.

"What's up, sweetie?" she asked.

Nicky fidgeted, drawing Agatha's attention.

"I know why Mami always asks us to come to practice," he confessed, avoiding eye contact.

Agatha raised an eyebrow.

"Oh?" she prompted, curious.

He continued, "The other moms make her nervous. They’re always come up really close to her, touching her and I can tell she doesn’t like it."

Agatha's mind whirled. She felt her jealousy rising, a fact she hadn't fully acknowledged before.

The vein in her forehead pulsed as red hot anger flowed through her veins.

Her mind flashed images of Rio leaving that morning, clad in running shorts and her toned arms on display in that tank top.

The notion of anyone making her wife uncomfortable stirred something protective—and undeniably possessive—within her.

It became clear that this was something Rio hadn’t mentioned, likely to avoid this very reaction.

 

Knowing fine well that Agatha had a very nasty jealous streak.

Determined to support her, Agatha realised that perhaps it was time she and Nicky made an appearance, to cheer Rio on and remind her (and everyone else) that she has a loving family in the stands.

Her mind raced with images of Rio, kind and polite, subtly distancing herself from the overly-friendly attentiveness of the other soccer moms.

Her thoughts veered back to those moments of Rio’s quiet disappointment, the small frowns that had crossed her features each time Agatha declined an invitation to practice.

Agatha realised that perhaps it hadn’t just been about wanting family support at games; it had been about creating a shield, a comforting presence amidst the unwelcome attention.

Determined to address this, Agatha turned to Nicky, placing a gentle hand on his shoulder and schooling her face to something more relaxed.

“Thank you for telling me this, honey,” she said softly.

“How about we give Mami a surprise visit ?”

Nicky’s eyes brightened immediately.

“Really? You mean we’ll go to the game?”

“Absolutely, we’ll be her personal cheer squad,” Agatha confirmed, a mischievous glint in her eyes.

Oh she was going to enjoy killing some soccer moms.

Agatha donned her sunhat, and a slightly all too revealing sundress, to make a point of course and grabbed a thermos filled with iced lemonade.

Nicky sported a cap with the team’s logo.

 

As they arrived at the field, Agatha’s gaze scanned the crowd, noticing a cluster of mothers gathered around the sidelines, chatting animatedly.

She spotted Rio almost immediately, standing holding her clipboard looking distracted.

But what Agatha had zoned in on mostly was the bimbo with long blonde hair who was laughing at something while running her hand up and down her wife’s bicep.

If looks could kill, this woman would have died the most painful death imaginable at that moment.

 

Rio spotted them from across the field. Her eyes widened with surprise, then softened with a palpable wave of relief and joy. As she jogged over, a smile spread wider than the sky across her face, leaving the other woman following her movements, her face slightly crestfallen.

“You’re here! And you look….wow” Rio exclaimed, pausing a distance away, a hint of uncertainty still lingering.

“Of course, we wouldn’t miss it for the world!” Agatha said, stepping closer, wrapping her arm around Rio’s waist and claiming her lips in a not so appropriate kiss and swallowing the gasp that came from her wife.

Pulling back, face flushed, Rio narrowed her eyes at her wife knowingly but didn’t dare say a word.

Agatha’s eyes were zoned in on the blonde woman standing open mouthed from the other side of the field, a noticeable blush creeping up her face as she walked away defeated.

Rio knew Agatha was here to mark her territory. And she couldn’t be happier about it.

“We’re your biggest fans.” Agatha had shrugged and went to claim a seat on the bleachers.

As the game began, Agatha and Nicky settled into the rhythm of the match, cheering heartily, their enthusiasm genuine.

Agatha found herself unexpectedly caught up in the joy of the team working together, of Rio’s clear, encouraging voice guiding each player.

Only slightly growling audibly when one of the soccer moms tried approaching her wife with some half assed excuse of conversation.

“Hey Susan, sit your ass down and dont distract the coach!” She yelled at one of the women who clearly wasn’t named Susan.

When the match concluded with a triumphant win, Rio made her way over to her family, her expression one of pure contentment and gratitude.

The other moms exchanged glances but seemed to pick up on the unseen boundary, maintaining a respectful distance.

Agatha realised then that it wasn’t about the game itself; it was about being present for the ones you love, about understanding and supporting each other’s worlds, even if they didn’t initially seem appealing.

Later, as they drove home with Nicky recounting every detail of the match, Rio reached over, squeezing Agatha’s hand.

“Thank you,” she murmured, her tone conveying more emotion than words could capture.

Agatha smiled, her heart full.

“I think I’m starting to see the fun in soccer,” she admitted, leaning her head against the window, feeling the warmth of family enveloping the car like the gentle summer sun.

Just Rio. Sleepy. Soft. The same.

Which, somehow, almost made the spiral worse.

The day crawled.

Not metaphorically.

Literally.

Ava had dance class at 9 a.m., which meant glitter, chaos, and a room full of small girls jumping and screaming. Then swimming lessons at 11, which meant damp towels, shrieking, and the aggressive smell of chlorine clinging to everything.

Nicky, traitor that he was, had arranged to spend the entire day at his friend Billy’s house, leaving Agatha stranded on the sidelines alone.

Alone.
With her thoughts.
And her phone.

Ridiculous, she scolded herself as she sat on the hard plastic bench beside the pool, sunglasses on, posture immaculate. You are a professional. You have depositions to review. You have emails waiting. You have an actual career.

Her brain, however, refused to cooperate.

Instead, it offered up a looping highlight reel of fucking glossy red hair and knowing green eyes.

Agatha sighed sharply.

This was absurd.

And yet.

Her hand moved on its own.

Instagram opened automatically, muscle memory taking over. She didn’t even need to type Wanda’s stupid fucking handle — Scarlet_Witch88 — because it was already sitting comfortably at the top of her search history like a personal accusation.

Agatha tapped it.

The glowing ring around the profile picture appeared again.

New story.

Of course there was.

She pressed it.

The first few slides were harmless. Kids at breakfast, cereal bowls and waffles. A close-up of Wanda’s own gay-ass croissant, captioned something fucking wholesome.

Agatha flicked through quickly.

Next video showed the team outside on the field in light rain, kids running wild and slipping dramatically in the mud. She slowed down, scanning each frame carefully for Rio.

Nothing.

She flicked forward.

Then paused.

Wait.

She tapped back.

The next story loaded.

A selfie.

Wanda, hood up in a black waterproof jacket, rain beading on the fabric. And there, unmistakable across the chest — the top of the word “COACH.”

Agatha’s stomach dropped.

The caption underneath made it worse.

“Coach’s favourite ❤️”

Agatha sat up so abruptly the bench creaked beneath her.

Coach’s favourite.

A heart emoji.

Again with the fucking hearts.

Her fingers tightened around the phone as her mind sprinted ahead of her.

Was that Rio’s jacket? 

Had Wanda borrowed it?

Was she wearing it?

Was Rio the one who took that photo?

Agatha nearly dropped the phone into the chlorine-slicked tile at her feet.

Her pulse kicked up.
This was no longer a vague wine situation.
This was a declaration.

Coach’s favourite.

That fucking bitch.

Across the pool, Ava shrieked with delight as she splashed into the shallow end.

Agatha stared at the screen.

She zoomed in on the jacket.

Adjusted brightness.

Tilted her phone slightly like that might reveal hidden truth.

The word “COACH” was bold. Familiar.

Her heart was pounding like someone had just thrown down a gauntlet at her perfectly manicured feet.

She inhaled slowly through her nose.

Exhaled.

Did it again.

She was not overreacting.

She was responding.

There was a difference.

Her thumb hovered over her phone for only a second before she navigated to Lilia’s contact and pressed call.

It rang once.

“Agatha?” Lilia answered, suspicious immediately.

“Hi, Lil,” Agatha said, voice smooth and deceptively calm. “I need a favour.”

There was a pause on the other end. “What did you do?”

“Nothing,” Agatha replied. “Yet.”

 

An hour later, the logistics were handled with ruthless efficiency. Ava was delighted at the prospect of an impromptu sleepover with her favourite aunt, already packing her rabbit and flaunting her twenty dollars. 

Nicky, far too perceptive for his own good, narrowed his eyes at his mother but said nothing as he confirmed plans to stay at Billy’s.

“You’re going up there, aren’t you?” he asked quietly as she handed him his overnight bag.

Agatha adjusted her sunglasses. “Your mami misses me… you know how she gets.”

“Mm-hm.”

She kissed his head before he could push further.

Agatha punched the hotel address Rio had texted her into the navigation system. The screen lit up with a clean blue route.

Four hours.

She gripped the steering wheel and pulled out of the driveway, two words looping mercilessly in her mind, each repetition tightening her jaw further.

Coach’s favourite.

Her foot pressed slightly harder on the accelerator.

If Wanda thought she could casually stake a claim with curated Instagram captions and borrowed jackets, she had another fucking thing coming.

 

 

Four hours and one aggressively curated “Betrayed Wife Energy” playlist later, Agatha pulled into the hotel parking lot.

She sat in the car for a moment, engine still running, jaw tight, pulse steady but charged.

She stepped out, heels clicking sharply against the pavement, and marched into the lobby like she owned at least partial shares in the building.

At reception, she softened instantly, voice slipping into honeyed charm.

“Good evening,” she smiled. “I’m with the Westview Soccer Team. My wife, Coach Vidal, checked in earlier with the team, but I think she may have accidentally put the room under her maiden name. I’m hoping to surprise her.”

The receptionist hesitated only briefly before glancing at the screen.

“Oh yes, Coach Vidal… she’s in 412.”

“Perfect,” Agatha replied smoothly. “Thank you.”

She didn’t break stride.

Outside room 412, she paused.

From inside, she heard Rio’s voice.

“Oh, Fuck, YES!”

Agatha saw red.

Her blood ran hot in an instant. She didn’t think — she just started pounding on the door, hard and fast.

The door flew open.

Rio stood there, eyes wide, hair a mess, dressed in boxers and an oversized white t-shirt.

“Agatha!? What’s going on?!”

Agatha brushed past her immediately.

“Where is she?!” she demanded, scanning the room. “Where is that little skank?!”

She checked the bathroom. The closet. Looked under the bed. Threw open the curtains like someone might be hiding on the balcony.

“Agatha, what the fuck are you talking about?” Rio asked, still frozen near the door. “Where is who?!”

Agatha stopped mid-search.

And that’s when she noticed what Rio was holding.

Her Nintendo Switch 2.

Agatha stared at it.

Then at Rio.

Then back at the Switch.

“So,” Agatha said slowly, slightly out of breath as she pushed her hair back from her face, “you …weren’t screaming because you had someone in here?”

Rio blinked.

Confused.

Then hurt.

She lifted the console toward her like evidence in court.

“No. I finally caught a shiny Feebas.”

On the screen was, unmistakably, some sort of… purple… fish. Ugly. Sparkly. Apparently important.

Agatha stared at it blankly.

“You… really thought I was cheating on you?” Rio asked quietly, looking like she’d just been personally kicked by fate.

Agatha opened her mouth to respond—
And the door, which hadn’t fully latched, creaked open.

Wanda’s red head popped around the edge.

“Rio? Is everything okay? We heard yelling.”

She stepped fully inside, closely followed by another woman Agatha did not recognise.

“Yeah, it’s fine, Wanda,” Rio said, still staring at Agatha with wounded confusion. “This is Agatha.”

Wanda’s face lit up.

“Oh! Hi! Oh my god, Rio has told me so much about you. It’s so nice to finally meet you.”

Agatha managed a tight, polite smile and a curt nod.

Then she tilted her head slightly toward the other woman.

“And who’s this?”

“Oh, this is Natasha,” Wanda said brightly. “She’s the coach for Eastview soccer.”

Natasha offered a small wave and an awkward, apologetic smile.

And suddenly, everything clicked.

“Coach’s favourite.”

Rio and Natasha looked at her in complete confusion.

Wanda, however, flushed instantly, eyes flicking sideways toward Natasha in a way that was unmistakable.

“Oh my god,” Wanda muttered under her breath.

The ducks lined up in Agatha’s brain with horrifying clarity.

The wine.
The heart emoji.
The jacket.
The tag.

It had never been about Rio.
It had never been about her.
It had been about Wanda and Natasha.

Rio cleared her throat gently.

“Could you guys give us a second?”

Wanda nodded quickly, tugging Natasha back toward the hallway. “Absolutely.”

The door shut.

Silence.

Agatha stood in the middle of the hotel room.

Then she closed her eyes.

Embarrassment washed over her in a slow, devastating wave. The last two days of spiralling, stalking, catastrophising, imagining prison visits and custody battles — all of it collapsed inward at once as she sunk down onto the edge of the bed. 

Her shoulders dropped.

Her breathing shifted.

And behind her closed eyelids, tears threatened.

Rio stood there quietly, watching her, the hurt still visible.

The silence in the hotel room stretched.

Agatha didn’t move at first. She kept her eyes closed like if she didn’t open them, this entire humiliating situation might rewind itself.

It didn’t.

When she finally looked at Rio, her composure was gone. Not shattered. Not dramatic. Just… stripped back.

“I owe you an apology,” she said quietly.

Rio’s shoulders softened a fraction, but she didn’t step forward yet. “You think?”

Agatha swallowed.

“I was wrong. Completely. Irrationally. Spectacularly wrong.”

Rio let out a slow breath, running a hand through her hair. “You drove four hours.”

“Yes.”

“You stormed into my hotel room.”

“Yes.”

“You called Wanda a skank.”

Agatha winced. “That one I regret.”

Rio folded her arms, though there was no real anger in it — more hurt than fury. “Agatha… what happened?”

That was the part she hadn’t rehearsed.

Agatha reached into her coat pocket, pulled out her phone, and held it out.

“Instagram happened.”

Rio blinked. “Instagram.”

Agatha unlocked the screen and opened Wanda’s profile, her movements slower now, less accusatory. She tapped through the stories she’d screenshot — the two glasses of wine, the heart emoji. The “Coach’s favourite ❤️” caption.

Agatha’s voice was tight. “You said Wanda brought dinner. Then the wine. Then the heart emoji. Then that.” She tapped the screenshot of the jacket selfie. “Coach’s favourite.”

Rio stared at the images for a long moment.

Her expression shifted.
Not defensive.
Understanding.

“Oh,” she breathed.

Agatha looked at her sharply. “Oh?”

Rio rubbed the back of her neck. “Okay. I see it.”

Agatha’s brows pulled together. “You see it?”

“If I didn’t know the context?” Rio said carefully. “Yeah. That… looks bad.”

Agatha let out a small, shaky laugh that had no humour in it. “It looked very bad.”

Rio exhaled slowly. “Wanda’s been dating Natasha for like three months. They’ve been low-key about it because of the team parents, but that jacket? That’s Natasha’s. The heart? Definitely about her.”

Agatha stared at the screen again.

It did look different now.

Less threatening.

More… obvious.

Rio’s voice softened. “You really thought I was cheating on you?”

Agatha’s jaw tightened.

“I thought,” she said slowly, honestly, “that maybe you’d found someone easier. Less… intense.”

Rio’s face changed completely.

“Agatha.”

“I know how my brain works,” she admitted quietly. “It builds stories. And you’re usually there to dismantle them before they get out of hand.”

“And this time I wasn’t,” Rio finished softly.

Agatha nodded once.

“I know you,” she added, voice rougher now. “Logically. I trust you. Completely. But sometimes the jealousy gets ahead of the logic. And then I’m… here.”

She gestured vaguely to the hotel room. The four-hour drive. The chaos.

Rio closed the distance between them.

“I get that you spiral,” she said quietly. “I do. I’ve known that since we met.”

She didn’t let go of Agatha’s chin this time. She held her there gently, making sure she was really listening.

“But it still hurts a little that you could convince yourself I’d do that to you.”

Agatha flinched, the truth of it landing.

“I know,” she whispered.

Rio exhaled slowly, thumb brushing along Agatha’s jaw. “You think you’re the only jealous one?”

Agatha blinked.

Rio gave a small, almost embarrassed huff of a laugh. “Baby, I have to actively stop myself from lashing out every time someone gives you that look.”

“What look?” Agatha asked faintly.

“The seductive one,” Rio said flatly. “The one where they clock how smart you are. How confident. How… fucking gorgeous you are.”

Agatha stared at her.

“I see it at your work events,” Rio continued. “At the field. At the grocery store. Men, women, doesn’t matter. And every single time, there’s a part of me that wants to rip their fucking head off.”

Agatha’s expression softened, surprise flickering through it.

“But I don’t,” Rio said. “Because I know you. I know you would never, ever cheat on me. Not in a million lifetimes.”

Her voice didn’t waver.

“That’s the difference,” she added gently. “I get jealous. Insanely jealous sometimes. But I don’t doubt you.”

Agatha swallowed.

Rio’s gaze softened further. “And I know where yours comes from.”

Agatha’s jaw tightened immediately, instinctively defensive.

Rio shook her head. “I’m not saying it to hurt you. I’m saying it because I understand.”

She brushed her thumb along Agatha’s cheek.

“When your bitch off a mom left, she didn’t just leave a house,” Rio said quietly. “She left you with the idea that people walk away. That love is conditional. That if you don’t hold on tight enough, you lose it.”

Agatha’s breathing shifted.

“And so when you see something that feels like a threat,” Rio continued, “your brain doesn’t go, ‘This is probably nothing.’ It goes straight to, ‘This is how it starts.’”

The hotel room felt smaller. Quieter.

“I’m not her, mi amor,” Rio said firmly. “I’m not going anywhere. Not because it’s easy. Not because it’s convenient. But because I chose you. And I choose you every day.”

Agatha’s throat burned.

“I don’t want easier,” Rio went on softly. “I want you. Even when you’re dramatic and stalking Instagram like an fucking FBI agent.”

Agatha let out a weak breath that might have been a laugh. “I was thorough.”

Rio smiled faintly, but her eyes were still serious.

“You don’t have to protect yourself from me,” she said. “I’m on your side.”

 

Later, they lay tangled together in the hotel bed, the earlier chaos reduced to something almost ridiculous in hindsight.

Agatha was tucked against Rio’s side, one leg hooked possessively over hers like she was making sure she couldn’t disappear overnight.

“You know I’m yours, right?” Rio said softly.

Agatha squeezed her tighter. “I know.”

Rio grinned in the dark. “Good. Because if you ever storm in like that again, at least let me finish catching the fish first.”

Agatha huffed.

“I married a nerd.”
