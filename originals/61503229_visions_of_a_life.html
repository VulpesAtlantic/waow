visions of a life

Agatha’s eyes water as the cold autumn air meets her skin.

“There it is,” she says, stepping out of her car. She looks up toward the sky, the dark blue of dawn still battling against the waking sun, and takes a deep breath. It burns in her lungs. “The smell of death.”

A car door slams shut from the parking spot beside hers. Wanda rolls her eyes through a strong gust of wind.

“It’s a cold front, Agatha,” she says. “Not the Ides of November.”

“I wouldn’t be so sure,” Agatha says. She falls into step next to Wanda. “There’s a certain aura of doom looming around this place today.”

The place? Westview High School. The aura? First day back from fall break. Fall break? The only week during the first marking period that Agatha gets to spend an extended period of time off her feet and on her couch with not a single email answered or paper graded.

(She’d never say she uses the time wisely.)

“Yeah,” Wanda sighs. “Things aren’t going to be the same without Mrs. Davis around.”

Agatha slows her steps, Wanda stopping just a few paces ahead of her.

“Hold on—” Agatha says. “Mrs. Davis left?”

Wanda tilts her head, her eyebrows furrowed in a similar confusion.

“Heart surgery?” she says. “Sound familiar?” She retreats toward Agatha, her expression growing more concerned with each step. “You signed her going away card.”

It’s a minor detail, that lilt in Wanda’s voice that suggests this is something that Agatha ought to know about, and Agatha scoffs.

“I did no such thing.”

She would certainly remember if Mrs. Davis of all people was taking a leave of absence, wouldn’t she? Especially if she signed the damn card?

“I saw your signature on it,” Wanda says. She steps closer, a hand reaching out. It trails Agatha’s arm lightly, her concern now on full display. “You feeling okay?”

“I’m feeling fine—”

“Because, we have a deal—”

Agatha shrugs off Wanda’s hand roughly.

“I said, I feel fine.” She doesn’t spare Wanda another glance as she stalks ahead, Wanda letting out a light sigh before her footsteps pick back up.

“Thanks for being such a good friend, Wanda,” she mumbles to herself. “I appreciate you looking out for me, Wanda.”

Wanda, the drama teacher—it so aptly fits.

Agatha pulls open the front door and steps aside, gesturing for Wanda to enter.

“Ladies first.”

“Would you look at that,” Wanda says. “You do have manners.”

Agatha hums, a wry smile pulling at her lips. “Think that’s my quota for the day.”

“I’ll take what I can get.”

The school is empty—quiet and warm—and the fluorescent lights that normally bounce off of the tiled flooring and shiny beige lockers that span the length of the main hallway are all dulled, casting a soft shadow that beckons them deeper into the building. Agatha basks in the silence, knowing there’s only a short forty minutes before grumpy teachers begin to arrive and then only a shorter fifteen minutes until half-asleep teenagers with JBL speakers in their backpacks litter the halls and disrupt the sanctity of her morning. It’s futile anyway, the basking, that is, because Wanda’s still hovering beside her, worry radiating off of her.

Like always, it’s overbearing at worst, endearing at best.

“You know, I would tell you,” Agatha says. “If something were—wrong.”

She doesn’t need to look at Wanda; the squinting eyes, the disbelieving smirk—it’s all there, she can feel it.

“Sure,” Wanda says. Agatha steals a sidelong glance, and Wanda returns it with a pointed glare. “Just like you told me last time?”

A huff of air escapes Agatha.

“That’s hardly fair.”

“What’s hardly fair was finding out from Nicky, two weeks later.”

“It was eight at night, Wanda,” Agatha says. “We had nowhere to be. Nicky was already fed, put to bed, I mean, what—would you have put a baby monitor in my bedroom?”

They come to a stop at the intersection where the main hallway splits, Wanda’s classroom on one end and Agatha’s on the other. Wanda runs a hand through her hair, silent for a moment.

(Deep down—deep, deep, deep—Agatha knows it isn’t fair. That Wanda worries as a symptom, not by choice. That losing people the way that Wanda has—her parents, her brother, Vision—would make anyone overbearing. And that Agatha, whose lack of worry is also a symptom, is still a choice. That losing people the way she has—the people she keeps locked out of her head—was all by choice. Agatha doesn’t reserve the right to worry.)

“Fine,” Wanda says, eventually. “Fine, you’re right.”

Agatha eyes her, suspicious. “That’s it?”

“You want me to trust you?”

Agatha nods. Slowly.

“Then I trust you.”

“Well.” Agatha purses her lips. “Only took eight years.”

Wanda shakes her head, unamused. Then, a door shuts from somewhere down the hall, a pull back to reality.

“Hey—don’t forget to stop by the lounge today,” Wanda says. “Lilia’s taking donations for that class trip.”

Agatha groans inwardly as she clutches her bag. She’d given Alice all of her cash a week ago for—

“That’s it!” Agatha gasps. “I paid Alice two bucks to sign that card for me.”

Wanda closes her eyes, letting out a long breath of air.

“I knew I’d never seen it.” Agatha folds her arms triumphantly. “Here you were, blowing a gasket.”

“You—” Wanda starts, but quickly gives up on whatever berating was about to come out of her mouth. “I’m going to go enjoy my morning tea and pretend you don’t exist for at least twenty minutes.”

“Give Lilia my blessings,” Agatha says as Wanda starts down the hall. “And tell Jen that I hate what she’s wearing today.”

Wanda’s laugh echoes down the hall.

“Right after I ask the superintendent to give you a raise, too.”

“Gotta be honest here, William—I don’t know too many people looking to steal US History textbooks.”

“Okay, maybe not stolen, but—I left it on a bench and when I got back, it was gone, so, I think on a technicality that’s stolen, right?”

Hope litters his features, that unwavering, delusional optimism she sees so often in her students.

(She tries to envision herself at that age, if there ever was a point where unwavering optimism as a concept ever felt like a possibility—a guiding light through her first fender bender, a warm hand when she got a D in geometry—but she comes up empty. She supposes, naturally, that it’s something she never really had in the first place.)

“I’m sorry, kid. It’ll have to be replaced.” She opens her desk drawer and pulls out a notepad, filling out a slip. “Take this to the office during your free period.”

“My parents are going to kill me,” he groans.

She holds out the slip. Smiles, pitifully.

“Hey—at least they’re around to kill you, right?”

The staff lounge, as if determined by some higher power specifically designed to cause the highest amount of psychological turmoil possible in Agatha Harkness’ life, is in the science wing; otherwise known as the wing that Agatha Harkness specifically does not enter unless someone is dead, dying, or on fire.

(That clause, of course, has yet to be tested.)

Her one saving grace, is that the next period has started by the time she makes it into the lounge, and she’s delighted to find it mostly empty. There are a few stragglers heating up food and grading papers, but none of the usual suspects are present, which means she can get in and out with no problem.

Agatha digs around in her bag for whatever leftover change she can scrounge up, successfully sourcing not only two quarters, but a dime as well. Pleased with her philanthropy, she drops the change in the bucket.

It’s then, that a grumble comes from the other side of the lounge—someone fiddling with the ancient coffee maker. Agatha looks around to see if someone is going to help, but everyone else has seemingly left the room.

“You have to hit it sometimes for it to—”

Her voice catches as the person turns around, becoming less of a person and more of a woman, and less of a woman and more of a ghost every second that passes. Her shoulders tense, and the rest of her words slip off her tongue, fading into nothingness as her mind becomes something that she’s suddenly lost the sense to access. She briefly wonders with what’s left of her wit if this is the day that her brain has finally decided to implode on itself completely, if Wanda wasn’t right to be concerned, and if she isn’t currently lying on the floor, convulsing as blood pools into the cavities inside her skull, hallucinating the woman before her.

Rio.

Agatha’s pulse jumps at the sound of her name spilling out of Rio’s mouth, her fight or flight activating in the tormenting mode of freeze.

(It’s unbearable, how entirely natural it is to hear, how years can suddenly mean nothing in the span of a few seconds, how everything is different, and yet in this moment—Rio standing in front of her, looking into her eyes, saying her name—it feels like nothing ever changed.)

Rio looks down at her left wrist, that same watch resting against her skin.

“I believe I’m about to go teach a bunch of sixteen-year-olds about the carbon cycle.”

“Sure am.”

Deep breath.

“Like—long-term?”

Rio cocks her head. “It would seem that way, wouldn’t it?”

“Okay,” Agatha says, still in disbelief. “Just—exactly, how long is long-term?”

Rio crosses her arms then, backing into the counter as she shrugs.

“I don’t know, Agatha,” she says. “Longer than short-term?”

Agatha’s fists close at her sides. It’s obvious Rio is playing coy for her own entertainment.

(Agatha wouldn’t entirely say that she doesn’t deserve it, but she knows if there’s anything in all of humanity that she absolutely doesn’t deserve, it’s Rio showing up at her place of work. Long-term.)

“Funny. That’s funny. You’re still funny,” Agatha says, despite finding it all incredibly not funny.

Rio purses her lips, giving Agatha a once-over. If she could read minds—

“And you’re still…”

She drifts off, and Agatha can’t help as she gives in.

“Still?”

Rio’s eyes return to hers, then shift upward as a sound comes out of the PA. She hums softly.

“Would you look at that,” she says. “Saved by the bell.”

“It’s the late bell,” Agatha corrects her.

Rio looks down at her watch in exaggerated surprise. “Which means I am late to class.”

Agatha smiles, certain it comes out entirely as a grimace.

“Well then,” she says. “Just our luck.”

It’s only forty minutes later that she finds herself whispering to Wanda in the cafeteria, back to the wall as they monitor lunch.

“Are you sure?”

“What do you mean, am I sure?” Agatha says. “That’s her.”

“Wow.” Wanda stares across the lunchroom, Rio slowly weaving around tables full of students. “I’ve gotta be honest—she is not what I expected for you.”

Agatha tilts her head as she takes Rio in. Her hair is longer than Agatha remembers it ever being—still dark and wavy, but neat, put together—and her clothing is a little more authoritative now, traded dresses and cardigans for crisp pants and shirts with buttons on them.

Wanda snorts.

“Young.”

Agatha rolls her eyes. “She’s older than you.”

“I’d always imagined it was some mutually destructive coming of age where two women find themselves together before growing apart—”

“Redhead?”

“Married.”

It comes out before she can stop herself, and Wanda’s face falls slightly at the comment, her hands coming together, fiddling with the wedding band that still sits loosely on her ring finger. They drift off into a comfortable silence, or, as comfortable as any silence can get in a room that’s all slamming lunch trays and teenagers screaming at each other despite only sitting about two feet apart from one another. Agatha crosses her arms. With Wanda, the only way out, is through.

“Seriously, Wanda?” Agatha snaps. Her eyes flash to Wanda’s, and she wonders if Wanda can see, if that stupid little smirk is proof that, yes—whether Agatha wants to admit it or not—she’s freaking the fuck out, and everyone can tell. She huffs. “We’re not having this conversation here.”

Agatha looks away as quickly.

When she’s sure no one has followed her out of the lounge, she heads straight for Rio’s classroom, not bothering to knock before stepping in and shutting the door behind her. “I don’t know what you’re talking about, Jen.”

Wanda Maximoff is a very annoying person.

“What I want, is for her to disappear from my life,” Agatha replies. “Forever.”

Wanda laughs, dropping into the seat next to her. “Right.”

“I do.”

“Okay.”

“I mean it, Wanda.”

Wanda flashes her eyes. “I believe you, Agatha.”

Agatha huffs, sinking deeper into the seat. “No, you don’t,” she mumbles.

“Just—hear me out,” Wanda says. “Is it possible that you don’t want to ask her for forgiveness because you’re afraid she might actually forgive you?”

Agatha shakes her head. “Is it possible that one day, you will finally stop asking me stupid questions?”

“What I’m hearing is that I’m right.”

“Fine, okay,” Agatha says, sitting up. “Let’s say I apologize—again—and she forgives me. Then what? We just date?”

“I think that’s what most people hope for, yes.”

Agatha drops her head into her hands. Groans. “I wish she never showed up here.”

“I don’t think that’s true,” Wanda says, quietly.

“Yeah?” Agatha asks, peering through her hands. “How do you figure that, exactly?”

Wanda shrugs. “A hunch.”

“Well, your hunch is wrong,” Agatha says, burying her face completely in her hands once more. “I wish the only thing I was thinking about right now was how many glasses of wine I can physically down over break.”

Agatha expects an instant berating, remember your nine-year-old?, but it doesn’t come. She looks up to find Wanda staring at her intensely.

“What?”

Wanda shakes her head. “It is so endearing to watch you grapple with your conscience.”

“I am not—”

“You are,” Wanda interjects. “You’ve been pushing this all down for years and now you’re finally facing the consequences of your actions, and it sucks.”

Agatha doesn’t really have a rebuttal for that.

(It is possible, that, despite her affinity for asking very stupid questions, Wanda sometimes says very smart things.)

Wanda pats Agatha’s thigh. “Come one,” she says, standing up. “You’re still driving the boys to the concert for me tonight?”

Agatha, thrilled to leave the prior topic behind them both, abides. “I told Nicky he could have candy if he sits through at least three songs, so we certainly will not be missing it.”

And, then, right before they reach the door, she remembers—

“What’s this I hear about a bet?”

Agatha can’t think of many things that she’d like to do with her one life in a physical body less than watch a bunch of high schoolers sing Silent Night off-key for two hours, but here she is.

(Back row, of course—if she needs to laugh, she’ll do it safely where only the late parents can hear.)

If nothing else, she’ll admit later, to nobody but Wanda, that the concert itself is cute. The backdrop looks good—no doubt there—and the students are all in matching black outfits that they don’t completely disappear into the background in, and it’s not even Wanda’s class, so the fact that she doesn’t have to pretend to like it already bumps it up a few points. Nicky stays for his required three songs, a bag of M&Ms sitting happily in his hands, and he doesn’t stay for a second longer, running off with Billy and Tommy the moment he’s free of the arrangement.

It’s only near the end, when the crowd is herded out into the main hallway and she isn’t quite sure where the boys have run off to, and she isn’t quite sure where Wanda is, either, that a small, tiny bit of panic rises up in her chest, and she knows they couldn’t have gone far, but it turns out that she doesn’t have to search for long because it’s near-instant that a soft cry erupts over the crowd, and she’d know that cry anywhere.

She pushes through the sea of bodies—smiling teens and parents with cell phone cameras getting twenty of the same photos—only to then hear another voice.

“Don’t worry, we’ll find your mom, alright?”

Rio comes into view first, her back turned as she kneels in front of a tearful Nicky, the latter instantly spotting Agatha.

“What happened here?” she asks, steadying her breathing as she crouches down. She pushes Nicky’s hair back and brushes a stray tear from his cheek, giving the rest of him a once-over. She catches Rio’s hand leaving his knee, and Agatha looks to her, hesitantly.

“The boys were running around,” she says. “This one took a hard spill.”

“Is that right?” Agatha says, rubbing his knee. He nods, still tearful.

(His sensitivity, straight from her, no doubt.)

Agatha frowns. She leans in closer. Whispers.

“Think it’s gonna fall off?”

He smiles, just slightly. Giggles. Rubs his eye.

“No.”

She smirks, ruffling his hair.

“Good.”

She gets him standing, tears gone as quickly as they came, and Wanda appears, then, all smiles until she takes in the scene before her—Billy and Tommy standing there looking like they’re waiting for the scolding of a lifetime, Nicky’s red eyes, Rio, standing hesitantly off to the side.

“What party did I miss?” she asks, suspicious.

“The boys decided Fly Me to the Moon was the perfect soundtrack to play tag in the hall,” Agatha says. She scratches the top of Nicky’s head. “This one banged up his knee.”

Wanda frowns. “Do we need to have another conversation on why there’s no running in the school hallway?” she asks, the boys all instantly parroting a synchronized, “No,” in response.

Then—

“It hurt less than the driveway,” Nicky says, entirely serious.

Wanda rolls her eyes, Agatha only shrugs.

“He makes a fair point.”

Rio lets out a light huff of air from the side—a laugh, if Agatha didn’t know any better—and it’s then, that Wanda seems to notice her again. Agatha can see the moment it clicks; an opportunity formulating in Wanda’s mind.

“Um—I can take the boys home. Get some ice on that,” she says. “Because…I—promised I would.”

And Wanda knows that Rio isn’t an idiot—knows that Rio knows that Wanda knows that Rio isn’t an idiot—but Wanda continues on before Agatha has the chance to interject.

(And, maybe, just maybe, the fact that Rio hasn’t walked off without a word yet, is proof of something.)

“I’ve got rocky road at my house,” Wanda says, eyeing Nicky. “Sound good?”

The resilience of young boys cannot be understated—the three of them immediately take off running toward the exit.

“Guess I better go,” Wanda says, wagging her brows.

Then, it’s just them.

“Well,” Agatha huffs. “A lot of tears just to run off again, huh?”

Rio smirks—sardonically, possibly, Agatha isn’t too sure—and she looks down, her arms crossed behind her. “Reminds me of someone.”

Ouch.

“Rio, can we—can we talk?”

Because as much as Agatha wants to prove Wanda wrong, she won’t last much longer pretending that it isn’t killing her to exist in the same place as Rio while this is all unresolved. To hear from third parties that she’s causing enough grief for other people to notice Rio moping. To watch Rio comfort her kid, no questions asked, no hesitations.

“Please,” she whispers.

Rio bites her lip, draws a long breath—an even longer exhale—and then she nods.

“Fine,” she says.

 

 

They end up in Rio’s classroom, dark, save for the dim lamp in the corner that Agatha is certain will be confiscated by admin during Rio’s first classroom check. Rio sits on a desk, waiting.

“I um—I thought you wouldn’t show,” Agatha says.

(And, not her best start, she’ll admit that.)

“I don’t run away from my responsibilities, Agatha.”

Agatha nods. She deserves that. She deserves everything Rio has already given her and will continue to give for the near and far future. She deserves a lot more than what Rio could even conscionably give, if Agatha’s being completely honest with herself. And, graciously, Rio continues, getting right into it. No holds barred—Agatha doesn’t even have to ease them in.

“If I wasn’t staying, would you have told me?”

Agatha stills. Looks Rio in the eye. “No.”

Rio nods like it’s the answer she was expecting. Agatha thinks Rio knows her well enough to have been.

“Honestly, Agatha—” She shakes her head, sighs. “I’m really not sure how I’m supposed to feel about this.”

Agatha looks down. “I wouldn’t know either,” she says. “If the roles were reversed.”

“The roles would never be reversed,” Rio snaps. “I would never have done what you did.”

Agatha toes the linoleum. She says it plainly.

“I know.”

She looks up, after a moment, Rio staring her down with a look that could most certainly kill. But there’s also something else, and that’s what hurts more. The sadness.

“So—what, you just had a fucking baby?”

Agatha nods.

“On your own?”

Nods again.

Rio turns her head, eyes focusing off into the distance. She crosses her arms, grinds her jaw.

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“I’ve been asking myself the same question for a decade, Rio.”

Rio scoffs. “Well, let me know when you figure out a good excuse.”

Agatha looks down, clasps her hands together.

“All this fucking time…” Rio mutters.

Agatha’s head raises. “What?”

Rio turns to her again, finally, tears clear as day right there in her eyes. Agatha swallows.

“All this time I thought you were mad at me,” Rio says. “That it was me. My fault.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Agatha asks. “You’re suddenly absolved of all wrongdoing?”

“I’m saying you’re not,” Rio replies. “That’s it.”

“Okay—fine, sure—” Agatha says. “We have established that I massively fucked up and caused entirely undue harm—”

“Look, Agatha, you’re the one who wanted to talk, so I’m talking.”

“Unreservedly.”

“Okay, I’m out—"

“Wait—” Agatha steps in front of Rio, holding her hands out. Rio settles back onto the desk. “Wait.”

“You have five seconds.”

“I just wanted to—” She groans. “I don’t know, Rio, I don’t fucking know, okay? I just—I thought you deserved to talk about it.”

“That’s the problem, Agatha,” Rio says. “You never fucking know.”

Rio pauses then. Stands again.

“Except—” she says, gestures to Agatha’s stomach. “Maybe some things, I guess.”

“That’s not fair.”

Rio laughs. Agatha winces as a tear falls.

“You know what isn’t fair, Agatha?” she asks. “You don’t even have a reason.”

“You’re acting like I walked into Claire’s and just decided to swipe a pair of earrings,” Agatha says. “There was a life in question.”

“Yeah, a life you hid from me,” Rio says, angrily. “That—that I slept next to. Made fucking breakfast for.”

Agatha wraps her arms around herself. It’s beyond to say, even for Agatha, presumptuous to assume, that there even is anything, but she has to try—

“Just tell me what you want from me,” Agatha says. “Anything. I’ll do it.”

“What I want, is for you to tell me why.”

Agatha throws her hands into the air.

“You said you weren’t ready.”

“I didn’t know you were already fucking pregnant.”

Agatha closes her eyes as she remembers; Rio had been very clear, in that moment.

(If you need a kid right now, that badly, you can have one without me.)

“No,” Agatha says. “No—I’ll own up to a lot, Rio, but you made your choice. That was a decision you made.”

“But you didn’t let me decide, did you?” Rio asks. “When it really, really, came down to it, you chose for me.”

“I—I thought that you—”

“What?” Rio interjects. “Seriously, Agatha, what? Did you think I was going to stuff you in the trunk and drag you to an abortion clinic?”

“No—”

“I mean—is that really what you think of me?”

But Agatha hesitates. And Rio knows her—knows every one-word response, every twitch of her eyebrow, every flick of her wrist—even after all this time, she knows what that hesitation means. What it implies.

Rio’s eyes close. “Agatha,” she says, her voice only a pained whisper.

“I didn’t believe it,” Agatha says, wiping away an angry tear.

“But you thought it?” Rio asks, eyes still shut, tight. “Like, that was a thought that you had.”

Agatha can’t deny it, no matter how badly she wants to. She whispers, but it’s still too loud in the empty room.

“You said you didn’t think I could do it, Rio.”

(How do you expect—)

Rio opens her eyes, and the hurt on display is nearly unbearable.

“Jesus, Agatha,” Rio says. “Do you know how worried about you I was?”

“I know it was a lot, but—”

“A lot?” Rio asks, incredulous. “I couldn’t eat, couldn’t sleep. Every time I couldn’t get ahold of you for more than a few hours I had to wonder if you’d finally wrapped your car around a tree or—or had gotten lost somewhere and couldn’t get back—meanwhile, you were just galivanting off at the fertility clinic, I guess.”

“It wasn’t—"

“Every time it lasted for even thirty minutes longer than usual, I had to reconcile that it could be getting worse, I had to wonder it if would be permanent—if you’d wake up the next morning not knowing who I was. Do you get that, Agatha?”

Rio hadn’t said any of this to her then.

“I—I’m sorry, Rio, I—”

“I don’t want you to be sorry about that part,” Rio says. “I just want you to understand that I never, not a million years, ever thought that you’d be a bad mother.”

Agatha thinks her heart does it; finally sinks all the way through. She looks at Rio through unshed tears.

“I know.”

Rio’s shoulders deflate slightly, as if some weight has finally been lifted by those two words alone. Though, not all.

“Then, why?”

And Agatha can’t hide from it anymore.

“I was scared,” she admits. “I was scared, and I ran. It’s horrible and it’s the truth.”

“Of me?” Rio asks again.

Agatha shakes her head. “I was scared that you’d be right. That I wasn’t ready, and I’d have this kid and forget my name and leave it in some shopping center and ruin our only shot, and I—I couldn’t handle that.” Clenches her fist. “I couldn’t live with messing up your kid, Rio.”

“So you took that risk alone?”

Agatha smiles, sadly.

“I’m used to messing up what’s mine.”

Rio’s brows soften. Furrow, just a little bit. She’s angry still, Agatha can tell. Rio’s anger has always been a quiet simmer compared to Agatha’s rage, but it hits all the same.

“I’m sorry,” Agatha says, firmly, definitively. And then—

Strong arms wrap around her, tight. Just how she remembers, heads fitting squarely, side by side—the most perfect match she ever believed she’d find. She sighs into the hug.

“I’m still mad at you,” Rio whispers.

“I’m mad at myself, too.”

A beat. “I looked for you.”

“I wasn’t ready to be found.”

She can feel Rio’s jerky head nod next to her, slow, hesitant.

“And now?”

“Where the hell do you keep your peeler?”

“Third drawer on the right.”

“Not there.”

“On the left.”

Wanda looks to the left. Frowns.

“That’s a cabinet.”

Agatha throws her hands up. “I tried.”

Wanda sighs. “Roasted potatoes it is, then.”

Wanda returns to the spread before her, a concoction Agatha didn’t even dare to question beyond confirming it’s something that Nicky will eat. Agatha looks down at her book, settling into the silence once more.

“No,” Agatha says.

A beat. “You sure?”

“It sounds like you want me to be nervous.”

Agatha’s jaw sets. She raps her fingers across the table.

“It’s possible that I’m slightly nervous,” she says. “Slightly.”
