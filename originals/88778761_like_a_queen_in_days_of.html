Chapter 1

Before she’s even closed the door of the faculty room behind her, Agatha knows she can kiss her research grant goodbye.

No matter how well-rounded and structured her proposal had been—she’d recognized the pitying look in the eyes of Director Woo as she’d concluded her keynote speech. The pictures of her fellow researchers at a recent excavation site in Egypt—flashing their smiles, thumbs, and some of their findings to the camera—had only seemed to deepen his sympathetic frown. 

Now making her way through the light but colorless hallways of Harvard’s anthropology department, Agatha’s mood sours with every yard she gets closer to her trusted desk in the Peabody lab on the fifth floor.

When she enters the room, a set of curious eyes pop up from behind the monitor on the desk neighboring her own. Before Agatha can silently wish for nobody to ask how her pitch went, she hears a pressing: “And?”  

Her favorite coworker leans to the side, her entire face now visible next to the monitor. Optimistically lifted brows deepen the wrinkles in her aging forehead: Lilia is, with her 68 years of age, their most senior researcher by a long shot. Whenever the team has dug up another ancient artifact from a recently discovered settlement, Agatha likes to jokingly ask whether Lilia remembers burying it herself.

Today, Agatha’s disposition doesn’t allow for much humor.
“Nope,” she sighs, plopping her lips on the ‘p’ to mask how defeated she feels about the whole ordeal. She eyes her desk, decides she needs caffeine first, and heads in the direction of the coffee maker instead.

“Nope how?” Lilia presses her palms against the desk, leaning back in confusion. “Did Woo already make a decision? He’s not usually that fast.”

“Not in writing,” Agatha admits reluctantly, searching for her own mug from one of the lower shelves. “But his face told me to go fuck myself three ways into—Lilia, where’s my cup?”

“Shit, I think I saw one of the undergrads pour half a carton of oat milk in it, right after you left.”

Agatha whips her head around, treating Lilia to an accusing, unimpressed stare.
“And you let him? Are you kidding me?” 

In response, Lilia scrunches her nose in a lazy attempt at looking guilty. “My bad.”

Agatha shakes her head, showcasing how she isn’t angry—she’s simply disappointed. “The ice is paper thin with you.”

“I’ll get another one for you,” Lilia kindly offers, halfway out of her office chair already when Agatha lifts a stern finger.

“No. I’m a grown woman, I’m getting my own mug back. What did the miscreant look like?”

“Tallish, lanky, like he hasn’t grown into his limbs yet. Black curly hair and about eleven years old.”

“Capiche.”

Agatha nods, ready for her mission, and exits into the hall. She makes her way down until she finds a room where a group of students is hunched over a series of polaroids. Knocking twice on the open door, she doesn’t wait for permission to enter.

“Dr. Harkness, anthropology,” she barks at the group, startling them into looking up. “Which one of you is ballsy enough to use my coffee mug?”

Agatha assesses the students in front of her, just as they look her up and down in return. Her eyes land on a boy who fits Lilia’s description, his hands wrapped around a standard white mug adorned with the Harvard logo.

“You.” She tips her chin at him. “Where’d you get that?”

“Uhm—from the lab in 552,” he answers dutifully, his raspy voice lower than Agatha had expected. He looks down at the cup in his hands in confusion. “Looks like just a standard mug to me, though.”

“Where in 552?” Agatha presses, her patience running thin.

One of the other boys claps the alleged culprit sympathetically on the shoulder. “Oh boy,” he smirks. “You’re in for it.” 

Good, Agatha thinks. At least she has a reputation.

“I’ll help you out here,” she says, not unkindly. “You got it from the bottom shelf. Hilarious, considering you look about seven feet tall and there are dozens of mugs higher up.”

The mug stealer’s brow furrows, clearly conflicted. “It wasn’t the last one or anything,” he weakly defends himself. “You could just get another one from—”

“And how,” Agatha snaps, her fuse lit in an instant. She stalks closer to read his nametag. “Pray tell, Mr. Maximoff, do you suggest I do that? Should I just drop the clowning and get up from this chair to reach the top shelf? Ask Jesus to let him pick up my mat and walk to get my daily dose of caffeine?”

“Oh, I—I suppose I didn’t think about that,” Maximoff stammers, raking a hand through his black curls.

“Must be nice,” Agatha retorts with a smile. “So here’s what’s gonna happen: you will wash that mug, put it back where you found it, and take another one for yourself.”

“I could just get one from the top shelf for you, Dr. Harkness,” Maximoff rambles, shooting up from his chair.

“Don’t make me repeat myself. I told you exactly how you’re going to solve this problem. I want you to remember.”

When Agatha re-enters the lab with Maximoff trailing behind her like a kicked puppy, Lilia shoots her a disapproving glare.

“Stop bullying the undergrads,” her coworker tuts. “Not everyone’s default setting is to consider accessibility for wheelchair users.”

“Would be weird if it was,” Agatha admits, keeping an eye on the treatment her mug gets in the sink. “But if they’re sharing a space with me, they better learn sooner rather than later,” she shrugs. Then she calls out: “It’s a rite of passage, Maximoff!”

Maximoff curtsies dramatically, having just placed the washed and dried mug back on the bottom shelf.
“It’s Billy,” he offers, before meticulously turning the handle outward for easy access.

“It’s a rite of passage, Billy,” Agatha says, nodding in silent approval. Upon registering his expectant look, she clicks her tongue and adds: “Still Dr. Harkness to you.”





When Agatha logs on the next morning, the first thing she sees is an email from Director Woo sent at 11:02 p.m. last night, titled ‘Research Proposal Harkness, Agatha PhD’. She groans loudly, places her elbows on the desk, and digs her knuckles into her eyeballs to relieve the already upcoming migraine. 

“It’s too early for those noises,” Lilia remarks, waving a dismissive hand. “I barely got any sleep last—”

“I got an email from Woo,” Agatha interjects, successfully silencing Lilia with a valid reason for the disturbance.

“Oh. And he said no?” Lilia assumes, dragging the corners of her mouth down in sympathy.

“I haven’t opened it yet,” Agatha huffs. “But you know when you look at the subject and you just…know?”

Lilia’s eyes widen, and she gestures at Agatha’s screen. “So it could still be a yes?”

Agatha rolls her chair back to make space. “Be my guest,” she says airily. “Open it. It’s not like I care anyway.”

Lilia gives her a reprimanding look, but steps in and bows over Agatha’s desk anyway. Agatha crosses her arms and stoically stares out the window while Lilia clicks on the email. When she hears her scrolling down, void of any commentary, Agatha can’t ignore the little flutter of hope behind her ribs. 

“Dear Dr. Harkness…like to thank you for sharing your proposal…impressed by your expertise, theory and unique perspective on homosexuality in ancient Egyptian settlements,” Lilia murmurs, scanning over Woo’s report.

Agatha sniffs and brushes her nose with the back of her hand, drops it in her lap, and starts plucking on the cuticle of her right thumb.

“Even though your proposal shows clear evidence of the academic and mental capacity to lead this research, the board expresses severe concerns regarding your physical limitations when it comes to overseeing excavation sites.”

“Oh fuck that!” Agatha shouts, slamming both her fists down on her thighs.

“Your proposed research entails challenges including but not limited to: national and international travel, navigating uninhabited territories, traversing narrow spaces like caves and tombs, and the aforementioned excavations. Despite your adequate display of intelligence and motivation, we do not fully trust in your ability to lead a team of researchers through these obstacles impossible for you to overcome due to your physical condition.
It is hence, with great sympathy, that the board has decided against financial support of this proposal and your appointment as a Principal Investigator.”

“Blah, blah, blah,” Agatha sneers, moving forward to usher Lilia out of the way—she’s heard enough.

“The board likes to emphasize that this decision has nothing to do with your lack of—oh, you bunch of narrow-minded pricks,” Lilia says as she steps out of the way, taking Agatha in with an expression that holds a middle between fury and concern.

A painful pinch replaces the flutter in Agatha’s chest. She resolutely moves the cursor to the trash-icon and deletes the email by clicking the button with a flourish.

“See?” she seethes, quickly realizing Lilia is an undeserving recipient of her anger. Lowering her volume, she continues: “I knew it. I don’t know why I expected something different. In fact—I don’t know why I created the proposal in the first place. Can’t walk? Can’t lead. Accessibility? Never heard of her.”

“Those idiots still think that not having working legs is a valid reason for discrimination,” Lilia sighs, dropping back down in her own desk chair. “Someone should teach these highly intelligent people that paraplegia doesn’t equal being stupid.”

“Woo has worked with me for years!” Agatha protests. “He knows it only takes some minor adjustments. This isn’t about a lack of understanding—it’s about money. I’m more expensive, so apparently my ideas don’t matter.”

“Oh sweetheart,” Lilia says, reaching out for Agatha’s hand. But Agatha can’t stand the pity in her voice or the powerlessness in her eyes, so she snatches her hand away.

“Just give me a few minutes,” she says in a strained voice, trying not to let the bomb in her throat detonate in public. She pushes away from her desk, heading for the accessible bathroom. “I just—I’ll be right back.”

“I would give you the whole world if I could!” Lilia calls after her.

Agatha doesn’t respond, bites back tears, conducts her rage into the strokes that make her wheels turn faster.

I know, she thinks. I know you would. But never those in power. 





The rejection lingers in the back of Agatha's brain for a few more days. She is grateful Lilia hasn’t brought it up since reading the email—she knows her well.

Agatha knows that if she survives today, she can spend the weekend on her couch with junk food and bad reality TV, and the whole matter should be forgotten by Monday.

Unfortunately, Director Woo seems to have other plans for her, and shows up at her desk roughly an hour before she’s about to clock out.

“Agatha, do you have a minute?”

“Oh, so now it’s Agatha?” She barely looks up from her work. “What happened to: ‘with great sympathy, Dr. Harkness’?”

Lilia nudges her with an elbow, making her sigh and ultimately taking in the director in front of her. There is an apologetic crease in his forehead.

“I have a minute,” she says reluctantly, leaning back and weaving her hands together on top of her messy, dark brown bun. She raises her eyebrows to prompt him into speaking.

“My office, please,” Woo says, motioning for her to follow him when he turns around.

Agatha rolls her eyes at Lilia, who expresses her support by telling her to “smile and wave, Agatha. Smile and wave.”

“I’m more of a punch and grin kind of gal. But I’ll behave,” Agatha adds quickly when she sees Lilia open her mouth in warning.

As Woo marches to his office, Agatha silently trails behind him, armoring up to let whatever commentary waits for her glide off of her like a duck. She’s had enough conversations about discrimination in the workplace, she’s tired of defending herself—and her own right to exist as a human being. And if Woo has called her in to make some sort of official apology so she doesn’t take his decision higher up, well, she’s not sure she’s in a very forgiving mood today.

When he opens the door for her, Agatha notices a couple of things: firstly, Woo seems to be in the middle of an online meeting, judging by the large screen on the wall displaying eight separate squares with live faces. She recognizes them as the board she held her presentation for earlier this week.
Secondly, another real-life person is already sitting in one of the chairs across from Woo’s desk.

“Have a seat,” Woo tells her routinely, gesturing at the only other empty chair while he returns to his own, where he immediately starts fidgeting with his microphone settings.

“Gee, thanks,” Agatha says monotonously, taking it upon herself to awkwardly drag the empty chair out of the way to make space for her wheelchair. Her struggle gets interrupted halfway through when the other woman in the room gently lifts the offending chair from her grip and moves it all the way into a corner.

“My god, what a boor,” the woman amusedly murmurs, soft enough for Woo to not pick up on. Then she moves her own seat up to make even more space. “You okay like this?”

“Perfect. Thank you honey,” Agatha replies, laying it on thick with her voice as she maneuvers her chair in the right position and puts the breaks on. Even though she believes human decency should be nothing special, Woo can stand to learn by example some more.

“Dr. Harkness, anthropology, meet Dr. Vidal, geology,” Woo introduces them, gesturing at each woman individually.

The other woman holds out her hand.
“Rio,” she says, offering her name accompanied by a crooked grin. “Rio Vidal.” 

Agatha gets temporarily distracted by the fine lines around her mouth, her button nose and the playful look in her deep brown eyes before she shakes her hand and provides: “Dr. Harkness.”

Rio’s soft snicker doesn’t go unnoticed, but there’s no response otherwise—both women turn their attention back to the director.

“I’ll get straight to the point, ladies,” Woo says, interlacing his fingers as he leans forward on his desk. “We received Dr. Harkness’ pitch earlier this week, and Dr. Vidal came in to pitch her project this morning. Now, after a short debrief with the board, we must unfortunately come to the conclusion that your proposals are so similar to each other that we can’t rule out plagiarism. We have decided to conduct an investigation for which we require all of your used source material, references—”

“What?” Agatha interrupts him with a confused hitch in her voice. She looks to the side, but Rio seems just as surprised as she is. “I’m sorry, wait—back up a second. Our proposals were similar? What is yours about?”

“I want to find proof of tolerance toward homosexuality in ancient Egypt,” Rio explains somewhat wearily. Upon noticing that Rio likes to talk with her hands, Agatha's gaze drops to the many rings on her fingers—then to her cargo pants, and ultimately the telltale spread of her firmly planted boots.

“That—oh, yeah. Me too,” Agatha says, tearing her eyes away from Rio to focus her attention back on Woo. “A coincidence, it would seem? It’s not like we tag-teamed it to improve our chances of getting approved. I don’t know this woman.”

“Maybe not, but the likeliness is far too strong to label it as a coincidence. We need to rule out plagiarism of other studies, unauthorized use of artificial intelligence, and so on, and so forth—”

“But why?” Agatha starts reeling. “You already rejected my plan, only because I simply can’t walk. And now you kick me when I’m down? Make sure to hit me in the kneecaps, Woo, because I won’t feel it anyway and you can get your frustration out over your wife not wanting to give you a proper b—”

“Because corruption is a serious matter, Dr. Harkness,” Woo interjects, slightly raising his voice as his own irritation grows. “If our own researchers are corrupt, I can’t just deny them funding—I can’t let them work here at all. As for the rejection you received earlier this week: I think we came to the reasonable conclusion that because of your physical limitations we can not trust you to—”

“Whoa, wait—corruption?” Rio finally snaps out of her confusion, now seemingly realizing both of their heads are on the chopping block. “From what I’m hearing, the only corrupt action that has been taken is denying a scientist her funding because of a physical disability.”

Agatha turns to face Rio, baffled at this handsome stranger coming to her defense. It’s not that she’s not used to it, but people usually only support her when they have something to gain, when they can be the hero. In this scenario, Rio could’ve easily benefitted from talking Agatha down to make herself more eligible.

“From what I understand, Dr. Harkness has worked here for a while,” Rio continues, waiting for Agatha’s nod of confirmation. “But I am new to Harvard’s anthropology department, and let me be honest with you, Director Woo: if blatant discrimination is the normal state of affairs here, I’m not sure I want to work here at all. A shame, really. Had you given it some more thought, I’m sure Dr. Harkness and I would’ve made great partners. Now don’t waste your time and money on this useless witch hunt—I won’t be offering my services to you anyway.”

Agatha’s throat constricts when Rio finishes making her statement. She can’t quite believe what she hears: Rio is backing her up to the point of sacrificing her own career. The fact that she’s being taken seriously by someone who doesn’t need her to prove it first, makes her choke up. Yet, her astonishment quickly turns sour, turns into anxiety.

“Don’t,” she pleads softly but firmly, reaching out a wavering hand before realizing that might be too intimate a gesture toward someone she’s just met. Then again—Rio isn’t exactly being trivial with hers either. But even if Agatha has always dreamt of mattering just as much as anyone else, she can’t let Rio be her able-bodied savior. “Just…don’t. Please.”

She expects Rio to give in, to take her words back, maybe ignore her altogether. What she doesn’t see coming is for Rio to turn her whole body to face Agatha, make secure eye contact, and tilt forward slightly.

“Listen, honey,” Rio says, off-handedly repeating Agatha’s earlier pet name back to her. “I’m a geologist, my expertise is just soil and rocks, so I might be veering off my lane here. But if the head of the anthropology department—a study of that what makes us human—can’t spare an extra penny to accommodate your genius proposal…? Which it undoubtedly is, if it’s so similar to mine,” Rio adds with a chuckle.

Agatha slowly melts in her chair, breathlessly hanging on to Rio's words. The more passionate and confident Rio gets when she explains her point, the further she leans toward Agatha.

“If the alleged best anthropologists in the world can’t extend their interest in the human race to all humans, one of their own even, are they really the people you want to work with? I won’t put words in your mouth, Dr. Harkness, so I’ll only speak for myself: I know I do not. And that has nothing to do with you; it’s a choice I make for me. Regardless of us being partners or not.”

At some point during her speech, Rio’s hand has landed on Agatha’s knee. Even though she can’t physically feel the warmth or the weight of the palm on her skin, she feels the implication simmer up through her torso.

She realizes then that this moment is bigger than just a groundless accusation or a failed research plan—Rio challenges her to expect better treatment for herself.
And that’s exactly where it hurts. Demanding proper coffee mug etiquette from undergrads is easy. Insisting to receive equal consideration from higher ups is much, much harder.

Agatha looks in Rio’s eyes and almost breaks at the sincerity in them. Rio might be right, but what other options does she have?

Her gaze falls, lands on the hand atop her knee, and she humorlessly chuckles at the strain in Rio’s fingers indicating that she’s squeezing her leg in encouragement.

“You know I can’t—uhm, I have no sensation in—” she stammers, bringing Rio’s focus to her leg. Rio withdraws her hand as if she’d burned it, as if she had no idea it was resting on Agatha’s leg in the first place.

A crackle from the speakers makes them both jump up, looking equally guilty even if they have no reason to be.

“Now I see!” One of the little squares lights up within a bright green frame, indicating the person speaking on the screen behind Woo.

“Pardon the interruption. Dr. Strange, editorial manager at National Geographic,” the somewhat pixelated man introduces himself. “As you may or may not know, Harvard anthropology and Nat Geo sometimes share research and funding for certain studies or documentaries. I hope you would forgive my very forward assumption, but am I correct in understanding that you two are together?”

Agatha feels a barking laugh traveling up her throat, just as Woo turns to the camera to tell the interruptor off.

“Dr. Strange, I’m not sure this is an appropriate—”

“I know, I know.” Dr. Strange throws up his hands until they’re just visible in the frame. He laughs curtly and continues: “It’s just—I heard you say ‘partners’, and then everything clicked. Even your attempt at getting your inexplicably similar projects funded makes sense now. I personally don’t think anything is wrong with planting as many seeds as possible, though I have to ask—”

“Dr. Strange, I think you’ve—oof.” Agatha blows air through pursed lips when Rio’s knuckles bump into her bicep. Before she can continue pointing out his false assumption, she watches Rio shake her head almost invisibly.

Strange’s camera glitches for a second, but nothing indicates his awareness of Agatha’s swallowed interruption.

“—whether you were planning on being on the team together, if one of you got appointed as the PI? And were you planning on keeping your involvement a secret throughout the entire research?”

“Excellent question,” Rio pipes up, crossing her legs and straightening her back as she formulates an answer. “We did discuss being on the team together. With Dr. Harkness’ anthropological background and my knowledge of geology, neither of us would misstand in any archaeological undertakings. Ideally, we’d share the responsibility as PIs, though we do acknowledge that such a construction would make it hard for us to separate our private lives from our careers. Ultimately, we’d be willing to find a fitting solution for this problem.”

Agatha stares at her in complete bewilderment. Where is Rio going with this?

On the screen, Strange nods empathetically.
“I see. I must admit, despite the board’s earlier rejection of Dr. Harkness’ plan…Given the circumstances, I am very interested in a collaboration between you and Nat Geo. I can only imagine how much traction your study will get if I’m allowed to use your relationship for marketing purposes. ‘Lesbian scientist couple searches for ancient predecessors in Egypt’,” he says, shaping the imaginary headline with his hands.

As Agatha’s brain slowly processes the somewhat unimaginable scenario unfolding in front of her very own eyes, Rio treats Strange, Woo, and everyone on the screen to a blinding megawatt smile. Agatha knows she has to make a decision now: play the game, or bow her head.

“So…It’s true?” Woo cautiously asks, his eyes darting between the two women. “You’re—you’re together?” 

Agatha forces her own lips to match the grin on Rio’s face. She releases the breaks of her chair and wheels herself closer to the woman she’s known for about twelve minutes. “Twelve years and counting,” she says proudly, placing her hand on Rio’s knee to mirror the earlier gesture.

“I…don’t know if I can forgive you for withholding this information during the process of receiving funding,” Woo says, visibly seeking his footing. “Even if true corruption can’t be proven, I’m convinced there should be a penalty for—”

“Ah, penalty, schmenalty.” Dr. Strange laughs jovially, successfully shutting Woo up in his attempt to further restrict the women in their pursuit. “Personally, I think that keeping your involvement a secret has been very understandable. Who knows, I might’ve done the same! You were just fresh out of luck, seeing as nothing gets past me,” he declares proudly, tapping his temple for emphasis. “If you can promise me that there will be no more secrets, no more lying, I think we can set up a second meeting to move onto the next stage of this shared proposal.”

Agatha’s heart thumps against her ribs, she can hear it in her ears. It’s really happening—this outrageous lie is giving her what she’s worked so hard for.

“Absolutely, Dr. Strange,” Rio promises, nodding solemnly at the camera. “No more lies from us.”

“Wonderful,” Strange smiles brightly. “Welcome to the Nat Geo team. And since we will be peers, you can call me Stephen.”

“Nice to officially meet you, Stephen,” Rio says, waving an awkward hand at the screen. “You can call us Rio and—” 

During the brief hesitation, Agatha realizes with a debilitating jolt that she hasn’t told Rio her first name.

“Agatha,” she whispers through her teeth.

“—Angela,” Rio finishes confidently.

“Agatha!”  

Rio’s eyes widen in horror, and meet her own. She tries to laugh it off: “Oh, how she hates when I call her that. They always write 'Angela' on her cup at the Starbies down the road. I was only joking. It's Rio and Agatha. Where there is a Rio, there’s an Agatha. Where there is an Agatha—”

“I think we get it,” Woo tells her, visibly unimpressed. “Well, I’ll email you each other’s contact information. Since this is a collaboration with National Geographic and Dr. Strange will provide the funding, my own involvement ends here. But do keep me informed on the development of the study, please.”

“Will do, Director Woo,” Agatha says with a winning smirk. “I can’t wait to update you. In fact—I’m already seated.”
