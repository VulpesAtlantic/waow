It’s Peter Quill. It’s Peter Quill. It’s Peter Quill.

She can’t stop replaying the words in her mind.

The closer she flies to Xandar, the bigger the planet grows through the windshield of her ship, the more she can’t focus on anything but those words.

It’s Peter Quill.

Her gut drops a little. She tries to remain calm, tries to deal with the whirlwind of emotions in her chest, but her breath stops in her lungs. The pressure builds up, choking her, and she feels light-headed.

It’s Peter Quill.

Apprehension pools in her belly. Confusion, too. So do jealousy and curiosity but it’s the fear that wins.

The edges of her vision get blurry. The colours become bleak. A sudden wave of nausea makes her suck in a sharp breath, but that only causes pain. A lump in her throat feels like a rock, pushing from the inside.

She winces.

And then tries again.

Inhale. Exhale. She repeats the process over and over—until the panic fades away. Until she doesn’t grip the armrest anymore. Until she can feel her own body, not lost in the rush of blood in her ears anymore.

It’s Peter Quill.

Gods, what was she thinking?

Her legs shake a little when she stands up. She slowly moves down the stairs, desperate for water. It never really helps—but every single time, she hopes it will.

Hopes.

Hope. What a foolish concept. It’s nothing but a mirage—one that kills more than a real, visible threat—but she can’t help it. The truth of how pathetic she is makes her want to scoff, but she only gulps down a glass of water. Then another.

Then she drops her head and closes her eyes.

The blood she drew for Thanos has gotten inside her lungs. It’s inside her heart, it’s running inside her veins, and she’s a matter of guilt, shame, and self-hatred. The sins she committed aren’t redeemable, the blood will never be washed from her hands, and she’s everything that’s wrong—but she desperately wants to feel right.

Her chest feels tight.

She’s terrified of never atoning for her past—while convinced it’s impossible.

It’s Peter Quill.

She shudders. In fear. In disgust. In hope.

Hope. Hope. Hope. She hates hope—because she hates herself—but she can’t give up on it. If she does, then Thanos will win—and she can’t give him that. He’s already taken everything. But not this.

The idea of never being more than what he made her be repulses her. Makes her sick. Every time. Even now, there’s a wave of nausea that rolls over her. Her throat goes dry, her stomach twisting and clenching, and when she reaches for another glass of water, her hand shakes.

Nova Prime’s words ring loud in her mind.

You know I will always go on any mission you send me, but I don’t do partners or teams, Irani. I work better alone.

It’s Peter Quill.

Three simple words, but she suddenly had nothing to say. Words failed her—but hope didn’t.

It flared up. It pulsed acutely in her chest, and she didn’t realize what she did until she agreed to the job.

What a fool. Sentimental, clueless idiot.

She scoffs, frustrated with herself, and pushes away from the counter. Nervously pacing around the tiny kitchen, she thinks of storming up the stairs and turning the ship away. She wants to forget about all the responsibilities. About the mission. About the fact that she’s trying to help and can’t just skip on a job.

She almost does that.

But when her legs carry her to the stairs and she grips the handrail for support, she realizes she can’t lift up her leg no matter how much she tries. Her body seems frozen, and she doesn’t know if it’s guilt or hope that keeps her tethered.

His world gut turned upside down too many times—and yet, he doesn’t seem tainted or cursed. He doesn’t seem like he’s drowning, or like he hates himself. She tried to look for the darkness she sees every damn day in the mirror, but she couldn’t see even a sliver of it. No shadows. No regrets. Only light.

Blinding, spanless light that’s always filled her with longing. Even now, a deep, strong wave of it washes over her heart, and she involuntarily tightens her hold on the handrail.

How did he find the light after everything he went through? She knows hell, she knows pain, and he went through it all. She could’t learn everything when she did her research, but the bits and pieces of what she learned were enough.

Loss of his mother. Abduction. New, unfamiliar world. Ego.

He lost so much—but he’s not suffocating.

Can she ever learn not to drown?

Hope squeezes all air out of her. So does the fear.

He thrives in the light. She’s withering in it. The difference between them couldn’t be more obvious. She knows she’s flying towards someone who’ll only shatter the broken pieces of her heart even more.

She doesn’t want him to highlight everything that’s wrong with her. She knows he will.

Her curiosity about him isn’t worth it. Her hope to have the same isn’t worth it. Nothing is— and yet, everything is worth it. Because if it’s not, then Thanos is right.

She’d rather die.

She swallows thickly. This time, when she tries to walk up the stairs, her body listens.

When she steps inside the Nova Corps headquarters, she forces herself to steady her breath. It’s too late to run away now. She’s already here.

The usual security protocols take little time. As always, a Corpsman on duty doesn’t really look her directly in the eyes. There’s no outward hostility toward her—but there’s also very little warmth.

They might be unafraid of her, but they don’t go out of her way to get to know her either. She reminds herself that this should be fine with her. What’s the point of engaging in conversations when she’s barely here in person, after all? And yet, something silly always knots in her stomach.

“Gamora.”

She immediately turns around, her gaze falling on Dey. The corners of his lips lift up a little, and he gives her a nod. “Flight was okay?”

He’s the only other person, except for Nova Prime, who ever looks her directly in the eyes and seems to be happy to see her.

She feels a tweak of warmth in her stomach. The feeling has always been mutual. Something about him has always put her at ease.

She steps to the side, closer to him, and nods. “It was. What do you know about the mission?”

He starts walking and takes the first hallway to the left. She hides a sigh of relief, realizing they’re going to the Nova Prime’s office. She’s already on the edge. Being in one of the open- space strategy rooms would have been suffocating.

She can never escape anyone’s eyes there.

Dey gives her a brief glance. “One of our scientists needs to collect soil and plant samples, and she needs protection.”

Gamora frowns a little. “Why is it a two person job?”

She accompanied scientists in the past. Even under Thanos, she learned that some plants are carnivorous and large, and it’s impossible to be surrounded by them without protection. But she’s good. Really good. No one’s ever been hurt on her watch—at least, not since she started working for Nova Prime.

Why do they need Peter Quill?

Dey wrinkles his nose a little. “They need samples from Gilea.”

“Ah.” She nods.

It’s an inactively active war zone. There’s no officially proclaimed wars, but the resources are so valuable that many opposing forces try to stake claim. Gold. Unique DNA species used for cloning purposes. Metals used for deadly weapons.

A paradise for thieves and criminals.

“Have there been recent attacks?”

No one likes to run into somebody else. No one’s happy to let each other go in different directions.

Dey shakes his head. “Not recently. All the activity has slowed down significantly, but we want to be careful.”

He presses his palm to the reader, and she leans against the wall. “Is the mission going to take long?”

He looks unsure. “Couple weeks maybe? It’s up to Calea.” Gamora gives him a confused glance, and he clarifies. “She’s one of the two scientists and the lead of the project.”

The elevator doors open up, and they both step inside. She spends the entire ride biting the inside of her cheek and tapping her foot on the floor. When they get to the right floor, she knows she’s running out of time.

Slowly exhaling, she asks, “Is Quill aware of this being a team mission?”

Is he aware that he’s going to be working with me?

She hopes she sounds nonchalant. She’s paranoid Dey will get suspicious. She’s never asked about anyone else before, after all. She never wanted to, not until now.

They step out of the elevator, and Dey doesn’t seem to read too much in her curiosity. “Yeah. I’m pretty sure he—“

“Dey.”

She recognizes the voice even without turning around. Her heart jumps to her throat.

Dey looks over his shoulder. “Quill!” He sounds delighted.

People always have this reaction to Quill.

She holds her breath as she turns around.

The sight of him, so close to her, leaves her slightly light-headed.

She has seen him—but never like this. Never face to face.

She remembers faltering the first time she saw him. She feels like faltering again, so she locks her knees. She doesn’t know what else to do, completely taken aback, and simply stares at him. Soaks him up, noticing all the details she’s never noticed before.

Seeing him less than two feet away is jarring.

That time, the first time she saw him in person, right as she was leaving the Nova Corps, he was surrounded by a group of people who all looked at him like he was the sun. They were smiling. Laughing. They eyes followed his every move, and his own laughter was deep, rich.

His scars made him irresistible. Her scars made her repulsive. When people barely looked at her, they couldn’t look away from him.

She was mesmerized that day. Jealous, too. Those feelings joined the feeling of respect and awe she’d already had for him, and haven’t left her since.

She doesn’t know what to do with those feelings now. They bubble in her chest, and her head spins.

The interest in his eyes—eyes trained on her—makes her want to run away. He takes a step closer, and hit with his scent, she stops breathing.

His lips stretch in a wide smile. “Hey! I’m Peter Quill.”

She blinks at him.

A smile. He’s giving her a smile.

No one ever smiles at her. In the past, there was fear. After she betrayed Thanos, there were suspicion and hatred, together with a huge dose of distrust and contempt. Now, she’s lucky if she sees wariness.

But a smile?

Never.

The realization that she’s not used seeing something so carefree, so normal directed at her hits hard. Beneath everything else, grief spreads. It’s so overwhelming that she wants to turn around, hop on the ship and never face another person ever again. Not if such a simple interaction leaves her cracked open and hurting.

Pain sits in her chest, and she’s unable to breath. It takes her a few tries before she can swallow the sad bitterness. Her voice is almost too hoarse. “I’m Gamora.”

Sometimes, regular people don’t recognize her face, but as soon as they learn her name, their faces lengthen. Quill’s smile doesn’t disappear, however. It only grows bigger, happier. “I know.” He offers his hand. “It’s nice to finally meet you. I’ve heard many great things.”

The question almost leaves her lips, but she stops herself the last second. It plays in her mind, though, over and over. How many bad things have you heard? She doesn’t think she ever wants to know the answer to this question—not just from him, from anyone. It’s one thing to try and forget all the bad things she’d done and fail. It’s another thing to be reminded that everyone else remembers her sins too.

She wraps her fingers around his hand, swallowing past a lump in her throat. “Likewise.”

His skin is warm. His touch is strong. His eyes, the same eyes that don’t leave hers for a second, leave a mark on her too. His gaze is deeply piercing. Almost like a physical touch in itself.

Goosebumps rise on her skin, and she barely hides her mortification. She feels the goosebumps, but can he see them? An embarrassment rises up, painful in her chest, and for a second, the world goes dark. There’s too many emotions, too many feelings and—

This is why she doesn’t do team work. This is why she doesn’t interact with other people. She doesn’t know how to.

She was taught to kill. She wasn’t taught to socialize.

But him? He was natural the first time she saw him from afar. He was natural the second time, too.

He’s natural now.

Everyone is.

The truth burns.

Her flesh does too, right where he’s still touching her, so she takes a step back and pulls her arm away from him. She doesn’t feel his fingers around hers anymore—but she still does. She feels his eyes on her face too. They’re reading. Learning. Figuring out.

There’s nothing good that he can learn.

Her mouth tastes sour. She turns away.

She wants to run away, but she refuses to let Thanos win.

“Come on,” she says, without looking at Quill or Dey. “Nova Prime is waiting for us.”

Protect the two scientists.

Come back alive.

Leave later today, as soon as the ship is stocked up with food and other resources. Shouldn’t take too long—it’s going to be just the four of them. She can’t decide if she should be petrified or happy about the fact.

Maybe, just maybe, she will be too focused on the mission to drown in her feelings. But she can’t remember being with three other people in an enclosed space at once.

Her skin itches in the anticipation of fear. The feeling intensifies every time she briefly glances in Quill’s direction. He’s sprawled out in the chair, looking almost lazy and bored.

He’s the epitome of calmness. She’s the opposite of it.

When the meeting is wrapped up and everyone slowly leaves the room, he turns to her. “I’m going to get food for the ship because I don’t trust them to give us all the fun stuff. Do you want me to get anything for you?”

She blinks at him. “What?”

He looks a little bit confused by her confusion. “I personally don’t want to eat ration bars and cured meats for the next few weeks. So I thought I’d go to the market and grab the good stuff?”

This doesn’t help. She frowns more. “The good stuff?”

“Yeah, like candy.” He face practically lights up. He sits straighter, and she can almost feel his excitement. “Or fruit. Or, you know, chocolate. I also want to stock up on drinks. And foom pies.” He looks at her expectantly. She doesn’t say anything. He prompts her again. “So… what would you like?”

She doesn’t have anything to say.

She’s never been asked a question like that. Her preferences haven’t been important to anyone. Including her.

She doesn’t know half of the things she listed. She doesn’t know what she likes.

Once, freedom used to seem like an opportunity to do things she hadn’t done before. Now, it only highlights all the ways she’s failing to feel—and be—normal.

Her spine is stiff as she shifts on her seat. “I don’t want anything. I’m fine with ration bars.”

“But—“

“Hey!”

They both turn around at the sound of a new voice. Nova Prime’s assistant, Elora, stands in the doorway with a sheepish smile. “I hope I’m not interrupting?” Her eyes are only on Quill.

Gamora steals a glance in his direction. His eyes shift to Elora. “Hey. What’s up?”

She walks into the room and steps around the chair he’s sitting on. Leaning against the edge of the desk, she smiles at Quill excitedly. “Remember when I asked you about those tapes?”

His eyes shift to Gamora and then return to Elora. “Can we talk about this later?”

“Oh.” Elora looks at Gamora for the first time. She doesn’t look hostile, but she doesn’t appear friendly either. Not like she did with Quill. “Are you guys talking about the mission?”

Gamora stands up. “No. We’re done.”

Quill’s eyes widen a little. “What? No.”

He places his hands on the chair armrest, as if to get up, but she speaks before he can move. “I’ll see you on the ship, Quill.”

She then leaves the room.

She knows when she’s not welcome. She’s never been welcome in her life.

Out, out, out.

She needs to breathe.

She thinks she can do this until she realizes how bad the sleeping situation is. There’s no separate rooms. There’s only captain’s quarter and bunks.

Bunks. There are protective shields but… bunks. They mean one thing: sleeping in the same room.

Hearing other people move. Breathe.

The white spots suddenly speckle her vision. The room spins, and her knees get weak. Blindly moving her hands around her, she tries to find anything for support, and when her fingers graze the metal wall, she sags against it, sliding down.

She can’t do this.

She’s good at fighting. She’s terrible at everything else.

People. Other people. Many people. She hadn’t been surrounded by people since before he destroyed her home world.

On the Sanctuary, her world was made of Thanos, Maw, and Nebula. It was just them—and the victims she tricked, used and killed.

Not this. Not normal people.

Her skin prickles. Tingles. The numbness spreads everywhere except her chest—there, there’s a whirlwind of pain and hatred. Hatred for Thanos. For herself. Anger. Misery.

She tries to breathe. But her lungs collapse in her chest, being pressed on by all those feelings.

Too much. Everything.

Nauseous.

She feels nauseous.

She gets soaked with sweat. Her jacket clings to her skin, and she blindly drags the zipper down, trying to get rid of it.

Hot. Too hot. The air is stuffy.

She recognizes the signs for what they are. Realizing she’s spiralling, she presses her palms against the cold metal behind her back. It’s not cold enough, but it’s the best she’s got.

Calm down. Breathe in. Breathe out. Slowly. Deliberately.

You’re safe. You’re safe. You’re safe.

She just doesn’t feel like she is.

She doesn’t think she can do this.

She can’t sleep in the same room. She has many walls around her heart—but she needs the real, physical walls around her body too. There needs to be a barrier. She doesn’t know how to live without one.

Breathe. In and out. In and out.

Her heartbeat slowly comes back to normal. Her vision isn’t as sharp as it needs to be, but it’s also not completely blurry. The tingling fades away. And when she regains her consciousness, finding herself on the floor, she once again thinks about Quill.

How normal he is.

And how—

How close. She hears him clear his throat. She hears his footsteps. Shivers run down her neck because… because those footsteps are almost right here.

Her spine tenses. She scrambles up to her feet just in time before he rounds the corner and almost collides with her.

His eyes aren’t as bright as they were just a few hours ago. There’s something dark, something heavy, but he tries to hide it by wearing a cheerful expression. But it seems too cheerful—almost like it’s not cheerful at all. “Hey! I was actually looking for you.”

She clears her throat before speaking. “Why?”

“Oh,” he shrugs, “I just wanted to offer you to take the captain’s quarters.”

She’s not used to casual offers and free things. It causes nothing but suspicion in her. “Why? You’re the captain of the mission. It’s your room.”

“I mean, yeah. But if it’s mine, it means I get to do with it as I please, right? So. I’m offering it to you.”

She still doesn’t understand. “Why?”

He folds his arms on his chest and casually leans against the wall, ankles crossed. “Why not?”

She doesn’t know what to say to that. Mainly because she feels too raw and emotional and she only wants to put some distance between herself and everyone else. Off-this-ship kind of distance.

She shakes her head. “Thank you for the offer, but I don’t think I’m a good fit for this mission.”

His face falls. She’s used to seeing this expression when she enters the room, not when she tries to leave. That startles her, makes her swallow thickly. And his expression, this mix of almost… unhappiness? of… disappointment?… it makes her confused.

“You want to leave.” It’s not a question. She still nods.

He bites the inside of his cheek, almost like he’s debating something. “Gamora…”

She steps around him without another word.

“I begged to work with you.” His voice is urgent. Practically desperate.

And it's that, together with the actual words, that struck her to her core. A shiver of something dances up her spine. She slowly turns around. “What?”

He pinches the bridge of his nose, looking embarrassed and defeated. He mutters under his nose, “This is not how… Dammit.”

Rubbing his face, he then drops his hand to his side. The look he gives her, the depth of his green eyes—it picks her apart. Completely. There’s no amusement, there’s no smiles, there’s no sun energy, and yet, she’s captivated. Fully. There’s no looking away. There’s barely even breathing.

It’s just his eyes.

“I’ve always wanted to work with you,” he says slowly, his voice deep and quiet. It grips her heart tightly. “But Nova Prime always said no. Said you weren’t a team member and she’d never force you. Which… I respected. But I still wanted a chance. And then… then it came.”

Her heart beats fast. I’ve always wanted to work with you. “Why?”

“Why what?”

“Why did you want to work with me?”

His brows dip. “Why wouldn’t I?” He sounds genuinely surprised. As if that is an obvious, normal thing—to want to work with her.

It’s not. It’s never been. The sad truth momentarily makes her defenceless against her own walls, against the need to be strong. So she says quietly, “You’re the only one.”

The look in his eyes hardens. “Then everyone else is a fool.”

She blinks. And her lips part. Nothing comes out.

He pushes away from the wall. Takes a step closer to her. Still doesn’t look away. She can’t either. “What you’ve done is admirable. I don’t think I’ve ever heard of a braver person. So I wanted to meet you.”

Something around her heart cracks. But she doesn’t let the gap grow wider. She doesn’t let the ice melt away. She reminds herself of the reasons it’s there. “Killing innocent people is admirable?”

For a moment, he wears an angry expression, “Don’t.” But then he takes a breath, erasing the signs of irritation. His eyes fill with compassion. Compassion. The air leaves her lungs. “Don’t,” he repeats, this time softer. “We all have a past, and yours is… you shouldn’t have had to survive the way you did.” He shakes his head. “It wasn’t fair, Gamora, and it wasn’t right. But what you did to betray Thanos and stop him? That was admirable.”

She swallows.

Everything inside her is simply… quiet. Not peacefully quiet. It’s numb. Because she doesn’t know what to say. Or what to do. Or how to react. There’s only her galloping heart. And the urge to—

To cry.

As in on command, her eyes burn. She instantly drops her gaze to the ground.

“Selfishly, I want you to stay. A lot.” Quill’s voice makes her hurt. It makes her breathless. “That’s why I want to give you the captain’s quarters. I know you don’t do teams, and I thought having privacy would make this a little bit easier for you.”

She still stays silent. She breathes quickly, in and out, trying to force the tears away.

“But I understand if you want to leave. I don’t want to make your life harder.”

Her heart clenches.

Quill takes a step away from her. “I’ll give you a moment to decide, okay? We’ll take off in about ten minutes.”

She doesn’t acknowledge his words, but she hears them loud and clear.

He steps around her. Then stops. “If you choose to go, I’m still glad we met.”

And then he leaves her. She stays rooted in the same place, lost, confused and hopeful. Probably more than ever. She stays there for minutes—until she feels the ship come alive.

Until they’re off the ground. Until they’re flying away from Xandar.

Until she allows herself to admit the truth she always pushed away.

There is curiosity. There is longing. There is questions. It’s always, How did he move on? It’s always, Can I? But it’s more than just those things, too. It all comes down to one thing.

Understanding.

No one ever understands—but can he? A former thief. An ex Ravager. Someone with a grey past. Not as dark as hers—but not pristine white either.

When you’ve seen the extent of pain and cruelty, can you understand someone else drowning in it?

She hopes, hopes, hopes. That leaves her raw. Completely bare.

It becomes so obvious to her then: hope has edges. She just doesn’t know if they’re sharp or soft.

She just knows they leave her split open.

She hides in the privacy of the bedroom until she manages to swallow her emotions down and compose herself enough. After, she squares her shoulders and leaves for the cockpit.

They have to discuss the mission in more detail at some point. Responsibilities. Plan of defence and attack, if it ever comes down to that. She can’t hide forever.

But when she nears the stairs leading up to the cockpit, she only hears laughter. Quill’s. But everyone else’s too. It takes her a second to realize that he’s telling a story. And the scientists, both of them, are amused. They’re practically howling. Reacting. Commenting and asking questions.

Engaging.

Something she can’t do. She has nothing to say.

It’s impossible to even remember the last time she had a conversation like that.

And there’s never been any laughter. Only orders. And threats.

After the betrayal, there was suspicion. Concerns if she was actually good, worthy of trust.

But not conversations.

She has been on her own for far too long.

She’s struggling in her darkness. Quill thrives in his light.

She’s craving to know how. She’s terrified to see another proof of her incompetence.

Her stomach feels heavy. Her heart is heavy too. She turns around and goes back to the captain’s quarters.

***

When she climbs up the stairs to the cockpit hours later, the scientists are gone. It’s just Quill. Something in her relaxes at that. She’d rather deal with one person than three.

“Hey,” she calls softly.

He instantly turns around, and his face lights up. He quickly composes himself, wiping off the expression of delighted surprise, and she appreciates it. She doesn’t want him to make a big deal out of her staying.

She still hasn’t processed why she chose to do that.

“Hey,” he says back. “How’s the room?”

She also appreciates him not verbally acknowledging it.

“It’s great, thank you.” Then after a moment she asks, “Are you sure you don’t want it back?”

He makes a face. “I used to be a Ravager. Trust me, it’s not a big deal at all.”

“Okay.”

Silence settles over them, and it’s the uncomfortable kind. That makes her frustrated. She came up here for a reason. And yet, even with having something to discuss, she’s struggling to find the right words—or any words, for that matter. How pathetic is that?

Quill points to a seat. “Wanna join me?”

Yes.

She flinches when she hears the voice inside her head. Damn it. No.

It’s that yes that’s the culprit of her current misery. If only she hadn’t been curious, hopeful, and desperate. But she has joined him—and so far, it’s not working out. She doesn’t need any more signs of how inadequate she is by choosing to stay.

She’s here for business.

So she shakes her head, “I came up here to discuss our strategy.”

“Oh?”

She folds her arms on her chest and shifts her weight to her left side. “When we land, I don’t think it’s wise for all of us to sleep at night. We could be attacked any moment, so I think someone always needs to keep watch.”

Quill nods in agreement. “I was thinking the same.”

It shouldn’t matter that he’s thinking the same things. It shouldn’t. And yet…

She clears her throat. “I can keep watch at night. This way, you get to sleep and accompany the scientists during the day.”

Quill takes a moment to react. He leans forward a little. “You want to do it this way?” Something about his tone makes her uneasy. It’s too deep. Too considerate. Too something. It’s not just a simple question. It’s loaded—as if he understands something that he shouldn’t.

She doesn’t want him to understand. There’s no need for him to know this plan is meant to keep her safe and sane. She doesn’t want to interact with anyone. She simply wants to do her job.

Her protecting everyone at night gives her exactly that.

Quill’s eyes are dark in the dim light, but they’re still full of things she doesn’t want to see. She clears her throat, trying to sound nonchalant. “Yes, I do.”

She wants to make something clear too. “I will break your arrow and leave you defenceless in a second. Hell, I’ll even cut out your prothesis.” She levels him with her gaze. “After I do that, I’ll let the members of your old clan know where to find you, and I’ll personally guard you until they arrive. You don’t have a choice, Yondu. It’s either that—or coming with me.” Something burns her throat. Something like anger and disbelief and irritation. “You want to break Peter’s heart, you do that in person. Stop hiding like a coward.”

He holds her gaze for a few long moments. Then he scoffs, shaking his head. “Bossy, huh?” He cocks his head to the side. “But what do you expect from daughter of Thanos?”

He’s trying to rattle her—but she stares at him blankly. “So you’ve heard about me then. Congratulations.”

You know what I’m capable of is left unsaid. Yondu must have at least some common sense because he weighs his options.

She knows what he wants to do—she watches him watch the arrow.

A promise. A warning. Everything in between. She lowers her sword, the edge of it coming in contact with Yaka.

She can play the game too. She’s not here to come to Peter with nothing.

Eventually, Yondu lets out a low, unhappy growl. “Damn Terran. Shoulda eaten him when I had a chance.” It’s only because she hears a certain level of warmth in Yondu’s voice that she doesn’t do anything.

“Get ready,” she repeats.

Yondu gives her a sugary-sweet smile. “As you wish.”

He pushes the duvet aside and gets up to his feet. Naked.

She makes a startled noise, instantly turning around. “You bastard,” she growls lowly.

He laughs, cold and not amused. “I’m out of bed. Ain’t this what you wanted?”

She feels her face contort. Breathing in and out, she says nothing.

***

He tests her patience in many ways. She doesn’t react to any single one of them, but on the inside, she’s fuming. The only reason she doesn’t snap is by entertaining the idea of someone getting him before next Christmas.

She refuses to go through the same experience again.

It takes her a while to realize what her thoughts imply. When she does, she freezes, startled. She plays with the truth in her mind, trying to admit the intention without actually voicing it, but she can’t.

Because she is thinking of staying.

For that long. And maybe… maybe even longer than that.

***

Hey Peter. Can you please meet me at the same bar we went to after the mission?

His reply is almost instant. You want to go there?

She will never step inside if she can help it, but she doesn’t know any other bars. She still has a mission to accomplish—even if Yondu is the main present now.

Not wishing to lie, she ignores Peter’s question. Can you meet me there in about an hour? I’m on my way back to Xandar.

Where did you go?

He’s asked that a few times since she left. She’s avoided the question every single time. I will tell you later.

***

Dey, Yondu is coming with me to Xandar. Just giving you a head ups.

You really like to make my job more difficult.

If he does something terrible, arrest him. But please make sure no one arrests him for his past crimes.

I hope it’s worth it.

***

“You are not to run away,” she says. Orders, really. “If you do, I will find you.”

Yondu only rolls his eyes.

“I’m serious, Yondu.” She folds her arms on her chest. “You are to wait at this bar until Peter arrives. You have to keep him occupied for couple of hours. What you do after doesn’t concern me.”

He waves her off. “I’m more interested in what you're gonna be doing in the meantime.”

“That’s none of your business.”

He snorts. “I’ve seen your ship.” His lips twist in a sneer. A condescending one. “Sentimental nonsense, those decorations. You’re gonna decorate Quill’s ship, aren’t you?”

She would be self-conscious if she wasn’t annoyed. Her first time giving a present—and Yondu is telling her she’s doing something foolish. She thinks if that was anyone else, she would probably let the fear inside her win. She would merge the voice of the person with the voice inside her own head. She would believe she’s making a mistake.

Luckily for her, it’s Yondu. His opinion matters less than anyone else’s ever has.

His regard for Peter’s feelings is approximately non-existent. He doesn’t get an opinion.

She doesn’t dignify his remark with a response. She only points to the bar. “Go inside. Now.”

“You’re forgetting something, sweetheart.”

She ignores the nickname—she already learned he enjoys it when she shows her frustration. “What’s that?”

“I ain’t paying my own money for that. Transfer me some units.”

Peter spent weeks searching for him—and he can’t even pay ten units for a cheep beer. What an astonishing parental figure. She takes a deep breath. “I’ll never understand why Peter loves you so much.”

Yondu shrugs. “I’ll never understand what he found in you. You ain’t hearing me complain ‘bout it.”

“You’ve literally been complaining for the past two hours.”

He shrugs again, almost in a What can you do way.

She pulls her holo out. “What’s your code?”

***

When she’s finally back on the Milano, she feels like crying. Inhaling the air, seeing the inside of the ship, she’s overwhelmed by one single word in her head.

Home.

She hasn’t had one in so long, she doesn’t even understand the feeling—or the concept—but she’s hit with the sudden revelation: this is home. She doesn’t want to be anywhere else.

For a moment, she regrets sending Peter away. She wants nothing more than to be here with him, Yondu be damned, but unfortunately, she’s the only one who can’t stand him.

Peter needs him.

She wishes Yondu needed him back. She wishes Yondu showed his love. Peter’s love is obvious, almost palpable. He deserves the same kind of affection back.

She slowly walks around the ship, soaking everything in. It’s almost surprising—but also so obvious—that there’s nothing to get accustomed to. Two days apart haven’t changed anything. She already knows every corner.

She already loves every corner.

Home. Home. Home.

Her heart flutters in her chest.

***

Holding the lights with one hand, she tries to tear tape with her teeth, wrinkling her nose at the bitter taste of the glue on her tongue.

The pictures made it look easy and effortless.

She’s struggling.

She’s not even supposed to be feeling tension in her shoulders—but she is. Either the Milano is much bigger than she ever realized, or she stole too many Christmas lights, but there is a lot to hang up.

The strings of lights like to complicate everything by getting tangled up.

When she untangles one part of the string, lifting it up in the air, another part instantly forms a knot.

She doesn’t understand how that happens.

Sighing, she hops off the chair, stretching her neck a little.

Just two more walls to go.

***

As soon as she puts a star on top of the Christmas tree, she hears a voice right outside the Milano. Peter’s voice. Her heart skips a bit in anticipation, and she rocks on her heels, clasping her hands behind her back.

Licking her lips, she takes a deep, calming breath.

A second voice joins Peter’s. Yondu’s.

Everything inside her churns. She didn’t expect audience.

Her eyes dart around in panic. Everything is decorated. Everything is bright, everything is festive, and suddenly she cares very much about what Yondu has—had—to say. The sneer he gave her burns her eyelids, and she closes her eyes, nauseous.

What was she thinking?

Stupid, stupid, stupid.

When the door starts opening, she makes a soft noise in the back of her throat. Retreat. Now. But apprehension slows her down, and before she can hide somewhere down the hall, Peter’s eyes find hers.

His wide grin cracks his face in two—or at least starts cracking. His mouth opens, possibly to greet her, when he notices the lights—and the tree, and the tall figurines of gods know who— and then his eyes widen, lips forming an O rather than a smile.

The look in his eyes has her unsure how he feels about this.

She bites her tongue to stop an embarrassing sound from coming out.

“‘Course you did that,” Yondu mutters behind Peter. “You better have some beer here, Quill.”

Peter doesn’t say anything. Gamora doesn’t either. A few seconds later Yondu sighs and moves past both of them, possibly in the direction of the kitchen.

Gamora isn’t sure. She cannot look away from Peter.

“I, uh… What is this?”

She’s asking herself the same. What. And why? This was such a bad idea.

She wants the ground to open up under her feet.

“I…” She trails off.

Peter’s eyes dart between her and the tree. She follows his gaze and tries to see everything through his eyes.

The tree is crooked and patchy. There’s couple spot where she didn’t put enough decorations, and now the green branches look naked. The lights blink too aggressively. She should have chosen another setting.

No blinking would have been nice.

No lights would have been even better.

Her throat closes up.

Peter slowly walks over to the tree, his hand hovering over the decorations, and she can’t see his face anymore. She hears his racing heart though. Good racing? Bad racing?

She doesn’t know.

He turns to her slowly—and a confused, delighted, bright smile takes his entire face. A smile. It finds home in her heart, and something in her relaxes.

“Gamora,” he practically whispers, “what is this?”

A smile reminds her how to talk. “I wanted to give you something.” Her voice is chocked, but she is already obvious and transparent. She might as well say the truth—even if it’s not the truth. “I… I was worried we wouldn’t find Yondu in time, so I thought I’d give you at least something. I didn’t want you to not celebrate.”

His lips part a little. Something soft settles in his eyes. Soft, tender, and so familiar she feels a wave of heartache sweep over her, knocking all wind out of her.

He slowly walks over.

When he stops in front of her, he’s close. So close. Too close—he’s practically towering over her, and she doesn’t feel trapped. She only feels at home.

“Gamora…” Her name is nothing but a breathless whisper.

She feels the quiet voice awake something in the pit of her stomach. Fire and desire—and the room, and everything in it, suddenly disappears.

His green eyes hold hers. The earnestness in his gaze leaves her breathless.

“At least something,” he murmurs, repeating her words, his tone is hushed and almost amused. “You already gave me everything when you agreed to stay.”

Her heart clenches so violently that she feels like she’s been stabbed. She wants to come up with something to say but her mind is blank. She fights the weird, hot haze. “You said that not celebrating Christmas would be like forgetting about your mom, and I didn’t want that.”

His hands cup her face, and she leans into his palm.

“I wouldn’t have not celebrated,” he says quietly. “I would have been with you. Christmas is about being with someone you love. I was. I am.”

I am.

She swallows thickly. And then again and again, fighting the urge to cry.

He rests his forehead against hers, his breath warm on her cheekbone. The proximity and the heat of his skin spins her head, and she takes a deep breath, unable to stop herself.

“It means the absolute world to me what you did. With decorations. With Yondu. This is the best thing anyone has ever done for me, Gamora, and I…” He trails off, his voice thick with gratitude. Her hands itch with the need to touch him, and she lifts up her arms, slowly wrapping her fingers around his forearms.

He leaves a soft kiss on her cheek.

She’s not even embarrassed about a soft exhale that falls from her lips.

She’s too busy trying to breathe. And replaying the kiss. And the words.

Everything.

“I am so grateful, but— No, wait, there’s no but— I just… I don’t want you to think you had to do it. Having you here was—and is—enough. It’s everything.”

She sucks in a shaky, uneven breath. Leans away just a little, wanting to look at him, and the words come pouring out of her. “No one has ever made me feel safe, Peter. No one but you. And— not just safe. It’s— well, everything else too. But I’m not good with words, so I thought I’d show you.”

Have I shown you? The question burns her tongue.

The answer blazes in his eyes. You have.

Relief—and happiness, and giddiness, and apprehension—spill in her chest. She has shown —and she’s alright. The world hasn’t ended. If anything, it’s brighter now—with the way Peter’s eyes shine with everything she’s craving and feeling back.

She is safe. The realization is freeing. So freeing.

“Thank you,” he whispers, his lips close to hers.

She wants them closer. A shiver of anticipation races down her neck.

Closing her eyes, she whispers into his skin, “Can I have that kiss you promised me?”

She doesn’t see his smile, but she feels the corners of his lips lift up. “I believe I promised you a million, actually.”

She doesn’t know where she finds the boldness—but she does. “Go on, then.”

She isn’t sure who moves first. She doesn’t know if there was even any space to move— maybe they just needed to shift a little. But his lips touch hers, and she is home.

Suddenly, Peter’s everywhere, and she doesn’t want anything else.

The kiss is searing, leaving her breathless, and she thinks she makes a soft noise. Or maybe Peter does.

She doesn’t know. She doesn’t care.

She only wants more.

Raising on her tiptoes, she slides her hands higher, until her fingers curl around his hair. Peter makes a soft moan, one she feels deep in her belly, and his hand drops her her waist.

His palm presses against her back. She goes in willingly, pressing her chest, her stomach, against him.

The softness becomes teasing. The teasing is playful—and then tender. It’s lingering. Long- lasting.

When his tongue touches hers, she whimpers.

His fingers curl around her jaw.

Hers instinctively curl around his waves.

When he breaks away, her knees almost buckle at the sight of him. The intoxicating desire makes her weak. The dark, intense hunger fuels her own. But it’s that other thing, that soft, tender, beautiful affection, that has her gasping.

She blurts out, “Earlier, when I was flying Yondu to Xandar, I kept thinking how terrible he is and how much I don’t want to do this next year.” She bites her lip. “Next year, Peter. I was thinking about it. I still am.” She gives him a small smile. “I want this.”

His lips press another kiss to her mouth.

She responds just as eagerly. Her hands slide down, brushing his shoulders—

“Well, ain’t a fan of you either, sweetheart.”

She freezes. A second later, she groans.

Peter already spent some time with him. Would it be terrible of her to message Dey and ask him to arrest Yondu, after all? The idea is tempting.

Peter pulls away from her, his eyes narrowed. “Get the hell out of here.”

Yondu doesn’t listen, of course. If anything, he laughs darkly, not bothered at all, and walks over. “Ain’t leaving, Quill. Your lady dragged me here against my will, so I’m going to stay for a bit.”

Peter’s face falls. “Please don’t.”

She looks at Yondu over her shoulder. “You’re free to leave.”

“And miss a chance to annoy you?” He cocks his eyebrow. “Not doing that. Maybe that will teach you something.”

She grunts. He just smirks.

“I can get you arrested,” she tells him.

He only snorts. “You wouldn’t risk upsetting Quill.”

“I will personally call Dey,” Peter says.

Yondu rolls his eyes. “Get over it, Quill. I’m staying for dinner.” He slaps Peter on the shoulder. “Now, let’s go. I’m starving. The faster we eat, the faster I leave you two alone.”

Peter lets out a long sigh, giving Gamora a sheepish, apologetic smile. “I see why you weren’t a fan of him.”

“Insulting me won’t make me leave.”

Gamora swallows back a groan. “You’re insufferable.”

“Right back at ya,” Yondu winks at her.

She presses her lips together.

“Let’s go, sweetheart. You’re here to stay, we gotta chat then.”

She counts to ten. It doesn’t help. She follows them both into the kitchen, quietly fuming.

But as soon as Peter’s hand finds hers, her irritation melts.

His smile is worth it. It’s worth everything.

He leaves a quick kiss on her cheek, whispering, “Thank you. And I’m sorry,” and she doesn’t mind anything at all.

She’s too busy feeling happy and safe.
