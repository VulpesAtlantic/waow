Summary

The monarchy of The United Realms is teetering on the edge of collapse. As alliances fracture and public support plummets, Agatha Harkness — the First Princess and the last of her dynasty — is blindsided by a devastating ultimatum: she cannot claim her crown unless she takes a bride.

Worse yet, her Regent has already selected the perfect match. Enter Rio Vidal, an outcast princess who openly scorns the nobility. Living like a rockstar and fronting an alt-indie band that rubs shoulders with anarchists, Rio despises the royals — and she hates the Harkness family most of all. But even the fiercest principles can be bought when the enemy of your enemy extends a hand.

Bound by mutual hatred, neither woman believes this forced arrangement stands a chance of surviving. Yet, the fate of an entire nation — and their very lives — now rests entirely on this alliance.

Welcome to a ruthless game of power. Tread carefully, because in The United Realms, betrayal is simply a matter of convenience, and checkmate always comes from the player you least suspect. The fall of one sovereign is merely the perfect opening for another to rise.

Chapter 1: Paper Crown

The Real Tribune: The Premier Newspaper of The United Realms

THE CROWN FIASCO

On Tuesday afternoon, the First Princess led an open-agenda session at the Harkensgate Legislative Chamber to address the mounting public demands of Salemgard. The hearing, however, quickly unraveled. On the very eve of her coronation, Princess Agatha Harkness stumbled over basic public policy questions. Her visible apathy and indifference served as a glaring reminder of just how disconnected she is from her own people. Ultimately, her silence sparked a fierce uproar from the opposition on the assembly floor and ignited protests in the streets.

Order was only restored when the Princess abruptly withdrew, citing a sudden indisposition. The session then resumed under the steady guidance of Lilia Calderu, Regent Guardian of The United Realms.

A pressing question remains: do we really trust the First Princess possesses the maturity to ascend the throne of the Twenty Realms? Is this the Queen the United Realms really wants?

***

Kate slid her rook across the marble chessboard with a smug smile. Agatha merely shook her head, pressing her lips together to suppress a laugh. Her sister’s eyebrows arched in a silent challenge, prompting Agatha to nod, feigning impression.

“Sometimes, Agatha, defeat is only a matter of time,” Kate said, beaming with pride.

Kate’s king sat tucked away in the corner, heavily shielded by a solid wall of pawns and a rook. It was a defensive strategy that perfectly mirrored her vigilant nature. Agatha might have even commended her for it, if not for one fatal flaw: Kate always underestimated the details. Vaulting over a row of black pawns, Agatha moved her knight in a precise L-shape, planting it on the F3 square with a sharp clack.

Kate’s smug smile vanished the moment she reassessed the board. Agatha beamed triumphantly. Her knight was aimed dead at the king, striking from an oblique, unblockable angle. Trapped by her own defensive pieces, Kate’s king was completely cornered on G1.

“Checkmate,” Agatha murmured with a soft chuckle, watching her sister sink into the sofa and cross her arms in a childish frustration.

“I don’t know why you still try. She always wins,” Wanda noted from a few feet away. The Princess of Rubria lounged in a white leather armchair, balancing her phone and a glass of champagne with her signature elegance.

Agatha smiled proudly. It was true — she never lost. And today was poised to be yet another day of glory. It was the eve of her birthday and the arrival of her royal majority, making it the most anticipated day of her life. The crown would finally rest upon her head, realizing her lifelong dream and granting her the autonomy she so desperately craved.

The Harknesses had ruled for centuries. After successive generations of absolute power, the crown was inextricably linked to their surname. Throughout history, not a single one of the Twenty Realms had dared to rise against them; the Harkness sovereigns were unquestionable. Known for their iron-fisted rule and unrivaled military strength, they hadn’t earned their power through the love of the people, but by imposing their undeniable superiority.

They were a dynasty not loved, but feared and admired with a wary, breathless reverence. At least, they were, until the tragic accident that wiped out the entire lineage. Everyone perished except Agatha, who became the living miracle of the crown — the last of her bloodline.

Her life was defined by the inevitability of heavy burdens. Her mother had died in childbirth, but Agatha’s destiny was sealed before she even took her first breath simply by being King Nicholas Harkeness’s firstborn: she was the future Queen of the United Realms.

At the time of her birth, there was little cause for concern. Her arrival was met with joyous celebrations, and the weight of her future title felt comfortably distant. King Nicholas was young, the eldest of three brothers, and the line of succession was robust. Nothing stopped him from remarrying and having a large family, yet he chose never to do so. Defying all expectations, the king never took another woman after the death of Evanora, the love of his life. Agatha remained his sole heir. Even Kate’s adoption didn’t alter the royal landscape, as the laws of the United Realms strictly required blood consanguinity for succession. Still, the family was large enough that the legacy seemed secure.

No one could have ever predicted that a single catastrophic event would reap an entire dynasty.

At eleven years old, Agatha — who had been born with the shadow of the crown already hovering over her — suddenly bore the crushing weight of being the very last of her name. She was the sole remaining legacy of a centuries-old dynasty.

For fourteen years, Lilia had ruled as Regent Guardian. Given the macabre fate of the royal family, the intensely overprotective upbringing she forced upon Agatha was entirely justified. Yet, it was also unbearably stifling. The future Queen was afforded zero autonomy, trapped inside a glass bubble and bound by meticulously calculated political maneuvers. To Agatha, the crown meant freedom. Her coronation would mark the dawn of an era of well-deserved recognition. Finally, she would be heard and respected. She would never have to take orders again; everyone would answer to her, whether they liked it or not.

Naturally, she was ecstatic for the big day to arrive, but it was quickly turning into a disappointment. Lilia had callously vetoed her grand birthday party, and to make matters worse, Agatha had wasted her entire morning trapped in an insufferable public hearing.

“Why are they taking so long?” Agatha sighed, her bored gaze drifting between her sister’s wary expression and her best friend’s relaxed indifference.

“You know Jennifer,” Wanda said, rolling her eyes. “She’s probably talking Lilia’s ear off about every agonizing detail. Out of all the days on the calendar, did they really have to schedule a public hearing right on the eve of your coronation? We have a party to plan, for god’s sake!”

“The hearing was scheduled specifically because of the coronation,” Kate pointed out. “Lilia is worri—”

“Exactly!” Agatha interrupted, siding seamlessly with Wanda. “It wasn’t enough for them to veto my birthday party; they had to ruin my princess day, too.”

“You are getting a party,” Wanda stated flatly. “It’s a coronation. I don’t care if we’re drowning in balloons or just sharing a cake, it will be a party — and the biggest in the history of the Twenty Realms. I guarantee it.”

“If Jennifer organizes my coronation—”

“Over my dead body,” Wanda interjected immediately. “She might have her twisted reasons for canceling your birthday, but your coronation? That job is mine, and I won’t let anyone take it from you.”

“We should get her drunk.”

“Oh, we will,” Wanda promised with a conspiratorial gleam, earning a wide grin from Agatha.

Wanda wasn’t just her best friend; the bond between them was as ancient as the United Realms itself.

Centuries ago, a great war had forged the continent into a single empire under the Harknesses of Salemgard, who claimed victory alongside their fiercest allies: the Maximoffs of Rubria. For generations, the two dynasties had been intertwined by blood and marriage. King Nicholas had been the first to break that tradition when he fell in love with Princess Evanora of Azuria. Yet, the alliance endured. King Nicholas’s best friend and appointed Hand of the King was Magnus Maximoff, meaning Agatha and Wanda were raised exactly as their fathers had been — inseparable from the cradle.

As the youngest Maximoff, Wanda didn’t hold significant political power. She possessed a purely honorary title — Guardian of the Silver Crown — gifted to her in childhood by King Nicholas, her doting godfather, simply to secure her an untouchable place at Court.

Defying the icy, stoic reputation of her bloodline, the Princess of Rubria was pure charisma. Adored across the continent, she was celebrated as the queen of high society, famous for her extravagant appearances and lavish events. With her effortless charm and infectious humor, Wand had naturally become the glowing public face of the United Realms — a stark, vibrant contrast to her father’s imposing legacy.

Agatha loved her fiercely. They were practically sisters, and the First Princess couldn’t imagine navigating her royal life without Wanda by her side.

“Did Lilia say anything about my coronation?” Agatha asked her sister.

Kate hesitated, opening and closing her mouth a few times as if struggling to find the right words.

Because of her position in the Royal Guard, Kate worked closely with Steve, who served as the eyes and ears of her godmother and Regent. If anyone knew what was happening behind closed doors, it was her. But Agatha didn’t like the hesitant, cautious look on her sister’s face.

“I know she said something. Spill it,” Agatha demanded, raising a brow. Kate shrank back defensively.

“She’s worried because the public, generally speaking… isn’t very fond of you,” Kate admitted gently. Her face twisted into an apologetic grimace that made Agatha’s eyebrows shoot up in surprise.

Of all the things to worry about. Agatha actually felt the urge to laugh.

“People weren’t very fond of dad, either,” she countered with a dismissive shrug, plucking a flute of champagne from the side table. “I’m a Harkness; I don’t need to be loved. People need to respect me, not adore me.”

“Girl, that concept is a bit old school, don’t you think?” Wanda chimed in, tilting her head.

Agatha opened her mouth to argue, but the heavy drawing-room doors suddenly swung open. Her godmother marched in, her brow deeply furrowed and her lips pressed into a tight, stressed line.

“That doesn’t look good at all,” Kate muttered, sinking deeper into the sofa cushions.

Lilia Calderu, her godmother and Regent Guardian, was easily the most respected woman in the Twenty Realms. She had originally arrived at court alongside Queen Evanora — her closest friend — and after the Queen’s passing, Lilia grew deeply trusted by the King, eventually raising the First Princess as her own daughter.

Over the years, her sharp political acumen, diplomatic grace, and balanced worldview earned her King Nicholas’s unconditional trust. He granted her the permanent title of Voice of the Realms — a position wielding immense influence over his decisions — and, in his final will, appointed her Regent Guardian. Until Agatha reached her royal majority, Lilia was the absolute pillar of the Crown, bearing the crushing responsibility of governing the United Realms.

Now, she was striding into the room with heavy, deliberate steps and a stormy expression Agatha knew all too well. Agatha suppressed an involuntary eye roll; nothing good was about to come of this. Flanking Lilia were her usual shadows, Steve and Jennifer, both wearing identical, grim expressions.

“Do you have any idea what happened today, Agatha?” Lilia snapped, her voice thundering across the room.

Agatha swallowed hard, but stubbornly tilted her chin up, feigning perfect innocence. “A hearing?” she offered, her tone dripping with mockery.

“You are unbelievable!” Lilia let out a sharp, humorless laugh, crossing her arms and shaking her head. Agatha merely arched an eyebrow, maintaining a blank, unbothered stare. “I have never been so disappointed in my entire life. We had to deal with protesters storming the Chamber! Hundreds of people calling for your downfall, Agatha! They would rather see anyone else on that throne but you.”

“There’s really no need to talk like that,” Wanda muttered, rolling her eyes. She casually inspected her manicure, pretending not to notice Lilia’s indignant huff or the fierce, hands-on-hips glare directed her way.

“No need to talk like that?” Jennifer echoed in disbelief. “You are really clueless, aren’t you?”

Agatha stood up, mirroring her godmother with hands on her hips, though her face remained an unreadable mask. She let out a long, exasperated sigh, making it abundantly clear how bored she was by this dramatic outburst.

“We deal with opposition all the time, don’t we?” the princess challenged, her gaze darting between Jennifer and Lilia. “Separatists, anarchists, fence-sitters, lunatics… Everyone wants to complain, but absolutely no one wants to actually govern.”

“Apparently, neither do you,” Jennifer fired back.

“I am your queen, Jennifer,” Agatha thundered, glaring daggers at the other woman — who looked completely unimpressed. “You had better watch your tone.”

“You are not a queen, Agatha. You are a princess,” Lilia interjected, her voice just as sharp. Agatha looked at her godmother up and down, her expression instantly darkening. “And if you keep acting this way, you won’t keep the crown for long once it’s finally on your head. What kind of queen doesn’t take her own people seriously? What kind of ruler do you actually want to be?”

Agatha broke eye contact and began to pace the room, letting out a ragged sigh of exhaustion. It didn’t matter what she did; it was never enough. Even when she intentionally did nothing — just to avoid making a mistake — she still couldn’t win.

“What did I do wrong this time?” she demanded, staring Lilia down. “I literally did everything you told me to do! I only left that hearing because Jennifer told me to. Otherwise, I would have stayed. I don’t care about the protests, I don’t have a problem with those people, and I don’t care whether they like me or not.”

“I pulled you out of there to protect you! To protect your crown!” Jennifer shot back indignantly. “That mob could have spun completely out of control in the blink of an eye, and the only reason it didn’t was because Lilia—”

“Then tell me what I did wrong!” Agatha shouted over her. “I did exactly what I was supposed to do.”

“Your problem, Agatha, is assuming the sheer weight of your dynasty will sustain your reign.” Lilia’s resolute tone sliced right through the princess’s bravado, leaving no room for argument. “We are in a different era, my child. Your father’s years are gone. For you to rule successfully, you need to be loved. You know that, but you just don’t seem to give a damn.”

“The crown is mine by right,” Agatha dismissed with an indifferent shrug. “What are they going to do? Dethrone me? Please.” She let out a soft, mocking scoff.

“Agatha, things are changing,” Steve interjected gently. His brow knit together in that perpetual expression of compassion that, right now, only made her want to roll her eyes. “You don’t have to do much. Smile, wave, and just look like you care. It really isn’t that hard.”

Of course Steve would say something like that. Agatha felt her cheeks burn with frustration.

Steve was her cousin, an illegitimate Harkness. A bastard. As much as Agatha despised the term — to her, he was a brother — that was exactly how the United Realms saw him: the Bastard Prince.

Born to her uncle Richard and a palace maid, Steve’s existence defied royal norms. While most dynasties swiftly disposed of their illegitimate offspring, King Nicholas refused to allow a Harkness, even one born of adultery, to be cast aside. He forced his brother to acknowledge the boy, ensuring Steve was raised within the palace walls. Still, he was denied the family name, officially registered as Rogers, the surname of the mother who vanished shortly after his birth.

Ostracized by a family that viewed him as a walking, breathing reminder of Richard’s infidelity, Steve found his sanctuary in the Royal Guard — the sole place of honor afforded to a bastard at court. Yet, despite his title, the United Realms revered him. He had risen swiftly through the ranks to become the youngest Commander of the Crown Guard in the history of the Twenty Realms, taking the post at just twenty years old. His brilliance extended far beyond the battlefield, too. Steve was an exceptional academic, his achievements at Harkensgate University nothing sort of stellar. By twenty-four, Crown Intelligence was mentoring his thesis on cybersecurity. He was a bona fide genius, and the public absolutely adored him.

“Of course it’s not hard for you, is it, Steve?” Agatha shot back, her voice dripping with sarcasm. “After all, everyone adores you. I bet they wish you were the First Prince instead!”

“But I’m not, and I don’t want to be!” he fired back, exasperated. “This isn’t about me or what the public thinks of me. This is about responsibility— your sense of responsibility, Agatha! I want you to be queen, but you have to actually show that you want it, too.”

“How about we all cool down a bit?” Kate murmured, shrinking further into the sofa cushions. No one paid her any mind except Wanda, who offered a soft chuckle before taking another sip of champagne.

“Of course I want to be queen, you all know that!” Agatha declared defensively. But as she met their unyielding, serious expressions, her defiance crumbled. She let out a defeated sigh, her shoulders dropping. “What do you want me to do?”

“I want you to mature.” It was the first time Lilia’s voice had softened. She looked at Agatha with genuine affection, letting out a long, weary sigh. “You have to understand how delicate the political situation is right now. A moment of transition is always a moment of instability and unrest.”

“But I am trying,” Agatha insisted, her tone genuine. “I really am. And I’m sorry for whatever I did or didn’t do today.”

Jennifer lowered her head, letting out a dark, sarcastic laugh.

“Whatever you did or didn’t do?” she repeated incredulously.

Agatha’s face instantly twisted in indignation as Jennifer pressed on. “Agatha, you—”

“Jennifer,” Lilia interjected sharply, a warning edge in her voice.

“Did you hear what she just said?” Jennifer demanded, her voice thick with disbelief.

“Okay, enough!” Wanda cut in, clapping her hands lightly from the armchair to draw all eyes. “We have this exact same argument almost every day. Can we skip to what actually matters? What does she need to do now?”

“Wow, Wanda. Truly, a monumental contribution…” Jennifer muttered.

“Look, Jen—”

“Enough!” Lilia’s voice cracked like a whip, cutting off the bickering instantly. Every person in the room stiffened, shrinking back into their positions.

“I really am sorry,” Agatha murmured, holding her hands up in a gesture of absolute surrender. “I never wanted today to go sideways, but honestly? I still don’t know what I could have done differently.”

“Unbelievable, Agatha,” Jennifer muttered, shaking her head. “You are truly unbelievable.”

Wanda clicked her tongue loudly, crossing her arms to draw the room’s attention back to her as she casually finished the last of her champagne. “Jen, all I her is you complaining. Not once have you actually stepped up and explained what she did wrong.”

Jennifer opened her mouth to argue, but Wanda silenced her with a casual flick of her hand. “I know you’re just doing your job, but nobody here reads minds. If she’s expected to act a certain way, she needs to be told, she can’t just rely on intuition. This whole monarch’s calling idea is a fairy tale. If times are this delicate, she needs a prior briefing, a solid stance. You throw her straight to the wolves and expect her to sprint, without even telling her where’s she’s going.”

“I made flashcards and—”

“Flashcards? Seriously?” Wanda scoffed, a laugh breaking through. “You’re constantly demanding she act like a queen, but what do you actually do to prepare her? She doesn’t even know half of what’s happening in parliament!”

“And is she even remotely interested in—”

“I already said I’m sorry,” Agatha cut in smoothly, shutting down the squabble before it could spiral. She turned her focus to Lilia, who was watching her with quiet intensity. “What do I actually need to do? I’ll cooperate. I promise.”

Lilia’s eyes locked onto hers for a long beat before she finally nodded, her severe expression softening.

“You just need to try a little harder, Agatha,” Lilia said. Out of the corner of her eye, Agatha caught Wanda rolling her eyes. “Nothing has ever truly been demanded of you, quite the opposite, in fact. But if you don’t start acting like a queen, you’ll never become one.”

“I want my crown. I really am sorry, godmum, and I’ll cooperate,” Agatha said, her tone suddenly dramatic. “I didn’t recognize the gravity of today. I messed up. I’m just a girl.”

Kate slapped her hand over her mouth to stifle a laugh, while Wanda sharply elbowed her, her eyes wide in reprimand.

“I’m sorry, godmum. I’m sorry, Jennifer,” Agatha continued smoothly, turning her focus to the Princess of Atlantia. “So, Jen, what do we need to do now?”

“Beautiful and sincere words, bestie,” Wanda deadpanned, offering a slow clap. “I’m practically in tears.”

“Wanda, I swear to god—” Agatha hissed through gritted teeth. She stopped short, however, the moment she caught Lilia’s arched, calculating eyebrow.

Straightening her posture, Agatha elegantly swept her hair over her shoulders. She cleared her throat and seamlessly shifted into a perfectly practiced royal persona.

“I am truly sorry for how the hearing unfolded. It was never my intention to appear indifferent to my people. I did not realize I was projecting that image, and I certainly does not reflect how I truly feel.”

“How does she do it?” Steve chuckled softly, leaning in to whisper over Kate’s shoulder. “She always has a perfect statement right on the tip of her tongue.”

“It’s a start, right?” Kate shrugged with a small smile. “At least she’s good with words.” Her smile vanished instantly, however, and she snapped to attention the second her gaze collided with Agatha’s icy blue glare.

Dismissing her sister, Agatha turned back to Jennifer. “What is the next step?”

Jennifer sighed, pulling her tablet from under her arm. She unlocked the screen, her eyes rapidly scanning the information. “I’ll draft a statement and put you on the air in half an hour,” she murmured, swiping briskly across the glass.

The princess nodded, her tension finally melting away as she let herself sink comfortably back into the sofa cushions.

***

Clutching Jennifer’s drafted speech, Agatha strode down the palace corridors toward the Royal Room with Wanda at her side. With every sentence she skimmed, her expression hardened into deeper disdain. She genuinely couldn’t fathom how her demeanor had been blown so wildly out of proportion. She had been honest in her answers, and when she wasn’t, she had simply opted for silence. Were her rest facial expressions really about to become a political talking point for alleged indifference?

To Agatha, the reality was starkly simple: not every monarch is loved. She wouldn’t be the first to rely on that premise, nor the last. It was the very foundation of royal history.

The truth was, she wasn’t indifferent; she was just entirely isolated from reality — and that wasn’t entirely her fault. Raised from birth within the palace’s gilded bubble, she would barely know that the outside world looked like if it weren’t for her secret escapades with Wanda.

Her entire existence boiled down to diplomatic lunches, calculated public appearances, memorized speeches, and the extravagant balls thrown by other dynasties. Her actual political participation was non-existent. Lilia had always deliberately excluded her from the real decisions and discussions governing the United Realms. Agatha had been fine with that arrangement; she’d never had much interest in politics anyway, having abandoned her monotonous royal mentorships at sixteen. Adolescence, after all, isn’t exactly renowned for its foresight. It felt deeply unfair to suddenly demand a regal statesmanship she had never been taught to wield.

As Wanda had so aptly out it, the monarch’s calling was nothing but a fairy tale. This sudden, frantic insistence on a flawless queenly demeanor left a bitter taste in Agatha’s mouth. She had been educated to smile and wave, and she had done exactly that. For years, she had comfortably accepted her role as a mere decorative prop. Yet, her godmother’s recent, explosive outbursts were quietly beginning to unsettle her.

“Do you think these crazy people, the ones at the protest, could actually threaten my crown?” Agatha asked, a rare note of uncertainty in her voice as they navigated the labyrinthine hallways.

“Agatha, for god’s sake! That’s crazy talk,” Wanda scoffed. “Nobody is ever satisfied with anything; it’s always been that way. You’re worrying over nothing.”

“Do you really think so?”

“When, in the entire history of the United Realms, has a Harkness ever been dethroned?” Wanda countered with a perfectly arched eyebrow.

Agatha shrugged, mulling it over. She had a point.

“I know. But lately, Lilia has been freaking out at me, and Jennifer won’t stop complaining. I’ve lost count of how many statements I’ve been forced to make just to clarify trivial nonsense, and we’re practically on the eve of the coronation. I’m just… worried,” she confessed.

Wanda merely shrugged. “When are those two not freaking out at you? It’s literally their job.”

Again, a fair point.

Upon entering the Royal Room, Wanda slipped to the back to join Kate, Jennifer and Steve behind the heavy broadcast cameras. Agatha gracefully took her seat in the gold-ornamented chair at the center of the room. The hair and makeup team immediately swarmed her, applying the final, meticulous touches to her appearance. Within minutes, the set was ready, and she was broadcasting live across all twenty realms.

Slipping on a well-rehearsed mask of gentle firmness, Agatha locked her eyes on the center of the camera lens. She straightened her posture and began.

“Today, I publicly and personally apologize to the United Realms,” she intoned, her voice somber and steady. “There are no words to adequately express my regret for what occurred at the public hearing. I have been feeling unwell for a few days; even so, I did not want to postpone my commitment to this nation. I erred out of imprudence, but that single moment does not define who I am.”

She paused, taking a discreet, steadying breath.

This is pathetic, she thought. These words are pathetic. She felt exactly like a wind-up robot.

“My commitment and my heart remain with my people,” she continued, “and I am truly sorry if my actions allowed for any other interpretation. To those who were heard today, I assure you that your needs will be met. Our parliamentarians are fully aware of your demands and are working tirelessly to improve the well-being of every citizen across the Twenty Realms. To those who were silenced by the ensuing turmoil, I ask for your forgiveness. I formally invite you to a new open-agenda hearing. Every voice matters in our realms, and together, step by step, we will continue to improve the United Realms.”

She offered a warm, practiced smile.

“I extend my gratitude not only to those who graced me with their presence but also to everyone who took to the streets in protest today. Every citizen has the fundamental right to express their frustrations and demands. After all, we share a common goal: the betterment of the United Realms. You are heard, and I swear to you that I am working every single day to govern for everyone, including you.”

Her expression was serene, yet her tone remained austere and resolute. She had been rigorously trained for exactly this kind of performance.

“I want to truly understand and address the issues that afflict us,” she declared, her chin held high in quiet defiance. “I was born, raised, and educated in the United Realms. I love this nation, and I am deeply grateful for its people. You expect a great deal from me, but I expect even more from myself. Nothing in this world matters more to me than our nation. Despite today’s frustrations, the Crown’s channels remain open so we may continue working toward our shared progress. I appreciate your support, and I commit myself to continuously giving you my very best.”

The moment the cameraman signaled that they were clear, Agatha let out a loud, bored groan. She flipped her hair over her shoulder and rolled her eyes dramatically.

“If she were an actress, she’d have an Oscar by now,” Steve chuckled crossing his arms over his chest.

“Well, at least this time she actually helped write the statement,” Kate muttered in her sister’s defense.

Jennifer’s eyes went wide, visibly offended. “She helped?” she shot back in utter disbelief.

“I did help this time! I told you exactly what message I wanted do convey,” Agatha chimed in, strolling over with her signature arrogant smirk back in place.

“Oh, yes, of course,” Jennifer nodded, her voice dripping with sarcasm. “And what were your exact words again? Ah, I remember now: tell the protesters to go to hell, and whoever doesn’t like me will just have to deal with it. Truly, I merely made a few minor stylistic tweaks to your profound words. Just a light touch-up.”

She flashed an acidic smile, but Agatha just nodded, looking entirely satisfied. “See?” she said, throwing her arms wide. “I am cooperating!”

“If I were you, I’d be careful,” Jennifer warned darkly. “You’re getting so full of yourself that pretty soon, that crown isn’t going to fit on your head.”

Agatha merely smiled and offered a dismissive shrug.

“I really don’t think that was a compliment, Aggie,” Steve pointed out.

The princess just laughed. “So what? She said the words crown on my head, and that is literally all I care about.”

Jennifer let out a long, incredulous breath, shaking her head. “People spend so much time worrying about Agatha’s character, when what they really need to worry about is her intellectual capacity. If she keeps this up, the United Realm is going to be governed by the infinite monkey theorem.”

The witty banter was cut short by Lilia’s sudden arrival. The Regent Guardian now wore a completely serene expression, her lips pressed into a rare, genuine smile. Approaching Agatha, she affectionately pinched the princess’s cheek like a proud mother, coaxing a wide, beaming smile from the girl.

“You spoke beautifully, darling. I am so proud of you!” Lilia praised.

“Lilia, I was the one who wrote that statement!” Jennifer interjected, absolutely indignant.

The Regent merely shrugged, warmly patting her pupil’s shoulder. “I know, darling,” she told Jennifer with a soft smile. “You are a brilliant angel who spins the best possible appearances out of whatever Agatha gives you. But I am proud because, this time, she actually wanted to contribute her own words. She showered herself to be genuinely willing to improve and cooperate.”

Jennifer blinked, utterly paralyzed by disbelief. Beside her, the others were fighting a losing batter against their laughter, desperately averting their eyes from one another to maintain even a shred of composure in front of the Regent Guardian.
