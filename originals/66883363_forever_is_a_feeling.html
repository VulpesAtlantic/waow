Agatha Harkness, beautiful, clever, and rich, with a comfortable home and a happy disposition, seemed to unite some of the best blessings of existence, and she had lived nearly thirty-two years in the world with very little to distress or vex her.

She was born into an already long lineage of wealthy people, the only daughter of New York’s most famous litigator, who had raised her with too much affection and indulgence. Her mother had died during a liposuction procedure when she was too young to even remember her, and from what Agatha had gathered about her reputation, it hadn’t been a particularly significant loss.

The real problem, if one insisted on calling it that, was that the power of having rather too much her own way, and a tendency to think a little too well of herself, had been, of course, a great source of enjoyment. But growing up without consequences turned her into a force of nature. 

That was also how she’d ended up with her best and oldest friend, Rio Vidal, as her business partner. Well, not quite yet. But she would.

The idea had been forming for a while, ever since she'd helped Alice—daughter of a rock legend and, somehow, a rising TikTok star—become the ambassador for Jennifer’s cosmetics brand. She hadn’t meant to get involved. She barely cared about internet fame, and on a good day, thought it was an unoriginal waste of time. But she’d been in the spotlight long enough to understand the grip it had on people.

She didn’t want it, exactly. But she loved the attention. There was a difference.

That meant she had millions of people following her, asking her questions about their hair and their heartbreaks, their product choices and their futures. She answered as many as she could. Not because she wanted to save them, but because she liked the idea of changing someone’s day with nothing more than a link to her favorite shampoo. 

And when she realized she’d accidentally turned Jen and Alice into a power couple—and a marketing success—just by running her mouth at brunch, she figured maybe it was time to do something real with her Harvard degree.

Of course, "real" in Agatha’s case meant glamorous and chaotic and mildly unhinged, but she knew better than to try doing all the boring parts herself. She would need someone to run the numbers, draft the contracts, keep the lights on. And there was only one person she trusted not to ruin the fun.

So naturally, she showed up at Rio’s apartment one evening with a bottle of wine, a half-baked business plan, and no backup plan if Rio said no.

The apartment was neat in the way that always annoyed Agatha on principle. She dropped her coat on the back of a chair, kicked her shoes off near the door, and made herself at home like she always did.

Rio didn’t even look up from the kitchen. “No.”

Agatha rolled her eyes. “You don’t even know what I’m going to say.”

“I don’t have to.” Rio appeared with two glasses and that annoyingly reasonable expression she always wore when she knew Agatha was up to something. “Whatever it is, it’s going to be ridiculous, expensive, and involve three lawsuits within the first year.”

“I’m flattered you think it’ll take that long.”

Rio just raised an eyebrow.

Agatha poured the wine herself, sank into the couch, and swung her legs up over the arm like she belonged there. Because she did.

She always had.

They had been raised like family, practically. Their fathers had gone to law school together, built an empire together, and then had daughters a few years apart, determined to pass on their friendship whether the girls liked it or not. But Agatha had liked it. Even if Rio had been frustratingly mature even at seven, even if she'd refused to climb the fire escape or sneak out of ballet class, even if she'd always been the one asking questions like what’s the plan and how do we get back in without getting caught.

She had always said no first. But she almost always gave in eventually.

That was the thing about Rio—Agatha had always been able to move faster than her, louder, brighter. But Rio was the one who stuck around. She never flinched. Never wavered. Even when they fought—and they did, often and spectacularly—Rio was still there, still showing up, still letting Agatha in, even when she didn’t deserve it.

Which is why this wasn’t a question. Not really.

“I’m starting an agency,” Agatha said simply. “Something real. For creators and influencers and people who want to build something instead of just burning out in six months.”

Rio gave her the sort of look she reserved for when Agatha claimed she didn’t need to hire a publicist and could absolutely handle her own press.

“I’m serious,” she said, tucking her legs underneath her. “I already have proof of concept. Jen and Alice—”

“Were a fluke.”

“Were the start,” Agatha corrected. “And I don’t want to build it with strangers who don’t get me. I want you.”

Rio blinked, and for a second, Agatha thought she might actually be considering it. Which meant she was winning.

“I don’t do startups,” Rio said slowly. “I like systems. I like insurance. I like sleep.”

“Which is why you’ll do the boring stuff. And I’ll do the rest.”

There was a long pause.

gatha let it sit. She wasn’t good at waiting. But for Rio, she would wait. She didn’t need to charm her. She didn’t need to beg. All she had to do was be honest, for once.

“I don’t want to do this with anyone else,” she said, softer.

She didn’t say I need you. That would’ve been too much. But she knew Rio heard it anyway.

And that was how it began.

Not because Agatha had convinced her. But because, at the end of the day, Rio always showed up for her.

Even when she shouldn’t.
