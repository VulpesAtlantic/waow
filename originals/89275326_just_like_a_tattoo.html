Summary

Rio Vidal is about to get her first tattoo ever. She's always been intrigued by them, but her fear of needles has kept her reticent to receive one thus far. But as long as she's getting one, she figures she might as well get the one tattoo that could give her a hint as to whom she's meant to spend the rest of her life with (aka her Soulmate). She doesn't quite know if she believes in that kind of thing, but it definitely couldn't hurt, could it?

Agatha thought that she'd found her soulmate already. She was ready to spend the rest of her life with Wanda, but a revelation unravels the whole thing between them, and Agatha is now desperate to find the woman she's actually meant to spend her life with -- if she can.

Just Like a Tattoo

The tattoo parlor was cleaner than Rio had thought it would be. 

She didn’t know what she had been expecting, but a waiting room filled to the brim with pictures of flowers, stars, moons, and other witchy things was not it. Alice had suggested it to her when Rio brought the idea of finally getting a tattoo.

But not just any tattoo. 

The tattoo.

The one sworn to help her find her soulmate. 

Rio didn’t know if she believed in all that, but if she was going to get a tattoo anyway, it might as well be this one. What was the harm?

If nothing else, she’d have a funny story to tell.

Besides, according to Alice, Lilia Calderu was the best at this kind of tattoo. 

“Her accuracy is scary,” Alice said when explaining it to Rio. “Like, most artists will get the gist of the first words your soulmate speaks to you, but Lilia gets the direct quote, every time.” 

She showed Rio her own wrist, where Jen’s words were etched into her skin forever: “Out of my way, Tiny Tim.”

“You two have such a strange relationship,” Rio commented.

Alice shrugged. “It works for us.” 

Rio was listening to her music on low as she waited for her turn in the main room, her eyes roaming over the designs plastered on the walls as well as the plants that littered pretty much every available surface. She was able to name each and every one of them within the first five minutes of being there, murmuring the words under her breath.

She had only planned on getting the soulmate tattoo, but if she had to choose something for a second tattoo (potentially, depending on how much this one hurt) she’d probably opt for a jonquil, her birth flower. Her mother had one tattooed on her forearm with Rio’s name and birthdate next to it. 

Rio probably wouldn’t mind having one that matched.

Again, depending on the pain.

It wasn’t that Rio was particularly sensitive when it came to pain. She could just tolerate certain types of pain better than others. And the pain she couldn’t tolerate was like torture to her.

She wondered if she’d even get through this tattoo.

She glanced up toward the front desk where there was a big, “NO REFUNDS!!!” sign in black and white on the wall behind the receptionist. 

She assumed that included the two-hundred dollar deposit she’d put down when booking this appointment. She could afford to lose the money, but it would still be stupid of her to waste it.

She hated wasting money.

It was just one tattoo, one phrase that her soulmate would say to her as their first greeting to her. It couldn’t be much more than one or two words, right? Probably just ‘hi, nice to meet you’ or something.

Simple. Easy. No sweat.

It’d be done before she realized.

Rio closed her eyes as the music continued to play in her ears. She took a couple of slow, deep breaths, willing her muscles to relax. She could do this. 

She opened her eyes and nearly jumped at the woman who seemed to have just appeared across the room from her. She was…gorgeous.

Rio felt her face warm and her palms sweat at the woman’s pouty lips twisting and her bright blue, oceanic eyes drinking in whatever was on the screen of her phone, her thumb swiping across it. She was dressed somewhat casually in a pair of dark wash jeans that looked as though they had been tailored to fit her perfectly, a purple button down shirt that was left open to reveal a black camisole, and a pair of thick black glasses that she’d pushed to rest on top of a mountain of wavy dark brown hair. Her feet were encased in galaxy-themed Docs and her sleeves were pushed up to her elbows, revealing a couple of tattoos that Rio couldn’t fully make out, but at least one of them was a bunny.

“Take a photo,” the woman said, making Rio jump at the sound of her voice over the low music still filtering through her ears; “it’ll last longer.”

Rio blinked rapidly at her, realizing that the woman was now staring at her straight-on, no longer interested in whatever was happening on her phone. Her head was tilted to the side and one of her brows was lifted in either a question or a challenge (Rio couldn’t tell).

Rio swallowed thickly, reaching up to scratch the back of her neck. She cleared her throat. “Fuck,” she said, “sorry.”

Suddenly, the woman’s gaze narrowed, her lips opening slightly as if readying themselves to admonish her again. 

Before she could, however, the door to the artist’s studio opened and a redheaded woman walked out, looking down at her wrist with a frown. The blue-eyed woman stood up, reaching for her.

“How’d it go, babe?” she asked, wrapping her arm around the redhead’s waist.

The redhead continued to frown, tears forming in her eyes as she looked at her…girlfriend? Wife? Rio had no idea, nor should she have cared. All she really needed to know was that she was taken. Besides, she hadn’t exactly made the greatest first impression, had she?

She cleared her throat and looked down at her lap, giving the couple some privacy as they made their way to the receptionist’s desk, paid their remaining balance, and headed out the front door.

“Rio Vidal?”

Rio looked up to see the tattooist, Lilia, smiling down at her, gently. She wondered how Lilia had known that she was her next client (there were a handful of other people sitting in the reception area) but decided that maybe Alice was right about Lilia being incredibly clairvoyant.

Or maybe it had just been a lucky guess.

“Hey,” Rio said, taking her earbuds out and tucking them back in their case. “Yeah, that’s me.”

“I know,” Lilia said. “And I am Lilia Calderu, but I assume you already know that since you’re the one that made the appointment with me, specifically. Are you ready for your next adventure?”

Rio shrugged. “It’s just a tattoo,” she said. “No big deal.”

Lilia looked her up and down, probably clocked the lack of any body art on Rio except for the ear piercings she’d gotten as an infant and the tiny stud she’d gotten in her nose as a drunken college sophomore. 

“Not just a tattoo, dear,” Lilia said. “The most important tattoo you’ll ever get.”

“Probably the only one I’ll ever get,” Rio snorted.

“We’ll see,” Lilia responded, cryptically, as they made their way into her studio.

Tears continued to trickle down her cheeks as she looked down at her right wrist, which was covered in a thin white bandage. “Oof, did it hurt more than you were expecting? I’m sure we can find something to ease the pain when we get home.”

“It didn’t hurt,” Wanda murmured. 

“No?” Agatha asked, squeezing her hand. She kept her eyes on the road, her gaze flickering over to the redhead every now and then. “Then what’s wrong? Why are you crying?”

“I’m not crying,” Wanda sniffled, looking out the window. She used her free hand to swipe away her tears.

“Babe,” Agatha snorted, “I can see the tears dripping off your chin. Seriously, what happened in there? Did the butterfly come out crooked or something?”

Wanda mumbled something unintelligible against the window. Agatha furrowed her brow, flicking her gaze in her girlfriend’s direction.

“What was that?” Agatha asked.

Wanda took a deep, shuddering breath as she turned to her girlfriend. She sniffled and wiped her face with the back of her arm and cleared her throat.

“I said,” she sniffled, “that I didn’t get a butterfly.”

“You didn’t?” Agatha asked. “But you’ve been talking about that tattoo for forever! You were so excited about it!”

“I…lied,” Wanda said, her voice so quiet that Agatha almost couldn’t hear her.

“About a butterfly tattoo?” Agatha scoffed. “Why? You know I don’t care what tattoo you get so long as it makes you happy, babe.” She squeezed Wanda’s hand.

Wanda slipped her hand out of Agatha’s grasp. Agatha frowned, immediately wanting to take it back, but Wanda hated when people touched her when she made her boundaries clear like that, so she refrained, waiting her out.

They had just pulled onto their street when Wanda finally spoke again.

“I got one of those quote tattoos,” she said, looking down at her wrist in her lap.

“Quote…?” Agatha pulled into a parking spot outside of the duplex they rented and put the car in park. “What do you mean?” she asked, turning fully to Wanda now.

“You know,” Wanda sniffled, tapping Agatha’s wrist with her fingers. “The quote thing.” She ran her thumb over the words on Agatha’s skin; there were only two, stark against her pale skin.

“Oh,” Agatha said, looking down at her wrist. “I thought you said you didn’t want to do that one, though. You said it would be a waste of money since–”

“Since we already know that we’re soulmates,” Wanda finished for her. “Yeah, I know what I said.” Her voice was a little choked up as a fresh wave of tears fell from her eyes. “I changed my mind.”

Wanda let out a sob, burying her face into her hands, her shoulders shaking with the force of her tears. Agatha’s eyes widened in panic, and she immediately unbuckled her seatbelt, reaching across to wrap her arms around Wanda.

“I’m so sorry…I’m sorry, Agatha…I’m so sorry…” 

“Sorry about what, baby?” Agatha asked, still rubbing her back. 

Wanda’s body shook with another sob before she pulled back, wrapping her arms around herself and avoiding Agatha’s gaze.

“Sorry about what?” Agatha repeated, her tone hardening slightly. Her heart still ached looking at Wanda’s tear-stained face and reddened eyes, but there was obviously something she wasn’t telling her. Something that must have been bad if it got this reaction out of her. “Wands, babe, sorry about what?” Agatha cupped her cheeks with both hands, trying to force her to look her in the eyes. 

Wanda still avoided her gaze, tears still leaking from the corners of her eyes. 

“I lied,” she said, her gaze on Agatha’s chin. Her own chin quivered. “I lied,” she repeated.

“Lied?” Agatha asked, furrowing her brow. “Lied about what? The butterfly? I don’t care about that. I–”

“Not about the butterfly, Agatha,” Wanda huffed, sounding frustrated, which was frankly unfair considering the circumstances. If she’d just be clear about what she meant– “I lied about the tattoo.”

“So you didn’t get my first words to you tattooed on you then?” Agatha asked, furrowing her brow in confusion. “But you just said–”

“They’re not your first words to me!” Wanda snapped, pulling away and unbuckling her seatbelt. She was out of the car and across the front lawn before Agatha could even turn off the car and stumble out of it.

“Wanda!” Agatha huffed, jogging to catch up to her. “Wanda, what are you talking about?” 

Wanda tugged her key out of her front pocket, but struggled to get it into the lock before Agatha had reached her. She dropped the key completely when she felt Agatha’s hand on her shoulder, pulling away and practically cowering against the side of the house as if Agatha was about to strike her.

Agatha held her hands up in surrender, splaying out her fingers as though showing Wanda that she was safe. She felt a pang of hurt at the thought that Wanda could even imagine she would do anything to make her feel unsafe.

“What has gotten into you?” Agatha asked. “What aren’t you telling me?”

Wanda flexed her jaw and relaxed her body, reaching down to pick up the key. It bumped against the lock a couple of times before sliding in. Wanda turned the doorknob and pushed the door open, but she made no move to step over the threshold. Instead, she turned back to Agatha.

“We need to talk,” she said.

Agatha’s chest filled with dread. She just nodded. “After you,” she said, motioning for Wanda to precede her into the house.

Wanda nodded, taking the first step inside.

It didn’t hurt too much. 

There were a few moments where Rio was gritting her teeth, but they were few and far between. For the most part, it felt like somebody was just scratching her with a particularly pointy nail. But she could see herself possibly doing this again.

Maybe.

With the right design.

Lilia, the tattoo artist, was also pretty chill as she needled in the ink. She asked Rio about herself, her hobbies, her interests, her family, her career…

She had a very soothing voice that acted as an effective distraction when her needle got too close to the joint. Rio felt comfortable in the chair, telling Lilia about her work with plants and her rock collection and her interest in paleontology. Lilia didn’t give her any weird looks or mock her for her interests like others had, which was nice. In fact, Lilia even asked her the difference between a Velociraptor and a Deinonychus, which Rio had been thrilled at because she could talk for hours about it. She could give TED Talks on dinosaurs.

By the time she was getting into the inaccuracies of the modern-day depictions of dinosaurs, Lilia had placed the last dot on her wrist. 

“All done,” she said, interrupting Rio’s stream of words. “You wanna check it out while I get you a bandage and some ointment?”

“Sure,” Rio said, feeling a bit disappointed that she wouldn’t get to finish her speech. She glanced down at her wrist, her eyes widening as the words processed in her brain, her mouth gaping open slightly. “Lilia…”

“What’s up, kiddo?” Lilia asked, sitting back down at Rio’s side with a roll of bandages and tape. “Did you get a good look? I know it’s longer than most – and ruder – but those are the best starts to a relationship, I think. If nothing else, this person will be very interesting.”

“How…how does your power work?” Rio asked, looking up at Lilia. “Like, do you hear the person speaking as you write down their words? Do you know who it is?”

“Not exactly,” Lilia said, cutting a square off the bandage to fit over Rio’s healing tattoo. “It’s more like a vision. I just see the words on your wrist when I look at you, like I’ve already put them there. I’m getting deja vu right this very second, in fact.” She chuckled as she sprayed Rio’s wrist with something foamy and cold before wiping it away with a fresh cloth. “It feels like it should be a paradox, but I’ve had many success stories come through this shop.” Her eyes glittered as she looked up at Rio with a grin. “I wonder if I’ll be able to add one more.”

Rio swallowed thickly as she looked down at the words on her wrist, which disappeared as Lilia placed the bandage over them and taped it down over her skin.

She barely heard the care instructions Lilia gave her, her ears ringing and her focus elsewhere.

“I’m not sure how this is possible,” Agatha said, looking down at the words on her wrist, then back at Wanda. “What are you saying?”

“I saw them before we met,” Wanda said, motioning to Agatha’s wrist. “The words. I saw them.”

“How?” Agatha asked. “How could you have possibly–”

“It’s not hard to catch sight of somebody’s wrist, Agatha,” Wanda scoffed, still wiping at the corners of her eyes with a tissue. She sniffled, clearing her throat.

“But, the first time we met,” Agatha said, “you literally almost knocked me on my ass because you weren’t looking. You’re telling me that you were looking? You did that on purpose?”

Wanda shrugged, avoiding Agatha’s gaze as she stared down at the covered tattoo on her own wrist. “It was the only scenario that I could think of,” she said, “where those words would make any sense.”

Agatha thought back to the day they’d met.

It had been three years prior. Agatha was pretty new to Westview. She’d been taking walks in the afternoons to try and familiarize herself with her new town on the weekends. There wasn’t much to do there, but there were a few cute, tiny shops in the town square. One of them happened to be a cute coffee shop that, apparently, every single person in town frequented – especially on Sundays.

Agatha was in line to order her oatmilk latte when she felt a bump from behind and turned to glare at the offender over her shoulder.

“Watch where you’re going!” she huffed at the redheaded woman behind her.

“Fuck,” the woman said, “sorry.”

Agatha’s stomach fluttered at that. She looked down at the familiar words tattooed on her wrist for the better part of a decade and felt her face warm as she got a good look at the bashful woman, turning to face her fully.

“Um,” she said, “no problem. It happens.” 

The other woman was younger, with big green eyes and a shy, but beautiful, smile. Agatha felt the attraction to her immediately.

“Let me buy your drink,” the woman said, “to make up for it. Please.”

Agatha’s heart fluttered at that, though she tried to play it off with a casual shrug. “Sure,” she said. “If you insist.”

The redhead seemed to see right through her, rolling her eyes affectionately as she held out her hand. “I’m Wanda,” she said. “Wanda Maximoff.”

“Agatha,” Agatha replied, glancing down at Wanda’s bare wrist. “Agatha Harkness.”

Agatha hadn’t concerned herself with the fact that Wanda didn’t get her soulmate tattoo. It hadn’t mattered because her words had matched Agatha’s tattoo. She was obviously Agatha’s soulmate. Agatha didn’t need any other proof than that.

Wanda had acted surprised when Agatha had revealed her tattoo to her. She had blushed and giggled and seemed so flustered to have found her soulmate at The Witches’ Brew, of all places, in downtown Westview, New Jersey. But she had reciprocated the attraction (obviously) and they’d carried on a relationship that moved quickly. 

Within six months, they were living together in their first apartment. A year after that, they’d found their current duplex, where Agatha sat on the couch, staring down at the words that had started the whole deception.

“Why would you do that?” she asked, her voice deathly quiet and calm.

“I don’t know,” Wanda said, her voice small as she sniffled.

Agatha wished she would stop sniffling like she was the victim in all of this.

“Don’t give me that shit,” she practically growled. “You know exactly why you did it. I’m the one that can’t understand why you would waste my time like that!”

“It wasn’t a waste!” Wanda insisted, starting a fresh wave of tears falling. “I love you, Agatha!” She reached for her girlfriend, but Agatha pulled away, holding her hands up as if shielding herself.

“You lied to me,” Agatha said, “about arguably the most important thing that a person could possibly lie about.”

“Those tattoos are bullshit!” Wanda huffed. “They’re not real!”

“Then why the fuck did you get one?” Agatha shot back. “What was the point? You knew it would reveal you as a liar! Why didn’t you just get the first words I said to you tattooed on your wrist? Why go for the actual tattoo?”

“I…tried,” Wanda admitted, looking away. “Lilia refused.”

Agatha made a note to add a twenty to Lilia’s tip for her next tattoo service.

“She offered to do the soulmate tattoo instead,” Wanda continued. “I figured, since we were already together and happy, that it might turn out that we are, in fact, real soulmates. I mean, my first words to you were–”

“Manufactured,” Agatha interrupted. “They were manufactured first words. There’s a reason they tell you not to share the words you have on your wrist with anybody, Wanda! It makes it harder to find your real soulmate!”

“There’s no such thing as soulmates!” Wanda shouted, almost directly into Agatha’s face.

Agatha stood and walked away from her, putting some much needed space between the two of them before she did something brash.

“You knew,” she said, through gritted teeth, “how important this tattoo was to me when we first met. You know that I take it very seriously. The second you realized just how seriously I take it, you should have told me the truth. Instead, you strung me along for–”

“You strung me along,” Agatha repeated, her tone harder, “for three. Fucking. Years!” She took a deep breath. “You knew how serious this was, how much I believed in it, and how much it meant to me,” she said, “and, still, you lied to me about it! Would you have ever even told me the truth if you never got the damn tattoo? If you had a butterfly on your wrist, would you have just let me believe that we were soulmates while my real one was out there, somewhere?!” She motioned out the window, where they could clearly see the street.

Is it always about you, you, you?!?” She turned back to glare at Wanda, who was giving her that face that infuriated Agatha. That stubborn, bratty expression that she was certain Pietro, Wanda’s twin brother, had been on the receiving end of plenty of times in their childhood. Like she was the innocent one and Agatha was actually the one in the wrong.

I would have laughed about it and still sat with you and even probably asked you out on a date. We might have ended up here, organically, and I wouldn’t have cared so much that you didn’t want to get the tattoo. It wouldn’t have mattered as much if you were just honest with me from the beginning.”

I would have been prepared for the possibility that we might not actually work out. And hey, who the fuck knows? I don’t want to hear anything else from you unless it’s to tell me how soon you can get your shit out of the duplex.”

“Last time I left him alone with you for more than a day,” Agatha said, “you forgot to feed him, and he ended up eating through the leg of one of our dining chairs. And then he pooped in your shoes. He’s coming with me.”

Agatha let out a humorless laugh. “Nice try,” she said. “No. You are. I pay the majority of the rent for this please. My name is on the lease. I’m not going anywhere. I’ll call Pietro for you, though.” She pulled out her phone and immediately started dialing his number while Wanda just let out a huff and stomped off toward the steps.

Agatha had to clench her jaw and look up to keep the tears from flowing as she heard Pietro’s voice pick up on the other end of the line.

“So what does it say?” Alice asked, walking into Rio’s apartment later that day, after she’d gotten home and spread the first bit of ointment over it. It stung a little bit, but it wasn’t so bad.

“Don’t you ever knock?” Rio huffed, blowing on the tattoo to help the ointment dry a little bit faster.

“Why would I?” Alice asked, reaching into the pantry and coming out with a Coke and a bag of pretzels. She tore the bag open and reached in for a handful of mini pretzels that she then shoveled straight into her mouth. Rio rolled her eyes as Alice padded to the couch, slipping right out of her sneakers and fitting herself against Rio’s side to peer down at her tattoo. “Hmm,” she said, “intereshting.”

“Can you please not talk with your mouth full?” Rio sighed. “You know that grosses me out.”

“Sorry,” Alice said, covering her mouth. She opened the soda and took a long sip, washing down the salty pretzels, then let out a soft belch. “Excuse me,” she said with a blush. “So, what’s the word, nerd?” She craned her neck to see Rio’s right wrist.

Rio rolled her eyes (somewhat affectionately) and held out her wrist for Alice to see what was written on it. This was usually frowned upon, but Rio trusted Alice. Besides, they’d already met, so it wasn’t like she would be able to manufacture some kind of meet-cute – also, gross.

“Take a photo; it’ll last longer,” Alice read aloud. “Huh. That’s a weird way to say, ‘hello, nice to meet you.’” She took another sip of Coke.

“Not if I’m being creepy and staring at them,” Rio murmured, looking down at the quote on her wrist.

“What?” Alice asked, furrowing her brow in confusion.

“I think I met my soulmate,” Rio explained, “at the tattoo parlor.”

“Seriously?” Alice asked. “Before or after you got the tattoo? What does her tattoo say?”

“Before I got the tattoo,” Rio said. “But…we didn’t really talk.”

“What?” Alice snorted. “Then how do you know she’s your soulmate?”

“Well, I mean,” Rio said, flushing. “We did say something to each other. Specifically, she said this,” she showed Alice her wrist.

“And what did you say?” Alice asked, taking another sip of Coke.

Rio hunched her shoulders in embarrassment. “Sorry,” she said.

“Sorry about what?” Alice asked.

“No,” Rio huffed. “My first response was to say, ‘sorry’ as in, ‘sorry for being such a creep and staring at you, but you’re just so pretty.’” She put her face in her hands, but her flush was visible to the tips of her ears.

“Aww, buddy,” Alice said, patting her shoulder. “That’s okay. Happens to the best of us.”

“Also, it was more like a ‘fuck, sorry,’” Rio added. “Definitely two words. So now she has a cuss on her wrist for all eternity.”

“You are the only person I know that says ‘cuss’ instead of ‘curse’ or ‘swear’,” Alice murmured. “Besides, how do you know that she even had a soulmate tattoo? She might not believe in that sort of thing.”

“Lilia is well-known for those kinds of tattoos, you said,” Rio pointed out. “Also, when I said my apology, she gave me this sort of…strange look. Like she knew.”

“Okay, so then why didn’t you talk to her more?” Alice asked.

Rio took a deep breath and reached for a fresh cover for her tattoo. “Because she has a partner already. And I’m no homewrecker.”

“She has a partner?” Alice asked, furrowing her brow. “But you’re her soulmate? That doesn’t make a lot of sense.”

Rio shrugged. “People can date people who aren’t their soulmate, Al,” she said. “It happens.”

“I think she kind of already did,” Rio said. “She was crying when she came out.”

“Oof,” Alice said. “Well, that’s gonna be rough, but at least you won’t be a homewrecker.” She raised her mostly empty Coke can in salute.

“Alice, I don’t even know her name,” Rio snorted. “And she probably hasn’t even broken up with her girlfriend. Maybe they’ll decide to stay together despite the tattoos. Who knows?”

“Well, you certainly won’t,” Alice said, “if you don’t at least try.”

Rio taped the cover onto her tattoo and sighed, patting it to make sure that it was secure. “If we’re really soulmates,” she said, “it’ll happen on its own. Organically. Just gotta trust the universe, you know?”

“You have too much patience,” Alice said. “It pisses me off.” She drained what was left in her Coke can as Rio snickered. 

The lease on the duplex ended two months after their breakup, giving Agatha the option to either re-sign as a single woman or find a smaller place to go.

She could afford it, so she signed another six-month lease. She had just gotten her office the way she liked it, anyway, and Scratchy didn’t do too well with change.

Wanda had moved fully in with Pietro, whose apartment was all the way in Eastview, which meant they almost never saw each other (thankfully) after she’d gotten the rest of her shit out of the duplex. She’d attempted to claim the couch and the king-sized bed, but Agatha still had the receipts and the charges on her credit card history to prove that she’d been the one to buy both.

She’d let Wanda take the dining set, though. Damaged chairs and all. She bought herself a nicer one on Facebook Marketplace, anyway.

When Wanda had come back a couple of days after their breakup to get the rest of her things, she’d attempted to reconcile with Agatha, practically begging for forgiveness (which, honestly, Agatha didn’t hate). But Agatha was serious about breaking it off.

She wasn’t lying when she’d expressed how hurt she was by Wanda’s betrayal. The soulmate thing was serious to her. She believed in it as wholeheartedly as she believed the sky was blue. Her parents had had mismatched soulmate tattoos, their marriage an arranged one that resulted in her miserable childhood. Her father had turned down his own soulmate in order to keep his vows to Agatha’s wretch of a mother.

Evanora Harkness hadn’t done the same. The second she met her true soulmate – a man with a shark-like grin and designer watches – she had packed up everything and left both Agatha and her father. Agatha was twelve years old at the time and her mother’s parting words to her were about how she’d never even wanted her in the first place, anyway. 

Agatha’s father, despite knowing that Evanora wasn’t meant to be the love of his life, had wallowed in despair for nearly a year after she left, barely clinging to his career to keep them both afloat.

Agatha had vowed that she’d not let that happen. She vowed to only be with the person whose tattoo matched her first words to them. She wasn’t going to leave anybody heartbroken due to a situation neither of them could really control.

That being said, she hadn’t been lying when she told Wanda that she’d have stayed with her if she’d just been honest from the get-go. Agatha was a fiercely loyal person to the ones she loved. She had stayed by her father’s side, cooking and cleaning for him after his wife left him. She had held his hand at the doctor’s when he was diagnosed with cancer while Agatha was in college just as he had held her hand when she got her soulmate tattoo at Lilia’s parlor. 

“If you ever find that person,” he had said, squeezing her fingers in his as she sat at his death bed only a year later, “don’t let them pass you by, like I did. Go after them. Build a life with them. Promise me, Aggie.”

“Don’t call me that,” Agatha had sniffled, squeezing his hand as tears fell down her cheeks. But then she nodded. “Okay, Dad. I promise. I’ll lock them down real good.”

Her father let out a weak chuckle. “That’s my girl,” he said, patting her hand in his as he closed his eyes. 

He passed quietly that night.

Agatha was determined to keep that promise. She didn’t entertain anybody that didn’t say that two-word phrase the first time they’d met.

Okay, well, that wasn’t actually strictly true. She had entertained them…once or twice. She didn’t need to be chronically single, after all. She was allowed to have a little fun. But the second she saw that their soulmate tattoo didn’t match her first words to them, all chances of romantic pursuit evaporated and she made it clear to them that things would not work out in the long-term.

Obviously, there were a few exceptions to this rule.

Namely, Wanda, whose wrist had been bare when they met. Agatha didn’t mind potential partners not having the tattoo, so long as they said the magic words to her when they met. Agatha was willing to make an honest try of it despite the circumstances.

But Wanda had manipulated everything. Right from the start. She had tried to control Agatha with those two stupid little words. She had tricked her. She had made her feel stupid. Agatha Harkness did not like feeling stupid.

Agatha Harkness wasn’t stupid.

Which is why she didn’t entertain the idea of taking Wanda back, even though she begged.

“It’s not that big a deal,” Wanda said, encroaching on Agatha’s space even as Pietro and Natasha, their hot, butch neighbor, carried the dining table and chairs out of the duplex to the waiting U-Haul outside. “They’re just tattoos. Lilia probably doesn’t even know what she’s doing.”

“Then why were you crying?” Agatha asked, lifting an eyebrow. They’d already had this argument. Several times. It was becoming tedious.

“Because I knew you’d be upset,” Wanda said. “Because you believe in it.”

“And shouldn’t that be enough for you?” Agatha said. “Why do you insist on downplaying my beliefs?”

“Agatha, I–”

“Don’t,” Agatha said, putting up her hands. “Don’t bother. Just go. Find your soulmate. Leave me out of it.”

“Don’t you even want to know what the tattoo says?” Wanda asked, holding her still healing wrist to her chest.

Agatha eyed the appendage for a long moment, then shook her head.

“What’s the point?” she asked. “We’re no–”

“‘I’m terribly sorry,’” Wanda interrupted. “That’s what it says. It’s something you’ve never said to me.” She took a deep breath. “You’ve never apologized for anything. Ever.”

Agatha took a deep breath and shrugged. “Then why would you even still want me?” she asked. “Goodbye, Wanda.”

Wanda had left then, in a huff. Still the victim in her own mind.

Agatha had wallowed for a week or two before moving on. She would not waste away for a year like her father had. She had nobody to care for her.

Except Scratchy. But he would sooner eat her than make sure she didn’t starve to death.

She thought a lot about that one woman from the reception area at Lilia’s tattoo parlor. The one who had said the two words on Agatha’s wrist, organically (otherwise she must have had crazy good vision to see Agatha’s tattoo from the other side of the room.)

Agatha had asked Lilia if she knew who that woman was, or at least what her name was so Agatha could look her up for herself.

“I don’t give out client information like that, Agatha,” Lilia had huffed. “That goes against HIPAA.”

“You’re not a medical professional,” Agatha pointed out. 

“Whatever the tattoo version of HIPAA is, then,” Lilia retorted. “You don’t get the information of any of my other clients.”

“But she might be my actual soulmate,” Agatha said, making her voice sad and pathetic.

“Oh, well, in that case…” Lilia said.

Agatha perked up. “Really?” 

“No!”

The line went dead and Agatha let out a groan.

The chances of that one woman being her soulmate were slim, Agatha knew. She’d been with plenty of other women who had said the exact same thing to her, mainly because they’d bumped into her or done something similar that warranted an apology upon first meeting.

(People really needed to start looking where they were going.)

Still, there was just something about that woman that stuck in Agatha’s mind. Maybe it was the way her cheeks had reddened and she’d immediately looked down at her hands in her lap. Maybe it was the flicker of her deep brown eyes between Agatha’s eyes and her lips for that half a second that she actually looked at her face. Maybe it was the soft curve of her cheeks or the swoop of her dark hair over her forehead. Maybe it was the gravel in her voice…

Didn’t matter, Agatha decided, because it was unlikely that she was Agatha’s soulmate.

Agatha refused to believe that she could be so unlucky twice in one day.

Lilia had been right: the words on her wrist weren’t her only tattoo. 

Since her last one healed, Rio had been thinking about little else than going in for another. Lilia’s speciality, she said, was the soulmate tattoo, but she also had a full sketchbook of ideas. She did runes and moons and stars and other witchy designs. She was even willing to tattoo somebody else’s designs onto a body so long as said body had permission from the original artist. 

That involved quite a bit of paperwork, so, instead, Rio just decided to peruse the digital sketchbook that she found via the QR code on Lilia’s business card. Lilia was incredibly talented at her craft. Her designs were all smooth lines and sharp points and soft colors. A lot of it was incredibly witchy, but some of it was just…hard to describe.

Rio ended up falling in love with a skull piece that had flowers popping out of the eye holes and surrounding the jaw, as if it was lying in the middle of a garden. It was intended as a full color piece, but Lilia had also uploaded a version that was just line art. 

Rio decided to go with the line art for a start, then maybe she’d consider having it colored in later on. She liked how it looked in black and white.

She made the appointment about a month after getting her soulmate tattoo. She still hadn’t run into the woman she was now certain was her soulmate, but she was still doing what she’d told Alice she’d do: she was trusting the universe. 

The universe was moving slow as fuck, though.

“I can get you in…six weeks from today,” Lilia had told Rio over the phone when she called to make the appointment.

“Six weeks?!” Rio exclaimed. “It only took two weeks to wait for my last appointment!”

“Yeah, well,” Lilia sighed, “I don’t know what to tell you, kiddo. Business has really picked up. People are getting desperate to find their soulmates. I had to pencil myself in some days off, as well. My old wrists aren’t what they used to be. All that buzzing is taking a toll. Six weeks is the best you’re gonna get. Is a Tuesday at three PM good for you?”

“I guess so,” Rio said. “Thanks, Lilia.”

“No problem, kid,” Lilia said, chipperly. “That tattoo is gonna look so good on you. I can already see it. Literally.”

Rio had snorted at that as she hung up. 

Six weeks. A Tuesday at three PM. Fine.

Rio can wait that long.

The day comes sooner than Rio was prepared for. Which was stupid, frankly. She knew she had six weeks to prepare for this tattoo. She knew that it was coming. It inched forward on her calendar every single week. Every single day. She received reminders from Lilia’s receptionist about her appointment. She received both emails and texts about it.

Yet, still, she was very nearly late to her appointment because she had gotten caught up baby-talking her gardenias – which were blooming beautifully, if anybody even cared, despite not being in their perfectly native environment. Rio’s greenhouse was filled with thriving plant life, so you’d have to excuse her for getting a little carried away caring for it all and losing track of time.

She brought a bouquet of freshly bloomed azaleas to make up for it.

“Hi,” Rio said, placing the vase of flowers on the reception desk, “I’m here for my appointment with Lilia. Sorry I’m late.”

The young man with the eyeliner at the front desk eyed the flowers, then Rio, chewing gum obnoxiously. “Name?” he asked, looking back at his computer.

“Vidal,” Rio said. “Rio Vidal.”

“Okay,” the near-child said. “You’re only, like, two minutes late. She hasn’t even finished the last person’s tattoo, so you can, like, chill over there or whatever.” He made a limp-wristed motion toward the seating area, where there were a few others sitting, waiting for their turns.

“Um, okay,” Rio said. “Do you want the flowers or…?”

The boy – whose name tag read ‘Billy’ with a little skull and crossbones painted from White-Out next to it – continued to smack his gum as he eyed the vase of flowers, then Rio, then the flowers, then looked back at his screen.

“You can leave them,” he said with a shrug, “or whatever, I guess.”

“...cool,” Rio said, leaving her beloved azaleas on the desk and shuffling over to sit down in a seat near the window. She immediately reached for her earbuds as she sat down, popping them straight into her ears and closing her eyes to allow herself a moment to relax. She pressed ‘Play’ on the Lofi playlist she’d been listening to on the drive over and took a deep breath.

Rio took more deep breaths and let her mind drift as the music filtered through her headphones. Her fingers twitched in her lap and she found herself counting them over and over again as she waited for Lilia’s voice to call out her name.

It wasn’t Lilia’s voice that broke her out of her trance, however.

“It’s you.”

Rio’s eyes opened slowly at first, then quickly when she recognized the exact shade of blue staring back down at her. Her face felt warm as her mouth dropped open. The woman above her was wearing a matching expression.

“You,” Rio gasped.

The woman’s lips turned up at the corners, one of her dark eyebrows lifting. “Me,” she confirmed.

“Rio, dear,” Lilia’s voice floated over the reception area as Rio removed her earbuds, “I’m so sorry. Agatha’s appointment ran over by a little bit. Just give me a few minutes and I’ll get you right in for yours, okay?”

“O-okay,” Rio stammered, standing up. “Take your time.”

Lilia gave her a knowing grin as she fluttered back into her studio.

Agatha, her brain said as she glanced at the blue-eyed woman. Agatha, Agatha, Agatha.

Agatha had a beautiful smile, her eyes crinkling and her teeth sinking into a plush bottom lip as she offered her hand to Rio.

“It’s nice to finally meet you,” Agatha said, “for real this time.”

“L-likewise,” Rio stammered, swallowing thickly as she reached for Agatha’s hand. She felt electricity run up her arm the second their palms touched. 

She wondered if Agatha felt it, too. She watched as the other woman’s eyes flickered down to glance at her wrist, her smile growing wider when she saw the words etched into her skin.

“So,” she said, “you gonna tell me your name or…?”

“Rio,” Rio said. “Sorry. My name is Rio.”

“Agatha,” Agatha said, tilting her head a little to the side. “So, Rio, what are you doing after this?”
