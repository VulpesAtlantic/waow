Summary

From the silver lighter she left behind to the ring on her finger, Leon realizes that while they skipped the paperwork, they spent over a decade cultivating a commitment that a marriage license could never be heavy enough to hold.

The afternoon sun poured through the living room windows like honey. It caught the edge of the ring on Ada’s finger, sending a glint of light dancing across the pages of her book.

Leon watched her from the kitchen island, his coffee forgotten and cooling. Her brows were knit in that specific, concentrated furrow he’d learned to decode years ago; she was reading something in a language he doesn’t recognize, her lips moving ever so slightly as she processed the text.

It had been over ten years (13 to be exact, but who’s counting? Leon, of course.) since they tied the knot. They hadn't walked down an aisle or signed papers that a clerk would file away in some dusty government basement.

"Traditional" was a word for people who didn't have aliases or scars from Raccoon City. For them, marriage hadn't been a ceremony, it had been a gradual surrender and acceptance.

His mind drifted, the warmth of the kitchen fading into the memory of a much colder, darker room.

[2005]

The click of the lock had been nearly silent, but Leon’s subconscious was a minefield of hair- trigger instincts. He was out of the tangle of his sheets and leveled his Silver Ghost at the shadow by the window before his brain had even fully shaken off the remnants of a nightmare.

"One more step and I paint the wall with you," Leon rasped, his voice thick with sleep and adrenaline.

The shadow didn't flinch, "Damn, Agent. It’s just me. You’re getting jumpy in your old age."

Leon didn't lower the gun immediately. His chest was heaving, the phantom smell of smoke from his dreams still clinging to the back of his throat. He blinked, the moonlight finally catching the sharp bob of her hair and the curve of a smirk.

"What the fuck, Ada," he gasped out, finally thumbing the safety. He dropped his arm, the weight of the metal feeling like a ton of lead. "You don't just sneak into a federal agent’s apartment in the middle of the night and not expect to get shot. I could have killed you."

"For the record, Leon, your locks were insulting. A Girl Scout with a paperclip could have handled that deadbolt." She stepped further into the room, looking entirely too composed for someone who had just looked down the barrel of a gun.

"Figured," he muttered, rubbing a hand over his face. He watched her with a mix of exhaustion and wary fascination as she began to kick off her boots. "What are you even doing here? Last I heard, you were in the South."

"The weather was humid and the company was boring." She didn't look at him, her fingers moving to the zipper of her dress.

Leon’s eyes widened and he pivoted on his heel, facing the closet. "Ada! What are you—"

"Oh, stop being so dramatic. Don’t act like we haven’t fucked in Europe. We’re past the point of modesty." He heard the soft rustle of fabric hitting the floor. "I need a place to lay low for tonight. Somewhere no one would look. And no one looks for a shadow in the lion's den."

"Second drawer to the right," Leon sighed into the darkness of his closet. "There are some old workout shirts. They’ll probably be like a dress on you, but they’re clean."

"Charity. How charming."

He heard her moving around, the floorboards creaking under her footsteps. He waited until he heard the rustle of fabric again before turning back. She was pulling a black shirt over her head. It swallowed her, the hem reaching her mid-thighs.

"Can’t an old friend crash for the night?" she asked, walking toward the bed. Before he could protest, the mattress dipped significantly under her weight. She slid under the duvet as if she owned the square footage.

"Crash? Alright…have the bed, I’ll take the couch outside." He began to wiggle his way out from under the covers, intent on a dignified retreat.

Her hand shot out from under the blanket, gripping his wrist with surprising strength. "Don't be such a princess, Scott. Stay put."

"Ada—"

"It’s freezing in here, your heater is loud enough to wake the dead, and you’re burning up like a furnace," she mumbled, already closing her eyes and pulling the covers to her chin. "I'm tired, I'm cold, and I'm not moving. Stay put."

Leon froze. He looked at her, then at his own pillows. "F-fine," he slid back under the blankets. He made sure to stay on the absolute far edge of the mattress, facing the wall. "But stop calling me Scott. It sounds like you're talking to a high schooler."

"You’d rather be called 'Lee' instead? It has a certain...boy-band charm."

"Yeah, no. I’ll stick with Scott, thank you very much."

A beat of silence followed, filled only by the buzz of the city outside and the sound of their breathing. Leon thought she had fallen asleep until he heard her voice again.

"Leo-bear sounds nice too, don't you think? Soft and cuddly. Very you."

Leon felt a traitorous chuckle bubble up in his chest. He peeked over his shoulder. Her eyes were shut, her features softened by the moonlight. She looked almost peaceful. "Go to sleep, Ada."

When he woke up the next morning, the space beside him was meticulously smoothed over. The only sign she had been there at all was the lingering, faint scent of her perfume—jasmine and something metallic—and her silver lighter sitting squarely on his nightstand. A silent promise that it hadn't been a hallucination.

[Present Day]

“Earth to Kennedy.”

The voice snapped him back to the present. Ada was no longer across the room, she was hovering directly in his space, the book held shut with one finger acting as a bookmark. Her eyes were dancing with that familiar playfulness.

“Penny for your thoughts?” she asked, tilting her head.

Leon didn't answer with words. He reached out, hooking a hand around her waist and pulling her firmly onto his lap.

Ada let out a small yelp of surprise before melting into him, her silk robe rustling against his pajama pants. He buried his face in the crook of her neck, taking in her scent. She still smelled like jasmine, though the metallic edge of danger had long since faded into the scent of their home.

“Just thinking about the night that started all of this,” he murmured, his voice muffled against her skin. He pressed a lingering kiss just below her jawline.

Ada shifted on Leon’s lap, her fingers tracing the line of his jaw. "You know, your hands were actually trembling that night," she teased, "I was half-convinced you were going to drop the basin and run out of the room."

[2009]

Leon watched as she took off her gear one by one.

Holster first, then her shoes, her dress, and lastly, her tights.

He memorized the pattern already.

“Ada, for once... just once. Please stay until morning.”

The words were out before he could filter them. He immediately regretted the rawness and desperation in his voice. It sounded like a plea, and in their world, pleas were a weakness.

Ada’s movements paused. “You know I can’t do that, Leon. Stagnancy is a death sentence.”

“I—I know. I’m sorry. It just slipped,” Leon mumbled. He rubbed his face, leaning back against the headboard. “Forget I said anything.”

He expected the click of the latch. Instead, he heard her heels hitting the floor. Ada turned, a playful, slightly incredulous roll of her eyes catching the moonlight.

“I didn't travel all the way from a freezing tundra just to leave the moment you decided to be a Hallmark postcard, Agent. Give me some credit.”

Leon blinked, his heart was about to burst out of his ribs. “Wait. You’re... you’re staying?”

“Do you want me to leave? Because if you’d used that tone back in 2005, I probably would have bolted,” she teased.

“Hurry up and scoot over, Leon. I’m exhausted, you’re hogging all of the good pillows, and your complaining isn't helping my headache.”

When Ada slid under the covers, she gravitated towards him naturally, tucking her head into the crook of his neck. Her arm draped over his waist; her leg hooking over his, anchoring him to the spot.

Leon lay perfectly still, remembering the very first time she’d done this, months prior:

She had fallen asleep first, and her body had instinctively sought his heat. He had spent hours staring at the ceiling, heart hammering so loud he was sure it would wake her. He was terrified that if he moved a muscle, the spell would break and she’d vanish into the night.

Now, it felt like a silent language. They fit together like jagged pieces of a puzzle, finally smoothed over by time. He closed his eyes, his hand rested over hers on his chest, and allowed himself to believe that just for a few hours…this was enough...even if his heart wanted more.

The next morning, Leon’s hand instinctively swept across the right side of the bed, searching for the warmth of her shoulder. His palm met only the cold, taut fabric of the fitted sheet. Of course.

He stared at the ceiling, a self-deprecating smirk tugging at his lips.

Stupid, Kennedy. Really. You’re thirty-two and you still fall for the same disappearing act. He’d been doing this dance since '05, yet he still expected the music to change. He begrudgingly forced himself out of bed, his joints stiff, his mood sour.

Coffee. I need enough caffeine to forget I have a heart, he thought, pulling on a pair of sweatpants.

He was halfway to the kitchen when the bathroom door swung open. Leon froze mid-stride, his brain short-circuiting.

Ada emerged, squinting against the sunlight. She was rubbing her eyes with the heels of her hands, her hair a chaotic, beautiful mess. She didn't even look at him; she just beelined back toward the bed like a homing missile, his shirt fluttering around her knees. She dived under the covers, disappearing under his duvet.

“What the fu—What just happened?” Leon said to no one in particular. He remained rooted to the floor, half-convinced he was still dreaming.

A muffled voice drifted out from under the duvet. “Are you planning on having a staring contest with the door all morning, or are you coming back to bed?”

Leon crossed the room in quick strides, diving back into the bed with a lack of grace that would have appalled his tactical instructors.

“You’re still here. You stayed,” propping himself up on one elbow to look at her. Ada peeked out from under the covers, one eye opening to track his expression. “Can’t have the birthday boy spend his morning moping alone, can I?”

Leon went still. He glanced at the digital clock on the nightstand. July 31st.

“You remembered...” His voice cracked. He’d been so buried in missions and her coming and going that he’d completely forgotten his own birth date. The weight of it… the fact that she’d kept track, that she’d chosen this day to break her own rules… made his chest tighten painfully.

“Of course I did, Scott. Don't look so shocked. It’s insulting to my intelligence.” She opened both eyes now, her expression softening before opening her arms in a silent invitation.

He didn't hesitate. He collapsed into her embrace, burying his face in the hollow of her neck. His shoulders shook as the first few tears he’d allowed himself in years finally fell.

“Ada, this...you have no idea what this means,” he choked out.

She pulled back just enough to frame his face, her thumbs wiping away the moisture. “Leon, look at me. I’m here. I’m right here.”

The conversation that followed was quiet, stripped of their usual armor, leading to a kiss that tasted like a beginning rather than an ending.

Later, as the shadows of the afternoon began to stretch across the floor, Leon lay tangled in the sheets, tracing lazy circles on Ada’s bare shoulder. She was tracing the faint, crimson marks she painted all over his chest, her touch light and lingering.

“You never told me yours, though... Your birthday.”

“You’ll know it when I want you to know it,” she replied, her voice back to its playful hum.

“Always the mystery. Don’t you ever get tired of being an enigma?”

“It keeps you hooked, doesn’t it, Agent?” She tried to shift her weight, she hissed as her legs tensed. “Remind me to never let you do that to me again. You’re a menace.”

“Not a chance, Ada.” He leaned over, pulling her up for a deep, slow kiss. “I’ve been waiting since 1998 for that. I wasn't going to hold back.”

[Present Day]

On anyone else, they might look messy and tacky. On Ada? They looked like high-end accessories. He marveled at how she seemed to defy time, growing more beautiful with every passing year. He felt a surge of gratitude—a constant reminder of how lucky he was to be the man who got to see her like this.

His fingers brushed her skin, and for a moment, the cloth became a bridge to the past. [2011]

The soft morning rays filtered through the half-drawn blinds. Ada shifted against him, her lips tracing a slow path from the hollow of his neck to the rougher skin of his jawline. She pressed a lingering kiss there, "Good morning, handsome."

His hand trembled slightly as he reached out, his thumb grazing the smooth fabric of the strap before resting on her shoulder. "It’s... you look incredible, Ada. You look like the woman I’d want to take to a place where nobody knows our names."

“I was just remembering the first time you bought this dress,” Leon admitted, resting his chin on her shoulder. “And the look on your face when I started crying in the middle of a high-end boutique... and in front of our closet.”

"I remembered." She let out a chuckle before turning in his arms, her eyes sparkling. “As for the dress, how do I look in it now?”

“You are more beautiful now than the first time you wore it, honey. The dress is just trying to keep up with you.”

Ada gave him a smile that made her eyes crinkle at the corners—a look only he ever got to see. “You just want me to pay the bill at dinner, don’t you?” she teased, reaching up to straighten his tie, pressing a kiss to his cheek. “Thank you, darling. Now, let’s go. We’re going to be late for our reservation."

They arrived in a small, historic bistro tucked away in a corner of the city where the cobblestones whispered of old secrets—the kind of place that valued discretion above all else.

“A reservation for Mr. and Mrs. Kennedy?” the receptionist asked with a knowing, polite smile.

Leon nodded, his hand resting firmly at the small of Ada’s back. He led her to a table positioned perfectly by a bay window, offering a view of the city lights reflecting off the river. Ada looked around, her eyebrows arching in amusement as she realized the usual bustle of the bistro was absent. The linen-draped tables were empty, the candles flickering only for them.

“You booked the entire restaurant, Leon?” she remarked as he pulled out her chair. “A bit dramatic, don't you think? Or are you just afraid someone will overhear me laughing at your awful jokes?”

“I’m a federal agent, Ada. I value my privacy and my talents for stand-up comedy,” Leon countered with a wink, taking his seat opposite her. “Besides, I didn't want to share the view with anyone else tonight.”

A young waiter, looking remarkably nervous, approached the table carrying a massive, overflowing bouquet of deep red roses and carnations. He cleared his throat, glancing at a small card tucked into the blooms.

“A bouquet requested by Mr. Kennedy for his...” He paused, his face turning a shade of scarlet that rivaled the roses. “For his... ‘Honey Bunch Sugar Plum’?”

The waiter looked like he wanted the floor to swallow him whole before excusing himself. Leon let out a loud, unapologetic chuckle, while Ada laughed wholeheartedly, a genuine, melodic sound that spread across the room.

“Honey bunch sugar plum?” she repeated, taking the flowers and inhaling their scent. “You’re lucky these are beautiful, Leo-bear, or I’d have to reconsider the marriage on the grounds of criminal cheesiness.”

"You won't." He stood up and planted a kiss on her cheek, “Happiest birthday, my love."

Ada’s smile turned tender, the playful light in her eyes giving way to something deep and grounded. “You’re getting way too good at this, hun. I’m starting to suspect you’ve been taking notes.

"Hey! For your information, I am an attentive husband. Always have, always will. Thank you very much," he playfully rolled his eyes. He has gotten sassier over the years, Ada noted.

"Yeah, yeah. Whatever," she shot back as she set the flowers down. The candlelight caught the glint of the ring on her hand. Leon’s gaze lingered there, his mind immediately pulled back through the fog of years to the night that band first found its home.

[2013]

For months, Leon’s apartment had been a tomb. Since the reports from Lanshiang had crossed his desk—the whispers of a woman in red falling to her death, not the clone. He had existed in a state of detachment. He moved, breathed, and worked, but the light behind his eyes had gone out.

Then, in the dead of a Monday night, he heard it. The soft snick of the window latch.

Leon launched himself toward the shadow by the glass, his movement fueled by a desperate, terrifying hope. He collided with her, his arms wrapping around her with a strength that bordered on frantic, as if he could physically tether her soul back to the earth.

“You’re alive. Heavens, Ada. You’re here,” he gasped, his voice cracking as he buried his face in the crook of her neck. He was shaking so violently his teeth rattled. “God, Ada... you’re warm. You’re real.”

“I’m real, Leon,” her own arms locking around his waist, her fingers digging into his back as if she were trying to fuse their heartbeats together. Her voice, usually so smooth and untouchable, was fractured. “I had to stay dead. I had to scrub every digital footprint, every shadow of a lead... I’m sorry it took so long. I’m so sorry.”

They stayed like that for an eternity, two survivors of a world that had tried to bury them both, clinging to each other in the dark.

When the tremors finally slowed, they retreated to the sofa. Leon didn't let her go. He kept one hand firmly on her wrist, terrified that if he broke contact, she’d dissolve into smoke.

“Stay right here," he retreated to his… no, their bedroom, returning with a small, velvet- covered box. His hands were still trembling, but his eyes were clear—clearer than they’d been in years.

“I had this grand plan,” he started, a huff escaping him. “A villa in Italy, or maybe that spot in the Mediterranean you mentioned once. But after China... after standing on that roof, still confused if the woman who sent me that text was you or a clone, I realized I don’t care about the 'where.' I just care that I’m not spending another second in this world without you knowing exactly who you are to me.”

He snapped the box open. Ada’s eyes widened.

Inside was a bypass wrap ring. Two shimmering, stone-encrusted butterflies met at the center, their wings intertwined. The stones were a deep, mesmerizing tanzanite—a violet-blue that sat perfectly between the red she had worn and the blue he had donned the night Raccoon City burned.

“Leon...”

“I’m not asking you to change, Ada,” he said, his voice dropping into that low, steady register of absolute conviction. “I’m not looking for a wife who waits by the window or a name I can file in a government ledger. I know you’re a ghost. I know you have a thousand lives I’ll never see. I’m just asking to be the one place you always come back to. I want to be the person who knows the woman the rest of the world isn't allowed to see.”

He took a shaky breath, his thumb tracing the line of her jaw. “I know the risk. I know that being linked to me makes you a target, and being linked to you makes me a liability. I don’t care.”

Ada looked at the ring, then back at him, her eyes brimming with a sudden, devastating moisture. She tried to pull her hand back, a choked sob escaping her. “Leon, you can’t. You have a life. You have a reputation. I’m a professional shadow... I’m a danger to everything you’ve built. You’re choosing a target on your back.”

“Let them aim,” Leon countered fiercely. He gripped her hand, preventing her from retreating. “I’ve faced biological gods, Ada. I’ve survived the end of the world more times than I can count. None of it—none of it—is as terrifying as the silence of this apartment without you in it. I’m not choosing a liability. I’m choosing my life. I’m choosing you... I hope you choose me too.”

Ada broke. She lunged forward, her forehead crashing against his shoulder as she wept, the sound raw and unburdened. It was the sound of a woman who had finally been found.

“Yes,” she choked out against his skin. “Yes, Leon. I’ll stay. I'm staying.”

He slipped the ring onto her finger, the tanzanite butterflies finally coming to rest. He pressed a long, lingering kiss to her forehead, holding her until her sobbing died down.

“Happy birthday, Ada. September 30th, right?"

Ada froze. The air in the room seemed to vanish. She pulled back, her eyes searching his with a sudden, sharp wariness—the look of a woman who had just realized her most secure vault had a leak.

“How?”, her voice tight. “There isn't a file that has that date. I made sure of it. Even the Agency thinks I was born in December.”

Leon gave her a small, tired smirk. He reached out, running his thumb over the back of her hand. “You’re right. The files are all decoys. But you’ve been telling me for years, Ada. You just didn't use words.”

He stood up and walked to the small safe in the corner of the room, returning with a handful of small, seemingly random items. He laid them out on the coffee table like evidence.

“The silver lighter you left on my nightstand in 2005,” he said, pointing to the first item. “The vintage cork from that expensive Bordeaux we drank in 2007. A spent shell casing from that rooftop in 2009. And this year... a receipt for a hotel in a city you weren't even assigned to.”

Ada looked at the items, her brow knitting in confusion. “I don’t see a pattern, Leon. Those are just... leftovers.”

“Look at the dates, Ada. Look at the timestamps,” Leon’s voice was soft, overflowing with a decade’s worth of observation. “For eight years, you’ve appeared on my doorstep within forty-eight hours of September 30th. Every. Single. Time. You’d tell me it was the anniversary of Raccoon City... that we were just 'celebrating' surviving the hellfire. And for a long time, I believed you.”

He leaned in closer, his eyes locking onto hers.

“But then I noticed that on those nights, you don’t act like a survivor of a tragedy. You act like someone who wants to be seen. You wear the perfume I like. You stay an extra hour. You let your guard down just enough to let me in. You weren't mourning a city, Ada. You were spending the only day that actually belongs to you with the only person who doesn't want anything from you but your time.”

Ada stared at the items, the realization hitting her with the force of a physical blow. She had been careful with the world, but she had been subconsciously honest with him.

“I realized eventually,” Leon continued, his voice thick with emotion, “that you were marking your own time. You were choosing to be born again, every year, in this room. With me.”

Leon reached into his pocket and pulled out a second ring—a simple platinum band. He slipped it onto his own finger. “I had this made to match. Look closer.”

Her eyes scanned the ring as she took his hand. The band featured a subtle, laser-etched design: a butterfly and a bear, their wings and paws entangled in a delicate knot. Hidden on the inside of both bands were their initials, engraved so small they were almost invisible to the naked eye.

“Now,” Leon pulled her back into his arms, his chin resting on top of her head. “Raccoon City doesn't get to own this day anymore,” Leon whispered, “We do. You do.”

[Present Day]

“You really do have excellent taste in accessories, hun,” she reached across the table, her fingers tracing the design on his ring before lacing her hand through his.

Leon lifted her hand to his lips, pressing a lingering kiss to her palm before resting his cheek against it.

“It wasn't about the taste, Ada. It was about the anchor,” he said softly.

They sat in silence for a moment, the weight of their shared history filling the room. For decades, September 30th had been a day of trauma which marked the end of their innocence. But through his stubbornness and her gradual surrender, they had reclaimed it. It was no longer the anniversary of a tragedy; it was the anniversary of the day the world gave him the woman across the table and he wholeheartedly accepted the offer.

“You know,” Ada murmured, her eyes reflecting the candlelight. “I spent a long time thinking I was better off alone. That love was a weakness people like us couldn't afford.”

“And now?”

Ada squeezed his hand, her ring catching the light one last time. “Now I think that having someone to come home to is the only thing that makes the rest of the world bearable. Thank you for not giving up on me, Leon. Even when I gave you every reason to.”

Leon smiled, a deep, contented expression. “I’m a government agent, Ada. I’m trained to see things through to the end. I'm stubborn like that.”

She raised her glass, the red wine shimmering like the gems on her finger. “To the man who waited.”

He raised his, his gaze unwavering. “To the woman who stayed.”
