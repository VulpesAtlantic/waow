Story Time

Go to college, they said. It will be some of the best years of your life, they said.

No one mentioned the fact that in his first year, Billy would have a professor that would get so side-tracked that it would be up to the students to keep her on topic (and they were only successful about half the time). They also didn’t mention that he’d have another professor that actually never got off topic or took a pause. Billy was convinced she didn’t even stop to breathe. That was just how fast her lectures went.

If he could, Billy would talk to whoever let Professors Vidal and Harkness teach the introductory courses for Biology and History and beg them to stop this insanity. 

“You’re so dramatic,” Kate told him with a roll of her eyes. “Vidal’s class is a breeze if you go through her PowerPoints and stop by her office hours, and Harkness’s is easy if you just take notes.”

“How am I supposed to take notes when she talks that fast and only lets us handwrite?” Billy complained. 

“Write faster,” Kamala said matter-of-factly. “Now, come on. Keep dragging your feet like that, and we’re gonna be late to Biology.”

Despite the peril the class was putting his GPA in, Billy had to admit that he enjoyed Rio’s lectures. Mainly because they actually had nothing to do with biology at all. Most of the time, it was just Rio talking about her wife.

“Okay, story time,” Rio said, just as she did every time. “Who here knows not to put foil in the microwave?” Everyone raised their hands, but she just grinned and said, “Come on, guys. Be honest. I know some of you idiots didn’t know that.” After that, a few reluctant hands lowered. “Excellent,” Rio said. “You’ll be happy to know that, along with you, my wonderful wife also did not know to put foil in the microwave.”

Laughter rippled through the auditorium as Rio nodded. “Let it be known, ladies and gentlemen and everyone in between,” Rio said solemnly. “You could be married for twenty years and still learn something new about someone.” Then, she launched into her story about how she brought home leftovers, conveniently wrapped in aluminum foil. Apparently, while she’d been in the shower, her wife had come home and tried heating it up for them. In the microwave.

“So, there I am, completely naked,” Rio told them dramatically. “I shut off the microwave, and we just stand there and watch the flames go out. After it’s all done, my wife looks at me like I’m the crazy one and goes, ‘Why are you naked in our kitchen? That’s so unsanitary’.” 

The entire class was laughing so hard that they were practically in tears at this point, Billy included. 

After hearing stories like this, Billy couldn’t help but imagine what Rio’s wife must be like. Her wife was apparently incredibly stubborn, like to her own detriment kind of stubborn. Billy hadn’t forgotten the story about how she’d nearly thrown up trying to prove that she could handle spice better than Rio. Her wife was also incredibly intelligent. Rio waxed poetics about how smart she thought her wife was. Her wife was also just as in love with Rio as Rio was with her. 

“Look at this!” Rio had exclaimed before she’d given them a quiz. In her hands was the latest Lego set of succulents. “My wife surprised me with this because she knew I’d be sitting here all day watching you losers take a quiz.” Rio’s smile had been positively dreamy as she said, “She knows me so well.”

Billy hoped he had a relationship like that one day. It seemed nearly impossible though. He could barely work up the courage to talk to his crush right now. Still, Rio and her wife gave him a little bit of hope.

Class ended with the only biology being mentioned as their next reading assignment, something Rio threw out at the end like an absent-minded thought and not the whole point of the course.

“I wish we had History first,” Kamala sighed as they walked to their next class. “It would be way more fun to be able to listen to Vidal as a brain break after Harkness bludgeons us over the head with historical facts for an hour and a half.”

“That’s something the advisors should warn freshmen about,” Billy said. “Actually, they should just warn the freshmen against Harkness in general.”

“Come on, guys,” Kate laughed. “Harkness is not that bad.”

Kamala gave her a look and said, “You’re just saying that because she’s hot.”

“Me?” Kate gasped. “I am a happily taken woman, and so is she.”

Both Kamala and Billy stared blankly at her. After a moment, Billy asked, “Harkness is taken?”

“Harkness literally has a ginormous diamond ring on her finger,” Kate told them. “Like, borderline ostentatious but just enough to still be tasteful.” She shook her head, laughing at them. “Look for it during class. It’s hard to miss.”

For both Biology and History, the three of them sat towards the middle. Not so close for their professors to actually perceive them all the time but also not far enough out that their professors (Harkness more than Vidal) would call on them as punishment for sitting in the back.

Initially, Billy wasn’t sure how Kate could have possibly seen a ring from this far back. That was until he actually saw it, and wow, she wasn’t kidding about the ring being huge. Billy wondered what the hell Harkness’s partner did to be able to afford that. He didn’t know much about rings, but he knew if a diamond was that big then it had to be a lot of money.

While Biology had been relaxed, History was always tense and dead silent except for the scratch of pen on paper and the sound of Agatha’s voice. Today, she was explaining the sociopolitical implications of the Salem witch trials and how such verbiage could still apply to their current day. It was honestly pretty interesting, as were most of her lectures, but it was the fact that she went so fast that made Billy’s head spin and hurt. 

At the end of class, when everyone began packing up, Kate politely asked, “Any plans for this week, Professor Harkness?”

Without hesitation, Agatha eyed Kate coolly and replied, “And that’s your business, how, Ms. Bishop?”

Billy and Kamala cringed, ducking their heads in hopes of Agatha not noticing them near Kate. “Cool, let me just kill myself,” Kate muttered under her breath, making Billy and Kamala cover their mouths to stifle their laughter. 

As soon as they left the auditorium, Billy elbowed Kate and asked, “Why the hell would you ever ask that?”

“I don’t know!” Kate exclaimed. “I guess I thought maybe she was all closed off and cold because no one bothered to ask her.”

Kamala slung an arm around Kate’s shoulder and sighed, “Oh, Kate, you’re too kind for this cruel world.”

“Shut up,” Kate laughed, pushing Kamala off of her. “I guess I was hoping Harkness would magically pull a Vidal and start talking.”

“Yeah, right,” Billy snorted. “Harkness would never. I bet she’d hate Vidal too, if she ever met her. That chaotic and messy and unprofessional? Harkness would eat her alive.”

“I think she’d like that,” Kamala said with a tilt of her head. “I mean, if she wasn't happily married.”

“Gross,” Kate said, wrinkling her nose.

“Well, let’s just hope they never meet,” Billy said. “Who knows how that would turn out.”

. . .

The semester went on, and slowly and painfully, Billy got used to his workload and professors. 

While he would never say History was easy now, it had certainly become a lot more manageable, and Biology continued to be a highlight of his week.

Rio leaned against her desk, hands loosely curled around the edge. “Okay, story time! It’s my anniversary in a week, and I need your help with a gift for my wife,” Rio proclaimed. Immediately, a few students, including Billy, gave her unimpressed looks. She noticed and shook her head, saying, “Don’t give me that look. I’m not some stupid husband who doesn’t know what to get.”

“We’ve been married twenty years,” Rio explained as she pushed off the desk to pace in front of the seats. “That’s a big deal, you know, so I bought her something for every year we’ve been together, but the last gift needs to be something good.”

Kamala called out, asking the question going through everyone’s minds, “You bought her twenty gifts?”

“Nineteen actually, but you’re going to help me choose the twentieth,” Rio answered with a toothy grin.

“God, how do I find a woman like that?” Kamala mumbled. She nudged Kate. “Hey, if Yelena ever does anything less than that for you, dump her.”

Kate rolled her eyes, but she still whispered back, “Oh, she’s definitely hearing about this.”

Their attention snapped back to the front as Rio went to her laptop and messed with the projector. A moment later, two necklaces popped up on the screen. On the left was a gold necklace with two small rings, one encrusted with diamonds and the other almost looking like there were screws. They were interconnected, forming a heart. On the right was a single gold ring, flat with fewer, larger diamonds embedded.

Billy’s eyebrows furrowed a moment before he realized why those necklaces looked familiar. “Those are Cartier necklaces,” Billy hissed to his friends. He pulled up his phone, searching up the brand.

“So, expensive?” Kamala guessed. “What, like five hundred?”

“Try five grand.”

Kate and Kamala gasped in unison, leaning in on either side of him to look at the website. There was no denying it. The necklaces on the projector were the ones on the Cartier website.

“I didn’t know professors made that much,” Kate said.

“I don’t think they do,” Kamala said. “Maybe Vidal has a side hustle.”

“Like what?” Billy asked.

Kamala shrugged, “I don’t know. Selling her feet or something?”

Billy and Kate couldn’t do much more than give Kamala a look of that screamed I-don’t-want-to-think-about-my professor-that-way before Rio clapped her hands together to get their attention again.

“Okay, voting time,” Rio said. “I’m kind of shit with jewelry, so I’m trusting your judgement, but if my wife ends up hating it, you’ll all fail for the semester.” Everyone startled, and Rio barked out a laugh. “Kidding, kidding.” But then she tilted her head and smiled, and suddenly, every student in the room got the feeling she was not kidding.

After a very official round of voting via raising their hands, it was decided that Rio would buy the necklace on the left with the two rings. “Aw, the one that forms a heart,” Rio said. “Aren’t you all just romantics?”

“Get her flowers too!” Kamala called out.

Rio winked, “Well, of course. And on that note, plants! Let’s talk plants.”

That was how Rio managed to talk about photosynthesis for a whole thirty minutes before she devolved into a story about how she used to give her wife plants when they were dating. “As gorgeous and brilliant as she is, my wife cannot keep a plant alive to save her life. I used to swap out the dead ones, you know? Just so she wouldn’t know she was killing them all.”

A student asked, “Did she actually care?”

Rio smiled, one so full of tender affection that the class as a collective swooned for a moment. “Yeah, more than she ever wants you to think,” she said. 

The remainder of class was spent bouncing between how different plants adapt to varying amounts of sunlights in their environments and Rio’s random suggestions of new plants she could buy for her home. It was one of her more confusing lectures, and the necklace was promptly pushed to the back of Billy’s mind.

He didn’t think about it again until the following week when he went to History. Sliding into his seat, he glanced at Agatha preparing her slides, only to do a double take. 

At first, there was nothing strange about her. Agatha looked the same as she always did. Slacks, button-down, glare fierce enough to kill a puppy. The thing was that Agatha always wore the same necklace. It was an older one, always tucked underneath her shirt, but the chain was always visible, an older, antique-looking gold. That was what made it all the more noticeable that she was wearing something new. 

Something familiar. Something just like the five-grand Cartier necklace Rio showed the class as an option to buy her wife.

Billy choked on his spit and nearly hacked up a lung trying to process what he’d just seen. 

“Guys,” Billy whispered. “Look at Professor Harkness’s necklace. What does that look like?”

Kamala and Kate looked up in unison, and Billy watched as their jaws dropped in recognition.

“I’m not crazy, right?” Billy said. “That’s the same necklace.”

“Say something,” Kamala whispered to him.

“Me?” Billy shook his head. “No way!”

Kate elbowed him. “You’re the favorite.”

“What? No, I’m not! Why would you– Professor Harkness!” Billy’s voice shot up a few octaves as their professor walked by their desks with papers in her hands. A quiz. Great. It was too late to back out now though, especially with Agatha staring at him expectantly. “That’s…” Billy fumbled for a moment. “That’s a nice necklace, Professor Harkness.”

Agatha raised an eyebrow and simply said, “Thank you.”

Billy brightened at the lack of bite in response and asked, “Where’d you get it from?”

“A store,” Agatha answered, and just like that, any semblance of conversation was shut down. “Have something to write with. Let’s see what you bothered to remember from the last lecture.”

After a, quite frankly, traumatizing quiz that he was sure was going to tank his grade, Billy trudged over to the library with Kate and Kamala.

“So, do we think it’s just a coincidence?” Kate asked. “Like, Harkness just happens to have the same exact necklace that Vidal picked out for her wife?”

“Maybe,” Billy said. “But do you really think those two are married?” He couldn’t imagine it with Rio’s easy-going, oversharing ways compared to Agatha’s hardass, private demeanor. Not to mention, the most fun Harkness ever seemed to have was in finding new ways to torture them with her course content. 

“Opposites attract,” Kamala shrugged. “Plus, they’re both hot.”

Kate nodded at this while Billy rolled his eyes. “That is not a good argument,” he said.

“All I’m saying is that I wouldn’t be surprised anymore.”

 . . .

Billy had seen a lot in his time in Rio’s course, but in early December, just before finals, Rio walking in with a literal child in tow was not something he could have ever predicted. This was why he was losing the bingo game they’d created for this class.

“That’s another square for me,” Kate crowed. “Professor Vidal comes in with a child, check.”

Beside her, Kamala groaned, “How the hell did she come in with a kid before an animal? I was sure she’d come in with an animal at some point.”

“Just one more square and I get bingo,” Kate said. “I’m picking something good for the dinner the two of you owe me.”

Billy shook his head and looked toward the front of the room where Rio was helping the little boy into the chair behind the desk. He looked maybe four, but more importantly, he was the spitting image of Rio. They had the same brown eyes, the same pointed chin, even the same smile. 

“Everyone, this is Nicky,” Rio announced. “Nicky, say hi.” The little boy looked up and waved, seemingly unperturbed to be sitting in front of a lecture hall full of college students. “Sorry about this, guys. His babysitter is sick, and we didn’t have enough time to find someone else to watch him.”

“He’s adorable,” a student called out.

Another student added, “He looks just like you.”

“He does, doesn’t he?” Rio said proudly. “Don’t say that in front of my wife though. It pisses her off to no end that she carried him, but he came out looking just like me.”

“Isn’t that some biological imperative?” Kate joked. “The baby comes out looking like the father?”

Rio laughed loudly at this and pointed at Kate, saying, “Now, maybe you’re onto something.”

As Rio went to her laptop to pull something up, Kamala leaned over to them and asked, “Did you know Professor Vidal had a son?”

Billy shook his head, “No, did you?”

“No!” Kamala exclaimed quietly. “All she ever does is talk about her wife, and yet she never let it slip that she has a son?”

“Maybe she likes to keep him private,” Kate said. “He’s still really young.”

“Maybe,” Kamala said. She propped her chin on her hand. “God, I wonder what his other mom looks like.”

“Do you need a reminder?” Billy teased. “We’ll see her in about two hours.”

Kamala snorted and sat back in her seat. 

Billy wasn’t sure if it was because her son was there, but Rio was weirdly focused today. It was nice though, especially since she was reviewing everything that would be on their final. (All multiple-choice, thank goodness. Agatha’s would be all open-ended because, Billy was convinced, she fed off their tears.)

Toward the end of class, one of the doors creaked open. No one reacted, too focused on Rio, until Nicky jumped up from his chair and shouted, “Mama!”

Everyone turned around, only to freeze when they saw Agatha standing at the door. She paid them no mind, holding her arms out to the little boy running straight toward her. If there’d been any doubt about who Rio’s wife was, it was gone now. 

Although Billy had more or less considered the thought they were married, it was another thing to actually see it in front of his very eyes.

Nicky leapt into her arms, and Agatha caught him with practiced ease. She settled him onto her hip and brushed his hair from his face. “Hello, sweetheart,” Agatha said, and everyone who was her student startled at her tone. It was nothing like the dry, sardonic voice she used on them. Instead, it was gentle and sweet. It was like she was a completely different person.

As Agatha walked toward the front of the class with Nicky, Rio greeted her brightly, saying, “Hey, love.”

“Rio,” Agatha said, lightly chastising, as she glanced at all of them. “You said your class ended at ten-thirty.”

“Did I?” Rio replied. She immediately wrapped an arm around Agatha’s waist, pulling her in and kissing her cheek. Billy watched in amazement as Agatha’s cheeks turned bright pink. She leaned in, whispering something in Rio’s ear. Whatever she said made Rio blink innocently at all of them and ask, “You didn’t know we’re married?”

There was an awkward silence until Kate answered, “Some of us suspected, but… Well…” She clamped her mouth shut. Not that Billy could blame her. He wouldn’t want to admit that they hadn’t been sure the two of them would be compatible.

“I talk about my wife all the time though,” Rio protested.

“Yeah, and that’s all you call her,” Kamala pointed out. "Your wife."

Rio’s eyebrows furrowed. “You really couldn’t tell it was Agatha?” An elbow from her wife made her wince and correct herself, “I mean, Professor Harkness.”

“What stories were you even telling them?” Agatha asked, just loud enough for them to hear.

At this, Rio started to squirm. “Oh, you know,” she said nervously. “Just stories.”

Agatha’s eyes narrowed. She set down their son and said to him, “Honey, go over there and plug your ears until mama tells you it's okay to listen. I promise we’ll get a treat later.”

“Okay!” Nicky all but skipped to the corner of the room and pressed his hands over his ears. He blinked wide, brown eyes at all of them. He didn’t seem fazed at all. That only made Billy more afraid.

Before any of them could react, or duck for cover, Agatha was on them. “You,” she all but snarled, pointing at Billy. “What has my wife been telling you?”

Billy glanced at Rio, gulping. Rio shook her head vehemently, but Agatha snapped her fingers, and Billy’s eyes went back to hers. Her icy blue eyes bore into him like knives, and Billy winced. He sent a silent apology to Rio, but he was not going to die or let Agatha fail him for this. 

“You put foil in the microwave,” Billy blurted out, and Rio immediately groaned and buried her face in her hands. “And you bought her Legos and she bought you plants that you killed and–”

“Stop.”

Billy’s mouth shut with an audible clack.

“Rio,” Agatha said slowly, voice like poisoned honey. Sweet yet dangerous. “Why the hell have you been telling your students all of this?” Then she paused, eyebrows scrunching together. “And what did he mean by the plants I killed?”

“Forget the plants,” Rio said quickly. She wrapped her arm back around Agatha again and said, clearly putting every ounce of charm into her words, “I was just trying to keep them engaged. Not all of us are as talented lecturers as you, right?” Rio turned to them, eyes wide with warning. “Right, guys?”

All of the students rushed to nod and agree, and Billy caught Agatha’s lips twitching into a smile.

“You’re not nearly as clever as you think you are, Vidal,” Agatha said.

“But aren’t I cute?” Rio replied with a grin.

“You’re something,” Agatha muttered. She raised her voice as she said, “Nicky! Time to go.” The boy beamed and ran over to her. Billy wondered just how much of that he heard. Considering he was Rio and Agatha’s son, Billy assumed he heard everything, hands be damned. 

Agatha held Nicky’s hand and scanned the auditorium. Her eyes landed on Billy and his friends. “Class is cancelled, for those who have me at eleven-thirty,” she said. “For your final, be prepared for fifteen short-answer questions, ranging from chapters one through twenty in the textbook.”

Rio let out a low whistle and said, “You never give them a chance to breathe, do you?”

“No,” Agatha replied with a smug smile.

Rio grinned and leaned forward, kissing her cheek once more. “And I love that about you.”

For the second time, would wonders never cease, Billy watched Agatha blush. “Come on, Nicky,” Agatha said. 

“Bye, mami,” Nicky said to Rio before turning to the class and adding sweetly, “Bye, everyone!”

Billy and the other students said goodbye and watched as Agatha and Nicky left. As soon as the door shut behind them, their eyes all darted to Rio, who leaned against the desk with her arms folded over her chest. She stared at them for a whole five, terrifying, seconds before she broke into a grin.

“Well, now that you know,” Rio said with a shrug, “who wants to hear about how Agatha burned toast for me on my birthday?”

Every hand shot into the air.

“Great! Story time.”
