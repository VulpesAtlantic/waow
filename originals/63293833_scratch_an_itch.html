Scratch an itch

There isn’t a worse time for all hell to break loose at your workplace than five minutes to seven on a Friday night.

And yet, that seems to be Sharon Davis’ fate when she finds herself in a heated discussion with a new whirlwind of a client who has just stormed in, soaking wet from the rain.

“Ma’am, I’m going to ask you one more time: please put the animal in a carrier,” she tells the upset brunette with a trembling voice. The woman in front of the reception desk at Vidal Veterinary Clinic is clutching onto a white and beige ball of fluff for dear life.

“There are no other animals around!” the woman screeches, hyperventilating while rocking the animal in her arms. “And he’s dying, what is he gonna do?! I’m not letting him out of my sight! Please, let him see a specialist!”

“I’m afraid I can’t sign up new clients at the moment, I’d advise you to find an emergency cl–”

“Did you not hear me?! He is dying! Let me get him to the vet! He won’t make it to another hospital!”
Two large tears dribble down her cheeks, stain her face with black streaks of mascara, and eventually cling to either side of her chin.

That's when the door to the treatment room opens with a creak, and a woman with big, worrying eyes, shoulder length black hair, and a big green puffer coat zipped halfway up, tentatively steps out.

“Sharon? What’s going on here?”

“Rio! We have a very upset lady here, who is desperate to be seen. I’ve already told her that you’re about to go home for the weekend–”

The upset woman eyes Rio up, and furrows her brows at her young and casual appearance.

“Mrs…?” Rio inquires.

“Harkness. And it’s Miss. But Agatha is fine. This is Señor Scratchy, and he–”

“Miss Harkness,” Rio smiles politely. “I’m going to have to ask you to visit the emergency–”

“Please!” The woman interrupts her, apparently startling herself with her desperate plea, as she quickly hides her face in the fur of the small animal in her arms. “Please… let him live.”

Rio quietly takes her in while the woman’s breathing hitches as she sobs. Her eyes are red and puffy, her wet hair clings to her face, and her pet is softly shaking against her chest.
And oh, she’s only human—

“I can’t promise anything,” Rio sighs. “But I can offer some of my time. What animal are we dealing with here?”

“He’s a rabbit, and he—”

“Sharon, sign Miss Harkness and her rabbit up, please. Then go home. I’ll lock up.”

Sharon exhales dramatically, and decides to keep her commentary to herself. The sooner she can leave, the better. 

 

 

Agatha treads on Rio’s heels back into the treatment room, frantically petting Señor Scratchy between his ears with her thumb.
Her eyes follow Rio's every movement as she flicks the big light back on, fully unzips her coat, and rips two fresh gloves from a cardboard box.

“Miss—”

“Rio,” the vet corrects her, while she presses a button for a large, iron table to emerge from the floor.

“Rio,” Agatha nods, as she gently places the trembling rabbit on the table when it has stopped ascending at waist height. “I don’t know what I’d do without him, he’s…”

Her words get stuck in her throat as she watches her tiny pet, usually so energetic but now void of any vitality, roll up into himself under the bright fluorescent spotlight.

Rio reaches out to him with a gloved hand and lightly strokes his back. “Any idea what could be wrong with him?” 

Agatha crosses her arms. Rio had looked too young to be a competent veterinarian, and now she’s proving her suspicions right.

“Aren’t you supposed to find that out?” she says impatiently. “Can you call the real vet? I’m not sure an assistant is what Señor Scratchy needs right now.”

“The real vet, huh?” Rio absently huffs while she lifts one of the rabbit’s ears to get a look at his face. She prods his nose and gets a good lock at his eyes by carefully prying them open. Then she turns to the row of pegs on the wall to take a lab coat, and puts it on.

“Yeah, can you call him?”

“If I knew you were a misogynist, I would’ve thought twice about helping you.” Rio turns back around and grabs two things from the pocket of the lab coat: a stethoscope, and an ID badge attached to a lanyard. 

She puts one ear piece of the stethoscope in, and carelessly throws the lanyard towards Agatha.

If Agatha’s face hadn’t already been red from the cold and from crying, her embarrassment when she looks at the badge would be more prominent. Still, she feels her ears burn when she reads Dr. Rio Vidal, DVM on her ID. 

“Well— seems like I made a false assumption, doctor,” she mumbles in an attempt at a half-assed apology.

“Rio,” the vet corrects her before she warms the chest piece of the stethoscope up in her hands. “Doctor makes me feel like a human doctor, which I, fortunately, am not. And besides,” she turns her wrist to look at her watch, “I’m off the clock.”

An overwhelming urge to protect her pet washes over her when Rio presses the stethoscope to a quivering Señor Scratchy. She has no choice but to trust Rio now.

“How old is he?”

“Six, and eight-and-a-half-months,” Agatha answers without hesitation. “I’ve had him since he was a little baby bunny.”

Rio blinks at her, and tries to hide a smile. “And since when is he like this?”

“It started just this afternoon. It looks like he’s in pain. He hasn’t eaten, doesn’t drink…”

“Well, Miss Harkness–”

“Agatha.”

“Agatha, it’s too early to know what’s going on, but he does indeed seem very dehydrated. How about we keep him on IV fluids overnight, and we’ll put him through some tests over the weekend?”

“What? Can’t you do something now?”

Rio takes the stethoscope out of her ear and puts it back into her coat pocket. “If it helps— he’s not in mortal danger. His lack of water intake does seem like his biggest threat at the moment. So if I let my night nurse take care of that, we’ll have a better picture of what’s going on in the morning.”

Agatha frowns— she’s not happy about this. She looks down at Scratchy and reaches out to him, scooping him towards her with both hands. A stray sob leaves her throat.
When she looks back up at Rio, she realizes the veterinarian is staring at her with an unreadable expression on her face.

During the silence that follows, Agatha registers the softness in Rio’s face, which contrasts with her earlier demeanor. She has feminine features, but carries herself with a dose of masculine energy, coloring her character somewhat androgynous. And despite her youthful look, she seems knowledgeable enough. Rio appears to be full of opposites, and Agatha wonders what other surprises hide behind those deep brown, blinking eyes.

“Alright,” Agatha eventually caves. “Is there a number I can call? When can I expect an update? And who is going to check in with him? Can I come back tomorrow morning?”

In an unexpected comforting gesture, Rio places a hand on top of Agatha’s, stroking Scratchy’s fur with her finger.

“Leave your number here; I will personally check up on him and call you if anything changes. If not, you’re welcome to visit him tomorrow.”

“You?” Agatha asks, the surprise clear in her voice. When Rio looks down in disappointment, she clarifies: “No, I mean— weren’t you supposed to go on break for the weekend?”

“I was.” Rio smiles. “And while I’m not a human doctor, I can see that your human heart could use the support of a familiar face throughout all of this. So I won’t transfer him— he’ll stay my patient and I will do anything within my power to make him better.”

Agatha is at a loss for words and sniffs loudly.

“Anything else I could offer you as reassurance?” Rio asks.

Agatha softly presses her lips to one of his droopy ears. “He likes to watch Jeopardy before going to sleep.” 


 

The night drags on. Agatha worries herself literally sick, and by eight the next morning, she’s had about 25 minutes of sleep.

By eight thirty, she’s fresh out of the shower when the phone rings. She picks it up before it can ring a second time.

“Hello?”

“Agatha?”

“Rio? Is he— oh god, is he—?”

“Don’t worry, I’m not calling with any bad news. Señor Scratchy is alive, and doing pretty well, given the circumstances.”

Agatha gulps. “The circumstances?”

“I think it’s best if you stop by for a visit. How soon would you be able to get here?”

“Fifteen minutes?” Agatha says, her breathing speeding up. “Make that twelve.”

“There’s no need to rush, please. Just get here safe.”

A soft click announces that Rio has hung up the phone. Agatha scrambles to throw on some clothes and two different socks, forgoes brushing her hair, and leaves the house as soon as possible. She’s in her car three minutes later, and arrives at the clinic after roughly ten minutes and probably a couple of speeding tickets.

“Where is he?” She slams her fists on the locked outside door of the clinic. “Rio! Let me see him!”

Her ears perk up at the sound of keys on the other side of the door, and a few seconds later Rio greets her with a warm smile. She’s already wearing her lab coat this time, and she steps aside. Agatha almost trips over her feet while racing to the door that leads to the treatment room.

“Where is he?” she panics.

“I’ll let you go to him in a minute,” Rio says calmly, tapping on one of the chairs in an invitation for Agatha to sit down. 

Agatha stubbornly stays up.

“I have a couple of questions for you first,” Rio says. “Do you have a yard?”

“Y-yes, I do.”

“Do you often let Scratchy free roam there?”

“Yes, why? I…” Agatha answers with a wavering voice. “Oh god, did he eat something? A toxic plant? I should’ve–”

“Come,” Rio says, with a nod towards another door that leads further into the clinic.

“Do you know what’s wrong with him?” Agatha asks, meekly following Rio into the next room.

“Yeah, I do,” the vet responds confidently, turning a corner to reach a hallway full of stacked cages.

“It appears that Señor Scratchy—” she crouches down to one of the cages at hip height, “—was in labor. Or should I say, Señora Scratchy?”

Agatha’s eyes widen until they nearly bulge out of her head. Inside the cage is her trusted friend Scratchy, happily nibbling away at a few strands of hay— and half-tucked under his furry belly are several miniature floofs.

The noise that leaves Agatha’s throat sounds pretty similar to a record scratch. “What?!”

“Congratulations, mama.”

“How many are there?!” she asks breathlessly.

Rio taps one of the iron cage bars. She suggests casually: “Will you hold onto this for me please?”

Agatha sheepishly obeys, and Rio answers her question.

“Eleven.”

“Eleven?!”

Agatha’s fingers tighten around the bar and her knees wobble. “What are you— what are you saying?”

Rio unlocks the cage with an amused smile on her face, and scoops up the nearest baby bunny; it’s completely black and about as tall as her pinky finger.

“That you’re about to go through one hell of a postpartum period.” With the index finger of her other hand, she strokes the baby bunny with almost surgical precision. “And that you should probably neuter your pets before assuming their gender.”

“Rio— could I have a glass of water?” 




Four days later, when Agatha’s living room looks like a maternity ward and her kitchen like a zoo nursery, the phone rings again. She reaches to grab it from the couch from her cross legged position on the floor, careful not to wake the four sleeping baby bunnies in her lap.

“Yes?” she answers the phone on speaker, her voice thick from lack of sleep.

“Agatha? It’s Rio. Just calling to check in— how’s motherhood treating you?”

Agatha whimpers and she picks up a fifth bunny to bottle feed it. “I don’t know if I can do this, Rio, I think— no! Not again!” she groans when a speck of the baby bunny’s poop trickles down her sweatshirt. 

Rio’s chuckle sounds strangely warm. “Would it help you if I came over to take a look? Give you some tips, some mental support?”

“Yes please,” Agatha gratefully breathes out. Her skin starts prickling and her stomach does a flip as she thinks about Rio coming over.

“How’s four this afternoon?”

“If that’s your earliest convenience? Then yes, perfect. Could you bring some puppy pads?!”




Agatha, for once, does attempt to work a brush through her hair sometime during the next few hours. She also puts light make-up on and spends a good amount of time pampering Señora Scratchy herself, before it’s time to restart the cycle of feeding her babies.

She’s about to feed the sixth one when the doorbell rings.

“Rio! Thank god!”

She smiles widely at the vet, who balances two megapacks of puppy pads in her arms and just has the upper half of her face visible. Agatha takes the pads and puts them on the floor in her hallway, inviting Rio in.

Once she steps into the living room, Rio snickers at Agatha’s setup. Scratchy’s giant wooden house is by the back door, a small playpen covered in hay adjoined to it. Scratchy is perched on a stack of wood shavings in the pen, gently nudging five of her babies around with her nose.

In the middle of the room, there’s another playpen, covered in soiled toilet paper, bunny hair, and milk stains. There’s a heating pad in the center, and the six other little baby bunnies are snuggled up against it.

“You got your work cut out for you, mama!” Rio whistles.

Agatha’s not sure why her brain feels fuzzy. “Yeah,” she sighs, placing her hands on her hip. “And now most of them are asleep. I’m spending most of my time recounting them to see if I haven’t lost one. Their eyes are still closed, so when they start crawling, they have no idea where they’re going.”

“Natural instincts, I’m happy to hear that,” Rio grins, touching Agatha’s upper arm and squeezing gently. Agatha’s pulse starts racing and she jolts up, stumbling toward the playpen with the yet to be fed half of the litter.

“Help me feed them?” she stammers, stepping over the net and looking around for the tiny bottles of milk. “The babies with Señor Scratchy— Señora Scratchy in the back, have already been fed. It’s how I keep count.”

Rio agrees and joins her in the pen, sitting down on the floor with her. She gently picks up two sleeping grey rabbits, one with each hand.

“Seems like they’re growing steadily,” she hums, and looks at Agatha. “You look stressed out, but at least you’re doing a good job.”

Agatha says nothing as she hands Rio a miniature bottle. The vet expertly places the nozzle in one of the grey bunny’s mouths and coos at it when it starts nursing.

“I don’t know what I’m gonna do with them,” Agatha sighs, when a different bunny latches onto the bottle she's holding.

“I take it you’re going to give them up for adoption once they’re old enough?”

“That’s what I initially thought, yes… but—”

“You’re already in love with them.” It’s not a question. Agatha shyly nods when the vet laughs knowingly.

“Sounds like you’re gonna need a lot more help, a lot more often.”

Agatha playfully nudges Rio’s leg with her foot. “What are you suggesting?”

“Well… if there’s no Mrs. Harkness, or a second mother to your soccer team of baby bunnies…” Rio stops herself while a beautiful shade of red creeps up to her cheeks.

“Then what?” Agatha smirks. When Rio avoids her gaze, Agatha brings a little black bunny closer to her.

“Come on, Rio, what were you gonna say? You’re not going to keep secrets from this face, are you?”

The black bunny’s nose twitches right as Rio’s eyes flick towards it— and Agatha knows she’s won. 

“I was gonna— offer myself up. A side hustle as a maternity carer, if you will.”

“Hmm.” Agatha nods with a disbelieving pout. She eyes one of the brown bunnies near the heating pad, who has woken up, and is now crawling toward Rio. “Alright. Well, yes please. I’d like that. It’s such a shame, though…”

“What is?”

“It’d be weird to ask my maternity aid to go for a drink with me, right? I mean, if you’d just help me out as Rio…”

“Oh my god, what a wild assumption!” Rio exhales, and tries to hide her face behind her nursing bunny. Unfortunately for her, the bunny is only the size of a fingerling potato. Then her eyes catch the brown bunny that had been crawling toward her; it’s furiously trying to climb her leg now.

“How do you keep them apart?” she tries to change the topic, scooping up the struggling animal.

“Honestly? I don’t,” Agatha laughs. “There’s five brown ones, two are white, another two are grey, one is black, and one is a carbon copy of Scratch. That’s as far as I get.”

The lively brown bunny in Rio’s hand is trying to break free, blindly clawing at her sweater with its soft and brittle nails. “Well, you should name them, at least!” Rio says, as if it’s the most logical thing to do.

“Name them? Rio, there are eleven of them.”

“Yeah, usually I’d suggest Louie, Huey, and Dewey, but then there’d still be— oh my god, can you stay still, Spidey?” she affectionately reprimands the brown bunny climbing up her sweater.

“There you go,” Agatha laughs heartily. “So that’s Spiderman.”

Rio points at one of the grey ones she’s fed. “That one was pretty feisty, he’d be the Hulk.”

“Oh, for sure. One of the brown ones over there is already a certified dick, he can be Iron Man. He’s inseparable from one of the white ones— so that would make him Jarvis.”

Grinning and snorting like teenagers, they name the other bunnies Captain America, Thor, Loki, Thanos, and Wolverine.

“Then we have these little ones left,” Rio says, picking up the black bunny and the bunny that looks so similar to Scratchy. “You’re not gonna be able to distinguish this one from his momma when he’s grown.”
The latter bunny’s back paw starts moving against his own ear at rapid speed, making Rio laugh out loud.

“Itchy!” she exclaims. “It makes sense. Scratchy and Itchy.”

The look Rio gives to the newly named rabbit, touches something irreversibly so inside of Agatha.

“And what about this lady?” Rio holds up the black bunny. “She actually cost me some grey hairs during labor, you know? She was the last one of the litter, much smaller than her brothers and sisters— she almost didn’t make it, but here she is. Strong and full of life.”

“Vidal,” Agatha says.

“Yes?” Rio blinks at her, baffled at hearing her last name.

“That should be her name. Vidal. It means life, right? Seems fitting. And with that name, she’d honor the reason she and all of her brothers and sisters are alive and well.”

Rio’s face contorts, but she says nothing. Her lips press together firmly, until eventually, she nods at the little black bunny.

“Hello, little miss Vidal.”



The two women make sure all bunnies are fed and still healthy, and carefully place them in the separate pen with their momma to snooze off against her body heat.

“So… it’s kind of interesting to watch such a… tough person like you having such a caring nature,” Agatha says when they finally sit down on her sofa. She hides her face in her glass of red wine.

“My, Agatha, are you flirting with your vet? ” Rio mocks her in a sultry voice, lightly scratching the other woman's arm with a fingernail and laughing loudly when Agatha slaps her hand away.

“I wouldn’t dare. My babies need clear boundaries. And so do I.”

“And what would those boundaries be?”

“No dating the vet? Imagine if they don’t know who their real mama is anymore.”

“Yeah, that would be a problem,” Rio nods thoughtfully, crossing her legs and leaning back.

Agatha’s eyes flick toward the crowded pen near the back door again.

“God, I can’t imagine how Scratchy ended up pregnant…”

“You can’t?” Rio’s eyebrows shoot up and she immediately uncrosses her legs again. Before Agatha can stop her, she says: “Because I can. And again, not a human doctor, but I even know a thing or two about that in humans.” 

Rio’s eyes land on Agatha’s lips. “In fact, Agatha, if you let me take you out, I’d be quite happy to show you how that works.”

Agatha snorts nervously when Rio slowly leans in. Her own eyes land on Rio's plump lips too, and god, have they always looked this lickable?

“On one condition!” she all but squeals before the vet is close enough to kiss her.

“Which is?”

“You better clear your schedule in a few months.”

Rio’s lip curves up smugly in anticipation. “Because…?”

“Because you’re going to have to neuter eleven— no, twelve bunnies.”
