Summary

Agatha Harkness has lived a long life, even at the age of thirty. Winding up as the Lead Hand on one of the most notorious ranches in the country wasn't on her to-do list as a kid, but it's a place she's called home for over fifteen years. Roping and riding and hard manual labor are all she's ever known as a working adult, and that's just fine by her.

However, being raised by one Guillermo Vidal comes with its own statutes and stipulations. It means that everything Agatha has, she works for and fights for, no matter what it is.

Be it the home she lives in, the decorative wildlife on her walls, or the mind-numbing loneliness of a lone wolf, it's all hers and what she wants and needs out of life.

Until it isn't.

Agatha has kept a wide berth around any relationship with Guillermo's daughter, Rio, but before she can even notice it happening, their worlds begin to collide.

Chapter 1: Act Appalled

Before the sun rose each morning, so did one Agatha Harkness.

Every single day, like clockwork with more regard for the position of the sun in the sky than any set of ticking hands, the wildest blue eyes opened in the darkness of a single story ranch house, one decorated with heads of bison, wolves, and coyotes alike. It was a daily ritual, one initiated by the lightning quick speed at which her heart would hammer in her chest.

Every early morning, it seemed, was spearheaded by some early morning nightmare or another.

For Agatha, finding horrors in her subconscious wasn’t a hard thing to do.

Keeping them at bay with eyes wide open was another story entirely.

Life was often a fickle beast, something she’d known from the young age of six – the first time her mother backhanded her across the cheek for a cross word or three. In reality, it was backtalk from a child and nothing more, but it was the first time Evanora Harkness had enough of her daughter’s mouth. It definitely was not the last.

Thinking about her childhood was pointless for Agatha; it did no good to look back. It served no purpose. If she spent all her time lamenting over what used to be, it would cloud her vision of the present.

Since she was fourteen, Agatha has called the Four 7’s Ranch home and nothing short of it. How she wound up there wasn’t so much as important as the fact that it was the first place in her life she truly felt like she belonged.

The owner, Guillermo Vidal was a formidable man; he stood at six and a half feet tall, had a full head of jet black hair and a dark beard of the same color that covered the entirety of his jaw. His eyes were a deep, deep green that almost looked brown under the cover of shadow. His chest was wide and his shoulder staunch – the kind of posture that offered a warning:

Fucking try me.

Running a ranch the size of Four 7’s was no easy feat; six hundred thousand acres had to be tended to and looked after each and every day. Of course not every inch of the property needed attention, but it took a team of fifteen hands and Agatha at the helm to lead the charge and make it happen.

Guillermo made sure it happened.

He was a strict man, knew what he wanted done and how he wanted it to be done. He barked orders and looked Agatha in her eyes every single time to make sure he was perfectly understood. As his lead hand, she would nod in agreement every single time.

Guillermo Vidal was not the kind of man who liked to be told ‘no.’

It was a lesson Agatha learned early on.

Winding up on his ranch had been nothing but pure accident; she remembered running and running for what felt like days, only to find herself weaving through woods that spilled into greener pastures. They were speckled with cows, but the barn she found at the treeline appeared empty and abandoned. It was some place dry, some place to hide away from the elements.

She managed to stow away for only a couple of days before her secret little hideaway was not so secret anymore.

The first kind eyes Agatha had seen in a week were that of Viviana Vidal.

While looking into the past was not Agatha’s favorite thing to do, she couldn’t help herself when she broke into the morning to see the other woman leaving for work in the city. Every single weekday morning, she would offer Agatha a bright smile and a wave on her way out, kindness on her face again and again.

Viviana was a beautiful woman, substantially shorter than her husband and with darker but gentler eyes. Her long, dark brown hair was often clipped along the back of her head in a makeshift bun, showing off her long neck and sculpted collarbones. If not for the fact that Agatha saw her in the light of a maternal figure, she would probably be brave enough to say she was hot. Well, lest she be kicked in the chest by Guillermo himself.

As much as Viv loved the ranch, it wasn’t her entire life in the same way it was for her husband. She helped out when she wanted to – mostly with the horses, which were her favorite – but her real passion was the law, and she was a damn good lawyer because of it. So she made her drive into the city (an hour and a half each way) each day to do what she loved.

It was admirable, Agatha thought, that the woman never had to work a day in her life but followed her aspirations anyway.

Then again, that seemed to be a trait commonly found in Vidal women.

It was a trait her daughter had, too.

Rio Vidal.

Nepo baby extraordinaire.

If she was being honest, Agatha didn’t know Rio on a personal level. She could say as much because she made it a deeply rooted mission in life to never know her on a personal level.

They attended the same high school and had spent over fifteen years on the same ranch, growing older, but very seldom was a word ever shared between them, not even as Agatha rose up the ranks of the Four 7’s farm. She found, in all actuality, it was much cleaner to not find her honey where she made her money… And lived rent free. It was mostly made easy by the fact that the majority of the other hands were men and held none of her interest, but sometimes her mind did wander.

Rio was a beautiful woman, much like her mother. She was a little taller than Viviana, but still a whole head shorter than her father. Her hair was shorter but still passed her shoulders and fell somewhere between the chestnut of her mother’s locks and the deep black of her father’s, almost always hanging in her face unless she was yelling.

She liked to do that.

It gave her an air of confidence and while Agatha would have found it attractive on any other woman, knowing it came from Rio was often offputting. She’d had everything handed to her, anything she could have wanted, and yet she walked around with so much anger in her. Agatha wished she knew why, but then again, she realized it wasn’t much of her business.

At the end of the day, no matter her position on the ranch, Agatha was still nothing more than the help.

It just so happened that ‘help’ had a relative meaning around those parts.

Guillermo might have been a formidable businessman above board, but below it lay a man who stopped at nothing to keep a strong hold on all he had.

By extension, that meant Agatha helped keep that strong hold, too.

In blatant terms, she was his number one, his general. She was the kind of woman who did whatever needed to be done. Whatever needed to be done. And she did it with no questions.

Someone caught out on the 750 cutting wire to herd the cows out?

Punishable by being tied to a chair with a good beating.

Someone was stupid enough to pick a fight with her hands?

Punishable by taking a couple of haymakers straight from her toned and muscular arms.

One of her own caught stealing from the man in charge?

Well, that one was a little more egregious.

It was punishable by means of a bullet straight to the head.

It didn’t bother Agatha, not the way it should have bothered a normal person. It didn’t eat at her conscience or keep her up at night. It didn’t make her feel like a bad person or that she should atone for her sins. No, not for Agatha.

For Agatha, killing was a part of life. That was a lesson she learned far before she ever stumbled her way onto Vidal property. When Guillermo realized it was simply a part of her DNA, he chose to use that to his advantage. She was his muscle, his unending threat, his way of telling his ranch hands and anyone who knew who he was that he was not to be trifled with.

He had a weapon on his hands, and he was not at all afraid to use her.

Gunfights were common, but they did happen every so often, leaving Agatha’s body riddled with scars (a couple of grazings on her right arm, a two-inch long one on her left elbow, a through and through in her left calf and one where a bullet needed to be fished out from beneath her ribs), something she thought of as further proof that the Four 7’s Ranch was protected and fortified, something no one who wasn’t meant to would ever see.

Those who did met their untimely end.

Usually, it was with a grinning Agatha bearing over them.

What a sight to behold that was.

Vicious, sharp teeth in a deep and twisted smile. Sometimes, those around her or those there to help often swore it was something she liked, something she took pleasure in. Maybe killin’ wasn’t for the faint of heart, but nothing about Agatha’s heart ever felt faint. It made her feel alive, like she was able to do the one damn thing she knew herself to be good at. It was her purpose, and it was one she would fulfill until she no longer could.

She owed that much to the man who gave her a life.

That day, at only fourteen years old, she could have lost it. He could have just as easily put a gun to her head and pulled the trigger, but something stopped him that day. Maybe it was Viviana’s pleading to help her, or the fact that she was speckled with blood, or maybe even that when he looked at her, he saw his own daughter, but whatever it was, she was thankful. He took her in, kept her busy with mucking stalls and filling troughs, and gave her a warm place to lay her head at night.

When she really looked at what her life was, she knew she owed it to Guillermo.

If it meant she got to rise every morning and watch the sun do the same from atop the back of a horse, she would shout it out every single day she needed to. Maybe there were those who would claim that the life she was living wasn’t much of a life at all, but she begged to differ. She saw incredible things, from sunrises and sunsets to the births of foals and calves. She tested her own limits day in and day out. She made acquaintances that kept her sharp and funny, even when the disdain blossomed.

What Vidal had given her was something precious, something she knew to behold and value at all costs. She had purpose, which was more than she would have ever dreamed for herself as a child.

On another morning still under the cover of stars, a startled Agatha bolted up in bed, her brow and neck covered in sweat.

Another nightmare.

Oh, what it was to have a decent night’s sleep. She wouldn’t have known, but it was something she often hoped for over the harsh reality of waking just after four in the morning. She sighed to herself and threw her torso back down into the mattress, groaning begrudgingly as she did so. It wasn’t so much that she didn’t want to face the day, but another thirty or so minutes of sleep might have been nice.

She could still hope, of course.

There were many nights ahead; perhaps one of them would be more fruitful in a restful night’s manner than the ones before.

After a cold shower to wake herself up, Agatha found herself falling into her usual routine, standing at the kitchen counter while staring out the window as her coffee brewed. She might not have needed the entire pot most days, but she brewed it and tanked most of it anyway, knowing that the boost helped keep her light on her toes throughout days where nothing was ever predictable. Just the same, as it clicked off and she poured herself a mug of the hot, black liquid, her gaze lifted to the window, her attention caught by a flash of light.

It was still impossibly dark outside, but under the light of the moon, a silhouette paced back and forth, the light shifting from side to side each time the figure turned directions. Glancing at the clock, Agatha noted it was just before six and realized who it was.

Viviana wouldn’t be out in the field so early, and she would not be making the shill sounds that were barely permeating the glass of the window.

Ah, Princess Vidal and her little temper tantrums.

Honestly, Agatha didn’t know much about her outside of the fact that she was Daddy’s Little Girl, and ran a consulting firm in the same city her mother worked in. She worked on cases like land grabs and redistricting, always trying to line her already fat pockets while working at Guillermo’s behest. While Agatha could appreciate the sentiment of doing his bidding, she was rather put off by the consistent ‘better than thou’ attitude Rio seemed to brandish.

She would hand it to her, though; she was good at what she did.

Her firm had outmanned and completed several hostile takeovers of smaller companies that felt pivotal to their operation and truth be told, that kind of cutthroat behavior was exactly what Agatha would have liked in a woman.

Any woman that was not Rio Vidal.

Even if not for the way she was constantly appalled by the way Rio liked to behave, Agatha knew better. Rio was Guillermo’s absolute pride and joy, even over the ranch itself. While he would have done anything and everything to keep the ranch secured and protected at all costs, he would have gone the extra mile for his daughter. It was a good trait to have, Agatha knew, but it also meant Rio got to do as she pleased and bear no consequences.

Agatha watched her pace for a little while longer before turning back toward the small kitchen to finish her first cup of coffee and pour herself another. When that one was halfway gone, she dumped the last bit and washed up the mug, leaving it to dry on the rack while she prepared for her day.

With her hair dry, she tied it at the nape of her neck and slipped into a plaid flannel and a pair of jeans. After just a moment of debate, she selected one of her buckles and fastened it on, adjusting the way her shirt was tucked. Happy with her wardrobe, she slipped her feet into her Double H boots and grabbed the white cowboy hat from its place on the rack, settling it atop her head before making her way out her front door and into the balmy – and still rather dark – morning.

When she doubled around toward the back of the house, and where the feed was kept in a large shed, she was reasonably startled to almost run directly into the youngest (okay, she wasn’t that young – she was only six months Agatha’s junior) Vidal.

“Fuckin’ christ, watch it, would you?” Agatha barked, her voice too loud for the hour of the morning.

“Or you could not scare someone for no good reason!” Rio snapped in return, her voice sour like spoiled milk left in the sun.

Agatha hated the sound of that already.

“Like that was my whole intention, Princess Rio,” Agatha grumbled, purposely knocking her shoulder against the other woman’s as she walked by, indignant by the response so early in the morning.

“Don’t call me that,” she clipped tightly, flipping her hair over her shoulder. As Agatha cast a glance back at her, she was almost amused to realize the other woman was still in her pajamas.

Shouldn’t you be getting your beauty sleep?” Agatha asked as she made it to the shed and pulled open the door, kicking it wide to keep it from snapping back shut. When she was sure it was going to stay put, she grabbed the wheelbarrow next to it and wheeled it around, beginning to load bags of feed straight into it.

“Someone’s nosy,” Rio replied. She didn’t make an immediate move to head back to the house and instead shifted her weight to one leg, sizing Agatha up as she loaded up the contraption in front of her.

“I live here.”

“Sometimes.”

Oops. That shouldn’t have slipped out. At that point, it just seemed antagonizing.

“And what do you care? You just do the same boring shit day in and day out anyway.” It was very obvious to Agatha that Rio was posturing; Rio knew the kind of things she took care of, the kind of harm she was capable of. She was just being so mouthy because Agatha would never turn that kind of ire and punishment on her, not only out of decency, but because Guillermo would rip her head clean off her shoulders if she so much as blinked at his daughter the wrong way.

Agatha liked her life. She wanted to keep it.

If that meant staying as far as humanly possible away from Rio Vidal, she was completely okay with that. Then again, that instance was no exactly helping her case.

“First of all, that’s quite rude of you. Second of all, I don’t care. I was just pointing out that your statement was not always true. You’ve got that fancy apartment in Bozeman, don’t ya? Couldn’t imagine a little thing like you bringing anyone home to daddy without a prior three year commitment.” She snorted at her own joke, knowing she was right.

Rio had never come home with anyone. No man, no woman, no anything, at least not that Agatha was aware. Then again, with an attitude like hers, it would have been a miracle for her to keep anyone around.

That was Agatha’s perception, at least.

The thing with Rio was that she was always so arrogant and full of herself. She was always so willing to rise to any challenge to a point where it was honestly a bit eye-roll worthy. She had to be the best, had to do the best job anyone could do, and she had to prove to her dad that every single thing he gave her was well deserved.

She was a bit of a try-hard, if you asked Agatha.

But, at least she had a resume to back it up.

A lack of a love life to show for it, too.

“That’s none of your business,” Rio spat. Oohh, Agatha touched a nerve.

At that point, Agatha knew all she was doing was pushing buttons, and she was right.

“Fuck you, Harkness,” Rio growled as she spun on her sandal-covered heel and marched back toward the main house, a good football field away.

Served her right for coming down and fucking up Agatha’s routine.

At the same time, it was a very stark reminder of the different worlds they occupied while still managing to share space. It was as if their orbits never quite intersected the way they needed to, but they occasionally bumped into one another and needed to reestablish an axis.

With a sigh and a shake of her head, Agatha grabbed both handles of the wheelbarrow and began her trek to the barn to feed the horses. Her first stop was a predictable one; her own horse.

“Good morning, Calliope,” she greeted in a sing-song tone with a small smile on her face. An American Paint Horse with a spotted black and white colored coat stuck her head over the gate, her cheek falling right into Agatha’s awaiting palm, just the same as every other morning. “Did you sleep well? Or were you keeping the boys up all night with all your ruckus?” she cooed at the horse, stroking her nose.

In response, Calliope whinnied and shook her head, as if to claim her innocence.

“Right, right. How could I forget? You’d never do a thing wrong in your life, hm?” she quipped with a chuckle. She gave the horse's snout a couple more long strokes before giving her a pat and setting off to begin the feeds of every horse in the barn. It was a painstaking task, but it was calm and often helped Agatha clear her head before a big day.

That morning in particular, however, something was on her mind.

Not something.

Someone.

Rio.

What was she doing out in the field so early? Why was she already so primed to be so damn defensive? Agatha knew it would never be her place to look for answers to those questions, but she also knew that it didn’t stop her from having them. For as annoying as Rio could be, something about her left Agatha often feeling perplexed and unsettled, almost like she was a puzzle with enough pieces missing that Agatha couldn’t actually see the whole picture.

She didn’t need to, anyway.

Rio was Rio and Agatha was Agatha.

Spoiled, rich, temperamental, bratty Rio.

Dedicated, scarred, measured, confident Agatha.

Their paths, in any life, were never really meant to cross but fate could be a fuckin’ asshole sometimes.

Whatever the reason, whatever the case, if Agatha hoped to get anything done that day, she was going to have to get her head on right and actually focus on the work at hand, not any personal or non-work thoughts related to the youngest Vidal.

After feeding all twenty-three horses (with another trip back to the shed because she ran out of feed), Agatha set to filling their water troughs just as the sun started to breech the horizon, casting a warm orange glow across the fields and forests that occupied the expanse of the Four 7’s ranch.

When she emerged with the wheelbarrow, a smile filled her face at the sight of Viviana scrambling down the stairs, her laptop case over one shoulder and her cup of coffee in the opposite hand. When she noticed Agatha, just as she did every other morning, a hand raised and waved as a warm smile filled her face.

“Have a good day!” she called.

“You too!” Agatha offered back, giving a shake of her wrist to say goodbye. She tucked her head and ventured back to the shed for a third time, this time to drop off the wheelbarrow and actually get to work.

Before that, though, she tucked back inside to finish off the rest of her coffee and found herself poised in front of the picture window at the head of the living room, simply basking in the sight of the early morning. When the front door to the main house opened, she half expected to see Guillermo stride through it, but was instead surprised at what she saw.

It was Rio. In full riding gear.

At six-thirty in the morning.

With a wrinkle in her brow, Agatha tossed the mug in the sink (and broke it, though she would worry about that later) and marched outside, trying her best to stop Rio in her tracks.

Rio didn’t care, she just kept marching.

Agatha was wrong, anyway.

They only needed nineteen horses.

Two of them were too old to take and two too young.

She should have known which one Rio was after.

Right. Because Kevin was nineteen fucking years old and while he was a good horse, and an Appalooosa, he was also not the kind of horse one usually kept around. The only reason Guillermo hadn’t put him out to pasture was because he was Rio’s horse. She’d been there when he was born, and she was the one to give him such a stupid name.

Rio was so fucking annoying. She was mostly annoying as it was, but she became even more annoying when she was right.

As Agatha appeared in the doorway, she did have to admit it was kind of a sweet sight.

With a heavy gust of air through her nose, Agatha waved her off and stalked off down the center aisle, prepared to rally the troops and tack up the horses for a long day ahead.

She knew that if she couldn’t stop thinking about Rio and what a fucking menace she was, it was going to be even longer.

No time for that.

No time for Rio.

Chapter 2: Imaginary Enemy

Spending time out on the rolling hillsides and vast expanse of land could be healing more often than not. For Agatha, it was her favorite place to be. It was a literal breath of fresh air; while she loved the ranch itself, there was something about the open air and smell of rain that always healed a part of her. Or, rather, what little parts of her that could be healed.

Agatha, as a whole, was a scar. She was one giant healed wound, something ripped open and mended time and time again. It was what life had done to her, what it had dragged her through. It was the knife, the one that tried again and again to exsanguinate her.

But Agatha?

Agatha could not be killed. It wasn’t necessarily that she was immortal so much as she was unreasonably and insanely… stubborn.

Winding up at Four 7’s was proof of that.

Cold, dirty, alone meant nothing to her when her sheer instinct was to do nothing more than survive. It could have gone any other way, including meeting the gnarly end of a Grizzly in the woods, but she followed her instincts and it gave her more than she could have asked for.

It gave her a life where she got to ride on the back of her only trusty ally on a clear day with a group of her hands, most of whom rode behind her because they knew full well she didn’t want to talk with them. They could be a rowdy bunch, but they were hard workers and she appreciated every ounce of effort they put into what they did. Sure, maybe it was different for them in that they worked so hard for a paycheck and room and board, but their efforts did not go unnoticed.

Agatha ran a tight ship and a few prior hands had called her unreasonable at points, but she knew what it took to run a ranch. She’d been learning from the best for over fifteen years; Guillermo had taught her well, enough so that he trusted her to take the reins, so to speak.

He was rather hands off and had been for a few years ever since he promoted Agatha and gifted her the cabin. It was a sizable place, but more than that, it was home. It was a place she could rest her bones and recharge when a long day got to her.

But then, there were days like that one. They were beautiful and clear, the sky streaked with the wispiest clouds that rolled on by overhead. They were opaque but thin like cotton, offering a brief respite of passing shade before floating on and being followed by more.

The ride was only a couple of hours to the 20th mile marker, but the crowd behind her grew rowdier the further they got.

“Hey,” she snapped over her shoulder as Calliope tentatively stepped into the creek to cross. “This ain’t a damn vacation and I expect you lot to act like it.”

Maybe it was harsh. Maybe it was a little more than was necessary, but they all straightened in their saddles nonetheless and gave curt nods, followed by rounds of “Yes, ma’am,” and “Sorry, ma’am.”

“Don’t call me that, you know better,” she scolded from ahead of them, another grumble of apologies sounding in return.

“Do you ever get tired of this, Agatha?” one of the hands, a man named Duke, asked.

She glanced over as he approached her side, clearly intent on conversation even if she was not.

“What, babysittin’ you assholes? Yeah, all the time,” she informed him, although it was an answer to a question he wasn’t asking.

“No, no. I know that. I meant…”

She looked at him as he stared off into the horizon, trying to find his words. He was a handsome man by the basic definition, with a sculpted jaw and methodically maintained facial hair, but he was a little arrogant and perhaps a bit full of himself. Despite the inflated head of his, however, he dedicated himself to his job, even if he could get a little rowdy in the after-hours.

“The ranch. The work. The day in and day out of the same thing. You’ve been here longer than any’a us… But you don’t seem like you want to leave any time soon.”

He had a point, and it was a correct one.

“I got everything I need right here. The sun, the rain, the cattle, this here horse. I don’t need much else.”

The man trotted at her side, quietly thinking before he voiced what was on his mind.

“Do ya ever want more?”

It was genuine, she could hear that much, and truth be told, she did. She wanted a woman to love, one to fall asleep next to, but not many wanted her or the lifestyle she brought with her. It was too much for them, said to be an avenue of employment meant for men who didn’t care about being lonely. It was a harsh detailing of what she did, and she vehemently disagreed, but it meant she was good for little more than a fun night or two between the sheets to any woman who might have shown some interest.

Then again, it could have been the hour and twenty minute drive from the city just to reach her cabin. That was a bit of a bitch, and she knew it.

For as lonely as she could sometimes feel, Agatha was a realist in knowing why women kept their distance. Perhaps they could have been smart for it, but at the end of most days, she still helped shut down the barns and crawled into a big but empty bed to do little more than give her life its due consideration before she fell asleep.

“Mr. Vidal has given me all I need,” she decided on, though it was intentionally vague. Yes, he gave her what she needed, even if she could not find all she wanted. Agatha could be a complex woman when she so chose, but it was easier to mandate herself to the bare necessities in order to get by.

Plus, not having a steady partner in life allowed for her to adhere to the more… extreme things Guillermo asked of her.

So no, she didn’t need more, even if she wanted it.

“That’s on us,” he corrected. “She sure as shit didn’t call me.”

Agatha breathed a sad laugh, nodding her head again. “You’re right. But you came anyway. You’re a good man, Duke.”

“I learned from the best,” he joked, giving her arm a light pat.

“Keep them in line for me today, will ya?”

He gave a resolute nod, accompanied by a faux salute. “You got it, boss.”

Agatha started to turn away but paused, her eyes locking with his. “Thank you. For everything.”

He shrugged and headed back toward the bunk house, calling over his shoulder. “Only givin’ back what I get!”

And with that, Agatha was left alone in the early morning hours, retreating to her home to spend her day off with the woman she cared for in whatever convoluted, twisted way she could.
