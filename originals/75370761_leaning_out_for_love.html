Summary

It's a simple proposition: Agatha Harkness, heir to a small branch of the old but faded Salemite coven, will be handfasted for two years and two months to Rio Vidal, heir to the noble First Coven. Hopefully in that time the two will produce at least one child to be heir to both covens, and when the time has passed, they can choose to remain together or part and co-parent. If and when their child outlives them, Agatha's coven—like many before—will be absorbed into the First and the child will be the leader of the most powerful and ancient coven in the world.

Of course, thanks to her mother, Agatha has little say in the matter, but she comforts herself with knowing the handfast will be short in duration. Once she meets her intended, however, she begins to have thoughts of a more permanent nature.

"Want me to take over? Someone needs to have a talk with April about putting inflammatory reading materials in the first classroom library."

"Kiddo, what'd you do?"

"Oh wow," Rio choked, slumping slightly to rest their head on the desk a moment.

"I tried to explain that maybe there's room for varying opinions," April said, "but apparently we only have one way of doing things around here!"

"You did it to disrupt the class, didn't you?" Rio rumbled.

April shrugged.

"Call me a disruptor."

Agatha backed away as Rio stood and went around to kneel to April's level.

"Hermanita, you gotta not... Do... That. That stuff."

"But... But you've said, and Leslie's said, that we can learn from all kinds of things."

"Sure, but kiddo, that... Stuff like that, you need context for stuff. Maybe, maybe if you'd taken the book to a teacher and asked them to explain it or something..."

Agatha tried not to laugh as she took a seat at Rio's desk, flipping through the quarter's ledger. It was almost done, would wind down at the solstice, but there was quite a backlog of things to check and get in order. Things to bring together, receipts to check against reports for a third time...

Rio stood, gently directing April over to the little sofa in the corner of the office.

"Who wants a meeting?"

Alice looked decidedly nervous, staring at her boots, hands twitching behind her back.

"It's Evanora."

Alice nodded.

"Says there's been a breach. Won't discuss of what with anyone but Rio."

Alice sizzled out again, and Rio sighed, moving to the phone on the desk and dialing over to the apartment. They rested their free hand on the desk, and Agatha placed her own over theirs, knowing they needed the contact as much as she did.

"Hey, Leslie, can you head up here to the office and collect April? We have a sudden meeting and..."

"I wanna stay!" April insisted.

Agatha went to the girl, sitting beside her.

"Trust me, kiddo, you don't wanna stay for a meeting with my boring old mom."

"Yeah I do. She's bad mean. I wanna tell her she's bad mean to her face."

"Not the best idea, April, she..."

The air sizzled again, and both Leslie and Evanora were standing there, each looking a little befuddled at the sight of each other.

There was a brief moment where Evanora Harkness, head of the Salemite coven, was doing the old sidewalk shuffle versus Leslie Knope, and then the elder witch growled angrily and flashed out a minor wave of power, knocking Leslie back a few feet and storming over to the desk. That, of course, got Rio's hackles up, but it got April's up even more, and she rushed over...

And kicked Evanora square in the left shin.

"You lay a hand on her and I'll do something even more stupid than this," Agatha growled.

It was nothing Agatha had not experienced before, but it had been long enough since it happened that she did not brace as she might once have, and she yelped, releasing her mother's wrist and backing away, tears springing to her eyes from the sharp stinging of her reddened cheek and the slow throb of a partly split lip.

"Look, I don't know why you've invaded my covenstead, bitch, or what you mean by pulsing my friend, threatening my sister, and striking my wife..."

"You've given shelter and aid to two traitors, formerly of my coven. My sister Valentina and her daughter Jennifer. They're here, in your covenstead, no doubt plotting some malevolent..."

"They're here because you disbanded their branch and disconnected them, along with the others," Agatha said, shaking her head and pulling back into the moment.

There was something, a sensation of warmth from nearby. Protection. Not the all-the-time protective buzz of Rio's power around her. No. This was different. Older, somehow. Something that did not even know it was switched on.

How did she sense that?

Evanora turned on Agatha, stalked to her, and glared in that way which, when Agatha was young, had actually been terrifying.

"You little filth," the older witch scoffed.
"What right have you to tell me anything? To judge my choices in managing my own coven?"

"I have every right as your heir," Agatha said firmly.
"As your heir and as the one carrying a future heir to both your coven and this one."

Quirking a brow, Evanora smirked.

"So. You managed to do your job, did you? Managed to be a proper vessel."

"Oh, I'm managing far more than that, Mother. I'm finally becoming who I was meant to be. Nothing you did, and nothing you can do, will stop me."

Reeling back as if struck, Evanora snorted out an offended breath, then turned to Rio again.

"I expect you to compel Valentina, if necessary, to present herself and her child at my covenstead within the week."

"And if I don't?" Rio glared.

The laugh that rippled out of Evanora somehow made Agatha feel nauseous.

"I should hate to have to truly call you out, child. Better witches than you have faced my wrath and not come through safe."

Then Evanora was gone in a dark sizzle, and both Rio and Agatha made their way over to the sofa where Leslie, still a little in shock from the minor blast, sat with April.

"I'm gonna call Bruce up here," Rio sighed.
"Have everyone checked out."

"I'm fine," Leslie insisted.
"Shaken up, but fine."

"And if you're not? Leslie, I'm strong, but I don't know if I can handle Ann and Ben's combined fury if anything happens to you."

After a little more back and forth Bruce, with Jen Kale at his side, appeared in the office. Agatha insisted he see Leslie first, but Bruce was rather insistent.

"You're bleeding, Agatha."

"It's just a little split lip. Not the first one my mother gave me."

Dr. Bruce Banner, a man who Agatha had thus far only ever seen as relatively quiet, mild-mannered, and even milquetoast, let out a faint, huffing growl.

"She... How often did she hit you?"

"You want a year-by-year breakdown, or a cumulative estimate?"

Bruce's whole face twitched, his eyes seeming to flicker in an odd way for a moment, and then he turned his back to Agatha and sighed, crossing to kneel by the sofa and check Leslie over. Was the doctor this concerned about every member of the coven?

Once Leslie was cleared, she headed down to the apartment with April, and Jen was the one to check Agatha's lip.

"Not a full split this time, at least," Jen sighed.
"Looks like your teeth just sliced up the inner skin a bit."

Agatha nodded.

"Good deal. Am I cleared? If so, put me back in the game, coach."

Jen rolled her eyes.

"Do we need to do a concussion assessment?"

"My name is Agatha Harkness, the year is 2026, and despite best efforts, an extraordinarily moldy mango is president."

"Right, okay, sharp as a tack then. I'll just..."

Jen turned and crossed the room to where Bruce was in quiet conversation with Rio. For her part, Agatha tried to shrug off the quiver of fear in her gut as she went back to the desk and carried on reconciling accounts.

Chapter 13

"Gonna try it again," Rio murmured, "when you're ready."

Agatha sucked in a deep breath, shaking out her hands.

"What if it hurts the baby?"

"The genny's on the deepest stepdown setting; it's safe. And if things do get too intense, mi amor, we stop a while. Be easy for me, since I'm powering the genny."

Smiling, Rio pulled their hands from the gloves a moment and approached Agatha, pulling her close.

"I love you and our child. I would never knowingly risk either of you. You are strong, Agatha, but you've had no chance to develop it. And our son is fated. He'll be born safe when the time comes, and for now he's safe in your womb."

After a moment, Agatha nodded and smiled.

"Okay. Yeah."

She had to admit, the sensation of the power flowing into her, even at the low level the genny was throwing, felt exhilarating once her own ability kicked in. They had only tried this training three times prior, but she had come away each time feeling fresh and energized and oh, so strong. Really, training on a genny was meant for young witchlings, to train them in countering and blocking blasts. Agatha's own birth coven had several, but of course she had never been allowed to train on them. Then again, since the power funneled into the machine was supposed to come from a parent or other close person, maybe that was a good thing. Evanora was liable to have shut off the stepdowns and fail safes. It would have been a way to kill Agatha outright under a scrim of plausible deniability.

As she watched Rio slip back into place beside the genny, Agatha found some part of herself excited for the next blast. She was coming into her own, learning not only those basics she had been denied, but discovering so much about her strange innate power as a siphon.

"You ready?" Rio asked.

"Ready," Agatha nodded.

The point of the exercise in Agatha's case was not to block or counter, but to accept the blast and see if she could develop the ability to fully control her siphon. It might take years, even all the rest of her life, but she wanted so much to learn. As the genny hummed and whined, she widened her stance. The blasts were small, but underestimation could be a problem.

With a soft thuum, the genny went off.

The blast was, of course, barely anything, hitting Agatha with less a feeling than a sense. It was akin to the feeling of walking near a room and sensing a TV was silently on inside without actually seeing. It struck Agatha with only the most vague tingle, but still there was a thrill of fear, a sense of being attacked, and something in Agatha's chest turned over, quivering with effort like the weakest muscle.

The green-gold power striking her upper chest suddenly went a deep, glimmering purple.

"You got it!" Rio called, but Agatha hardly heard, her senses too enraptured as her siphon pulled and began to draw in the power being relayed through the genny.

Oh, warmth. Endless warmth and strength and power, real power. No more wanting, no more waiting, just power power power, here and now and always, swirling into her, into the very core of her being, rebounding, redoubling, lifting up her heart, her spirit, wiping out all that doubt of her own strength and ability. She could do so much with enough power. She could keep herself and those she loved safe, she could keep control of any situation, she could not stop never stop had to keep going too much too much but she finally felt strong finally felt powerful finally...

Rio wrenched their hands out of the gloves of the genny with a struggle, and both Agatha and they fell to the floor.

Agatha lay there on the rubbery mat of the floor, breath coming in strange, deep gasps, whole body still tingling with the borrowed power. Slowly, painfully slowly, Rio crawled to her, pale and panting.

"Fuck," Rio choked.
"Fuck, Agatha, it... The genny... Couldn't stop it. It... You... Okay?"

"I'm amazing," Agatha giggled, feeling orgastically spent and delightfully over full.

Turning her head, she saw how pale Rio was, how trembling, and some part of her realized she must have drawn too much. Desperate horror welled in her, and she twisted over, grabbing Rio and pulling them into the tightest embrace she could muster.

"Ow," they chuckled.
"Strong, mi amor. You're real strong right now."

"I'm sorry, I didn't mean... I didn't know I could! I... What..."

They eased back slightly from the embrace, propping themself up on their elbow, a trembling hand smoothing hair back from Agatha's face.

"You're powerful, Agatha. So, so powerful."

Rio smiled, but then their face spasmed into a pained expression. They shuddered, eyes rolling back, and slumped unconscious to the mat. Panic shooting through her, Agatha struggled to kneel, taking Rio's hand and squeezing.

"Love? My love? Rio? Rio, please..."

"What..."

"What's wrong? You all right?"
