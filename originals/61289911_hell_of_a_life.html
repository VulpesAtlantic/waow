Summary

Surviving the war was never the goal—it was just a bonus. Her true mission was clear: kill Thanos. For years, that singular purpose consumed her.

Then he came.

The man she had known, the man she hated. Yet somehow, he was the one who, piece by piece, started to heal the cracks in her broken world.

He was also the man who made her pray—something she never thought she’d do. Praying for both of them to make it through. Not just to survive, but to actually have a chance. To live, to exist outside the war that was slowly destroying them both.

Present Day

“Welcome, assets of Xandar.”

The room was packed with women from every corner of Xandar, faces from countless species, each unique, each carrying the same look of wide-eyed fear. Some were whispering frantically in languages Gamora's translator didn’t recognize, while others clung to one another, trembling.

“I am Captain Korak,” he announced. “You are here to serve Lord Thanos. Resist, and you will find yourselves very sorry.”

Korak’s gaze swept over the room, lingering on each woman with a calculating glint.

“From now on, this will be your place until the auction starts,” Korak declared, his tone dismissive as he gestured around the room.

Gamora’s heart sank at the word auction. Her pulse quickened, dread coiling tightly in her stomach. She had heard whispers among the other captives—rumors that Thanos was not merely taking them for servitude but for something far worse.

“Lord Thanos plans to repopulate with a better civilization will begin in a few weeks, and you are all part of it.”

“What does he mean?” one of the women cried out, her voice breaking through the heavy atmosphere. “We’re not livestock!”

“Repopulate after wiping out half of the population, that's a joke!” Gamora’s voice rang out, eyes fixed defiantly on Korak.

A tense silence followed as Korak’s attention zeroed in on her. He regarded her with a cold, assessing gaze before a smirk crept onto his face. Slowly, he strode toward her. When he stopped in front of her, his voice dropped to a dark, mocking tone.

“This one here belongs to Star-Lord,” he gestured toward her, ensuring every woman in the room could see and hear.

“She’s not one of you. She’s different,” he continued with a sneer. “Star-Lord made sure to reserve this one for himself.”

Gamora felt the room shift. Whispers broke out in hushed tones, spreading unease through the gathered women, each one eyeing her as if she were an intruder, as if she had brought this upon them. Her stomach twisted, but she straightened her posture, refusing to let them see any sign of weakness. She knew the name too well—Star-Lord, the fabled, almost mythical soldier Thanos held above all others, a figure feared across the galaxy, he was merciless, loyal to Thanos, killed any who opposed him without a second thought. Yet she had never imagined crossing paths with him, let alone being claimed by him.

The murmuring grew louder, suspicion turning to resentment, jealousy, even anger. A few of the women looked away, eyes narrowed, avoiding her gaze with something akin to disdain. To them, she was no longer one of them. She was someone marked, someone who didn’t deserve their trust.

“What are you talking about?” Gamora’s voice was low, hands clenched by her sides, shaking.

“What’s wrong? Didn’t think you’d be protected, princess?” he taunted, leaning in. “Star- Lord made sure to claim you himself. That’s why you’re acting so brave, isn’t it? You think you’re above everyone else here because you have his protection.”

“That’s a lie!” Gamora snapped, feeling their stares, the silent judgment. “I don’t know him.”

Korak’s smirk only deepened as he stepped closer, tilting his head to regard her with fake amusement.

“Lying won’t help you here, girl. They all know the truth now.” He turned back to the others, addressing them with a twisted grin. “See, that’s why she’s bold. Because she’s special— Star-Lord’s little pet. Thanos wouldn’t want him upset, now, would he?”

The women looked at her with renewed venom, and Gamora could feel the intensity of their silent hatred. She gritted her teeth, fighting against the urge to retaliate. Korak was playing her like a pawn, twisting everything to isolate her further, to make her a target.

"From what I understand, Star-Lord is very… particular about his belongings.”

“What’s so special about her?” one woman spat, her voice thick with jealousy and bitterness. “Why does she get to be spared?”

“She’s already claimed by Thanos’s most loyalists, that’s why,” Korak replied smoothly. “Unlike the rest of you, she has a special place. She won’t be joining you in the auction because she’s already been chosen.”

A ripple of agitation ran through the crowd, and a young woman with pink skin and fearful eyes took a shaky step forward, her voice laced with anger.

“Why did he choose her?” she demanded, her gaze accusatory. “Does she know Star-Lord personally?”

“He’s lying! I have no connection to Star-Lord; I’m not here by choice!” Gamora's voice cracking.

“You’re wasting your breath, Gamora,” he taunted. “They know the truth, and they’ll treat you as you deserve. Enjoy your place as the privileged one—whether or not you admit it, you’re different.”

Why her? Why would Thanos’ most ruthless soldier choose her? She barely knew of Star- Lord beyond the tales that whispered through hushed circles—a masked figure, brutal, a weapon honed to perfection under Thanos’ command. Nobody knew who lay behind that mask, nor what twisted ambitions he harbored. And yet, for reasons beyond her comprehension, he had chosen her.

A young woman with pale violet skin and deep-set eyes glared at her, her voice a mocking hiss. “That must be nice,” she sneered. “To already have a master lined up. No auction, no uncertainty—you’re practically royalty compared to the rest of us.”

She clenched her fists, the anger bubbling to the surface, defying every instinct that warned her to keep her head down.

“I don’t know anything about this, just like the rest of you!” she snapped, her voice sharp and trembling with rage. “Don’t act as if I’m happy about what Korak said. Do you think I want this?”

The group of women around her fell silent, but their expressions remained stony, unsympathetic. In their eyes, she saw resentment harden into anger. It didn’t matter what she said—they saw her as an outsider, already separate from their suffering.

“Easy for you to say now,” another woman muttered, her voice bitter. “You don’t have to worry about who’ll be bidding on you in some twisted market.”

“None of you know what he might have planned,” she countered, the panic edging her voice despite her best efforts to keep calm. “This isn’t some blessing—he’s a monster. Whatever he wants, it’s bound to be as twisted as anything else Thanos has done.”

But the women only exchanged bitter glances, a few crossing their arms as they continued to watch her with mistrust. It didn’t matter that she was standing in the same dark room, that she was shackled just like the rest of them—Korak’s poisonous words had driven a wedge between her and the others. She had become the enemy in their eyes, and they resented her all the more for the isolation she bore.

One of the women let out a low, derisive laugh. “Oh, but we’re sure you’ll be just fine,” she said, her tone cutting. “With a powerful monster like Star-Lord waiting for you, how could you not be?”

Before she could even think, she hurled herself toward the woman, grabbing her collar with a fierce grip.

“Careful with what you say,” Gamora hissed, her voice low and threatening.

The woman’s laughter faded, replaced by surprise as Gamora’s fierce gaze locked onto hers.

“You think you can just sit there and mock me?” Gamora continued, her grip tightening as she leaned in closer, forcing the woman to meet her gaze. “I am not your plaything, and I won’t be made a fool of.”

Before the woman could respond, another girl rushed forward, trying to pull Gamora away.

“Let her go! You’re only making things worse!” she shouted, panic lacing her tone as she tugged at Gamora’s arm.

“Get your fucking hands off me!” She snarled, shaking the girl off with enough force to send her stumbling back. Her focus snapped back to the woman still trapped in her grasp, her breathing ragged with fury.

The woman, despite being cornered, smirked again, her voice dripping with disdain. “And what are you going to do? Beat the truth out of me, Gamora? It won’t change the fact that you’re a whore.”

Without hesitation, Gamora hurled the woman to the ground, her body landing with a loud thud. Before anyone could intervene, Gamora straddled her, pinning her down with the force of her weight. Her fists flew, each punch landing with a sickening crack against the woman’s face. Blood splattered across her knuckles, but Gamora didn’t care. She was lost in her rage, each strike an outlet for the storm raging inside her.

“Please, stop!” someone yelled, but their voice was distant, drowned out by the roaring in her ears.

She didn’t stop. She couldn’t stop. The woman beneath her writhed, trying to shield her face, but Gamora’s relentless assault was too much.

It wasn’t until the searing pain of electricity coursed through her body that Gamora finally froze, in a second her muscles seized, vision blurred, and she felt herself being wrenched away from the woman, who was now sobbing and bloodied on the floor. Gamora’s limbs flailed weakly as the guards dragged her back, her breath coming in ragged, uneven gasps.

The metallic taste of blood filled her mouth, and she realized it wasn’t just the woman who had been hurt. Her own lip was split, and her fists throbbed, swollen from the repeated impacts.

The guards shoved her roughly into the corner of the room, their batons raised in warning. “Stay down!” one of them barked, his tone sharp and commanding.

Gamora glared at him, defiance flickering in her eyes even as her body betrayed her, slumping against the wall. The cold metal cuffs around her wrists bit into her skin, the final insult to her already battered pride.

And that was how she ended up in this cell, alone.

Days turned into weeks, or maybe months. She had lost all sense of time.

Her knees pulled tightly to her chest. The walls around her were slick with moisture, the air suffocating. The faint, musky smell of mildew mixed with the metallic tang of rusted iron bars. She was utterly, completely alone.

Irani was gone. Nebula was gone. Everyone was gone.

Her resistance had been crushed under Thanos’s might. All their efforts, all their sacrifices, had amounted to nothing. And now, Thanos stood triumphant, a conqueror of Xandar.

Her head throbbed, a relentless, pounding ache that came and went in waves. It felt like something had been stolen from her—a part of herself wrenched away. No matter how hard she tried to focus, to remember, she couldn’t figure out what it was. The uncertainty ate away at her, an itch she couldn’t scratch, a wound that wouldn’t heal.

The cell was always dimly lit, the flickering light from a single bulb above casting eerie shadows on the stone walls. It was perpetually cold, the kind of chill that seeped into her bones and refused to leave. She had no mirror, but she didn’t need one to know she was fading. Her strength, her spirit—everything that had once defined her—was slipping away.

At first, she’d tried to fight it. She had raged, screamed, and thrown herself against the bars until her hands were raw and bleeding. But no one had come. No one had answered. And eventually, she’d stopped. The silence was the worst part, a deafening void that swallowed her whole.

Sometimes, she thought about giving up. It would be so easy, wouldn’t it? To just let go, to slip into that quiet darkness and finally find peace. She thought of her parents, of Nebula, of Irani, and everyone else who had fought beside her. Maybe they were waiting for her. Maybe, if she closed her eyes and stopped breathing, she could join them.

But then, Irani’s face would flash in her mind. Her steadfast gaze, her calm voice, her last words before she fell in die: “You have to survive this war.”

Her daily existence had been reduced to a monotonous cycle. She counted. She counted the fingers on her hands, over and over, as if to reassure herself that she was still real, still whole. She counted the strands of hair that fell from her head, catching them on her fingers and letting them drift to the ground. She counted the footsteps of the guards as they passed by her cell, each one echoing faintly in the distance.

Food came twice a day. She marked the difference between the two meals to measure time. Breakfast was a bowl of tasteless porridge and a cup of water. Dinner was a plate of Orloni meat, stringy and overcooked, accompanied by another cup of water. At first, she had refused to eat, spitting the food back out in defiance. But hunger had eventually driven her to give in. Now, she ate mechanically, without thought or feeling. It was survival, nothing more.

The pounding in her head grew worse with each passing day, like a hammer against her skull. Sometimes, it brought flashes of images—disjointed, fleeting memories that vanished before she could grasp them. A voice, a face, a laugh that seemed so familiar but disappeared quickly.

She pressed her hands to her temples, trying to will the pain away. It didn’t help. It never did.

Her body had grown weaker, her muscles wasting away from lack of use. She could barely stand for more than a few minutes without feeling lightheaded. The proud, stubborn woman who had once stood against Thanos and his armies was now a broken shell of herself.

And yet, she kept counting. Because if she stopped, if she let her mind wander too far, she was afraid she might lose herself entirely. Counting was the only tether she had left, the only thing keeping her grounded.

But as the days stretched on and the darkness closed in around her, she wasn’t sure how much longer she could hold on.

The first thing she became aware of was light. Harsh, sterile, and glaring, it pierced through her closed eyelids, pulling her unwillingly from the fragile cocoon of sleep she had managed to find. Gamora’s eyes fluttered open, wincing as the brightness burned into her retinas. The sudden change was disorienting, the ceiling above her foreign—too high, too clean, too bright compared to the dim, oppressive walls of her cell.

The unfamiliar sensation of clean fabric beneath her fingers told her she was no longer in her cell.

The room spun wildly around her, and for a moment, she couldn’t form a coherent thought. Her dry, cracked lips moved of their own accord, and a single word slipped out in a hoarse, trembling whisper.

“Help.”

The word felt alien, weak, and desperate. But it had escaped before she could stop it.

As her surroundings slowly came into focus, she realized she wasn’t on the hard floor anymore. She was lying on a narrow, uncomfortable hospital bunk, the sterile scent of antiseptic filling the air.

“You’re awake.” A calm, female voice cut through the haze of her disorientation. Gamora turned her head slightly, her muscles protesting the movement, and saw a woman standing at her bedside. Dressed in the stark white uniform of a healer—or perhaps a medic—the woman regarded her with an expression of detached professionalism.

“Help,” Gamora croaked again, her voice breaking on the single syllable.

The healer’s face softened slightly, a flicker of what might have been pity crossing her features. “It’s okay,” she said, her tone soothing but distant, as if she had said those words a hundred times before to other broken souls. “This will only take a moment. It’s just a quick test."

“What… test?” she managed to rasp, her throat dry and raw from days—weeks?—of silence.

Before the healer could respond, another figure approached—a nurse, younger, her expression blank as she clutched a file against her chest. “Gamora Zen. Twenty-three years old,” the nurse recited in a clipped tone, her eyes not even glancing at Gamora.

The healer nodded, acknowledging the nurse with a brief glance. She moved to Gamora’s side, her hands deft and impersonal as they began their work.

Gamora flinched when the healer touched her hair, the sensation foreign and invasive. The healer’s fingers combed through the tangled strands with clinical precision, separating and inspecting as though she were handling an object instead of a person. Gamora’s stomach churned with a mix of anger and shame, but she lacked the strength to do anything other than lie there and endure.

“Hmm,” the healer murmured, jotting something down on a notepad. The sound of the pen scratching against paper grated on Gamora’s nerves, a reminder of her helplessness.

Her hands moved to Gamora’s face next, pressing gently along her cheekbones, jawline, and temples. The touch was detached, mechanical, as if she were evaluating the condition of a piece of furniture rather than a living being. Gamora’s fists clenched weakly at her sides, her nails digging into her palms.

The healer’s voice broke the silence, low and almost conversational. “Beautiful face,” she remarked, as though commenting on a painting in a gallery.

The words, casual and offhanded, felt like a slap. She wanted to retort, to lash out, to demand that the woman stop treating her like a thing—but she couldn’t. Her body, so drained of energy, refused to obey her commands.

Her mind screamed at her to move, to fight, to run, but she was paralyzed, pinned by her own weakness. Tears welled in her eyes, a bitter cocktail of frustration and humiliation, but she refused to let them fall.

The healer stepped back, her pen gliding smoothly over the notepad as she spoke without looking up. “Her vitals are weak but stable enough. Record the malnutrition markers, and she’s for Star-Lord.”

That name again.

Gamora’s pulse quickened, a faint flicker of defiance stirring within her, but it was quickly drowned out by the overwhelming fatigue that gripped her body. Her head throbbed, the pounding pain making it hard to focus on anything other than the sterile, blinding lights above that feel suffocating.

“Understood,” the nurse replied curtly, scribbling in the file without sparing Gamora a glance.

Summoning what little strength she had left, Gamora tried to speak. “What… is this for?” Her voice came out as little more than a strained whisper, cracking under the effort.

The healer glanced at her with annoyance. “Just a little check before you’re delivered to your master,” she said dismissively.

Master. The word sent a cold wave of dread coursing through Gamora’s veins, making her feel even more hollow than before.

The healer’s hands moved again, this time pressing firmly against Gamora’s shoulder. Her fingers dug into the muscle, testing its firmness with an impersonal precision. “Superior strength,” she murmured approvingly, almost to herself. “And a certain resilience. No signs of injury or old wounds.”

Gamora flinched as the cold fingers traveled along her collarbone, pressing and prodding with clinical detachment. Each touch felt like an invasion, stripping away another piece of her already fragile sense of self.

The healer continued her examination, her movements methodical. Her fingers trailed down the line of Gamora’s sternum, pausing near her ribcage. Though her touch was deliberate and devoid of any overt malice, every moment felt like a calculated attack on Gamora’s dignity, leaving her raw and exposed.

“Hold still,” the healer murmured, her tone devoid of empathy. It wasn’t a request; it was a command. Her hands probed the flesh around her ribs.

“Star-Lord has requested you be kept in prime health,” she continued, sounds frustratred. “He prefers things… perfect.”

The words were a slap in the face, though Gamora lacked the strength to react. Perfect. The idea felt like a cruel joke in her current state. She was anything but perfect—malnourished, weak, her spirit battered by months of isolation and despair.

“How long has she been in the cell?” the healer asked abruptly, turning to the nurse with an almost accusatory tone.

The nurse flipped through the file with practiced efficiency before replying, “Six months and twenty-three days, ma’am.”

The healer froze for a moment, her brow furrowing in disbelief. “Are they insane?” she snapped, her voice suddenly sharp. “She’s malnourished, and she’s likely developed agoraphobia.”

Her eyes flicked back to Gamora, this time filled with something akin to frustration, as though blaming her for the condition she’d been forced into.

Gamora’s head throbbed harder, the pain now an unbearable drumbeat that seemed to echo through her entire skull. She couldn’t muster the strength to respond, let alone move. Her limbs felt like lead, her body betraying her in its exhaustion.

The healer let out a sharp, frustrated exhale, her irritation palpable in the sterile, suffocating air of the room. She scribbled furiously on her pad, her pen nearly tearing through the paper with each stroke. “This is unacceptable,” she muttered under her breath. “Star-Lord will be furious.”

The nurse stiffened but kept her composure. “It’s not our responsibility, ma’am. Korak are the ones who’ve been managing the captivity of the women,” she stated.

The healer’s head snapped up, her sharp glare pinning the nurse in place. “Do you even know who Star-Lord is? He’s insane. He doesn’t care who’s responsible. He’ll only care that she’s deteriorating under our watch. Look at her!” She gestured to Gamora, her voice rising with each word. “She’s clearly dying.”

The nurse hesitated, glancing at Gamora’s frail, battered form. Her eyes flicked to the healer’s pad, and she nodded reluctantly. “What do you want me to do?”

“Check her head,” the healer ordered, her voice clipped but commanding.

Without hesitation, the nurse retrieved a handheld device and pressed it gently to Gamora’s temple. The device hummed faintly as it scanned, its small screen flickering with lines of data. The nurse watched the results appear, her face tightening with concern.

“This… says she’s lost several memories,” the nurse finally said, almost hesitant.

“What’s the cause?” the healer demanded, her sharp tone slicing through the tension in the room.

The nurse hesitated, glancing down at the device’s screen again as if hoping she had misread the results. “The Mind Stone,” she said at last, her voice barely above a whisper.

The healer froze, her face contorting with disbelief. “What?” she snapped, snatching the device from the nurse’s hand and studying the data herself. Her eyes darted across the screen, and her lips pressed into a thin, grim line. “This is bad. This is very bad.”

Gamora groaned weakly, her head rolling to the side. The pounding in her skull had intensified, a relentless, searing ache that made it difficult to process their words. Her voice was barely audible when she rasped, “What… what happened to me?”

The healer ignored her, too absorbed in the data. “The Mind Stone. Of all the things…” she muttered under her breath, her hand tightening around the device. “No wonder she’s in this state.”

“She was exposed to it directly,” the nurse added, her voice trembling slightly now. “The scan shows significant neurological disruption. It’s likely the stone not only erased parts of her memory but also damaged the neural pathways responsible for retaining them.” “Of course, it did,” the healer snapped, slamming the device onto the counter. “Who in their right mind exposes someone to the Mind Stone without precautions? And now we’re the ones left to clean up this mess?”

Gamora groaned again, louder this time, her voice ragged with pain and frustration. “What… are you talking about?” she managed to force out, each word an effort. Her eyes fluttered open, glazed and unfocused, but filled with a flicker of desperation. “What… did they do to me?”

The healer glanced down at Gamora, her brows furrowing as she studied the broken woman lying before her. For a fleeting moment, her stern expression softened into something resembling sympathy. “They used the Mind Stone on you,” she said bluntly, her voice quieter now, tinged with irritation but not without a trace of pity. “To control you. To break you.”

Gamora’s heart clenched at the words, her throat tightening as the pain in her head pulsed sharper, like a cruel drumbeat. She forced herself to speak, her voice barely above a whisper. “Who?” she asked, her lips trembling. “Who did this to me?”

The healer straightened slightly, brushing a lock of hair out of her face with an air of disdain. “Who? Of course, Korak,” she replied, as if the answer were obvious.

Gamora’s brow furrowed, confusion swirling with the pounding ache in her skull. “Korak?” she repeated, her voice rasping with disbelief. “I haven’t seen Korak since… since I was thrown in the cell.”

The nurse and healer exchanged a glance, their expressions momentarily mirroring each other’s confusion. “You’re certain?” the healer pressed, leaning closer to Gamora as if trying to discern whether she was lying or simply mistaken.

Gamora closed her eyes for a moment, willing herself to push past the pain clouding her thoughts. Her memory felt like a tattered map, pieces missing, some parts blurred beyond recognition, but one thing she was certain of: the silence. The deafening, unbroken silence of her imprisonment.

“I’m a hundred percent sure,” she said, her voice steadier now, though her body remained frail and trembling. “No one ever came to that cell. No one. It was just… me. Silent. Empty. No sounds. No visits. Nothing.”

The healer frowned, her fingers drumming against the edge of her clipboard. She turned to the nurse. “Could it have been during the battle?” she asked, her tone thoughtful but still clinical.

“Maybe,” the nurse offered, her voice tentative. She glanced down at the device still in her hand, its display showing an array of data on Gamora’s neural state. “If she encountered the Mind Stone on the battlefield, the effects could have lingered. Even if she doesn’t recall it consciously, it could explain the gaps in her memory.”

The healer nodded slowly, as if the pieces of a puzzle were beginning to align in her mind. But Gamora’s confusion only deepened, her thoughts swirling with unanswered questions. Could they be right? Had the Mind Stone been used on her during a fight? Had someone— something—tampered with her memories in the chaos of battle? But why? To what end? Why not just killed her?

She groaned softly, lifting a trembling hand to her temple. “It hurts,” she murmured, the words escaping her lips unbidden.

The healer’s gaze flicked to her, her expression still professional but not entirely devoid of empathy. “That’s to be expected,” she said, her voice firm but not unkind. “The Mind Stone’s effects aren’t just mental. It disrupts the neural pathways, creates physical pain. If it’s been left untreated for this long…” She trailed off, shaking her head.

Her memories felt like scattered fragments of glass, sharp and broken, cutting deeper with every attempt to piece them together. “Why would they do this?” she asked, her voice trembling but laced with a flicker of defiance. “What… what do they want from me?”

The healer didn’t answer immediately. Instead, she glanced at the nurse, who shifted uncomfortably under the weight of Gamora’s question. Finally, the healer sighed, tucking the clipboard under her arm. “I don’t know,” she admitted, her voice quieter now. “But whatever the reason, it’s not over. Whoever did this to you… they had a purpose. They don’t use the Mind Stone lightly.”

“Will she recover?” the nurse asked cautiously, her voice barely above a whisper.

The healer didn’t answer immediately. She stared at Gamora, her eyes scanning the hollow cheeks, the gaunt frame, the haunted eyes. Finally, she shook her head, her expression grim. “Physically, perhaps. But her memories? Her mind?” She exhaled sharply, shaking her head again. “It’s impossible to say. Damage from the Mind Stone is… irreversible in most cases.”

Gamora’s head throbbed harder, the pain mingling with the anguish of the healer’s words. She closed her eyes, willing herself to block out the reality, but the healer’s voice pulled her back.

“She’s still alive, though,” the healer continued, almost to herself now. “And that means she still has a purpose. Star-Lord won’t care about the details as long as she’s functional.”

The nurse hesitated, shifting uncomfortably. “What do we tell him?”

“We tell him the truth,” the healer replied coldly, straightening her posture. “She’s malnourished, mentally compromised, and in critical condition—but she’s alive. If he’s as insane as they say, that’s all that will matter.”

Gamora’s heart sank, the faint ember of hope she’d clung to extinguished by their words. She was nothing more than a tool, a pawn in a game she didn’t understand. And now, even her own memories, part of her, had been stolen from her.

The healer’s voice cut through Gamora’s spinning thoughts. “We’ll continue the examination,” she said briskly, her hands moving lower. She pressed lightly beneath Gamora’s ribcage, her fingers cold and clinical as they brushed along her abdomen. “Too thin,” she muttered, turning toward the nurse with a scowl. “Seriously, did they even feed her? How do they expect her to survive like this?”

The nurse shrugged uncomfortably, avoiding eye contact as she scribbled notes on her pad. “She’s been in isolation for over six months. Rations were minimal to keep her subdued. Orders came from higher up.”

“Idiocy,” the healer snapped. “This will have to be corrected immediately. Star-Lord will expect a partner capable of carrying strong children. Her body must be properly nourished and fortified.” Her hand lingered on Gamora’s abdomen, her expression critical. “Zehoberei strength is well-documented. It will balance out certain… weaknesses.”

Gamora’s lips parted slightly, her breath hitching as she tried to process what she’d just heard. Her stomach churned violently, the bile rising in her throat as the healer’s words echoed in her mind. Carrying strong children. The phrase felt like a slap, reducing her existence to nothing more than a function—a tool for someone else’s twisted purpose.

The healer straightened, crossing her arms. “Terrans,” she continued with a dismissive wave of her hand. “They lack the strength, the resilience of other species. Their bones are too fragile, their lifespans too short.” Her voice dripped with disdain, as though the very idea of frailty disgusted her.

Gamora’s brow furrowed despite the haze of pain clouding her mind. “Terran?” she rasped, her voice faint but steady. “Who’s… Terran?”

The healer’s lips curled into a smirk. “Star-Lord, of course. Surprised, aren’t you?”

Star-Lord is Terran? This ruthless figure, Thanos's prized enforcer, the same man who had climbed Thanos's ranks with ease and brutality—was a Terran? The revelation hit her with a wave of revulsion. She had spent years imagining the figure behind that notorious name, picturing him as a monstrous alien with powers to match his cruelty. And yet, he was human —a creature from a race she'd once heard was gentle and frail.

Her head lolled to the side as the healer leaned closer, watching her with a mixture of triumph and exasperation. “Nice try,” the healer muttered, shaking her head. “But not this time.”

The next time she woke, her entire body felt like it had been crushed under a mountain. Pain radiated from her spine, sharp and unrelenting, and her muscles ached as though they had been stretched and torn apart.

Gamora blinked, her vision swimming as she tried to focus. The healer stood over her, arms crossed and an infuriating smirk plastered across her face.

“You’re free of the body modifications now,” the healer said, her tone laced with mock sympathy. “Don’t even try to fight back. The healing process will take longer now without your precious implants.”

Gamora’s chest tightened as the words sank in. The modifications are gone. The enhancements that had been a part of her for so long—the ones she had chosen to make herself stronger, faster, more capable—were gone. Torn from her body, leaving her weak and vulnerable.

She gritted her teeth, refusing to let the tears brimming in her eyes fall, but the pain was too overwhelming. She could feel it deep in her spine, an empty void where the implant had once been, supporting her, giving her the edge she needed to fight back against the horrors she faced.

“You’ll be delivered to Star-Lord tomorrow,” the healer continued, her smirk widening as she saw the tears slip silently down Gamora’s cheeks. “I hear he’s looking forward to it.”

The thought of being handed over to him, broken and defeated, was unbearable. She clenched her fists, her nails digging into her palms as she fought to contain the storm of emotions threatening to consume her.

Through gritted teeth, she muttered, “You’ll pay for this.” Her voice was hoarse but steady, a quiet promise filled with venom.

“Right, right, Gamora. I’m sure I’ll regret this someday,” she said with a mockingly sweet tone. “But for now, I’d say this is a fair farewell, don’t you think? Your last night here, all nice and cozy.”

Her smirk deepened as she leaned in closer. “You might want to rest up. Tomorrow’s going to be... eventful.”

Gamora didn’t respond. She didn’t have the strength to lash out, but she locked eyes with the healer, pouring all her hatred and defiance into that one glare. It was a silent vow, a promise that no matter how weak or broken she felt now, she wouldn’t stay that way.

As the healer walked away, leaving Gamora strapped to the bunk, the room fell silent save for the faint hum of the fluorescent lights. Alone in the sterile, suffocating space, Gamora let the tears fall freely, her body trembling with pain and frustration.

They dressed her in a stark black gown, its fabric heavy and constricting, paired with a white bonnet hat that obscured most of her face. The ensemble wasn’t just clothing—it was humiliation, a deliberate choice to rob her of dignity and identity. Her hands trembled as she tried to steady herself, but the sharp pain radiating from her back made every movement excruciating.

Korak grabbed her arm roughly, dragging her forward. His grip was iron, and his smug expression made her blood boil.

"You finally get to meet him, huh?" Korak sneered.

Gamora clenched her jaw, refusing to react. She wouldn’t give him the satisfaction of seeing her pain, even though every step sent fresh waves of agony through her spine.

Korak yanked her toward a sleek black vehicle that stood waiting in the dimly lit courtyard. Its design was unlike anything she had seen on Xandar before—angular, ominous, and utterly devoid of the elegance her planet had once been known for.

He shoved her unceremoniously into the back seat, her body collapsing against the cold, metallic surface of the interior. "Do not try to run," he said, his voice cold and taunting. "He’ll hunt you down. You can’t escape him."

He tapped the vehicle’s exterior, and the door slid shut with a hiss. Gamora’s world darkened as the windows tinted themselves, sealing her inside. She could hear the faint whir of the vehicle’s engine as it began to move, gliding smoothly down the empty streets.

Escape? The word felt almost laughable. She could barely sit upright, let alone run. Her back throbbed with relentless pain, and every slight jolt of the vehicle seemed to drive a dagger deeper into her spine. Maybe I won’t need to escape, she thought bitterly. Maybe this pain will kill me before I even arrive.

Her gaze flicked around the vehicle. There was no driver—just her and the sterile silence of the autopilot. It felt more like a coffin than a car. She let her head rest against the cool surface of the window, staring out at the desolate streets.

Xandar, once vibrant and full of life, was unrecognizable. The streets were lined with crumbling buildings, their bright facades replaced by a dull, oppressive gray. The people shuffled along the sidewalks with their heads bowed, their movements mechanical and lifeless.

Her heart ached at the sight. This wasn’t the Xandar she had fought for, the Xandar she had once called home. It was a shadow of its former self, crushed under Thanos’ regime.

As the vehicle glided forward, a disembodied voice broke the silence. “Miss Zen is in pain. Here is the prescribed medication.”

A small compartment opened in front of her, revealing a tray with a few pills neatly arranged.

Gamora stared at them, her face contorted with disgust. “Shut the fuck up,” she hissed, slapping the tray away. The pills scattered across the floor, clinking softly as they rolled into the corners of the vehicle.

The machine made no reply, its silence almost mocking her outburst. She hated it—the cold, calculated indifference of technology designed to control, to subdue. It reminded her of everything she loathed about this new reality.

She leaned back, her breaths shallow and labored, trying to push through the fog of pain and despair that threatened to consume her. If this vehicle malfunctions and crashes, maybe that would be better, she thought grimly.

The vehicle continued its smooth, unrelenting journey, and Gamora could only watch as the remnants of her world passed by in a blur.

And it stopped abruptly. Gamora’s pulse quickened as she stared out at the sight before her. A massive compound loomed ahead, painted in a shade so dark it seemed to absorb light. The structure was cold and menacing, like a nightmare brought to life. Her stomach churned with a mix of pain and dread as recognition set in—this was Thanos’ Sanctuary.

The compound stood at the heart of Hala, Xandar’s once-thriving capital city. Hala had been the first to fall when Thanos decided to conquer Xandar, years before. She hadn’t been here since those days, and the transformation was staggering. The vibrant streets she remembered were gone, replaced by bleak ruins and oppressive silence.

Before she could gather her thoughts, the vehicle’s door slid open with a soft hiss. A figure was already waiting outside—a gaunt, pale creature whose sharp features made him look more alien than human.

Ebony Maw.

His presence sent a chill down her spine. He approached with a slow, deliberate gait, his yellowed eyes locking onto her.

“Welcome,” he said, his thin lips curling into a sneer, “Star-Lord’s pet.”

Gamora’s fists clenched at the insult, but she forced herself to stay silent. She knew better than to let anger cloud her judgment, not here, not now.

Maw lifted her from the vehicle using his telekinetic powers, her body jerking upward without her consent. He wasn’t gentle. The invisible force slammed her to the ground with just enough force to hurt.

She bit back a groan, her back screaming in protest. Pain radiated from the spot where her implant had been ripped away.

Maw grabbed her arm, dragging her inside the compound. Every step sent fresh jolts of agony through her body.

"Enough," a deep, commanding voice called out from behind them, stopping Maw in his tracks.

Gamora froze. She didn’t need to turn around to recognize the voice—it was unmistakable.

Star-Lord.

Maw released her arm abruptly, and without his support, her knees buckled. She braced herself for the cold, hard floor, but instead, a gloved hand caught her mid-fall.

The grip was firm, steadying her, and for a fleeting moment, she felt the briefest reprieve from the constant pain. But when she looked up, all she could see was him.

He towered over her, his broad shoulders casting an imposing shadow that swallowed her entirely. His mask, with its glowing red eyes, obscured any hint of humanity. The hood over his head added to his terrifying presence. From this close, she couldn’t tell if he had hair or even a face beneath the mask.

Despite the pain and fear threatening to consume her, Gamora glared at him with open disgust. She wouldn’t let him intimidate her.

"Beautiful," he said, his voice deep and measured. “Just like I remember.”

Her breath caught in her throat. Just like I remember? The words sent her mind reeling. Did they know each other? The thought was absurd. Her heart thudded loudly in her chest, betraying her growing unease.

Star-Lord’s gloved hand moved to her back. She flinched instinctively, her body reacting to the memory of countless hands that had dragged, restrained, and hurt her. But his touch wasn’t harsh—not gentle, but careful, as though he were conscious of her injuries.

She hated that it caught her off guard.

Without a word, he pulled her forward, his grip shifting to her arm. The sharp tug sent a fresh wave of pain through her, and she hissed despite herself. He ignored her reaction, she had no power here.

They stopped in front of a massive metal door, its surface etched with intricate patterns that glowed faintly in the dim light.

Gamora turned her glare on him again, her defiance burning through the haze of pain. He didn’t react, his masked face giving nothing away.

The door slid open with a low rumble, revealing a chamber that took her breath away—for all the wrong reasons.

At the center of the room, seated on a massive throne, was Thanos.

His hulking form was unmistakable, his presence suffocating. The golden Infinity Gauntlet on his hand gleamed menacingly, each stone pulsing with raw, unimaginable power. The sight of it made her stomach churn.

“Son,” Thanos rumbled, his voice like distant thunder.

Gamora’s heart sank. Son. She had suspected some connection between Star-Lord and Thanos, but this? This was worse than anything she could have imagined.

"Father," Star-Lord replied, his voice steady and unyielding. “Here’s my choice.”

Gamora’s head snapped toward him, her glare turning venomous. Choice? Did he think she was some kind of object to be presented, traded, or discarded?

The audacity.

Star-Lord’s grip tightened on her arm, his strength unyielding. His gloved hand felt like an iron shackle, and despite her pain, she refused to show weakness. Gamora glared at Thanos, her brown eyes burning with defiance even as her body betrayed her fragility.

“You’re quite famous, Gamora,” Thanos said, his voice dripping with mockery. He leaned forward on his throne, the gauntlet resting on his massive hand shimmering in the dim light. The Reality Stone, embedded within the glove, pulsed faintly.

“And here is my son, insistent on bringing you home,” he continued, his deep voice reverberating through the chamber.

Gamora’s lip curled in disgust. “Your son is a monster,” she spat, her voice cutting through the tense silence, “and you’re pure evil. Neither of you deserve—”

Her voice cut off abruptly, her breath catching in her throat. She clutched at her neck, panic flashing in her eyes as she tried to force out the words.

Thanos’ smile was cold, cruel. The Reality Stone on his gauntlet glowed brightly, casting an eerie red light across her face.

“Better,” Thanos said simply, the single word laden with malice.

Gamora struggled, her body jerking against Star-Lord’s hold. But he was too strong. Even with only one hand, he held her effortlessly, his grip like a vice. Her strength was nothing compared to his, especially now—weak and broken as she was.

“You’ll remain silent until I allow you to speak,” Thanos declared, his tone leaving no room for argument.

Gamora glared at him, the fire in her eyes undiminished even as she fought against her silenced voice.

“Now, now, Peter,” Thanos said, leaning back into his throne, his gauntlet resting on the armrest with a casualness that belied its immense power. “Show her who you really are.”

Peter? She wracked her brain, searching for a connection, a memory, anything. Who was Peter?

“Yes, Father,” Star-Lord said, his voice steady and obedient.

With one hand still gripping her arm, his other hand moved to the edge of his hood. Slowly, he began to pull it back, the fabric sliding away to reveal his head.

The first thing she saw was his hair—thick, dark brunette strands, tousled but clean. Her breath hitched, not because of recognition but because it made him seem more human, more real.

Then, his fingers moved to the side of his face, just behind his ear. With a faint shimmer, the mask dissolved. It didn’t fall away like a piece of fabric; it disintegrated into nothingness, revealing the face beneath.

Gamora staggered, despite his firm hold on her arm. Her body jerked back instinctively, but there was no escaping him. Her knees threatened to give out entirely as she stared at him, her mind racing to process what she was seeing.

The man beneath the mask wasn’t a stranger.

It couldn’t be...

Her heart slammed against her ribcage, her breathing shallow as her mind fought to connect the image in front of her with the one buried deep in her memory.

His face was leaner now, more angular, the soft edges of youth long gone, replaced by sharp cheekbones and a jawline that spoke of years of survival and adaptation. Time had carved itself into his features, but his eyes... those eyes.

A deep, green, piercing, the same ones she remembered from years ago. Yet, they were different now. Colder. Harder. They no longer held the nervous vulnerability she once associated with him. Instead, they reflected something darker, honed and dangerous.

Her knees weakened, and her body swayed slightly in his unrelenting grip.

And then, Bright eyes.

"Bright eyes," he said at the same time her mind recalled his voice inside.
