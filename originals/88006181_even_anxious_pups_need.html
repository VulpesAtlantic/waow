Chapter 1

The fresh air market was Agatha’s favorite place in Paris. She loved heckling with the vendors, taking the time to find the best-looking lettuce, and making the cute cheese monger blush when she flirted with her. But, undoubtedly, the best part of the Fresh Air market was that it was approximately 3,432 miles away from the Salem Pack. 

 

So, when a feeling of doom settled thickly over Agatha, making the hairs on the back of her neck stand straight up, and a cloyingly familiar scent filled her nostrils, Agatha told herself that she was imagining things. The smell was probably some ludicrously expensive cheese that was too sophisticated for her American palate. 

 

Agatha went back to purusing the homemade jams, but when a firm hand gripped her upper arm, she turned sharply. 

 

“Mais qu’est-ce qui se passe, putain?” She snarled, trying to yank her arm free so she could upper cut whoever the fuck just laid their hands on her. Agatha pulled away harder, but the hand stayed firm, and a flash of all too familiar claws protracted, piercing cleanly through Agatha’s leather jacket and scraping into her skin. 

 

This was when Agatha realized the smell had not, in fact, just been in her head.  Agatha clenched her jaw and met her mother’s gaze head-on. The fucking cunt had found her. 

 

Time had not been kind to Evanora. Her skin was sallow, and her hair was completely gray. Deep lines creased her forehead, and although she still had the aura of a powerful alpha, she seemed smaller, somehow. 

 

“Come, Agatha,” Evanora said, lip curling into a snarl as she tried to pull Agatha away from the market. 

 

Agatha planted her feet and didn’t move an inch as her heart dropped to her stomach. Evanora’s grasp became tighter, and she laughed. “I can smell your fear. You always were such a coward.”

 

Agatha grit her teeth, “Let go of me.” 

 

“You think I’m that idiotic? I know the moment I let go, you're going to run.” 

 

“Who wouldn’t?” Agatha spat. 

 

“Daughters who respect their mothers.” 

 

“You’re no mother to me.” Agatha hissed. People were starting to stare. Good. Let them. 

 

Evanora leaned closer. Agatha flinched at the rancid smell of her breath. “You’re lucky I didn’t kill you the moment I found you. You broke an oath to your pack, I’d be in the right.” 

 

Agatha’s heart sped up. She’d seen her mother kill for much smaller indiscretions. 

 

“If you wanted me dead, you would have just sent one of your henchmen.” Agatha narrowed her eyes. 

 

Evanora smiled slightly and looked around. Agatha followed her eyes and saw four of her mother’s seconds all standing guard, and ready to pounce. 

 

“All this backup, I’m flattered.” Agatha quipped. 

 

“If you run, they’ve been instructed to kill,” Evanora said. She finally dropped her hand from Agatha’s arm and protracted her claws. Agatha watched as her own blood dripped from her mother’s fingertips. 

 

“You’re coming home, Agatha. I’ve let this little Parisian farce go on long enough. You’re not a bohemian. You have responsibilities and duties.” 

 

Agatha felt dizzy. Her sense of freedom had been completely unfounded. Her mother had been keeping tabs on her the whole time. Of course. Agatha swallowed thickly and wiped furiously at her eyes. She was an idiot to have believed otherwise. 

 

“If you knew where I was, why are you just showing up now?” 

 

“I thought you’d come to your senses and come back on your own volition.”

 

Agatha snorted through her tears. “Fat chance.”

 

“Someday you’ll understand that this is for the best. A  wolf needs her pack. Time is already running out, and I can’t speak here; anyone could be listening. We’re leaving immediately. I’ll explain everything when we’re safely home.” 

 

“Paranoid as ever, I see,” Agatha muttered. 

 

As Agatha followed her mother out of the market, she thought of her third-story apartment with its view of the Eiffel Tower. It had all her favorite clothes, books, and trinkets. She realized with a strange finality that she would never see the apartment or anything in it again. Agatha let her mother usher her into a town car, through Charles de Gaulle Airport, onto the first-class section of a plane, through Logan Airport, into another town car, and finally into the house that Agatha had promised herself that she’d never set foot in again. 

 

By the time they finally arrived at the Harkness estate, Agatha’s ancestral home and the Salem pack’s headquarters, it was almost three in the morning. 

 

Agatha walked into the house on autopilot. It was almost like no time had passed. The air was as stale as ever, and all of the furniture in the house was the same as Agatha remembered. 

 

Evanora had offered to explain what exactly was happening as soon as they got home, but Agatha was dead tired and had just spent the last ten hours at her mother's side. She needed sleep and to get the fuck away from the alpha. Whatever reason Evanora had for dragging Agatha back into this hell could wait. 

 

But, instead of going straight to bed, Agatha found herself walking past her old room and pausing in front of another closed door. Before Agatha even had the chance to knock, the door was opening, and her Aunt Lilia was pulling her into a tight hug. In every generation, there were only a few wolves born to a pack who had the biology to become alphas, and Lilia, like Agatha, was not one of them.

 

Oftentimes, females who weren’t alphas were used for marriage alliances—either to ensure power stayed in the right bloodlines within a pack or between two different packs for peace. Lilia was no exception. She was originally from the New Jersey pack and was used as a bargaining chip in a peace treaty between that pack and the Salemites. She had a short and fruitless marriage to the Salem Pack’s last alpha, Evanora’s brother, before he died.

The alpha’s death was a loss that nobody in the pack really seemed to mourn–apparently, his temper was even worse than Evanora’s. 

 

After her brother’s death, just weeks before her daughter’s birth and the consequent untimely death of her mate, Evanora became Alpha. She offered to let Lilia return to the New Jersey pack, but she chose to stay. Agatha never understood why. 

 

Agatha crumpled into Lilia’s arms, shaking with sobs. 

 

“Shh,” Lilia whispered into Agatha’s hairline. Agatha breathed in Lilia’s powdery fresh scent and felt herself relax as the older woman rubbed soothing circles over her back. “You’re going to be all right. You will survive this.” 

 

Agatha pulled back from Lilia and wiped her nose with her sleeve. “Doesn’t feel like it.” Agatha laughed bitterly. 

 

“I made your bed with fresh sheets, and have some pajamas laid out for you. C’mon, let's get you settled.” Lilia said. 

 

Agatha let Lilia lead her to her childhood bedroom. It was odd that in the six years Agatha had been gone, her room could stay exactly as it had always been, waiting for her to come back as if no time had passed. 

 

“Try and get some sleep,” Lilia said softly, lingering at the door. 

 

“They’re making me marry him tomorrow, aren’t they?” Agatha asked, even though she already knew the answer.  

 

Lilia sighed. “Things are changing here. The situation might not be as bad as you think. I wouldn’t have let your mother get to you if I thought you couldn’t handle it. ” 

 

“You knew they were coming to drag me back, and you didn’t think to warn me?” Agatha hissed. 

 

“If I reached out, it would have incriminated both of us. Besides, the cards told me your path isn’t as dark as it once was.” Lilia said and slipped out the door.

 

What a cryptic, kooky ass bitch. Agatha shut her eyes tightly and tried to pretend she was back at home in her apartment in Paris as she fell asleep.

Because Agatha was not an alpha, she’d always known she would never follow in her mother’s footsteps. This wasn’t a problem–Evanora was a miserable fucking bitch, and  Agatha had no desire to end up like her. From a young age, Agatha understood that she would grow up and leave the Salem pack behind. The pack had no use for her, and she had no use for them.

 

But, as Agatha got older, she realized that Evanora was nothing if not obsessed with the legacy of the Harkness bloodline. This meant she planned to do everything in her power to make sure Agatha gave her a grandchild who would grow up to be the pack’s alpha. So, the day Agatha turned eighteen, Evanora drafted a marriage contract between Agatha and Ralph Bohner, the second most dominant Wolf in the pack. This was the catalyst for Agatha’s latest escape, but Agatha was no stranger to running away.

 

The first time Agatha ran away from Harkness estate, she was six years old, and she’d just experienced her first shift. It hadn’t been a full moon, and she was ten years younger than the age when most Weres experience their Inaugural Turn. She had been playing in the woods, pretending to be a wolf, and then suddenly, she was one. She felt electric; colors were brighter, sounds were magnified. She felt like she belonged to something greater than herself, and it was riveting. Agatha ran straight through the forest for hours, until her body wore out and she abruptly turned human. This was a stroke of good luck, as she’d actually wandered all the way into Human Territory. If she’d entered Human Territory in wolf form unannounced, she would have been shot on sight. A human police woman found Agatha and returned her to the Salem Pack. 

 

The next time Agatha ran away, it was more calculated. It was her sophomore year of high school, and she had just learned about Turning Suppressors in Were health class. The idea of being able to take a pill that would let her completely blend in and live among humans sounded too good to be true. Agatha snuck into her mother’s medicine cabinet, stole an entire bottle of Turning Suppressors, hitchhiked to Boston, and spent a month sleeping on park benches before her mother tracked her down and dragged her home.   

 

Agatha’s last escape was her crowning achievement. The night before she was supposed to be married and mated to Bohner, Agatha booked a one-way ticket to Rome and stole $50,000 from the pack’s treasury. Lilia had been in on it, of course. She’d known first hand what life married to a monster was like, and at that time, Lilia had been willing to do anything in her power to make sure Agatha’s fate was different than her own. Lilia worked as the pack's secretary and ‘accidentally’ left her computer open with all of the pack’s banking information pulled up. 

 

Agatha spent three months living with a distant cousin of Lilia’s in the Sicilian mountainside, getting paid to teach English to the local children before moving to Malta. To avoid being traced, Agatha never stayed in one place for more than three months. The stolen $50,000 bought her a high-quality fake passport, a forged college degree, and a lifetime's worth of turning suppressors. She had a decent grift as a traveling English tutor, and she’d never been happier. Agatha should have known it was too good to be true. People like her didn’t get to live happily ever after. People like her got abducted by their toxic family and married against their will. 

 

Now that Agatha was back, Evanora would want her mated before she could escape again. Which meant the wedding would be happening as soon as possible.  After an impromptu kidnapping and unexpected transatlantic flight, Agatha felt like absolute shit. She woke up in a cold sweat, and then the nausea hit. She bolted to her ensuite bathroom and emptied the contents of her stomach into the toilet bowl.

 

“You need to shift.” 

 

Agatha jerked at the sound of Lilia’s voice. How did she not hear her come in? Instead of facing the older woman, Agatha slumped further onto the floor, resting her head against the cool porcelain of the toilet seat.

 

“Why are you in here?” Agatha groaned. 

 

Lilia croached down next to Agatha and rubbed between her shoulder blades. “You expect me to just go about my morning to the soundtrack of you puking?” Lilia quipped. “C’mon, kiddo. Let's get you up off the floor.” 

 

“If I’m sick, she won’t make me marry him today.” Agatha croaked as Lilia helped her to stand. 

 

Lilia sighed. “We both know that’s not true, and you’re not sick, you’re in withdrawal. This is the first time your body has gone without suppressors in years. You’re fighting nature. Brush your teeth, and meet me outside. We’re going for a run.” 

 

Lilia was using her ‘I’m not fucking around voice,’  and Agatha knew from experience that the old bitch wouldn’t leave her alone until she did as she said. Lilia was right. Weres were supposed to wean off suppressors, and Agatha had gone cold turkey. Her body was in shock, and shifting was the only cure. 

 

Agatha splashed water on her face, stripped out of her pajamas, and focused in on her body. She felt her blood buzzing and gave into the sensation; a brief moment of darkness and then everything was heightened; she was in wolf form. 

 

Agatha lowered her torso and stretched out her spine, and the sensation was nearly orgasmic. How had she gone six years denying herself this? Even if she was going to be nothing more than a warm body for a power-hungry shithead, at least she’d be able to do it in whatever form she chose. 

 

Agatha glanced in the mirror and was briefly startled by her appearance. In the time since she’d last shifted, her wolf form had aged. She was leaner than she remembered; her muscles must have atrophied from lack of use. Her pelt, a deep chestnut, was wilder and longer than she’d ever seen it. It wasn’t unlike her human hair. The most striking difference was a thin stipe of white fur starting at her nose and extending all the way down her tail. Agatha’s eyes were the only part of her wolf she recognized. The blue eyes of her human form always turned deep violet when she was a wolf. Superstition taught that a were who’s eyes changed color between forms was a bad omen, and Agatha’s life was incriminating evidence of that. 

 

Lilia was waiting for her at the edge of the forest. Her wolf form looked exactly the same as when Agatha last saw it. She had a dark gray pelt that always looked thick and healthy. 

 

How do you feel? Lilia’s voice rang inside Agatha’s head. It had been so long since Agatha had been near another wolf in wolf form that hearing Lilia telepathically was a surprise.

 

Never better, Agatha answered. Race you to the top of the hill? 

 

Lilia puffed out air, the wolf version of a laugh. I’m flattered you think I can still make it up there. My knees aren’t what they used to be. Let’s go to the meadow. 

 

Agatha followed as Lilia ran. The crunch of leaves under her paws sent a satisfied tingle up her spine. Agatha picked up the speed and raced in front of Lilia, her heart hammering excitedly in her chest. She kept a steady pace, but then realized Lilia was no longer behind her. 

 

Agatha slowed and turned around. Lilia was at the very edge of her field of vision.

 

You run ahead. Get reacquainted with your wolf. I’ll see you at home. Lilia said. 

 

Is this you giving me a chance to run away? 

 

You wouldn’t get far. Your mother has extra surveillance all along the perimeter of the pack's property. 

 

Of course she does. Agatha huffed and sprinted deeper into the forest. 

 

Agatha made it to the top of the hill and found her favorite boulder. The sun was hitting it perfectly, and it was warm to the touch. Perfect for a quick nap. She was about to lie down when the air changed; It felt more charged and carried the scent of camphor, chamomile, and a pleasant musk. Agatha felt the strange urge to bow down and realized that another Were was the source of the smell, and their pheromones were stupidly strong. She could practically taste the dominance and power rolling off of them. 

 

Was some other alpha visiting from another pack? Agatha didn’t recognize the scent as someone from Salem, and her mother would never let someone with so much dominance be part of the pack. This Were could easily overpower her and become the pack’s alpha. Agatha resisted the urge to lower her body submissively to the ground, and instead raised her hackles, growled a low warning, and turned to face the wolf who rudely ruined her opportunity to nap. 

 

The Were was absurdly gorgeous. Shiny jet black fur covered a body rippling with muscle. Their face was so symmetrical that Agatha realized those quacks who talked about sacred geometry might actually have had a point. There was something mischievous in the crook of the Were’s mouth, and their light amber eyes seemed wise but kind. 

 

Agatha had never felt desire in wolf form, but that was the only way to describe what was happening. There was a warmth low in her belly, and an overwhelming need to lower her middle back and elevate her hindquarters. She felt her tail instinctively swish to the side, exposing her private parts, before she realized what was happening. With a shock of horror, Agatha understood that her body was presenting itself for this wolf to mount it. 

 

Agatha was no prude. She’d felt up her fair share of girls in the janitor's closet in high school, and was fairly well known in the lesbian hook-up scene in all of the cities she’d lived in, but she had never even thought of having a sexual experience outside of human form. Agatha felt immobilized as she stared into this Were’s eyes. The Were was practically pumping out pheremones, and Agatha could see its desire rising between their hind legs. Highly dominant Were’s were able to change their reproductive organs at will, so Agatha couldn’t be certain of the wolf’s sex, but the fact that they had enough restraint and decency not to already be on top of Agatha led her to believe that this was likely a female. If this were in human form, Agatha wouldn’t have hesitated to make a move, but sex for Were’s in wolf form was different. When two wolves have sex, they’re mated for life. Agatha didn’t know this wolf, and they could very well be worse than Bohner. Agatha took one last look at this stunning Were and sprinted away. A reckless part of her was disappointed when the Were didn’t chase after her. 

 

Agatha ran all the way back to the house, shifted, rinsed off in the outdoor shower, and stole someone else’s towel before heading back inside. Agatha didn’t have any clothes to change into (the joys of being kidnapped), so she went straight into her mother’s office, clad only in a towel to figure out the finer details on why she was dragged back to Salem so abruptly. Agatha opened the door to her mother’s office without knocking. There seemed to be some sort of meeting going on between Evanora and a Were that Agatha wasn’t familiar with. Agatha could only see their back, as they were facing away from the door, but Agatha would have sensed if it was a pack member she knew.  

 

The unfamiliar Were’s posture was far more relaxed than Agathe would’ve expected of someone who was in the presence of her mother. They had shiny, dark hair pulled back into a loose bun at the nape of their neck. 

 

Evanora’s eyes widened at the sight of Agatha dripping water onto her heirloom oriental carpet. She cleared her throat. 

 

“Agatha,” Eveanora exclaimed, eyes narrowing. “We were just discussing you.” 

 

The new Were turned, looking at Agtaha with an unabashed curiosity. Agatha saw that the Were was a woman, and she was gorgeous. She had full, plump lips and a strong jawline. Agatha met her eyes and recognized the unique amber color; this was the Were from the woods. Like her Were form, her human face was stupidly symmetrical. 

 

“I’ve heard a lot about you, Agatha.” The were smirked. Her voice was low and teasing. Agatha felt hostile towards this Were. She didn’t have time to deal with some kiss ass wanna-be alpha who was trying to curry favor with her mother. 

 

“How good of you to meet with the plebeans, mother,” Agatha said, staring directly at the Were. The Were quirked an eyebrow at Agatha and laughed quietly. Agatha felt annoyance prick at her skin. 

 

“Rio, leave us. Agatha has spent much time away from the pack.” Evanora said. “Please excuse her insolence.” 

 

“Of course, alpha. Te veo, Agatha.” The Were said, lowering her head to the ground in deference to Evanora and Agatha. Agatha rolled her eyes. 

 

The Were left, and Evanora narrowed her eyes at Agatha. “You’re not in Europe anymore. You can’t just walk about naked, Agatha.” 

 

“I would gladly have changed into something if I’d had time to pack for my trip.” Agatha snarked. 

 

“I instructed Lilia to provide you with a wardrobe before I collected you and to arrange it in your room. You have clothes, child.” Evanora shook her head. 

 

“Tell me why I’m here,” Agatha demanded. 

 

Evanora went back to looking through papers on her desk. “Put on clothes first, you look like an idiot.” She waved her hand dismissively. 

 

“No,” Agatha said. “Why am I here, Mother?” Agatha asked again. 

 

Evanora looked up at her and sighed defeatedly. “We need to secure the Harkness legacy.” 

 

“Are you dying?” Agatha asked bluntly, trying to keep the excitement out of her voice. She’d dreamt of this day. 

 

Evanora sneered. “Try not to look so gleeful, Agatha.”

 

“So you really just hunted me down so I could marry and pop out some pups just to provide you with a successor?” Agatha asked. “Why now?” 

 

“I hunted you down, so you could help me maintain my power. I have reason to believe one of my seconds is planning to kill me” 

 

Agatha laughed. “You’ve gone soft. You were always paranoid, but this is a whole new level.” 

 

“I need you to be my eyes and ears.” 

 

“Even if someone was planning a coup, why do you think I’d help you?” Agatha challenged. 

 

“Because you are a Harnkess and it’s your duty.” 

 

Agatha laughed in her mother’s face. “We both know that means nothing to me.” 

 

Evanora pressed her lips into a firm line. “If there is a coup and you help me end it, I will give you $500,000 and never contact you again.” 

 

Agatha stared at her mother, trying to figure out if she was lying. 

 

“You’d still have no blood successor,” Agatha said, skeptically.

 

“But I would die in power and dictate who leads the pack next.” Evanora sounded resigned but sincere. 

 

“So you’re not forcing me to marry Bohner?” Agatha asked, trying to keep her voice from showing too much joy. 

 

“Ralph died, Agatha,” Evanora spoke bluntly. “A hunting accident .” 

 

Agatha knew this meant he had crossed her mother, and she’d disposed of him. For the first time since Agatha had seen her mother at the fresh air market, her shoulders relaxed. She wasn’t being sold off like a broodmare. It would probably only take Agatha a few weeks to suss out any rumblings of a coup and report back to Evanora, and a chunk of cash and the chance to leave the pack forever were too good an offer to refuse. 

 

“Fine.” Agatha said, “I’ll do it. I’m sure you already have a plan.  Who is your suspect, and how should I get close to them?”

 

Evanora smiled, but it was all teeth. “The Were I’m concerned with is Rio.”

 

Agatha nodded. This made sense. Rio was maybe the most dominant wolf Agatha had ever been around. 

 

“I’m surprised you allowed her in the pack.” 

 

Evanora frowned. “Rio was born into the pack. Her parents passed from the pseudorabies outbreak that killed my brother after you were born. She was five and was sent to a distant, packless relative. Lilia had wanted to take her in, but Rio was a troublesome child. I didn’t want you to be influenced by her.”

 

“You banished a five-year-old?” Agatha asked, aghast. 

 

Evanora scoffed. “Don’t be so dramatic. After you ran off, Rio came back to the pack.”

 

Agatha scoffed. “Voluntarily?” 

 

“Rio understands responsibility and obligation,” Evanora said pointedly.

 

“But you think she’s trying to kill you?” Agatha asked. 

 

“Perhaps,” Evanora said evenly. “You and Rio will be mated tonight. You’ll live with her and will be able to gather intelligence for me.” 

 

Agatha’s blood went cold. “What?” She spat, struggling to breathe. 

 

“Don’t act so surprised.” Evanora laughed. “You were always supposed to wed my successor.”

 

“I can’t just befriend Rio?” Agatha asked tersely. 

 

“Is that a skill you have?” Evanora said drily. Agatha wanted to stand up and slap her mother.

 

 “A marriage is the quickest way for you to have the most access. If a coup is in motion, you’ll help me dispose of Rio. She’d never suspect someone as weak as you to be dangerous” 

 

“And if there is no coup?” 

 

“You stay married for a year, and then I help you fake your own death, and you get your payout.” 

 

A year was enough time for Agatha to produce Evanora with an heir. “You still want me to continue the bloodline” Agatha whispered.  She felt sick to her stomach. Evanora had probably made the whole coup up just as an excuse to lure Agatha back. She probably expected Agatha to succumb to her ‘instincts’ as a non-dominant wolf and be complacent with a mate and pups. Fta fucking chance. 

 

“If Rio is innocent, and there is a child, perhaps you’ll find happiness.” Evanora shrugged. “If you still choose to leave, I’ll allow it and double the money if you sign away your parental rights.” Evanora said casually.

 

 Agatha felt dizzy. She’d do everything in her power to make sure that didn’t happen. If she ever decided to have children, it would be under her own volition, and Evnaora would never be allowed within hundreds of miles of them. 

 

“I have business to attend to,” Evanora said, standing from behind her desk. “ Do we have a deal, Agatha?” She asked, extending her hand. 

 

Agatha swallowed. A year of her life spent playing house and spying for her mother in exchange for complete freedom. She could survive anything for a year, right? And Rio may be an obnoxious kiss-ass, but Agatha couldn’t deny that she was attractive. Rio also could have taken advantage of AGtaha back in the woods and hadn’t. The bar was in hell, but other Weres wouldn’t have thought twice before taking advantage. Agatha figured there could be worse people to be stuck with for a year. 

 

“We have a deal,” Agatha said, and shook her mother’s hand, putting on a brave face.

 

Once she was safely back in her bedroom, Agatha sobbed. She'd been fucking trapped. But, Agatha knew she'd get out of this. Evanora was clever, but Agatha was more so.  She just had to stay true to the course and never, ever find herself in Wolf form with Rio. When she finished crying, Agatha went to the sink to splash some water on her swollen eyes.  She glanced in the mirror and saw that, like her wolf form, her hair now had a streak of white running through it.
