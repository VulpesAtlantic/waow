It’s late enough in the day that only very few people are left, leaving the corridors of the history department airy and deserted. Ursula clicks her tongue in displeasure as she walks back to her office from the kitchen, a refilled cup of coffee in one hand and a stack of student essays in the other.

Junior scholars keep whining about how hard it is to make it in academia, but Ursula feels very strongly that most of them would do better if they actually put in some work. Four o'clock in the afternoon in the middle of the week and the offices are as emptied out as if it is the middle of the night – no one is cut out for the realities of a career in academia anymore, from what she is seeing.

And don’t even get her started on the students, she thinks sourly as she rounds the corner to the innermost corridor. Just handing them a book is enough to make them cry these days, it seems.

There is one now, she sees when she looks up, lingering outside of Agatha Harkness’ office (a useless academic if Ursula ever saw one). Ursula takes in the young woman’s black-clad, tattooed demeanor with equal parts dissatisfaction and deep suspicion.

“It’s no use standing around there,” Ursula says as she passes her by. “Professor Harkness has already gone home for the day.”

“Oh,” the girl says, sounding disproportionately disappointed to have missed Agatha’s paltry office hours. “Do you know if she will be in tomorrow?”

“No idea,” Ursula says, unlocking her own office two doors down. “I would suggest sending her an electronic e-mail.”

She doesn’t close the door all the way behind her, leaving it opening a crack to make sure that the student leaves.

Listening intently, the coffee cooling on the desk next to her, Ursula soon hears steps fading away. It’s quiet for a minute, and then the steps return, only to fade away once more after another few minutes.

When she’s sure the girl has gone, she sticks her head out of the door and inspects Agatha’s door. There is a note on it. Ursula grabs the reading glasses she’s left perched on her head and puts them on as she walks up to Agatha’s office.

She squints to read the note the girl has left behind. Seems I missed you. See you during office hours I guess xx

Ursula doesn’t give much for the informal way students engage with their teachers nowadays, but this is a little much, even by their usual lax, disrespectful standards. She shakes her head, mutters a little under her breath, and retreats to her office to do get some work done.

 

She has all but forgotten about the young woman the next time she sees her, a couple of weeks later, but she instantly recognizes her.

Ursula is standing by the printer in the hall, copying assignments for the week’s classes. She is in a foul mood; she had assigned her TA to take care of it, but the girl had claimed some form of ‘family emergency’ and immediately left the state.

This time, the young woman strolls through the corridor like she owns it, dressed in a leather jacket and with sunglasses covering her eyes, despite being indoors in autumn. She has a takeaway cup of coffee in each hand and from one jeans pocket, Ursula can see a university keycard dangle. So not a student, then.

Ursula watches as the girl pushes Agatha’s door open with her knee, and disappears inside, closing it with a firm click behind her.

She returns a while later, divested of one of the cups of coffee. Ursula peers at her warily as she passes with a cheerful “Hello!” Her dark red lipstick looks a little smudged.

The printer makes tortured noises as Ursula glances between Agatha’s door and the girl’s retreating form.

She is still stationed by the recalcitrant printer fifteen minutes later, trying to understand the error messages that’s showing up on the tiny display, when Agatha leaves her office. She is humming some sort of tune, as if she’s having a great day. When she looks up at Ursula cursing at the printer, she gives her a quick wave and says “Hello!” almost as cheerfully as the girl did.

Ursula’s eyes narrow, the hateful printer momentarily forgotten.

When Mark steps into his new office, it is with a feeling of great anticipation. He’s been asking to move out of the subterranean hallway – not very affectionally nicknamed ‘the Crypt’ by the faculty due to its lack of windows and generally funereal vibe – for years and now he can finally wave goodbye to his chronic Vitamin D insufficiency and the damp, moldy corners of the cellar office for good.

His brand-new window is rather small and high up on the wall, granted, and the office itself a bit cramped, but that’s nothing he lets stand in his way of his glorious fantasies of a future of increased efficiency, where his papers are never rejected, and he rises to become the star of the history department. Perhaps he will even start to work out, finally get rid of that constant crick in his neck. And start bringing in lunch to work, instead of relying on overpriced sandwiches from the cafeteria that gives him heartburn.

In short, he thinks as he sits down, with great satisfaction, in his new office chair in front of his new desk, it’s all downhill from here.

Even the coffee on this floor seems less burnt than in the basement kitchenette. Maybe in this new, beautiful office he will finally kick his habit of leaving half-empty coffee cups all around his desk until they start to grow new ecosystems or he accidentally knocks one over on some unsuspecting student’s term paper.

He’s so pleased with this new development, the bright path ahead of him, fully focused on readjusting the chair to perfection, that it takes a while before he registers the sound. It’s not until he rolls the chair back into position and toggles his mouse to wake the screen back up that it starts filtering in and he realizes that he’s been hearing it for a while.

He hears, a bit muffled, the sound of someone... moaning? He pauses, cup of coffee in his hand, and listens.

“Oh, oh, oh,” comes through the walls from somewhere. “Oh, OH, yes, yes!”

Surely, it must have a reasonable explanation, he thinks, watching the bleak, wintery sunlight falling into the room with a mounting sense of dread inside. Some sort of student prank, some weird quirk in these old, groaning buildings – perhaps someone who is really excited about getting a research grant.

“Oh yeah,” he says a little tiredly, but in a much calmer tone than Mark feels the situation warrants, as he unlocks the door to the office opposite Mark’s. “That’s just Agatha Harkness and her girlfriend. It happens a few times a week,” he adds, as if it’s a regretful act of nature no one can do anything about.

Dave shrugs. “You get used to it. Or you may wanna go down Gail’s path–” he points toward the office on the other side of Agatha Harkness’, “–and purchase a good set of earphones. She probably has some recommendations, if you want ‘em.”

With that, he disappears into this office, leaving Mark gaping in the corridor. To his left, he hears the sound of what he dearly hopes is Agatha Harkness finishing.

It’s ten o’clock in the evening and Angelica has finally tapped out the final words on her term paper. She generously grants herself a short celebratory break where she moves her head from side to side and wiggles her shoulder to work the stiffness out of her body that has been perched over her laptop for the past five hours.

It’s tedious work, and she has a strong inclination to skip it and just submit the essay as is so she can finally get on with her life, but she doesn’t like handing in half-assed work and the deadline is not until midnight anyway.
