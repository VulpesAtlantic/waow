Summary

Agatha Harkness has recently moved to a secluded ranch in California with $50,000 and a dream. The dream in question? To be left blissfully alone to wallow in their misery. So when her peace is disturbed by a bird, and a highly endangered one at that, trashing up her yard, she is not happy. Soon, they are complaining loudly down the phone at a local conservation biologist, who agrees to stop by and help them out. Agatha was not expecting said conservation biologist to be hot.

Nuisance butch rancher Agatha and long-suffering bird-nerd Rio falling in love with the help of a (very literal) wing-man. Or: an excuse to write an Agathario fic featuring my favourite birds.

Agatha, bright-eyed and inquisitive, begging her mother to give her just one dollar to spend at her school’s upcoming bake sale, had once been told, in no uncertain terms, that “money cannot buy you happiness, Agatha.” Evanora had then promptly sent her to her room for the rest of the evening.

Agatha had begrudgingly accepted the punishment, figuring it best not to point out the discrepancy that apparently money could buy your way into heaven. That was if Evanora's monthly donations to their church held any merit, and you were meant to be happy forever there. She’d learned already at that age that it wasn’t worth challenging her mother on these things.

Now, forty years older and in no doubt that Evanora’s soul is as far from heaven as it is possible to be, Agatha knows the old ghoul had been wrong. Agatha is the closest to happy they’ve been at any point in the last year, and it had only cost them a fifty thousand dollar down-payment, plus fuel costs for a four-day, cross-country relocation.

Calling it a ranch is perhaps generous. The property consists of about four modestly sized but empty fields worth of dry grass and shrubs that, by the looks of it, haven't been used for any sort of farming in years. Overlooking the steady downhill slope of the neglected land, and the trees and hills beyond it, is the equally rough looking house that Agatha has moved into.

It's one storey, with a single bed and bathroom, and an open plan kitchen and dining room space. A couple of steps leading up to the front door constitute what could generously be called a porch, and the outside is painted a faded blue, bleached over the years by sun and dulled under layers of dust and dirt.

The tired-looking man who had sold the ranch to Agatha had been overjoyed to have someone finally show interest in the property. He'd been trying to get rid of it ever since his father died and left it to him five years prior. Apparently the crumbling, long-abandoned barns and storage sheds at the perimeter coupled with the objectively pretty shit quality of the land had been a deal breaker for other potential buyers who'd heard "Central California Ranch", seen the asking price, and thought they'd struck the sort of gold people hadn't come to California for since the 1860s.

Apparently, by some definitions, it can't even technically be called a ranch anymore, considering the last livestock had been sold off years ago, and the only other animals it has seen since are the very feisty family of opossums who have made their home in one of the old cattle barns.

Fortunately for him, Agatha doesn't give a fuck about cows. No, she is there for the blissful solitude of the surrounding twelve miles of dry, dusty, uninhabited land. 

Agatha hasn't always yearned for solitude.

As a child it was all she’d known. No siblings, no real friends at school, and a mother to whom taking care of Agatha was a mild inconvenience on a good day. Connection was what Agatha had craved above all else as a child.

And though her circle was never large, eventually they managed to find their people. She’d had Lilia, she’d had Jen and her partner Alice who she'd met that time Lilia successfully convinced them to go to the town’s one lesbian bar. Now she was alone. Or almost alone, as they were regrettably reminded of by the muffled grunting, and persistent scratching of claws on the hardwood floor. Let it not be said that that rabbit did not live up to his name.

If Agatha’s back crackle like a rusty chain sliding between gears as she forces herself out of bed, then that was between them and God. And Scratchy.

The sun beats down outside, filtering in an oppressive haze through the kitchen windows; Agatha was not looking forward to scraping away the layers and layers of dust caked onto the glass. That was definitely a problem for another day. With Scratchy pacified, chewing his way through his now-refilled basket of hay, Agatha slowly begins mentally trying to catalogue the tasks she would try to tackle today.

When Agatha had been sold the house, she’d been warned at length about the “undesirable state” of its furnishings. The man had even gone so far as to offer to help Agatha cart it all the way to the nearest city to dump it all when she arrived.

Agatha however, had never been in the practice of accepting help from men, their general attitude in life being ‘I’m butch, I can make it work.’ Agatha also was not about to hire a separate moving truck to lug all of their existing furniture across the country when it could be sold along with her house. So they’d decided to make do with what little, beaten up furniture the property came with.

They were confident the majority of it could be fixed up. At the end of the day, anything utterly unsalvageable could just be turned into emergency firewood, and replaced with something from Facebook marketplace.

Despite Agatha's best attempts to liven the space up with whatever trinkets, photos, and books had survived her pre-move, Marie Kondo-style clear-out, it has become apparent that, small as the house is, its perhaps a little large for just one person.

Even one person with a pet rabbit and a sizeable collection of model cars, and multiple boxes full of Lilia's many many tarot decks that they can't bring themselves to throw away.

With train sets and children’s books scattered across the floor. A folded up telescope resting by the back-door that Nicky could have used to make the most of the clear un-light-polluted night skies. Between funeral arrangements, and getting Evanora's house sold and Lilia's business merged with one owned by some guy Billy's father knows, the last year hasn't exactly provided a break for Agatha to sit back and think about her future.

After a brief battle wrangling her bed-tangled hair out of her face and into what could generously be called a ponytail, Agatha grabs the tenon saw from her toolkit, and crouches down beside the chair.

When moving in, Agatha had made the uncharacteristic decision, spurred by a burst of new-house-new-me motivation, to try and bring all of their belongings inside that same evening. She’d patted herself on the back, and told herself that tomorrow-morning her would be thankful. And so, despite not having unpacked them all yet, everything they’d brought out to California was, in theory, sat in one of these boxes.

The problem with something being true in theory, though, is that theory doesn’t factor in Agatha’s unfailing ability to procrastinate. And in true chronic-procrastinator fashion, they'd gotten 90% of the way through this endeavour and then decided to finish it later.

Because ‘tomorrow morning’ had then turned into ‘Friday afternoon’, which turned into ‘next weekend’, which turned into ‘when I need it’, and eventually, Agatha had completely forgotten about the last few bags. One of which, of course, contained her First-Aid kit.

So now, the thing she needs sits alongside the few bags of rubbish they’d accumulated over the last two weeks in the bed of her truck, covered securely with a tarp sheet which would ideally need two working hands to work loose.

Letting out a frustrated yell loud enough to startle Scratchy, Agatha stomps across the room to the front door and wrenches it open. What they aren't expecting to see are the several pairs of yellow-and-red eyes staring back at her.
 
Perched in an arc, around the rim of the bed of Agatha’s truck, are undoubtedly the largest birds she has ever seen in her life. Mottled pink heads snap in Agatha's direction and they scatter.

Humongous black wings unfold so large they block out the horizon line behind them as a number of the birds, spooked by the door opening and Agatha’s shocked exclamation, climb up and away into the sky.

A couple of birds remain however, focused entirely on the truck bed. Or more accurately, Agatha now realises, on the tarp covering their trash bags, which the birds are digging their beaks into, tearing open with a fervour.
 
What Agatha hadn’t initially noticed when the first lot of birds had flown away, too awed by the immense span of their wings, but notices now looking at the stationary birds, are colourful tags fixed to the birds’ wings.

Each is a small coloured square- vinyl maybe- attached to the top of one, or both, of the wings. And in bold black or white font, each has a number printed on, two digits, standing starkly out against the solid coloured tag.

Agatha's eyes widen as they realise just what they're looking at.

Agatha wasn’t an idiot, they had briefly researched the place they were moving to: the closest nearby civilisation for supplies and garbage disposal, the legality of smoking weed, and the sorts of wildlife they might come across out in the middle of nowhere.

If anybody had asked, she'd have shrugged smugly and told them she was environmentally conscious and just liked to be prepared. Really, she just wanted to know she could still somewhat easily access frozen chicken strips, and wether or not she'd have to smoke by a window to air out the smell in the event a cop showed up to question her about the inconsistencies in their tax forms.

And judging by the sheer size of the birds tearing through her tarp as if it were a carcass, condors they most certainly are.

However, after that month, he started to improve more steadily. He was still in treatment for a few weeks, and then kept in an outdoor pen to re-acclimatise for even longer, but today was finally the day, when the weather was nice enough, and there was enough light in the day, to release #1103 back into the wild.

Rio moved past Agatha to unlatch the back panel of the truck bed, and hopped up to perch on the edge of it.

"Don't you want to get any closer to watch it?" Agatha asks. The view here is nice, but the two workers by what Agatha now recognises is the carrier crate holding #1103.

"But you aren't allowed to, authorised personnel only past that gate." She points at a gate about 120 feet away.

"I know that, but wouldn't you prefer to be closer to the action, I'm happy to wait back here for you." Rio looked at Agatha like she'd just said the most outrageous thing she had ever heard.

"Of course I wouldn't, Agatha. I'm watching this with you. I see condors up close every day. And besides, you're the reason was even able to be treated, there's nobody else I'd rather have the honour of watching this with than you."

Agatha can feel her eyes fogging up, until Rio speaks again.

"And I also have snacks for us to share." She opens her purse and dumps an assortment of chips and cake and candy out beside her. Agatha can't help but laugh at the absurdity of it, even letting out a snort.

Rio blushes, and explains herself self-consciously,

"Well I figured since dinner probably isn't happening now until we swing by a Sonic drive-through on the way home later, I'd dash down to the nearest gas station before you arrived to get us some stuff."

"Thank you, my love, I love," they pick up a a mini tube of Pringles to read the label, "ketchup flavoured Pringles. Yum."

Rio snatches them from her, defensively.

"They had very limited stock, okay, and beggars can't be chosers. If you're going to make a fuss about my Pringle selection then maybe I'll just eat them."

She tears open the seal on the tube and shoves three chips into her mouth.. Immediately, they are spat back out into Rio's hand, and Rio is making exaggerated noises of disgust.

"Okay, those are awful. Maybe lets just stick to the Skittles?"

"That sounds like a plan to me." Agatha snorts again, nudging Rio's knee with their hand, a silent request for Rio to budge over so then can sit next to her.

They get comfy, and then a voice calls over to Rio.

"We good to go, boss?" they call, and Rio sticks a thumb up in the air.

"Yep! Let him go!"

The moment the metal grate is removed from the front of the crate, #1103 is hopping out. Even from back here Agatha can see how he's changed whilst in captivity. In the last few months the dark grey black of his head has started to become mottled with pink and yellow. He's growing up.

The thought is oddly bittersweet for Agatha. Obviously she could never claim to love the birds nearly as much as Rio does, she has really come to care so intensely about #1103.

He stands on the edge of the cliff, wings unfurled with his tags and trackers on full display, but not moving to take off.

"Does it usually take this long?" Agatha whispers, not looking away from #1103.

"Can do," Rio murmurs back, "we take it at the bird's pace. Some soar straight out and others take twenty minutes to even step out of the crate."

"I was thinking," Agatha begins.

"That's a dangerous thing to be doing, careful you don't give yourself a headache." Rio interjects, earning her a playful jab in the side from Agatha's elbow.

"He doesn't have a name because he hatched at the national park, right?. But now he's been rereleased here, don't you think it would be nice for him to get one?"

"I mean I don't think we've ever done it before, but I don't see why we couldn't. Why, what were you thinking?" Rio asks lightly, but Agatha suspects she already knows.

"I mean maybe it's silly but I like the idea that there's a version of Nicky out there somewhere who got to live. I know I can't literally give him a second chance at that, and that that bird isn't in any way a stand-in for my son but, you know- #1103 did get a second chance, and considering he brought us together in his own way, I feel like the universe meant for me to meet that bird."

Rio is just smiling at Agatha with a deep fondness, but Agatha starts backtracking at the absence of verbal validation.

"Okay, fine, you can tell me its a really stupid idea, I can take it."

"Agatha, I think it's lovely, and I think it fits him perfectly."

"You really do?"

"I do."

Agatha pulls Rio in for a kiss. It's purposeful, and tender, and the moment they break away and fix their eyes back on the horizon, Nicky finally takes one step, then another, and lets the air beneath his wings lift him. He soars up, higher and higher into the sky above their heads, before disappearing off into the sunset, over beyond the far side of the canyon.
