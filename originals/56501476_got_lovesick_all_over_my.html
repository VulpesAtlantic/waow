Summary

in which Gamora realizes she is indeed in love with Peter Quill

The Milano sits on the volume of endless space, the galaxy and its stars drifting outside its window sills. Gamora was the only one awake at this hour, with nothing but the sound of metal clinging unto one another, echoing the halls. She sees a glimpse of herself in Godslayer , its polished uru metal an indication that she needs to get to bed, considering that they are tasked to report to the planet of the Sovereign People tomorrow in the afternoon.

She lays it down right next to the many weapons out for display in the workshop, some of which are Peter’s, while most are of Rocket’s own creations. Gamora makes sure the blade is stored out of anyone’s reach, or more specifically, out of Groot’s reach.

She will never get tired of the view outside her window sill, a dance of constellations and galaxies, all out of arm's reach from her bedroom window. Gamora stares into the endless void of twinkling starlight, with nothing but the faint static ringing in the spaceship, a sign of peace and quiet amidst their admittedly very rough and tumble life. No amount of bounty in the world can replace the view, the life, she has now.

She stands up, finished admiring the melancholy of space outside when she notices a large figure underneath her bedsheets, snoring.

Peter is sleeping soundly in her bed, his body curled up beneath her duvet covers. He snores softly, his sound almost like the static heard all over the Milano at this hour. Beside him, in his large, calloused hand lies little Groot snoozing away as well, cuddling Peter’s thumb.

“Peter,” She kneels down and whispers in his ear, hoping to wake him up so she can finally sleep in her own bed.

“Mmm,” Peter murmurs in response, but Gamora isn’t so sure that it’s an act of acknowledgement as she watches him continue to snooze away.

She stands up, unsure of what to do next considering that she doesn’t want to wake Groot up as well, especially at this hour. Gamora sighs and sits by her bedside, a couple of inches away from Peter’s warm, curled body. He’s wearing another old shirt of his, sweat still clinging onto the fabric. But Gamora must confess, he doesn’t smell bad at all. In fact, there was a hint of sweetness in his scent this time, reminding her of one of the perfume shops they visited a while back in Xandar.

“How about this?” Peter sprays cologne on his arm, raising it to Gamora’s face.

“Peter,” She says, pushing his arm away from her face. “You know that my sense of smell is enhanced. That ‘cologne’ is repulsive.”

In all honesty, it doesn’t smell that repulsive, Gamora thought. It was the strength of the smell that threw her off guard. She wouldn’t mind Peter smelling like one of her childhood candies, but she still wants him to smell like Peter, but of course she could never tell him that.

“Oh, sorry.” He quickly cleans it off his arm, his cheeks glowing pink. “I thought you’d like it, you said you like sweet.”

“But tone it down please.” She coughs as she heads for the exit. “I would much rather prefer you smell like sweat than puking in the Milano every time you come near me.”

Peter continues to sleep soundly on her bed, unaware that the prettiest girl in the galaxy is currently inches away from him. Gamora continues to look at him, watching as his chest rises and falls against the covers. He twitches slightly in his sleep, but continues to snore softly. His snoring a melodic harmony with the quiet static of the Milano. The soft hues of galaxies outside Gamora’s window illuminating his face, with constellations drawn out on his own.

She has never noticed the cluster of freckles etched on Peter’s face. Maybe she had, but paid no mind. But along with it came a handful of small scars lying underneath, the stories that came with it, Gamora does not remember. She remembers one of them, which he told her when he was drunk on very strong Asgardian mead, an alcohol they got during their earlier days of the formation of the Guardians.

“Gam’ra,” Peter slurring his words, “You should try this.”

The smell of alcohol practically reeks through Peter’s entire body, the scent worse as he opens his mouth. He almost trips as he walks towards Gamora, who was admiring the view from the rooftop bar. She catches him with her arms, the glass spilling the alcohol all over the floor.

“Peter, how much did you drink?” She says, pulling him up.

“Only a lil bit,” He hiccups, his body weight shifting to hers, evidently slowing her movements. Even with Gamora’s layered clothes, she could still feel Peter burning through his clothes. She brings the back of her hand to his forehead, feeling Peter’s body temperature getting higher by the second.

“Let’s get you back,” She says as she gestures back to the ship.

It was almost midnight in Xandar, or whatever the midnight equivalent was to Xandar, Gamora didn't bother to correct herself. She trudges towards the Milano , which sits outside at the bar’s parking lot. Its iridescent silver shines as it sits under Xandar’s moon, its light illuminating the cobalt pavement Gamora currently drags Peter on. As she heaves every step, she wonders what she could be doing now, flirting with a person by the bar? Highly unlikely. She grimaces as she imagines Peter’s own attempts of flirting earlier in the night. She remembers watching from afar, in between the silhouettes by the dance floor, or those who travel from the drinks to the DJ.

Gamora often wonders how Peter can stomach the sheer will to flirt with a girl at every bar on every planet in every galaxy. She studies his face. Its often shining tan turned to a pale hue as the mead continues to take into effect in Peter’s system. She notices a small cut, bleeding onto one of the clusters of freckles that sprinkle across his face, much like stardust.

“Peter, where did you get this?” Her head gestures at the cut.

He grumbles by her side.

“We’re almost there,” She rolls her eyes.

The Milano was quiet, unlike the commotion and ruckus they had previously been in about an hour ago. Gamora hands Peter a cup of water. They both sit at the leather bench by their workshop, Peter’s body slump against the metal wall. She grabs a damp towel, one she found as she rummaged through Groot’s belongings, and she applies it against the cut on Peter’s face.

“Does this hurt?”

“No,” He says, without slurring his words but the scent of alcohol still reeking. “I’m actually kind of liking this.”

Gamora could only roll her eyes, “Sure you do, Quill.”

The humming static of the Milano harmonizes with Peter’s soft snoring. Gamora watches on her bedside as Groot nuzzles his way deeper on Peter’s chest, their breathing, the rise and fall, almost identical. Her gaze shifts to Peter’s hair, now shining, as the shooting star that currently drifts outside her window reflects its light on his blonde, curly hair. She reaches for a bundle of curls that sits on his nose bridge, away from the uniformity of the bunch. She tucks it behind his ear, carefully as to not wake him (and potentially Groot) up.

Gamora often wonders how Peter’s hair would feel in her hands. Sure, she held it many times before as he asked her to comb it for him for formal events but she has always wondered–no, craved for the feeling she is feeling now. What it felt like to touch Peter’s hair in the intimacy and comfort of her quarters. To stare at his face, painted with the boundless constellation of freckles and just hold it, no cuts, no drunk flirting. To smell his scent, not off expensive cologne from far away planets and civilizations but of his sweat and odor, a scent Gamora would liken to home.

She smiles to herself as she walks towards the other side of the bed, and lays beside Peter.

There was a significant amount of space between them, but enough to feel his warmth. Gamora wonders what it would be like to feel this warmth, skin to skin, face to face. What it would be like for his arms, scars, freckles and all, to be wrapped around her. His scent enveloping her like she was a part of him from the very beginning. The humming static of the Milano , his soft snoring, almost harmonizing with Gamora’s thoughts.

But she thinks, she knows, this would be fine for now.
