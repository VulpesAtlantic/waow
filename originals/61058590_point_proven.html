Chapter 1: The Loudest Silence

She hadn’t thought for a second Rio would actually do it.

That she would, after however many years of pursuing Agatha across the globe, suddenly respect Agatha's wishes and disappear from her life.

But she had and Agatha was losing herself to the silence.

Several months had passed since what remained of their makeshift coven had managed to crawl their way to the end of the Witches Road. That had been the last time Agatha had seen her, only a few hours after that final clandestine meeting, where Agatha had agreed to deliver Billy to Death, but only if Rio promised to disappear from her life, for good.

In the end though neither Billy or herself had been forced to walk with Rio through that final veil. For, it had come to light that the road itself had been crafted, in it's immense entirety, by Billy’s subconscious and that the three women he had inadvertently killed in his accidental hex; Mrs Hart, Alice, and Lilia, were a substantial replacement for his soul's second chance.

Billy had been horrified at the exchange, had even tried to fling himself at Rio's feet and insist she take him instead, but Agatha had thrown him behind her with a quick flick of purple light and demanded Rio take the offer.

The three women should never have died on the road, after all. It was a total fluke that they had been killed, and killed by Billy no less, making the trade a fair one.

“You have your souls, the balance has been restored,” Agatha had yelled, enjoying the euphoria of her magic brimming beneath her skin once again following Billy's generous donation, “our deal stands. You have no further business here”.

Rio had paused, her dark eyes flickering towards Agatha before dropping to the burnt grass at her feet; another victim of their magical brawl. She appeared uncertain, almost hesitant as though she was waiting for something else from Agatha, for something more; more words, more actions, more magic even but Agatha, furious as she was, remained stern and continued to stare at Rio with an arched eyebrow, waiting for her to finally, at long last, leave her alone.

Tense silence reigned between them for several long moments. Then, just as Agatha was gearing up to blast Rio with another bolt of magic, Rio nodded to herself; a single sharp jerk of her chin down towards her chest. Her dark eyes drifted towards Agatha, tracing upwards across the lines of her body, before finally settling on her face. Their eyes met and Agatha felt her breath freeze in her lungs at the aggrieved acceptance in Rio’s gaze. It was an expression she had never seen her wear before, one consisting of fatigue, exhaustion, and heartbreak.

Stubborn as Agatha was though, she said nothing.

Rio watched Agatha's face for a short moment, but when she didn't speak, nor show any sign of remorse or regret she nodded again, almost as though trying to coax herself to take action, before raising her dark hood over her head and turning towards the shadows.

Agatha had been expecting it then. For that familiar, frustrating te veo to echo throughout the garden, which would then be her cue to start sniping and snipping once more about Rio and her frustrating insistence on following Agatha throughout her life.

Yet, those two words never came. Instead, Rio murmured something else, something new. Something that made dread blossom and bloom in Agatha’s stomach, until it felt like she was going to choke on the sharp brambles now growing between her ribs.

“Nuestro amor merecía un mejor final”.

Our love deserved a better ending.

It probably said something quite profound in itself that Agatha had never thrown the parchment and the quill away.

But she refused, as she did with so many painful parts of her life, to give that particular thought too much introspection.

Instead she chose, after three quarters of a bottle of whiskey, to pull the parchment and quill from the chest in her basement, where she kept the few treasures she had chosen to keep over her long, long life and take them to the living room, where she set them down on the coffee table.
