Sweet Melody

Agatha tried to stifle a yawn, trying to ignore the bodies bumping into her and the faint smell of marijuana in the air. Beside her, Nicky was practically vibrating in excitement, eyes glued to the empty stage.

For his thirteenth birthday, Agatha bought him concert tickets to his favorite band, The Coven , and now, here they were in the small, cramped venue downtown to see them. It was worth it though, seeing just how happy Nicky was, even before the concert started. Besides, it couldn’t be that bad. Agatha actually enjoyed the band’s music since it reminded her of what she listened to when she was about Nicky’s age. Not to mention, it was an all-women band, and if that wasn’t a sign she was raising her son right, she wasn’t sure what was.

“What do you think they’ll play first?” Nicky asked, turning to his friends, Billy and Tommy.

From the other side of the boys, Wanda shared an amused look with her.

When Agatha bought the tickets, she mentioned it to Wanda since their sons were inseparable and all loved the band. It also helped that Agatha had some adult company too. She scanned the crowd, unsurprised to find most of the attendees to be teens to young adults, but it still made her acutely feel the fact that she was here as a chaperone.

Biting back a curse when she was bumped into for the fifth time in the last three minutes, Agatha called out to Wanda, “I’ll be right back. Watch Nicky for me?”

Nicky looked up at her, eyebrows furrowed. “Are you okay?”

“I’m fine,” Agatha said. She knew it was probably uncool, but she ran a hand through his hair, brushing the long strands out of his face. “Just need some air before this thing starts. Stay with Wanda, and don’t go anywhere, okay?”

“I’m not five, mom,” Nicky rolled his eyes. “I can stay in one spot.”

Agatha almost pointed out the fact that he hadn’t been able to stay in one spot since they came in, but this was supposed to be his birthday gift. She’d save the embarrassment for later. Agatha painstakingly inched her way through the crowd and let out a relieved sigh when she finally made it back outside. 

There was no one else out here at this point besides some security guards, so Agatha just walked down a few steps and dug through her purse for a cigarette. She’d been meaning to quit for years, pretty much since Nicky was born, but she couldn’t quite stop. Although she’d cut down on how often she smoked, she always itched for one when she was stressed or overwhelmed.

As she took a step closer, Agatha first noticed the ink along one of Rio's arms, leaves and flowers intertwined and disappearing into her shirt. Then, she swallowed as she realized the arm holes were cut just low enough to show a glimpse of the side of her breasts. Her eyes lingered for a second longer than they should have before darting up to meet brown eyes.

“Thanks,” Rio hummed. As she took it, their fingers brushed, and Agatha fought back the urge to shiver at the touch. She wasn’t quite successful because Rio cocked an eyebrow and asked, “Cold?”

“A tad,” Agatha lied. To further sell it, she rubbed her bare arms.

The latter was a little more difficult. Agatha’s outfits mostly consisted of button-ups for work or stained t-shirts for at home. After some digging though, she found a black tank from her college days with thin straps and three buttons at the top. Back in college, Agatha would have undone all of the buttons, but now, so many years later with her son, she wore it completely buttoned.

“Here.”

“A shirt for a half of a cigarette?” Agatha said with a smirk. “Hardly a fair trade.”
