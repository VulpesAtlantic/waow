Summary

Rio just wanted to get away from her noisy siblings so she could read her book in peace. What she finds when seeking out her neighbor's swingset is something that she could have never predicted: a lifelong friend.

And maybe something more.

Day Seven of Fluff Week: Friends to Lovers. In this case, Childhood Friends to Lovers.

High School AU, including Prom. So much fluff and a tiny pinch of angst.

Notes

I really love childhood friends to lovers fics, so I decided to run with it for this one. These two own my whole heart.

Also, I hope that you'll picture Alanna Ubach as Marisol (Rio's mom) because that's who I've been picturing.

The Girl Next Door

The Girl Next Door

“Give it back!” 

Rio sighed, putting down her book as her siblings ran into the backyard. Javi was holding Yessie’s doll over his head, snickering as she attempted to take it back from him. He was nearly half a foot taller than she was, so Rio knew that she wasn’t getting it back anytime soon. 

She just wished they could be quieter about it.

“Come and get it!” Javi laughed, waving it around above his head.

Suddenly, it was snatched from his hand by Santi, their eldest brother, who was a good foot taller than Javi and six years his senior.

“Hey!” Javi whined. “Give it back!”

“I will,” Santi said, shoving him aside and handing the doll to Yessie, who clutched it against her chest. 

“Thanks, Santi!” she exclaimed before skipping off to play in the sandbox, where Rosie, their eldest sister, was building a sandcastle. Yessie showed her that she’d gotten her doll back, and Rio hoped that would be the end of the noise.

Of course, though, it wasn’t. 

Javi jumped on Santi’s back as soon as it was turned to him and the two began to wrestle and shout in the middle of the backyard.

Rio sighed and closed her book, pushing herself to her feet and jogging past her quarreling brothers to the back door of the house. 

“Mami?” she said, poking her head inside.

“Yes, mi vida?” Marisol asked as she rolled out the dough on the kitchen table. 

“I’m gonna go to Titi Lilia’s for a little bit to read, okay?” Rio informed her mother, still clutching her book to her chest.

“Okay, mija,” Marisol said. “Only for a little bit, though. Dinner will be ready in a couple hours, okay, and I need you to wash up and help set the table. It’s your turn.”

“Okay, Mami,” Rio said, nodding. “Love you.”

“Love you, too,” Marisol said, blowing her a kiss with a little flour on her hands. Rio giggled and shook her head before running back outside.

Her brothers were rolling around on the ground, hitting each other and pulling on one another’s hair and clothing. Rio jogged around them, pushing her glasses up her nose, and headed for the fence that separated her yard from their next-door neighbor’s.

There were a few loose boards that she was able to move out of the way and squeeze through to pop into Lilia’s yard, brushing a little bit of dirt off of her jeans and t-shirt. Rio checked her book to make sure that it didn’t snag on something and tear before she started toward the lone swingset in the middle of the yard, her usual favorite spot to sit and read.

She paused, however, when she saw something – or rather, someone – she wasn’t expecting to see.

There was a small, brown-haired, blue-eyed girl sitting on one of the two swings, her hands clutching the metal chains next to her head. Her toes were just barely digging into the ground beneath her seat. She wore a gray dress that had a few holes in the capped sleeves and the bottom of the skirt. Her hair was loose and fell wildly around her head, going down to nearly her butt.

Rio paused when the girl looked up at her, her eyes widening slightly.

“Oh,” Rio said, looking back at the fence as if she had opened some sort of portal to an alternate dimension. “I’m sorry,” she said. “I didn’t know anybody would be here.” She swallowed thickly and pushed her glasses up her nose. “Do you know Lilia?”

The brown-haired girl nodded, her shoulders hunched and her cheeks pink. “She’s my Nonna,” she said. 

“Nonna?” Rio echoed. “Is that like an abuela? Uh, a grandma, I mean.”

The girl nodded. “Yeah,” she said. “She’s my grandma. I never met her though.”

“You never met your grandma?” Rio gasped. She knew both of her grandmas. She even knew her Mom’s grandma.

“She’s my daddy’s mom,” the girl said, blushing and looking down at the dirt on her feet. “I live–lived with my mother.”

Rio knew that Lilia had a son, but she’d never met him. There were pictures of him around her house that Rio had seen when Lilia invited her family over for dinner. He had the same blue eyes and brown hair as this girl…

“Do you live here now?” Rio asked.

The girl nodded, and Rio noticed that her eyes became wet, a tear escaping down her round cheek.

“I’m sorry!” she said, stepping forward. “I didn’t mean to make you cry. Lilia’s really nice! You’ll like living here with her!”

The girl nodded, sniffling. “I know,” she said. “She is. I just…I kinda miss my friends. And my bunny.”

“Your bunny?” Rio asked, walking forward a little more until she was in front of the other swing. “Where is it?”

“My mother gave him away,” she said, her chin quivering. “She said he was dirty, and I couldn’t have him anymore.”

“That’s awful,” Rio said. “Well, Titi Lilia has a cat! His name is Oracle. He’s really nice.” She shrugged. “For a cat.”

The girl gave her a watery chuckle and nodded. “He is,” she agreed. “I just miss Scratchy. That’s my bunny. He had really soft fur.”

“I’m sorry,” Rio said. 

They were silent for a long moment. Rio sat down on the vacant swing.

“I’m Agatha, by the way,” the girl – Agatha – said after a long moment of silence. “Agatha Harkness.”

“I’m Rio Vidal,” Rio said. “Titi Lilia lets me come over sometimes to get away from my siblings.”

“Are they mean?” Agatha asked, her eyes wide.

“No,” Rio sighed. “Just loud. I don’t like loud. Especially when I’m trying to read.” She held up her book.

Agatha eyed the cover. There was a girl holding a pig on a farm. And there was a spider web. Huh.

“What’s that book about?” she asked.

“You never read it?” Rio asked, her eyes widening in surprise. Agatha shook her head. “It’s about this really cool spider named Charlotte who helps this pig not get eaten.”

“How can a spider help a pig?” Agatha asked, furrowing her brow.

“By writing words in her web!” Rio said. “I’ve read it, like, five times already, but I like it a lot! You wanna read it?” She offered the book to Agatha, who looked at it like it would bite her.

“I…” She looked away. “I can’t read,” she said. 

Rio’s eyes widened at that, her mouth gaping open a little bit. Agatha had to be around her age, which meant that she was probably going into third or fourth grade soon. Rio had never met somebody her age who couldn’t read.

Even Yessenia, her baby sister, who was five, could read some books.

“Well, that’s okay,” Rio said. “That means that I can read it to you! I need to practice reading aloud anyway. Miss Danvers said that I was too quiet last year.” She rolled her eyes. There was no such thing as ‘too quiet’ in her opinion.

“You’d really read me the whole book?” Agatha asked, surprised.

Rio shrugged. “Why not?” she asked. “It’s not that long! And it’s a really good book! Trust me, you’ll like it.”

Agatha looked a little unsure for a long moment, glancing between Rio and the book in her hand, before finally nodding. “Okay,” she said. “That would be okay, I guess.”

“Awesome!” Rio said, swinging a little bit with the force of her excitement. She opened her book and cleared her throat. “‘Chapter One,’” she read, “‘Before Breakfast. Where’s Papa going with the ax?’”

As Rio read, Agatha listened, turning her body slightly in Rio’s direction and leaning against the chain on the swing, her toes still barely brushing the ground. 

Rio read for as long as she could before she heard her mami’s voice calling for her over the fence: “Rio! Time to come wash up for dinner, mi alma!” 

Rio got to her feet and called back: “Coming, mami!” She turned to Agatha with an apologetic smile. “Sorry,” she said, “but I gotta go help set the table for dinner.”

“Okay,” Agatha said, obviously trying to hide her disappointment. “I should probably go inside, too. I think Nonna is making spaghetti for dinner. It should be ready soon.” She stood from her swing, and Rio noticed for the first time just how skinny Agatha was. Her dress hung on her frame like it was two sizes too big for her. 

“You’re gonna love Titi Lilia’s spaghetti. She makes it the best.” She dropped her voice. “Even better than my mami.”

“Rio!” Marisol called out again. “You have one minute to appear!”

“I’m coming!” Rio called back. She smiled back at Agatha. “I’ll come back tomorrow,” she promised, “so we can finish reading the book together. I’ll come back earlier, in the morning. Okay?”

Agatha nodded, giving her a small smile. “Okay,” she said. “I’ll be here. I think.” She furrowed her brow. “I don’t know what Nonna wants to do tomorrow.”

“I’ll ask my mami to ask her,” Rio said, already heading to the fence and pulling up the post. “See you tomorrow, Agatha!” she said, disappearing through the fence.

“Bye,” Agatha said with a little wave, once she was already gone.

“Mami,” Rio said, an hour later at dinner, “did you know that Titi Lilia has a nieta?”

Marisol nodded, placing her empanadilla down on her plate after taking a bite. She reached for a napkin to wipe her mouth. “Yes, I do know that,” she confirmed. “I assume you met her today when you went over?” 

Rio nodded. “I did,” Rio confirmed. “Her name is Agatha, and she’s really nice.”

“That’s good to hear,” Marisol said with a soft smile. “Lilia said she’s going to be living with her from now on, so you’ll be going to school together. I expect all of you,” she looked around at her other children, as well, “to be kind to her. She’s had a rough start.”

“What does that mean?” Yessie asked. She was seated next to Rio, kicking her little legs as she ate.

“Her mami is mean,” Rio said, frowning. “She gave away her bunny!”

The thought of it made her throat tight. She didn’t have a bunny, but the thought of her mami doing that to her made her chest hurt. She thought of their dog, Benny, and how much they all loved him. She couldn’t imagine her mother giving him away. 

She felt the nudge of his nose under the table and looked down to see his scruffy face and those big brown eyes staring up at her. Sneakily, she slipped him a little bit of the chicken from her empanadilla before anybody noticed. He ate it, happily, and licked her foot before flouncing away like the spoiled little mutt he was.

“Yes,” Marisol sighed, sitting back in her seat. She glanced across the table at Santiago, her husband and the children’s father, “that is very upsetting. She must be very sad about that, but we don’t bring that up to her, okay? It’s impolite.”

“Okay,” Rio said. “Well, we hung out today. I read Charlotte’s Web to her, but I didn’t get to finish it, so I was wondering…can I go back tomorrow?”

“I’ll call Lilia and see what she says,” Marisol told her. “I think she’s more focused on getting Agatha settled right now. The poor girl has been through so much in just a short amount of time.” 

“Like what?” Rosie asked.

“Just…a lot,” Marisol said. “We’ll leave it at that. Eat your dinner.”

Lilia was happy to have Rio come and spend time with Agatha the next morning, she said. She liked that Agatha was already making friends. So Rio got dressed around ten AM and rushed through the backyard to meet Agatha on the swing set, her book against her chest.

Agatha was sitting on the swingset again, dressed in a nicer-looking blue dress with a daisy pattern all over it. Her hair was brushed and pulled back into a braid that fell over her shoulder. Rio paused when she looked at her.

She was so pretty…

“Hi, Agatha!” Rio greeted as she came through the fence.

Agatha turned to her and immediately gave her a soft smile that made Rio feel weirdly fuzzy inside. “Hello!” she said, waving. “Look at the dress my nonna bought me!”

She stood, showing off the daisy dress as well as a pair of matching ballet flats.

“It looks really good,” Rio said, nodding and trying not to blush. “I don’t usually like dresses, but yours is pretty.”

“Thanks!” Agatha said, looking a little more energetic than she had yesterday. “I like your shirt.”

It was technically Javi’s shirt, but it had been Santi’s before him. It had rainbow dinosaurs on a gray background. Rio really liked dinosaurs, so she had stolen it.

“Thanks,” Rio said, looking down at her shirt, tugging on the hem. “You ready to keep reading?” she asked.

“Well, I’m not really the one doing the reading,” Agatha pointed out.

“But you’re listening!” Rio said. “You’re hearing the story. My papi says that’s almost the same thing.” She trotted over to the swing and plopped down, opening her book to the marked page. “Ready?”

Agatha sat back down as well. “Yeah,” she said, angling her body in Rio’s direction. 

Rio continued to read the rest of Charlotte’s Web, doing voices for all the different characters and making Agatha giggle. Rio liked the sound of Agatha’s giggle. 

When she finished, Agatha frowned. “That’s it?” she asked. “She just…died?”

Rio nodded, sighing. “I’m sorry,” she said. “I knew that was going to happen. Sometimes, spiders just…die.” 

“That’s dumb,” Agatha said, furrowing her brow. “I don’t like it when things die. It’s not fair.” Her eyes looked a little bit wet again.

“Everybody dies,” Rio said, “eventually. That’s what my papi said when our abuelo died. His papi. It sucks.”

“Isn’t sucks a bad word?” Agatha asked, blushing a bit and looking around like somebody was about to chastise her for repeating it.

“Don’t tell on me,” Rio said, teasingly. 

“I won’t,” Agatha said, smiling. She was silent for a long moment, just swaying on her swing. “My dad died,” she said.

Rio’s eyes widened at that. “I’m so sorry,” she said. “Titi Lilia didn’t tell us that.”

“It happened a couple of years ago,” Agatha informed her. “I don’t really remember him, anyhow. I think his name was Nicholas. He was in the army.”

Rio had known that part. There were pictures of him in camouflage in Lilia’s living room. There was a flag on her mantle in a triangular box. 

Why hadn’t Lilia ever told them that her son died? She wondered if her parents knew and just didn’t say anything.

“Do you have any more books?” Agatha asked.

“Uh, yeah,” Rio replied, thrown by the change in subject. “Tons. You want me to read you another?”

Agatha shrugged, her cheeks turning pink. “If you don’t mind,” she murmured.

“Of course not!” Rio said with a grin. “I’ll go get another one. Be right back!”

She made a beeline back to the fence, before pausing and turning to look at Agatha.

“Agatha?” she said.

“Yeah?” Agatha asked, tilting her head.

“I’m sorry about your dad,” Rio said.

Agatha sighed and nodded. “Me too,” she said.

Rio nodded and ducked through the fence.

10 years later…

“I’m back!” Rio announced, squeezing through the fence posts.

“Took you long enough,” Agatha laughed, swaying gently on the swing set. Her hair fell in gentle curls around her shoulders and she wore a lavender-colored dress that fell to just above her knees.

“You know,” Rio snorted, “you can read now: you don’t need me.”

“But you do all the silly voices!” Agatha complained, pouting at her. Rio was weak for that pout, and Agatha knew it. “It makes it easier to concentrate on the story.”

“I’m sure Audible narrators do the same,” Rio laughed, sitting down on her swing.

“Why pay for Audible when I can have you for free,” Agatha said, with a shrug, grinning at her. “And you read me way more than just one measly story per month.” 

“True,” Rio said, rolling her eyes. “I am way undervalued.”

“Underpaid,” Agatha corrected. “I would never undervalue you.”

Rio felt warmth in her chest at that. She picked at the tear in the knee of her jeans, at the hem of her Snoopy shirt.

“Before I start,” she said, “I have a question for you, but feel totally free to say no if you want.”

“Go for it,” Agatha said, facing Rio entirely, like she always did.

Rio took a deep breath, swallowing thickly and bending the paperback in her hands a little bit. “Do you,” she said, “have a date to Prom?”

Agatha’s eyes went wide at that. “You know I don’t,” she said, “but thanks for rubbing it in.”

“Shit, no,” Rio said. “Sorry. I didn’t mean to…” 

Agatha let out a little cackle. “Rio, relax,” she said. “I’m just teasing you. But, yeah, no. No date to Prom as of yet.”

“Cool,” Rio said, then grimaced. “Not cool,” she said. “Sorry. I just meant…um, would you like to go together…with me?”

“I know what ‘together’ means, Ri,” Agatha snorted. 

“Right, sorry,” Rio said, feeling her face flame. “I just figured that, maybe, you and I could go…as friends?”

“Is that a question?” Agatha asked, still teasing.

“Yes?” Rio shrugged, her palms sweaty as she continued to abuse the book in her hands.

“Well,” Agatha hummed, “we’re definitely friends. Otherwise, why have I been hanging out with you for the better part of a decade.”

“I always thought that I was just nice to look at,” Rio said, striking a silly pose that made Agatha giggle.

Rio was weak for those giggles as well. She didn’t know if Agatha knew that.

“So,” Rio said, “what do you say? Wanna be my date to Prom?”

She left out the ‘as friends’ part, partly hoping that Agatha would think it was accidental, and partly hoping that she’d actually…like it.

Rio had known since she was thirteen years old that she had a crush on Agatha. It was somewhat of a shock to her the day she looked at her across a circle of teenagers playing Spin the Bottle and found herself hoping that Agatha’s spin would land on her.

It had landed on Wanda Maximoff, a red-headed dolt of a girl whom Agatha had proceeded to date for the rest of middle school.

Rio had always wondered if that could have been her holding Agatha’s hand as they walked down the hall together. She wondered if they would have broken up, like Agatha and Wanda had, and ruined their years’ long friendship. 

She had been the one to pick Agatha up after Wanda broke up with her for some nerdy exchange student – a boy, at that. She couldn’t stand the thought of hurting Agatha in a similar way.

So she’d kept her feelings a secret. She continued to read books to Agatha, long after Agatha learned how to read and quickly even became one of the top students in their class, thriving in the right environment under her beloved Nonna. Lilia had treated her much better than Agatha’s own mother ever had, and had paid for tutors to help her learn what she should have in her neglectful mother’s care.

She had eaten lunch with Agatha when she finally joined a regular class – still with a student aide to help her – and hung out with her at recess and after school. She and Agatha had become almost inseparable in the last decade.

Rosie had teased Rio for her obvious crush on Agatha for years, pinching her cheek and calling Rio pobrecita whenever Rio complained about Wanda or the other girls that Agatha dated throughout the first few years of high school.

But now Agatha was single, and Prom was right around the corner. This was her chance – probably the only one she’d ever have.

“Okay,” Agatha said, grinning. 

Rio blinked at her. “Really?” she asked.

“You thought I’d say no?” Agatha snorted. “It’s not like I have so many other prospects. Jen’s already going with Alice.”

“Jen was an option?” Rio asked, feeling a small surge of jealousy.

It was ridiculous to feel that way, considering Jen and Alice had been dating for well over a year at that point. She and Agatha had only kissed once, in ninth grade, on a dare, and they’d immediately made disgusted faces at each other. Jen had pretended to gag while Agatha gave her the finger.

They were best friends, naturally.

Still not as close as Agatha and Rio. 

“Probably not,” Agatha sighed. “She did invite me to share a limo with her and Alice, though, so I wouldn’t be alone. Because, you know, I’m apparently such a loser.”

Rio snorted at that. “You’re not a loser,” she said. “You’re just…intimidating.”

“You’re not intimidated by me,” Agatha pointed out, then furrowed her brow. “Are you?”

“No,” Rio lied. “Not at all. But that’s because I know how sweet you really are when nobody else is looking.”

“Take that back!” Agatha gasped, mockingly. “I am not sweet! I’m a stone-cold bitch!”

“Agatha, darling,” Lilia called, opening the sliding glass door to the house. “Would you and Rio like some cookies and milk? They’re fresh out of the oven!”

“Ooh,” Agatha said, smiling like a little kid. “Yes, we would!” She stood from the swing with a cute little hop, and Rio snorted.

“Absolutely,” Rio said, “a stone. Cold. Bitch.”

Agatha flipped her off the second Lilia’s back was turned, then she took her hand and tugged her into the house.

Rio’s heart was pounding in her ear as she looked at herself in the mirror.

She had opted for a suit for Prom, but she didn’t want to look like some boring penguin in simple black and white. Her suit was dark green with a silky vine pattern going through it in an almost metallic purple color. She had sent a picture of it to Agatha when she was trying it on inside the store, and Agatha had sent back a bunch of fire emojis, so, obviously, she’d approved. 

In turn, Agatha had sent her a picture of her dress, which was long and flowy, with a full skirt that had a layer of lace over it with a floral pattern. The dress was sleeveless, which Rio had seen when Agatha took a picture in the mirror at the store, showing off her bare collarbone. 

Rio had nearly crushed the phone in her fist.

She sent back a thumbs up.

Rio had opted to go without a shirt beneath her suit. The neckline didn’t plunge too deep, and she thought it looked better without the light green button-down. She did, however, wear the purple vest that matched the vine pattern. She paired it with a metallic purple pocket square. 

“You look so handsome,” a voice said from behind her. Rio turned to see her mother and her eldest sister, Rosie, standing in the doorway, smiling at her with wet eyes. 

“You think so?” Rio asked, self-consciously, playing with her lapels. “You think Agatha will like it?”

“I think she’s going to like it a lot,” Rosie said with a teasing grin. “She’s gonna be on the floor when she sees you.”

“Rosita,” Marisol huffed, nudging her in the arm. “Callete! You’re gonna make her nervous.”

“Ay, Mami,” Rio snorted. “I’ve been nervous since the second I asked her.”

“There’s nothing to be nervous about,” she said, cupping Rio’s cheek. “You two have been kindred spirits since the day you met. This is just the natural next step.”

“What if she doesn’t feel the same?” Rio asked, frowning, her throat tight and eyes starting to burn.

“Then you remain friends,” Marisol said, her voice gentle, “and you move on.”

Rio couldn’t fathom moving on after that, but Agatha was more important to her than a stupid crush, so she nodded. She’d find a way. 

“Now,” Marisol said, “let us do something about this hair.” 

Rio’s hair fell to just below her chin. It was long enough to put up in a decently high ponytail, showing off her undercut. She’d caught Agatha staring at her a couple of times the first time she’d gotten the haircut. She knew that she liked it by the way Agatha’s cheeks had bloomed pink when she was caught.

Rio felt her stomach flutter when she thought about it.

“I think we should put it up,” she said, decisively. 

“Okay,” Marisol said, with a nod. “I’ll go grab a comb.” She pressed a kiss to Rio’s temple then fluttered out of the room.

“So,” Rosie said, once their mother was out of the room, “you’re planning on telling her how you feel tonight?”

“No,” Rio huffed, turning back to the mirror and messing with her hair. She saw her sister watching her in the reflection. “I don’t know,” she admitted. “It could ruin everything.”

“Or it could make things infinitely better,” Rosie said, smirking. “You owe it to yourself to give it a shot.”

“But what if–”

“What if, what if,” Rosie snorted. “Rio, you gotta stop worrying about the what ifs. You’ll never accomplish anything if you let them hold you back. Besides,” she added, “it’s obvious that Agatha feels the same way.”

Rio scoffed. “What?” she asked. “How could you possibly know that?”

“Because I’m not oblivious like you, tonta,” Rosie huffed, poking her in the forehead. “Seriously, is there anything in there or is it just a bunch of rocks and dinosaur facts.”

“Fuck you,” Rio retorted. “Dinosaurs are cool.”

“Language!” Marisol chastised as she entered the room again.

“Lo siento, Mami,” Rio said, hunching her shoulders as Marisol moved her to sit on the bed. She sat behind her and started to comb back her hair as Rosie took a seat at Rio’s desk, watching them.

“Tonight’s going to be great,” Rosie promised, watching Rio gnaw on her bottom lip. “You’re going to have a lot of fun, dance like a dork with a pretty girl, and probably stay out all night as is tradition. No matter what happens between you and Agatha, you’re gonna be alright. I promise.”

“You can’t promise that,” Rio muttered.

“Five bucks says I can,” Rosie huffed.

“You’re on,” Rio said.

“Where did you two come from?” Marisol snorted, pinning most of Rio’s hair back neatly and leaving a few wavy strands to frame her face.

“Outer space,” Rosie and Rio answered in unison. It was an answer they’d given since they were little girls. They grinned at each other while their mother rolled her eyes.

“Agatha’s here!” Yessie said, poking her head inside Rio’s bedroom. “Aww, Riri! You look so dashing!”

“Thanks, Yoyo,” Rio said, grinning at her baby sister. Yessie was fifteen, brace-faced, and temperamental. She immediately frowned at the childish nickname. Rio only gave her a cheesy grin.

“Done,” Marisol said, tugging gently on Rio’s ponytail. “Now, for makeup…”

“I have eyeliner and mascara on,” Rio said. “I don’t need anything else.”

“Not even a little lipstick?” Marisol practically whined.

“Oh, she’ll be wearing it by the end of the night,” Rosie said with a knowing smirk. 

“Shut up,” Rio huffed, surging to her feet and shoving her sister’s shoulder as she slipped into her black Keds (because she was not about to grimace her way through a night in pinchy dress shoes). “Ten dollars says you’re wrong.”

“I’ll take that bet,” Rosie said, shaking her hand. “Now, go. Greet your date. Give her a kiss from me.”

“Pendeja,” Rio muttered under her breath, as she made her way out of the bedroom.

“Language!” Marisol chastised.

“Lo siento!” Rio called back, jogging down the steps.

Agatha was standing in the foyer with Jen and Alice. Like Rio, Alice was wearing a dark suit, but hers was black with a pink dress shirt underneath that matched the baby pink of Jen’s gown. Jen was already tall, but she towered over them all in her heels, looking like a tall, puffy cupcake in her princess-style dress. There was a jeweled headband on her bald head and long, white gloves on her hands.

“Hello, Princess Peach,” Rio greeted, receiving a middle finger from the other girl. “Mario,” she said, looking down at Alice, offering her a fist to bump.

“Luigi,” Alice retorted, matching her energy as she returned the bump.

“You two are ridiculous,” Jen muttered. “Nice suit, though.” She grinned, glancing at the girl standing next to her. “Wouldn’t you say so, Agatha?” she asked, nudging her arm with her elbow.

Rio turned her gaze to Agatha, who had been partially hidden by the giant that was Jennifer Kale, and her knees nearly gave out.

The photos that Agatha had sent her of her dress didn’t do it justice. The skirt was fuller than it had seemed in the photos, and the floral pattern on the lace was enchanting. Rio found herself wanting to reach out and trace the delicate-looking petals with her fingers. 

The bodice of the dress had a similar pattern, but there was no lace – only silk. Silk like the vines on Rio’s suit. She didn’t know how she hadn’t realized that before. Her hands itched to reach out and feel that, as well. 

Jen was giving her a knowing grin as Rio took in Agatha’s dress, then her hair, which looked professionally done. Her waves and curls had been piled on her head and held back with bobby pins and clips, braided in some places. It looked intricate and as gorgeous as the girl wearing it.
