Summary

When Nathalie's condition gets worse she and Gabriel are forced to concede he needs a new administrative assistant to take the burden off her. All they have to do is act normal. That should be fine right?

Chapter 1

When Claude had been mulling over whether to take the job everyone he’d spoken to had warned him about the man he'd be working for if he did.

Even the women in HR who'd offered him the job had seemed apologetic, and not just because it was only a temporary role.

However what they don't necessarily know, or apparently seem to remember in the case of his friends, are the absolutely mad things he's been expected to do before. When he'd started working he'd never have thought finding a Michelin-starred restaurant in Paris for a business lunch which would cater to a fruitarian would be one of the easier things he'd have to do but it turns out that the richer people are more detached from reality they become.

Whatever his actual job title it's his job to make sure their lives run so smoothly they don't even notice that abyss exists and that’s included dealing with a lot of things he hadn’t even known existed before.

All rich people are weird. Mostly in the same ways if you boil down to it. How bad can Gabriel Agreste be?

Sure the man basically never leaves his house but while it’s makes things difficult for the rest of the company by the standard of rich eccentrics he’s not even sure that signifies. Honestly it’s almost a stereotype and frankly Claude is in favour of anything that reduces travel arrangements.

The only thing that sets Gabriel Agreste's particular flavour of weirdness apart from standard rich people weirdness is the missing wife but Claude prefers to leave the true crime podcasts to his girlfriend and ignore all the conspiracy theories there.

Besides, while it’s nearly always the husband in Elodie’s podcasts he’d actually seen Gabriel and Emilie Agreste together back when the founder of the company he worked for actually did venture into the outside world and M.Agreste certainly hadn’t looked like a man considering murdering his wife. Quite the opposite in fact.

It’s funny how when he’d been considering whether to take the role he’d barely given a thought to most of this and had been much more focused on the luck of Gabriel offering him another role at a higher salary after the retirement of the FD he’d been working directly below, and one that was a temporary placement so he’d be ready to move with Elodie when she took up her new job in Marseille later in the year.

It had seemed like the perfect job.

Some silly ideas sprouted by idiots with too much time on their hands had seemed like nothing in comparison.

Now standing in front of the mansion the rumours seem much louder.

Still he supposed it was time to stop hesitating and ring the buzzer.

A camera dropped down from the gate and a women’s voice queried his identity,

He introduced himself and was told to come in. The gate opened automatically and he walked up to the front door which was opened before he had time to request entrance again.

“M. Berger, welcome to the Agreste Mansion. I’m Natalie Sancoeur, M.Agreste’s current administrative assistant.” a pale professionally dressed woman said proffering her hand.

He shook it. “Please Claude is fine”

Her lips pressed together clearly unimpressed. “M.Agreste prefers formality.”

He wondered where the red streak in her hair fitted with formality but then he supposed they did technically work in a creative industry, and he’d seen much odder in the members of the company actually working in the design department.

“I see.” He said, already feel wrong-footed, “I didn’t mean to give offence.”

“None taken. Right, you’d better come in then so I can give you the tour. We’ll be working together over the next week. Hopefully that will be time enough to get you settled in.”

He glanced round the lobby as he followed her further into the house.

It was very...white.

Despite working for Gabriel he'd never made much attention to the brand's output. Still he'd never had the impression the designer was as allergic to colour as this interior suggested.

Even the portrait of the designer and his son at the top of the stairs had them dressed in monochrome. Or mourning he supposed based on the expressions. Was hanging a funereal portrait without confirmation your wife was dead proof you knew she was?

Yet Emilie Agreste had been gone for over a year. He supposed that was enough to go through the various stages of grief and reach acceptance. After all the chances that she was alive after all this time seemed pretty non-existent.

People didn’t just re-appear after that sort of time short of some Miracle.

Still the painting seems to hold his attention, as if he couldn’t break away from it. He was sure serial killers were supposed to collect trophies, he might not really listen to those podcasts but that was definitely a thing, but he wasn’t sure that this really qualified and besides it was only one person M.Agreste was suspected to have killed so it wasn’t like he was a serial killer even if he had murdered his wife.

Which he hadn’t.

Probably.

Anyway the man’s business was fashion. Being dramatic was practically part of the job description.

Unlike him Mlle. Sancoeur had kept walking clearly well-used to the strange atmosphere of the house.

"You'll be working in here. Mainly." She said opening a door off the hallway to an office. Well there was a desk, chair and computer at least if little else. Apparently the minimalism was consistent through the house.

He tried not to react to the smell of fresh paint permeating the room. “Mainly?”

“As you know M.Agreste prefers not to attend most events in person, you’ll need to be his representative out in the world, and while he does have a driver and bodyguard, the job includes a certain amount of supervision of M.Agreste’s son Adrien as well.”

“Yes, I’d heard.”

“Good. The next door along is the atelier. You should not enter this without ringing through first. In fact you probably won’t be able to as the door is usually kept locked. Both for proprietary reasons and because M.Agreste dislikes his creative process being interrupted.”

“I see.”

She must have noticed his discomfort and raised an eyebrow. “You’ll get used to it.”

“I’m sure.”

She brought him round wherever else in the house she deemed it worth him knowing; the kitchen, the dining room, Adrien’s frankly unreal bedroom, and then lead him back to the office to start explaining the actual job.

For the first time some level of emotion entered her voice as she tried to explain exactly what was upcoming soon, what needed to be done, who to put through automatically on the phone, who to stonewall and more as if she didn’t have a few days to ease him in.

Intermittently she’d break off coughing but she waved off his offer to get some water.

Still he wanted to offer her some reassurance, "Don't worry, your system will be just as you left it when you return."

To say she reacted might have been overstating it but there was the faintest widening of her eyes as she said. "Return?"

"Um, HR gave me the impression this was temporary cover while you took sick leave. Is that incorrect?"

What illness exactly Mlle. Sancoeur had hadn't been disclosed, but if it wasn't for the fact she honestly did not look well then the pause and glance towards the wall dividing them from M.Agreste's atelier before she responded might have made him wonder if sick leave was a euphemism, and gardening leave might have been the better term.

"No, that's correct." She said.

When it reached Mlle. Sancoeur’s last day Claude felt he had mostly gotten to grips with his new role. The last few days had been as normal as working in a set-up like this could be. Was wasn’t much but was something he was capable of managing.

He understood why they’d recruited inside the company because having some idea of the brand’s operations certainly helped. Still he could tell following Nathalie Sancoeur was going to be a hard act to live up to and that was allowing for the fact that he wasn't being expected to do everything she'd done. Some of it had been delegated with new instructions only to send it to M.Agreste when it was actually finished.

Other parts. He hadn’t been told they’d been passed back up but looking at certain email trails and paperwork he had a suspicion she’d been taking on work M.Agreste should have done himself.

Not that he had much chance of confirming that since he had yet to speak to his boss in person rather than on the phone or the tablet but he couldn’t say he regretted it.

Technically Claude had met him, if you consider knocking on a door and having some papers taken from you with no words of thanks meeting someone. Claude didn’t.

Still those few interactions that only furthered his impression that if he got through this whole thing without meeting M.Agreste in person at all he’d be happy enough and he rather thought his new boss thought the same.

In fact Claude was starting to form a decided dislike for the other man. Not because he was abrupt and demanding-that wasn't news to him; but while the company had always taken care of Claude and his colleagues well its ethos clearly didn’t stem from its namesake. The more time he spent with Mlle. Sancoeur the clearer it became that she should have stopped working before this.

Beside the coughing and how she often clung to the table as she stood up or sat down she also seemed exhausted. He’d come back from lunch one day and found her asleep collapsed forward on her desk.

If she’d worked back in the company offices with other people around there was no way she’d have been allowed to have continued working this long. Here the only other person around was M. Agreste himself, who clearly didn’t care enough to do anything.

He’d vented to Elodie about it over dinner the other night and she’d pointed out that from his description of his working conditions saying the two of them worked together sounded like a bit of stretch.

However given how much they were in contact, including video calls, it was hard to believe nothing had been noticed, and if Gabriel Agreste had been somewhat aware his assistant wasn’t well and never followed up on it that wasn’t really that much better than if he’d let her continue working knowing how bad she was.

He glanced over at her desk which had gone oddly quiet in the last minute or so. She was frowning at a stapled pile of paper. Either she felt his gaze on her or she was going to speak anyway because she looked up at him and said “This needs signing off and returning today.”

“Ah.” He acknowledged but she made no motion to rise. He wondered at her hesitancy at what should have been a routine part of her job. Their boss was abrupt but she must be used to that “Do you want me to take it in to him?”

She set the papers back down on her desk, and seemed to consider his suggestion, but she sighed and went “It’s probably better I do it in case he has any questions, but if you could run it out to the post afterwards that would be great.”

Having made her decision she headed for the door of the Atelier and knocked confidently without any sign of her earlier hesitance. M.Agreste looked irritated when he opened the door but silently waved her in and shut the door behind her.

Which was odd now Claude thought about it.

His expression hadn’t looked any different to whenever Claude had brought anything to him to action, but while Nathalie had told him to let M.Agreste know if anything important or urgent was there, she hadn’t said anything and he’d automatically invited her in.

It could have been that he recognised the documents she’d brought over and did have questions for her on them. Certainly it wouldn’t be unlike the man not to waste any words vocalising any of that, but there barely seemed to have been time for him to notice what she was holding, rather on seeing Nathalie she’d been waved in automatically, or perhaps that was habitually?

Though perhaps that wasn’t so strange. He was working for someone notoriously opposed, especially in the last few years, to interacting with strangers. Maybe once they got more comfortable with each other the Atelier would cease to be off bounds to Claude. Perhaps Mlle. Sancoeur was trying to avoid professional offence by making it obvious how differently she’d been working with M.Agreste to how she’d been.

That was the prosaic explanation and Claude wished he could believe it but certain things still didn’t add up. That would suggest a comfortable working relationship between them and her hesitancy today hadn’t been the first time Mlle. Sancoeur had seemed loath to draw their employer’s attention and it seemed like more than an attempt to hide any discrepancy in their treatment.

If she’d been hiding her illness in an attempt to stay at work then that was pointless now. He thought back to initial question on his first day as to whether there had been a falling but if the signs of M.Agreste favouring Mlle.Sancoeur were slight then the signs of him being unhappy with her were non-existent.

Claude resisted the urge to bury his head in his hands. No doubt if he did so that would be the minute Nathalie would reappear and he had no interest in giving her the impression he couldn’t handle the job. The job was fine, he was quite capable of doing the job, and he just need to ignore the rest of it and focus on that.

It was probably the move from working in the offices with the lots of other people to working in this mausoleum of a house with only Nathalie & the Agrestes to that was unsettling him. Once he adapted his brain would stop trying to generate conspiracy theories but even Elodie wouldn’t take seriously.

He looked at the clock. Nathalie seemed to be taking a long time but at least it was almost the end of the workday and soon he’d be able to go home and forget about the Agrestes for a bit.

Unfortunately at that point a blonde head poked out the side of the doorframe followed by an “Um, Monsieur?”

“Yes Adrien?” he answered his hopes for a punctual end of the day collapsed and he wracked his brain as to whether he’d forgotten a photoshoot for the boy. He was sure none of his usual evening extracurriculars fell this day of the week.

“Is Nathalie around?”

“She’s in with your father.”

“Oh.” Adrien almost sighed clearly disappointed but without any real surprise. Claude attempted not to mentally file this as further evidence that Mlle Sancoeur had worked far more closely with their boss than he was set up to be doing.

The boy remained hovering in the doorway awkwardly holding a laptop which at least didn’t seem to suggest he expected to head off to a photoshoot.

If Adrien had a query about scheduling something then it would be Claude’s sole responsibility to sort that out soon and he supposed he really should be getting the boy used to it. “Did you need something?”

“Um, I was going to ask her to look over my essay before I printed it off.”

“Does she usually do that sort of thing?”

“I guess? I mean I’m generally ahead at school but I can’t really ask my Father.”

“No. I suppose not.” Claude replied straining to keep his voice neutral as he glanced at his clock on his computer again, if Nathalie didn’t appear soon then those documents were going to miss the post, and he was going to miss the end of his day. “Well I suppose I could have a look at it for you since she’s busy.”

He didn’t recall helping Adrien with his homework being one of his job requirements but apparently it was so he’d best get used to it. He supposed it either fell under “minimising interruptions to M.Agreste” or “organising Adrien’s day”

“Would you? It’s not the content, she doesn’t do my work for me, but I just need someone to make sure it makes sense and there’s no obvious errors.”

“And that’s all?”

The boy blushed. “Well, Nathalie says I can be a bit long-winded at times and gets me to cut bits down, but I’m not expecting you to do that.” His embarrassment made Claude wonder if perhaps this wasn’t in fact part of his job requirements, but the boy was so undemanding he could easily see how Mlle. Sancoeur might feel the need to help him out more than was professionally demanded, especially after his mother disappeared.

“Well give it here then.”

Adrien passed over his laptop. “Should I sit here” he gestured at Nathalie’s chair “while you go through it?”

“Hmm? That seems unnecessary-I’m sure a boy your age has more interesting things to do with your time, I’ll review it and flag anything necessary up the way I do for your father and send it back to you.”

“Oh ok. That’s not how, I mean sure that makes sense.” Said Adrien though it took him longer than Claude expected to leave given he’d have thought Adrien would be delighted by the idea of more free time. Still evidently the change in staffing was going to be as much as an adjustment for Adrien as his father even if the teenager was significantly more polite about it.

The essay was well written as far as Claude could tell although it had been a long time since he’d been exposed to this sort of writing. Business formal was its own animal and the crossover between it and school essay writing lay mostly in the lack of informality.

Nor could he remember much of book it was about to know how correct Adrien’s reading of it was but it seemed an intelligent argument being put forth in the esssay at least.

He read through the essay adding comments where Adrien seemed to have become too caught up in his argument to check precisely what he was writing and tried not to keep glancing at his on screen clock.

Nathalie continued to not return from the Atelier.

When he got to the end of the essay he saved it and sent a text off to Adrien letting him know it was ready for him. He looked over towards the Atelier again then at his phone. Neither Nathalie nor the documents had re-appeared and at this rate they’d miss the post anyway so there seemed little point in him waiting to take them out to the street for collection whatever he’d offered Nathalie earlier.

Decision made he shut down his computer and picked up his coffee cup to rinse out in the kitchen. He’d made the mistake of leaving it overnight in an office once and the science experiment that resulted was enough to stop him doing it ever again even if he was already late finishing work.

He headed to the kitchen and washed up the mug in the sink. As he put the coffee cup down he heard a crash come from the direction of the offices.

Rushing towards the sound he almost crashed into Adrien running the opposite direction.

The boy barely stopped to blurt “Can you get the first aid kit? I need to find Gorilla,” before running on.

Claude turned back towards the kitchen and grabbed the kit before sprinting back towards the direction Adrien had come from.

He found Nathalie lying cradled in their kneeling employer’s arms with half her face covered in blood which was dripping on the floor.

He’d not known what to expect but he was still frozen by the sheer amount of blood. He couldn’t even see the wound itself but he realised it had to be somewhere under the pool of blood on her forehead the flow seemed to vaguely originate from.

“Stop standing there and give me something to stop this with.” snapped M.Agreste breaking him out of his frozen shock.

Claude fumbled with the catch and found a box labelled first aid dressing. Why they made these things so hard to open he would never understand.

He passed the white material over to M.Agreste who almost snatched it from him and pressed it to Nathalie’s head. Thankfully she seemed conscious but while she was sat more upright now she remained reliant on M.Agreste support to remain there.

Claude hovered unsure of what help he could offer.

“Should I ring for an ambulance?”

“That’s unnecessary. We’ll be faster driving to the hospital than waiting for one.”

Nathalie pressed her hands to the floor to push herself further up and something in Claude’s chest relaxed at the sight even as she started to speak “It looks worse than it is anyway.”

“Nathalie you can’t see it,” said M.Agreste “You’re probably going to need stitches.”

Adrien ran back with his bodyguard who helped the two of them up and helped bundle Nathalie into the car. Adrien took the first aid kit off him in case they needed something more to dry up the blood and ran into the car after them leaving Claude feeling like a total idiot for having less sense in an emergency than a fourteen year old.

He sighed and looked back at the hallway. He hadn’t been left with any instructions but the least he could do was clear this up. The cleaning service wasn't supposed to come until Thursday and even if he could move them to tomorrow he could hardly leave this here overnight for the Agrestes to come back to once they returned from the hospital.

He texted Elodie saying he’d be even later and he’d explain when he got home.

Looking over the mess he noticed first the bottom half of the glass Nathalie usually kept with her to sip from during the day and then smaller shards shattered about. He supposed that explained the blood-she must have tripped holding it or something similar. Better that than hitting your head hard enough to cut it or whatever he’d thought had happened.

The documents she’d gone in to show to M.Agreste lay on their desk mocking him. Rationally he had no responsibility for what had happened today but that didn’t stop him from googling the later post collection in Paris to seen them over to.

Chapter 2

Until he’d actually entered the mansion Claude had been able to put what had happened to the back of his mind. People had to go to hospital to get cuts glued or stitched up all the time and she’d gone before the nightly post bar rush. There had been no real mystery to the situation-he’d known she had dizzy spells and there was the broken glass he’d found.

It was a little too neat that it was the last day she had been scheduled to work, but coincedences happened and really that was a lucky one. Perhaps it was an irregular way to handover a job but that was it, and he kept telling himself that.

However once he was inside he couldn’t stop feeling on edge. He worked in an office, or he had until now; it wasn’t as if he was accustomed to blood in his workplace.

He tried to tell himself that he was being silly and he needed to get back to work, and with some panic he remember that that started with making sure Adrien got off to school alright, or at very least contacting the school if it turned out he hadn’t had enough sleep at night to make it. So despite how it still felt like he was intruding when he sat anywhere in the house other than his office he went in search of the boy.

The last thing he expected to find instead was for Nathalie Sancoeur to be sat at the breakfast bar in leggings and an oversized jumper. A dressing covered by what looked like a large transparent plaster covered her right temple.

Seeming completely unware of the oddity of her existence there she said, "Ah, is that Adrien's schedule? I can go through it with him if you want to make a start on answering emails. The coffee machine is over there if you want one."

Years of work meant he was unable not to offer any response but he was also too confused to come up with anything better than "Um."

"I would advise taking one in for M.Agreste before his 9:30 conference call, but until he gets used to you it's best you try and minimise in person contact time with him."

"Right." He answered. The small part of his brain that delighted in the confirmation of his suspicions regards her working practices was drowned out by the larger part that was screaming about what on earth was going on.

Claude decided to take up her suggestion of getting himself a coffee.

Perhaps after that it would make sense why the woman he was replacing, who was supposed to have finished work yesterday, who he'd seen literally collapse in a pool of blood yesterday was sat in the Agreste mansion and talking about scheduling.

Surely the company couldn't be expecting her to make up for the hours she hadn't completed yesterday, because again she'd collapsed in a pool of blood.

Before his thoughts could spiral further into what job exactly he'd signed up for Adrien ran into the room looking far too awake for someone who must have got back from the hospital at goodness knows what time.

"Nathalie! You're up! I was going to check on you but Father said not to disturb you. Are you feeling better?"

"Thank you for asking Adrien, I'm much better than yesterday. Honestly."

"Good. You're still staying though right?"

"For the next few days at least. Anyway you should be eating rather than talking. M.Berger here has your schedule to go through."

"About that Mlle.Sancoeur, I might take you up on your earlier offer."

She took the tablet from him with far more pleasure than anybody should ever show when given anything work related. Especially when said someone was clearly too ill to be doing any work.

He attempted to get through his emails (and those forwarded from her out of office) on his phone while she went through the boy's day with him but it was hard to concentrate as he rapidly rethought his ideas of Nathalie Sancoeur’s relationship with the Agrestes if she was living with them now.

Once Adrien left with his driver he looked back up at her.

"So you're living here?"

She rolled her eyes. "That's overstating it. After my turn yesterday M.Agreste and Adrien wouldn't hear of me going home in case another attack came on, and there was no one there to help. I'll manage to escape once they calm down in a few days."

The hairs on his arms stood up on end. "Escape?"

"Metaphorically.” She looked unconcerned but then she usually did, “it's not worth the argument and I wouldn't want to upset Adrien. Besides I can't pretend there aren't advantages in being here rather my apartment. Better coffee for one."

“I see.” He took a sip of his coffee. “What exactly happened yesterday? Is there anything I should be doing to help?”

He did not add “Should you even be out of bed?” though he was sorely tempted to. He thought you had to monitor people after head injuries but he wasn’t actually sure what for. Were you supposed to keep them awake or was that something else? If victims were supposed to stay awake then he supposed that would explain her being out of bad.

“I fainted I think. I’ve been having dizzy spells as part of my condition but it’s not usually that bad. Unfortunately I happened to be holding a glass at the time and it shattered hence my lovely accessory.” She gestured towards the dressing.

He supposed it fit with scene he’d found.

“But you’re ok?”

“I don’t have a concussion or anything if that’s what you mean. It doesn’t really even hurt that much. I’ll be fine, even if it’s a bad place for a scar.”

“Well that’s something I suppose,” He mentally grasped for a non-awkward way to end the conversation “Time’s getting on, I should be getting to work.”

He liked to think he did not flee the kitchen for the comparative freedom of his office.

Whatever was going on here it wasn’t the coffee keeping Nathalie Sancoeur here; Of that much he was sure.

It did make sense to stay around other people if you were going to be randomly fainting and injuring yourself but, while he might have to give M.Agreste the slight credit that he clearly did care for his former assistant after all apparently, he didn’t understand why she was here and not with her family or at least with friends.

He tried to concentrate on his work and one advantage of the barrage of emails life had decided to send him today was that at least he had little time to wonder at what exactly what going on.

Idiot suppliers and customers were within his capabilities and besides it wasn't as if he'd have to dig through his predecessors emails to discover what was going on with any disputes because apparently his predecessor was going to be here indefinitely!

Work may not have been as good a distraction as he'd hoped.

Still it did mostly drown out the chorus of utter confusion in his day until M.Agreste rang through and in the first time since Claude had known him actually asked after his son this morning.

It was admittedly an almost rhetorical question about whether Adrien had got to school alright thrown in after a conversation about the business but considering Claude would have wondered if M.Agreste knew he had a son if not for the boy being the face of his brand this was pretty earth shattering.

So while he had the sense not to admit he'd let her do part of his job he hadn't thought much of saying "and he seemed glad to see Nathalie at breakfast" at the end of his sentence.

From the silence before any response this was a mistake.

"What.”

Unsure where exactly he’d gone wrong he repeated. “Adrien got off to school fine and seemed relieved to see Nathalie at breakfast?”

He wished for once that his employer would just make normal phone calls instead of video calls and he could have been spared the glare being bestowed on him.

“Why was Nathalie at breakfast?”

“I.don’t know sir. I suppose she was hungry?”

He realised he probably sounded like a bit of an idiot but he didn’t think he quite deserved the condescension in his boss’ voice when he replied. “You suppose she was hungry.”

“She was there when I arrived sir, I didn’t ask her why.”

M.Agreste pressed his fingers to his temples and exhaled before saying “Please inform me if she comes downstairs again.”

He then cut the call.

Claude wondered if this was the end of it as he added “Monitoring Nathalie Sancoeur” to his updated mental job description which continued to increase day by back from the one that HR had sent him over originally. He could probably get them for false advertising or something.

But moments later he got confirmation that M.Agreste was not satisfied by the video call as he heard a door slam followed by steps upstairs.

Claude gingerly propped the door to his office open to listen for any sound. Technically Mlle. Sancoeur wasn’t his colleague anymore so it wasn’t his place to intervene in any argument between M.Agreste and his, well, whatever exactly Nathalie Sancoeur was to him.

Besides had it been Elodie hurt like that yesterday then he probably wouldn’t have been very happy at her getting out of bed either, and he’d ignored far less justified arguments between the people he’d worked for and their families.

Still she had said escape even if she was joking, and Mme.Agreste had disappeared without explanation, and it just didn't seem right not to at least listen.

But no sounds reached downstairs. He supposed that was a good thing. The house was too large for him to hear normal conversation from the upper floors and who knew what room she was even in, but he would have noticed yelling even if he wouldn't have been able to make it out.

Through the open door he saw M.Agreste come back down the stairs and return to his office. He didn't look happy but then Claude has never actually seen him looking happy.

He got out his phone and texted Nathalie “R u ok?”

He got back “Tell M.Agreste I’m fine.”

He wasn’t sure whether to be insulted at the suggestion he’d only ask after her health if told to by his employer.

His phone buzzed again but it was Elodie, not Nathalie, asking if he’d seen the latest on the Ladyblog. He texted back “not yet” and pulled the website up on his phone expecting to see reports of a new Akumatision though he hadn’t had any news alerts on his phone.

Though Paris was overdue for a Hawkmoth attack and the Ladyblog did tend to outpace traditional news sources on Ladybug and Chat Noir news. How a teenager kept outperforming actual professionals on Ladybug and Chat Noir news he’d never understand.

Instead he got a rather perplexing headline.

HAWKMOTH STOPPED AKUMATIZING ME

Beneath was a video interview with the not-akumatized almost victim. Claude sighs as he shut the door. It had been on workplace relationships generally. And it had been mostly about making sure Adrien didn’t think his father and Nathalie’s situation was normal. And anyway it’s good for Adrien to know how to deal with both sides of the equation and—

Well he feels pretty vindicated on thinking Adrien needed to know how to deal with being harassed.

“You know,” the teenage female model said, “I bet your father would love it with we dated. It would be such good publicity. He’d have to let us out more for social media photos for the brand you know.”

Adrien’s smile was becoming more and more fixed, “I’m not really interested in dating at the moment Lila. I need to deal with school, and look, I realise you want friends and I’m flattered you’d chose me but when I want to date someone I want it to be because I love them.”

The girl wasn’t quite good enough to hide her irritation, but managed a smile of her own, “we wouldn’t have to actually date. Just pretend to.”

Even from a distance Claude thought his ‘what the actual hell’ face was probably less good than Adrien’s.

And indeed Adrien only looked mildly confused when he said, “Why would I do that?”

“Because,” she said with a flick of her hair, “then your father would let you out of the house more, and we could hang out with the rest of the class.”

Claude’s heart sank at the idea that Adrien knew this girl outside work. At this point he was thinking that even crazy-swimming-pool-hanger-on-girl might have been an improvement. She was pretty short but she might have had a cute enough face for editorial and at least she wasn’t actually trying to get Adrien to date her.

At least Adrien had the sense to draw back in suspicion. “Why would you want to do that?”

“You’re Adrien Agreste. If you like me then other people would think I’m worth liking.”

“Lila, I’ve told you. You don’t have to lie to make people like you. Just be nice to them.”

The girl laughed, “I think you have had a very different life to me Adrien.”

“Alright,” Claude said to the photographer who’d theoretically been showing him the shots on his camera but in fact they’d both been listening to the kids, “I’m going to go break that up.”

Enough was enough. Adrien was working. He shouldn’t have to deal with this whatever damage the girl already had. He was starting to think maybe he needed to send an entire school year off to therapy. Maybe he could convince his employer it would have a good return on investment as a charitable expense.

They’d get tax relief and they’d have way less akumas for at least a few months before the year below them grew up enough for their own set of partially romantic insecurities. “I don’t suppose you could call the whole thing off and re-schedule with another model?” The photographer said, without quite begging him but coming pretty close.

Claude looked at him in surprise. “Do you think I have that sort of pull with M.Agreste?”

The man shrugged, “I hoped Mlle. Sancoeur did after the last debacle with this one, but apparently not.” He shook his head, “How can this girl have zero chemistry on camera with him Adrien she’s draping herself over him off camera.”

Claude snorted at the mess in front of them. “Would you say he has chemistry with her off camera?”

Adrien certainly didn’t seem interested in the slightest and the boy could actually act.

“Touché.” The photographer acknowledged.

Claude sighed and called his charge, “Adrien. Can you come here?”

The boy looked far too relieved to be able to be out of the girls claws, and nearly ran over to them.

Adrien almost bounced on the balls. “Did you have feedback?”

“I think we’re going to try a few shots of just you,” Claude said, and got a thankful nod from the photographer. Hopefully they could salvage something from this shoot. “But Adrien.”

“Yes?”

“Are you OK?”

Adrien looked at him, far too confused for a boy that had just been rescued from an awkward situation, “I’m fine.”

Claude hesitated, because if Adrien was fine then maybe this was just teenage drama, but still “She seemed a little pushy.”

“That’s just Lila.” Adrien said with a shrug. “I can handle it.”

“That’s not something you should be expected to do. Do you remember your course?”

“I’m not,” Adrien shut his mouth suddenly after a look of pure offense as realisation passed through his eyes.. “Wait. You’re saying she’s the one crossing boundaries?”

“Yes.” Claude agreed. “That’s exactly what I’m saying.”

Adrien wrinkled his nose. “I hadn’t really thought about it like that. I mean I know her from school so it’s not as if we just work together but yeah if it’s coming off that way to other people too then I can talk to her.”

Claude was slightly thrown by Adrien’s confidence. “You can?”

The boy drew himself up to his full, growing if not yet impressive, height. “We have an understanding.”

It would have been more convincing coming from the boy’s father but still, “you do?”

Adrien hand went to the back of his neck awkwardly, “Lila’s a little overeager to establish herself in Paris. She travels around a lot for her mother’s work you know? She’s lonely. I get that. But I’m not that used to all that social stuff either so maybe I’m missing things when she goes too far?”

“Yes.” Claude said dully. “Yes, you’re definitely missing things.”

This poor boy had no idea what normal even looked like if he thought that was normal. Then again he apparently thought his father and Nathalie were normal.

“Huh,” Adrien said.

Despite his talk with Adrien Claude still felt the need to say something to the girl while they were watching Adrien posing.

“Oh,” she said looking up at him, “Hi. He’s so good at it isn’t he?”

She paired it with a more convincing smile. She’d was going to be pretty good at the whole feminine manipulation thing once she’d grown up but right now Claude thought she reminded him more of kitten sharpening it’s claws than a cat extending them out in comparison to half the women he had to interact with on a daily basis in the fashion industry.

“He is.” He said. “And he’s better when he’s not distracted.”

Lila blinked up at him, “distracted? Are you, are you saying I’m distracting to him? We’re friends. I’m sorry if you don’t think we’re professional enough but,”

His sympathly didn’t quite extend to not-quite-convincing faux shows of acquiescence. “You’re not.”

Clearly affronted the girl’s eyes narrowed. “I’m sorry?”

“Your behaviour with Adrien is inappropriate. It needs to stop.”

Her mouth fell apart as her eyes widened and she gasped, “I’m just trying,”

“I know what you’re trying. But he’s not interested.” He said, and then because he was somewhat sympathetic to the girl given that the rest of them that weren’t born rich had to work at it and she was clearly trying but, “And frankly you’re not good enough to get away with it.”

Her attempt at pure confusion was actually rather decent. “What?”

“Adrien’s naïve and he’s only doing this because of his dad. You’re not and I think you actually have ambition.”

She continued with that clearly constructed to be cute frown. “Isn’t that a good thing?”

Claude winced. “It can be,” and he didn’t even begrudge her that, “but if you keep trying to take shortcuts like that it’ll blow up in your face. Frankly, though it’s not been in much evidence today. I think you have a chance of making something of yourself but not if I have to report back to Adrien’s father that we have to blacklist you.”

This time, the girl smirked. “Oh, Adrien’s father won’t blacklist me.”

Claude suddenly felt a little off course. “I’m sorry.”

“Don’t you know?” She twirled her hair around her finger, “I’m his new muse. He understands me. He’s going to make me the face of his brand.”

His stomach turned over in horror and suddenly his impression of the young teenager in front of him playing with forces she didn’t quite grasp yet felt accurate in a far more concerning way.

He tried to get his voice steady. “You’ve met M.Agreste?”

The girl smiled, and there was an actual delight in her eyes, that made his sudden nausea worsen, “Of course I have. You see, he was upset I’d snuck in to help Adrien with his homework so I went an apologised and once he actually met me he understood I was such an asset to the brand.”

Claude had never before actually wanted Mlle. Sancoeur and M.Agreste to be in a relationship before. Wanted to be sure he actually cared about the women, yes, though he was pretty sure he did at this point. Wanted their relationship something normal, certainly. But he’d never actually wanted them to be together romantically. For her sake as much anything.

Right now he wanted M.Agreste to be in love with his former assistant with every fibre of his being.

Because it he was then at least Claude had some hope that it was unlikely he was having an inappropriate reaction to an actual child the literal same age as his son.

“I see,” he tried to gulp down his burning building up in his throat, “and how exactly do you do that?”

“I think he just recognised my natural talent,” the girl said and his heart sank as she added, “of course he’s not the first. Back in Italy I had any number of fashion houses interested in me you know. I had to tell Donatella,”

“Yes right,” he cut the girl off, “but I need you to know there are people you can talk to?” “Talk to?” Lila asked with a frown, “like the press? The real press? Not jumped up fakes like the Ladyblogger?”

“Oh my god,” Claude said, “you’re the one that claimed to be Ladybug’s friend.”

“I can neither confirm or deny that,” Lila said. “Given you know, Hawk Moth.”

“Yes I understand, but Lila?”

“Yes?”

“If anyone tries to make you do anything you don’t want to do then you can tell me. OK? Or anyone else here? You don’t just have to obey M.Agreste because he’s important. If anything’s wrong I can take you to the police station?”

Just because he worked for the man and because he was despite his own best effects almost becoming sympathetic to him that didn’t mean he wasn’t going to make sure this girl didn’t get whatever help and support she needed.

Tears started to build up in Lila’s eyes, and his hands awkwardly clenched at his sides. In the circumstances it didn’t seem right to touch her.

“No one can help being akumatised,” Lila said.

“I know,” Claude answered totally thrown by the twist in the conversation.

“It’s not my fault it happened more than once. It’s not just me. It’s that Pigeon guy, and one of the other girls in my glass has been akumatised way more than I have. I don’t know anything about Hawk Moth to tell the police. Why would you be so cruel just because you don’t like that I’m friends with Adrien?”

“No one blaming you for being akumatised,” Claude said feeling relief seep through him at her response even as he now had a new reason to treat her with kid gloves, “That’s Hawk Moth’s fault not yours.”

“Thank you,” the girl said weepily. “It’s so nice to meet someone who understands.”

He stared at his phone. It seemed a neutral enough comment.

“Is it true M.Agreste himself hired Lila?”

Nathalie’s answer came back almost immediately. “Unfortunately yes. Why? Is she being a problem on set again?”

“Yes.”

“I can try talking to him.”

“does he actually think she’s good?”

This time there was a long gap before she responded.

He tried not to constantly check his phone for a notification but eventually he got back.

“I think she reminds him of Emilie.”

Claude promptly felt sick again.

Claude stared at the Atelier door then turned around, and headed into his own office, before ringing through on the tablet.

Sometimes discretion was the better part of valour. Especially with how awkward and fraught the topic was.

M.Agreste picked up the call, “yes?”

He didn’t give any further sort of greeting. Unfortunately Claude was used to that at this point.

“We’re back from the photoshoot.” Claude announced.

“I see.” His boss said, “did it go well?”

“Adrien put on an exemplary performance.”

The boy’s father nodded, “as always.”

“His partner for a shoot was rather less so.”

M.Agreste scowled, “who was the female model? I don’t have time for failures.”

For once that actually felt like a reassurance rather than a future problem and Claude tried to keep his face as blank as possible as he said. “Lila Rossi.”

“Ah.” M.Agreste said. “That’s unfortunate.”

This was the moment. He knew it. And he didn’t want to bring it up but if he was any sort of decent human being he knew he had to, and he did like to think of himself as a fairly decent person.

Uneasy as anything he ventured. “Sir?”

“Yes?”

“Is there a reason why we can’t just dismiss Mlle.Rossi?”

The man dismissed him. “She’s Adrien’s friend.”

“It didn’t look like that today.” M.Agreste didn’t answer him immediately so he added, “her advances appeared to make him uncomfortable.”

His boss stared at him through the tablet screen“Advances?”

“She kept suggesting they should date. Apparently you’d approve.”

Once again the man scowled, “That’s not our,”

He didn’t continue after his sudden stop and it took every bit of Claude’s professionalism not to show the disgust on his face, “you’re what?”

M.Agreste didn’t answer his question. “She doesn’t have to work with Adrien. There’s lots of other work out there isn’t there?”

Claude tried to contain his sigh. “She’s too young for the catwalk. But maybe in a year or so?”

“Well find her something.” M.Agreste said. “And maybe schedule me a call with her.”

He tried his best but some of his incredulity leaked through in his. “You?”

“Yes.”

“I just,” Claude’s fingernails bit into his legs through his trousers under the table. “don’t think that would be very appropriate sir.”

His boss looked at him as if he was an ant he wanted to squish. “I’m sorry?”

“You’re not in charge of hiring or staffing. People might find it rather odd you meeting with her by yourself. Especially given her age.” Claude just about managed.

“Her age?”

He’d gone far enough by this point. “She is a teenager sir.”

“Yes, that’s why she works with Adrien.”

Either the man truly was oblivious or Gabriel Agreste was a truly exemplary actor. For once Claude was glad for all the weirdness in the house because the very fact he’d been exposed to it suggested the former not the latter.

“She referred to herself as your muse sir.”

“She what?” M.Agreste barked, seeming fully offended, “Emilie’s my muse. Or perhaps Adrien. That’s very presumptuous of the girl to claim. I definitely need to talk to her.”

“Perhaps someone in the legal department should handle that conversation. It certainly seems like something worth heading off.”

“Yes. She seems to have quite forgotten her place.”

Reassuring as that response almost was, “that wasn’t my major concern sir.”

“What was your concern?”

Uneasily he volunteered. “It rather seemed like a possible PR issue.”

His boss didn’t quite seem to get the point. “What?”

“She’s Adrien’s age sir.”

“So?”

“So. I think most people would be rather uncomfortable with an actual child replacing your late wife as your muse.”

Despite the man’s near permanent tan, which honestly Claude had questions about, M,Agreste went pale as the implication finally hit him.

“You think? Lila? She’s, I might exploit teenagers but I don’t exploit them that way. God. If anything she’d be like the daughter I could have had not,” he seemed to physically recoil over then screen, “anything else.”

Claude recalled Emilie Agreste’s laughing green eyes. But also fair skin and blonde hair that didn’t quite fit. And then he looked at his employer with his olive skin. And he remembered Nathalie Sancoeur’s dark hair.

And he decided it was better not to comment on it.

“That’s all well and good sir,” he said, “but should I make the appointment with legal or HR?”

“Please do.” M.Agreste said.
