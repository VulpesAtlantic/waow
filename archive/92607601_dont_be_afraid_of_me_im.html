Summary

“Shall we?”

Rio jumped at the voice, playful but heavy and echoing in her mind and breaking her stupor, that voice sank in her flesh, in her bones and Rio knew, before turning around that the stranger, her stranger was standing next to her cab.

“You.”

“Me,” the stranger smiled.

And Rio opened the door for her.

Chapter 1: One

 

Rio had thought about stopping for a cup of coffee at the convenience store near the end of 127th Street; it was late and perhaps the bitter black coffee would chase the growing sleepiness that crept along her spine since her 6th fare of that night; although she could still taste the slightly sour remnants of her last cup on the back of her tongue, Rio decided that the usual twitchiness of her muscles that came with drinking yet another double shot of espresso was worth enduring to make her night go by quicker. 

By the time the decision had been made to park her cab along the road, leave her seat even for a minute, and stretch her legs on the warm curb of this street where people were fanning themselves with all kinds of papers or magazines in the hopes of escaping the insufferable heat of their homes, someone had already hopped into the back of her taxi.

“Drive,” said the woman. 

Because it was a woman, Rio glanced at her in the mirror but only caught sight of her profile, hidden behind long strands of dark hair. 

The stranger didn’t look at Rio, didn’t buckle her seatbelt, didn’t say anything else. 

She only fixed her gaze on the street, the busy street of a spectacularly warm summer night where Rio hoped to have purchased her fourth cup of coffee. 

But Rio didn’t drive, because Rio needed directions.

She needed to know whether to drive west along 127th St or to turn around east, she needed to know if, at the next intersection, she would have to turn left or right, if she went straight, did she go until the river or would she need to stop before that. 

People didn’t tell Rio to just drive, they gave her an address or a direction, sometimes it was just “North” or “South” but Rio could work with that; she was good with directions.

So no, Rio didn’t drive, she sat in the rumbling car until the woman – the stranger – gave her more to go on, she was strange, this stranger, she sat at the back of Rio’s cab with her shoulders straight and her head held high, perfectly calm for someone with such a vague request, seemingly content to be in the backseat of this cab on this particularly warm night on this busy street, as if, being here, with Rio, was exactly the place she ought to be.

People aren’t themself in Rio’s cab; people are rarely themself in any situation that involves a stranger driving them around, people fidget, people feign ease, people are late or stressed, they talk – sometimes too much; people are too tense or too relaxed, people are drunk or worse, but this woman, this stranger was everything those people aren’t.

She was herself and only herself, calm in the back of a cab at midnight in the middle of the city on a warm, warm summer’s night.

“Drive,” she said again, somehow calmer and yet firmer, as if Rio’s hesitation was entirely predictable and not at all an inconvenience to her mysterious plan of driving aimlessly around the city.

So, Rio drove. 

The windows were down, letting in the warm air of the night.

Rio limited the use of her AC as its noise and its smell squeezed her brain in ways she’d rather not drive with, it elicited an ache behind her eyes and she always had the distinct sensation that the vile, artificially cold air would soon creep into her lungs, into her bloodstream, into the very cells of her being and change her on a fundamental level.

Rio knew that this was false, this was a fantasy; she knew, because people told her so when she exposed her concerns to the other cab drivers, none shared her fears, all laughed, discreetly, they thought, but Rio knew well enough that the other cab drivers looked at her funny.

She turned left on Madison Avenue and kept going despite the steady silence of the woman, who was still turned to the window watching the streets, maybe, as the warm summer air gently swept through her hair. 

The woman didn't react when Rio took another turn, when she stopped at a red light, when she drove through the busy streets of the city.

Rio could have easily forgotten her, her silence and her stillness absolute in the back of her car, but the woman’s silence and stillness had the opposite effect.

Instead of forgetting her, there was little Rio could do to divert her attention from this woman.

Her silence and her stillness infused the whole car until Rio had to breathe her in, she imagined this silence, this stillness swelling in her lungs, seeping into her blood and spreading in her body through the thousands and thousands of arteries, veins and capillaries that made her.

Oddly, Rio didn’t fight this intrusion in her system, it didn’t feel twitchy like the coffee or toxic like the AC, the silence and the stillness of the stranger, coated her burning nerves with a welcoming coolness, and with her eyes fixed on the road, Rio didn’t feel sleepy or twitchy anymore, just silent and still.

The woman shifted, just a bit, just a hair and Rio understood that with this miniscule shift that the stranger wanted her to change directions, so Rio did, almost immediately.

She turned a corner, then another and drove until she found a bridge.

Even now, years later, Rio still couldn’t tell you what the bridge was called.

Taxi driver every night of every week of every month for five years, along the same streets of the same city that never sleeps just as Rio never sleeps, always driving endlessly, people to places, places to people and still, Rio could not tell what bridge she crossed to drive the stranger away from Manhattan.  

The car went east perhaps or north, Rio recognized the streets that she knew so well, the buildings that she used to pass by, every night of every week of every month of every year.

After enough time a place sinks into you like wine on a linen cloth, it settles into your synapses and into your brain, it leaves a stain that remains even after thousands of washes.

Driving around, Rio’s brain created something more than a map, something deeper than topology, an understanding of the city, of its systems, of its ways.

But then and there, when Rio turned the wheel, she didn’t follow the map in her brain, when Rio slowed at a stop sign, drove again, turned left or right, she followed the woman, the stranger, sitting at the back of her cab, the stranger drove through Rio, through the city into this very warm night.

And Rio, for the first time in her entire existence, didn't think.

Her silly, endlessly buzzing little brain was finally quiet.

The woman drove Rio deeper into the city, in places Rio knew but didn’t recognize.

She drove twitchy little Rio deep into her sense of calm. 

She drove to the rhythm of the pulsing city, above its masses, slithering between buildings, driving for the sake of driving.

The air became cooler as they reached wider streets, the city became quieter as people went to bed.

Only then remained the silence and the stillness of the car, silence and stillness now shared between them like the gift that it was.

Her yellow cab was now the only constant in the sea or darkness, a last bastion of silence and stillness, so precious, eternally driving between the faceless buildings of this mad city-

“Stop here.”

It stung her ears, sharp and violent in the deep silence.

But Rio stopped.

The woman didn't move for a second and Rio almost thought –hoped! that she misheard, but she couldn’t have misheard because she stopped and there were no thoughts in her stupid little brain so if Rio stopped it was because the stranger decided it.

Rio felt her eyes on the back of her neck, they stung almost as much as her voice, calm, firm, decided, like everything the woman, the stranger ever did.

Her neck prickled, she waited for the silence to be broken again, she couldn’t tell why but her breath accelerated, her heart beat furiously in the depth of her ribcage and the woman simply waited.

It took everything, everything in her blood and in her nerves to turn around.

The stranger was closer than Rio would have guessed, she was here, here before Rio and Rio never did see her face before, God she didn’t see her face but she saw it now, how blinded can Rio be by a face? It was a face, just a face, Rio screamed in her head, but it wasn’t.

The woman, smirked, aware, very aware and Rio tore her gaze away from that face –what a face! – to the woman’s hand outstretched to her, a few bills offered to her.

“Keep the change,” She said, and Rio took the bills.

The movement felt off, so off even though it was her job, a transaction that took place every night of every week of every month of every year since she started; Rio drove, the passengers paid, but then, when the woman, offered her money, Rio wasn’t so sure what to do with it anymore, this drive wasn’t a fare, because it wasn’t Rio that drove them around but the stranger through Rio.

The woman, the stranger, left without a word, only a last glance in her direction, a glance so real that it got embedded into the synapses of Rio’s stupid little brain, for ever and ever in her grey matter, the blue of her eyes, bluer than the deep, deep sea, when the water is silent but so powerful.

Rio watched her cross the street, somehow nonchalant in her hurry, she walked, elegant, powerful, knowing that the world would sooner stop spinning before she would ever be inconvenienced and so she caught her bus.

Her bus?

Her bus.

The stranger hopped on a bus.

Who takes a taxi to a bus stop?

Long seconds or maybe minutes pass while Rio, sitting in her cab, kept her eyes fixed on the street where the woman disappeared into a bus, an ordinary bus.

She watched as said bus left the curb, left the street, left Rio, alone in her car lost, now that the woman, the stranger, had left in an ordinary bus.

Why take a bus when one can take a cab? This woman, this stranger was so out of the ordinary that imagining her in an ordinary bus made Rio uneasy. 

The image conjured in her head clashed with everything she thought to have perceived of this woman.

Although, when she thought more about it, her thoughts still sluggish and fuzzy, like having been woken up after a long restful night, she couldn’t really say what made the stranger clash with the ordinary bus.

She didn’t dress particularly fancy, she was a young woman, a young woman like so many in the city, that took buses to go to places, so why was the thought of that woman, that stranger, in a bus among others so jarring to Rio?

Rio didn’t start for another minute, now the ordinary bus was long gone, the woman, the stranger, was driven away by another driver into the depths of the city, where she would go on and live life as Rio stayed here alone in her cab.

That was it! For her to be driven by another driver, that someone else, someone random would be privileged to drive the woman, the stranger, when Rio felt as if she and only she could understand the cataclysmic implications of driving her around – no, not to drive her around but to be driven by her into the bowels of the city.

Someone climbed in her cab and Rio growled at this new passenger, they paid her no mind, throwing an address at her then minding their own business and Rio drove.

Gradually, her senses came back together and it was a few blocks away that she reconnected with the map in her mind.

What a relief! she thought as she led her cab into the avenues, in the streets, over  the bridges, what a relief to know where she was driving, to have a clear direction, to understand so completely how the city operated.

The stranger from before had led her into murky waters, what a lovely thing to know where one is going, Rio thought to herself, to be safe, to be locked in the route.

The night continued as it always did, Rio drove, people came and went, all the same, sitting at the back of her cab, requesting fares that appeared so boring to Rio now.

She felt safe in her little cab, touring a city she knew by heart, streets she had driven through hundreds if not thousands of times.

How dull.

Rio had never held such grudges for those unimaginative routes, a club to a home, a bar to a hotel, a hotel to a home, a home to a club, again and again and again, the same fares with the same people, drunk and joyous in the warm night, in the city.

When the sky turned pink and the fares became much quieter, her cab filled with people dozing off, Rio thought of the stranger again, she looked at the summer sky, reflecting on the windows of the tall buildings, grey and pink and beautiful. 

Rio thought about beauty and the stranger.

If the stranger could see the pink light right now, would she find beauty in it? 

Sunrises are easy to find beautiful, when the light hits the top of the buildings but not yet the street, when it is day above and night below, when most sleep but not Rio, Rio is struck by beauty.

It is her favorite part of the job, not driving, not the city, certainly not the people, but the beauty.

It is offered to her, every night of every week of every month, the beauty is there for her to look at.

But now, on this night, where for the first time ever, Rio, at the wheel of her cab, didn’t drive but was driven by a stranger, she couldn’t see the beauty without thinking of her, of her hair, of her eyes so blue, of her silence and of her stillness.

Beauty is here to be found, but for the first time, that wasn’t enough for Rio.

She wanted, more than anything, to show the pink sky, the pink light on the tall buildings to the stranger and say “see? there is beauty here,” and the stranger would look at the pink sky, at the pink light on the tall buildings and agree that yes, there is beauty here.

Suddenly it was all that mattered to Rio, to show beauty to the stranger and look at the beauty with the stranger’s eyes, she ached so profoundly for it that the drunk man sitting at the back of her car tapped on the partition and pointed out that they were driving in the wrong direction.

Rio barked something to the effect of him not knowing the way to his own ass as she swerved expertly into the growing traffic.

To her own stupor, though, he was right and Rio had to admit that thinking about the stranger made the stranger drive through her again, and terrifyingly and beautifully, for a second, Rio had lost the map imprinted in her mind.

She dropped him off, and the idiot tipped her generously as he stumbled out of her cab, her last fare for the night and Rio reflexively drove to the diner.

 

 

Open at all hours, the diner smelled of plastic and vinyl and vinegar and coffee and grease. 

Rio went because all cabbies went there to have breakfast or dinner, she went because the others went and they shared meals and stories and cigarettes and Rio didn’t like the diner, nor did she like its food but she went because that’s what one does when one drives all night or all day and one is alone with strangers that climbed into their cab.

The cabbies are easily recognizable, a bunch of them were already sat in the diner, at a table near the back, they were laughing and eating and Rio mechanically went to them, they didn’t stop laughing or eating when she sat at their table, they didn’t even really look in her direction except maybe Jennifer Kale, who eyed her like a cat eyes another cat, not hostile yet but cautious.

Rio sat and ordered dinner but she didn’t eat when the plate was brought to her, not because she wasn’t hungry but because the act of eating seemed too mundane for the night she just had so she raised her eyes to the table, looked at the other cabbies and asked “Have you ever had a stranger drive through you?”

And the others stopped talking, stopped eating and stared at her, Jennifer Kale rolled her eyes, like she had been expecting Rio to speak and for Rio to say something nonsensical and absurd, but Rio hadn’t said something nonsensical nor absurd, she had asked a very fair question, one she wanted an honest answer to. 

Unfortunately the others didn’t seem to share the sentiment, as they looked at her and stopped talking.

Someone said something in an overly kind tone, but Rio didn’t want kindness, she wanted an answer.

The others offered suggestions of what they thought she meant, but Rio could not have been clearer with her question. 

She thought to rephrase it but couldn’t find words that made her point clearer and the others didn’t understand what she meant. 

They tried to, did she mean an accident? No, not an accident, did she let a client drive in her place? No, that is forbidden, did a passenger comment on her every turn? (some were annoying like that) no, her stranger hadn't said anything.

But the others were soon disinterested in her question, they offered their own stories of annoying passengers and strange encounters, they all had stories, each more interesting than the last but Rio didn’t want stories, she wanted an answer and no one gave her one.

By the time her meal had cooled and she had pushed every bit of potato around her plate, Rio had given up on getting an answer, so she rose and went to pay and went to go home and sleep, maybe sleep before starting her next night when, at the cash register, Alice Wu-Gulliver laid a hand on her arm and leaned closer.

Rio didn’t jump because Alice was kind and she was always fair to Rio even when the others laughed at her.

“Are you ok?” Alice asked, and Rio nodded, she was tired she said.

People that were odd always said they were tired, Rio had learned. 

Saying you were tired excused a lot of strange behaviors and Rio didn’t think her behaviors to be strange but the others seemed to think so, so it was probably the case.

Alice didn’t remove her hand from Rio’s arm, in fact she leaned even closer and said something Rio hadn’t dared to hope for, she said: “Tell me about the stranger.” 

Something in Rio’s chest bloomed. 

She hadn’t realized until now how she wanted to tell people about the stranger, so Rio spoke, she tried to make her voice soft and her words go slow as to appear casual.

So Rio didn’t say much and Alice Wu-Gulliver listened, Jennifer Kale joined them and surprisingly listened as well when Rio told them that the stranger didn’t say much and that Rio drove her around the city with no aim until oddly, the stranger got off, paid her and hopped into a bus.

“A bus?”

A bus.

“What bus?”

And Rio cursed herself for not having looked at the bus’ number, she didn’t know anything other than it was an ordinary bus, one that drove through the city like she drove through the city.

“You’re sure it wasn’t a money thing?” Alice asked, and Rio shook her head, the stranger had paid and tipped.

“She took the bus because she didn’t want you to know where she lived,” said Jennifer, final.

Rio frowned, why would the stranger not trust her—

“It’s not about you, it’s about her, she was probably fleeing something.”

No, the stranger was calm, so calm in fact that Rio got calm driving her.

Jennifer and Alice shared a look that Rio didn’t understand, and Rio was getting tired for good this time and she wanted to be home and to sleep, hopefully sleep before the next night.

“Rio drove a serial killer around,” Jennifer snickered. 

Rio frowned, she didn’t like that, didn’t like that Jennifer Kale would joke about the stranger, her stranger, who was so silent and so still and  drove Rio’s cab through Rio in the warm night in the city.

So, Rio shook her head and went home to her bed.

Rio didn’t sleep.

It was too hot and too bright, and the thought of the strange stranger couldn’t leave her mind.

Her voice kept ringing in her head and Rio replayed their interaction again and again and again, until the stranger’s words didn’t mean anything anymore, simply just notes of a melody stuck in Rio’s tired brain.

And as the day went by, Rio laid stiff as a plank on her bed, bare as the day she was born over the covers, she began to dread her next night, the night in which she’ll have to drive people around but not her stranger, a night where she will undoubtedly be alone, so alone with people sitting in the back of her cab.

Rio told herself that she would never see the stranger again, that the odds of driving her again were so small that they couldn’t be taken into account, that this was it, that the few (too few) moments where they were driving around together on the warm night in the city was the last time they ever would exist together.

But this was wrong, Rio knew this like she knew her own name, she didn’t hope to see the stranger again, she knew that she would, because why then would she have climbed into the back of her cab and driven through Rio on the warm night in the city? 

Why would they have met that one time if it meant never meeting again?

 

 

At seven pm, Rio got up, showered, dressed and found her cab, her very own cab waiting for her parked down in the streets.

And she was tired, and she was twitchy already even without coffee and she was nervous, why was she nervous? 

Rio drove to her first call, where dispatch told her to pick a client up, a tall building to a restaurant and her first fare was done, she was hailed next by a man and his mistress and they fought and fought at the back of her cab, but Rio didn’t give a shit.

Rio didn’t give a shit, because all Rio could think about was the stranger, her stranger, how she had been, so calm and still at the back of her cab, how, even now, with a random couple sitting behind her, Rio swore that she could still feel the stranger’s eyes on the back of her neck.

And Rio sat straighter, drove better, forced her hands to be steadier, because even in her absence, it felt as if the stranger were watching her, and Rio wanted to be good for her, she needed to be perfect for this stranger to take one look at her and smile.

Dispatch next sent her to the airport, accolades on the parking lot, tearful reunions, joyous departures, Rio waited in the pick up line for her next fare.

Rio had never taken a plane, had never travelled overseas or even just outside of the state, yet, Rio had more mileage than most people here, mileage won by going round and around the same streets of the same city, every night of every month of every year.

And Rio never minded not having travelled, she never had needed to go outside of the country, the state, the city, she was fine in her little cab, beauty was here to be seen, no need to go far away, but now she couldn’t help but think of the stranger, of the travels she had probably done, of the things she had seen, so many things, Rio was sure, because the stranger let out a sense of assurance so profound, so deep that it could only come from a perfect understanding of one’s place in the world.

Only someone who had seen the world could be so certain of their life.

Could the stranger show Rio the world? Would she take her hand and bring her to places Rio had never seen before? Rio would very much want to be led by her stranger to a new understanding of the world where she would grow to occupy a new place in it.

While a family of tourists settled in the back, Rio loaded their luggage into the trunk.

The children were tired and screaming; Rio was also tired and would have liked to scream too, but that would be frowned upon, so she drove instead, back to the city.

“Is it always as hot as this?” asked the father, he had a thick accent, one Rio couldn’t quite place, and frankly she didn’t have it in her to care.

She smiled though, and looked at them in the rearview mirror, “welcome to Satan’s backyard!” She said cheerily, “I hope your hotel has AC!” she added with a voice she didn’t recognize.

He laughed, maybe a bit uneasy, so Rio turned on the radio.

Music filled the car and Rio didn’t care for it, all music sounded the same to her and the family in the back certainly didn’t care for the radio and Rio would have been happy to drive in silence with only the sounds of the city and the rushing wind in her ears to accompany them along the warm streets, but the man wanted to chat and Rio would have rather stuffed her ears with burning wax than to chat with him so she listened to the radio instead.

The music was too happy and upbeat and loud and annoying but Rio didn’t change the station because what if the music was  even happier, even more upbeat, even louder and even more annoying.

A reprieve from the musical assault on her ears came as she drove over the Queensborough bridge.

The underwhelming monotone of news presenter seemed to make the news seem even less exciting: there was a disputed law making its way to Congress, conspiracies about the unusual summer heat and the consequential water restrictions, and a body found in Upper Manhattan

Nothing there piqued her interest, so Rio focused on driving, with the journalist’s voice going on and on in the background about that new law.

As the traffic stalled and Rio’s cab came to a halt, Rio could do nothing but drum her hands on the wheel, look out the window, and listen to the radio.

A dead body in Upper Manhattan. 

A man, late fifties, left in an abandoned building, gutted and surrounded by a pool of his own blood.

The father in the back tapped the partition and gestured to his kids.

Rio understood and changed the radio station, the music was more chaotic there, a fast paced string of notes that left Rio slightly nauseous.

She had been in Upper Manhattan last night.

Rio thought back to the busy streets and the countless people chatting and laughing and sweating, all so alive in the warm neighbourhood.

Had she driven past that man? Had she been near him in his final moments?

Rio dropped her passengers off in front of a nice building on a nice street in a nice neighborhood, and she wondered what it would be like to sleep so far from home, in a place where everything is taken care of; somewhere so bougie that the appliances and decorations are all curated with the same goal: to give the impression of taste and convenience.

A pulsing headache started to build behind her eyes, and developed around her temples encasing her head in an iron vice.

She rolled her shoulders, her shirt sticking to the faux leather of her seat and she could feel sweat pearling and rolling along her spine. 

Dispatch sent her away. 

It was further than she would usually drive, but it did her some good to drive out in the setting sun. 

It was still too warm, but Rio rolled all her windows down and sped down the road while buildings became fewer and fewer and vegetation licked at the edges of the asphalt.

She rarely got the luxury of driving alone for long periods of time, she was always in service to someone, a tyrant sitting at the back of her cab. 

Sure, she was in service right now, one could argue, but Rio didn’t mind, she relaxed slightly into her seat, the wind wild in her hair, the sound deafening in her ears.

Rio arrived at the destination, a gas station outside of the city, slightly off the road, nestled into a little patch of trees. 

It was starting to get dark now, the sun had set behind the hills and the sky was taking this blue tint that left everything appearing slightly purple.

Deserted.

Empty.

Not a soul around.

She parked next to a phone box and waited a couple of minutes, but no one came, so Rio got twitchy again. 

She fidgeted with her hands, and a vague sense of unease grabbed her.

Rio stepped out of the cab, slamming the door behind her.

The station was empty, its neon lights stark against the dimming sky, insects were amassing around the lights, they buzzed in their ever-going chase, a flock of swallows were circling the station, their shrieks reverberating on the concrete.

She watched as a moth got caught in a spider web hanging from the telephone box. 

She watched, enraptured as it tried to fly off, organizing its own death in its futile attempt at escaping, exhaustion soon got it and the spider slowly crept to administer the final blow.

And there was beauty to it too, a cruel and harsh beauty, one that was a bit scary to Rio, one that felt like she could get lost in.

“Shall we?”

Rio jumped at the voice, playful but heavy and echoing in her mind and breaking her stupor.

The voice sank into her flesh, into her bones and Rio knew, before turning around, that the stranger, her stranger, was standing next to her cab, wearing a tight sleeveless top and a skirt Rio quickly averted her eyes from.

“You.”

“Me,” the stranger smiled, relaxed and knowing that Rio’s breath had been taken away by her sudden appearance.

And Rio opened the door for her, she never did that, not usually.

People were too hurried to let her open their door, they jumped in and gave her an address, but tonight, the stranger waited for Rio to open her door for her, like this was a Rolls Royce and not a clunky yellow cab.

She took her time climbing in and Rio more than ever, felt like a servant, bowing her head to a queen.

She closed the door softly, with more care than this door had ever been treated to, and circled around to her own seat.

Rio could have gotten a good look at her stranger but she didn’t dare to raise her eyes, out of respect perhaps, or reverence, or maybe even fear.

Fear because this stranger had been in her head all this time since she last saw her hop on that bus miles away and Rio had logically reasoned that they would never meet again; fear because the odds were too good to be true, because dispatch sent her here, not by chance, not by mistake but because – and there was only one explanation for this –  the stranger, her stranger had requested her and her only, and as bile rose to Rio’s lips at the thought of being handpicked by this being, by this queen, it hit her that this little rendez-vous also meant that she was aware of Rio in a similar way that Rio was aware of her.

Rio didn’t speak and the stranger didn’t speak and Rio drove away from the gas station.

They cruised  back towards the city, on the open road, between the trees and the growing night, and the stranger didn’t speak nor move.

Like the night before, her silence and her stillness filled the car and wrapped around Rio, whose brain was now as silent and still as the stranger.

Had Rio had some semblance of brain power left, she would have thought to ask: how did the stranger find her; why did the stranger request her specifically to come pick her up far, far away from the city in the warm evening? 

What was the stranger’s name; who was she; what did she want with Rio? 

Rio would have been certain that this stranger had to have some sort of plan for her  because otherwise she wouldn’t have requested her, but Rio’s brain was empty and fuzzy and none of those questions passed her lips.

When they reached the city, the stranger silently directed her through narrow streets that Rio knew but didn’t recognize. 

They drove along a route that slithered between blocks in a pattern that didn’t make sense if the aim was to go somewhere but did make sense if the aim was to simply drive on this very warm night in the city.

When Rio’s cab arrived on an avenue –an avenue she knew so well yet couldn’t name, traffic stopped them. 

Rio became suddenly worried that the stranger would be displeased by the halt, by the fact that they were now slow, so slow between other ordinary cars, when Rio’s car, with the stranger sitting on the backseat, was so far from ordinary.

Rio fidgeted in her seat and drummed her hands on the wheel, and she was ashamed of the traffic. 

She was ashamed that they were now stuck in it, ashamed of the ticking meter that added a dollar every five minutes, and she was ashamed that this stranger, this queen would be subjugated to something so vile and so boring as a traffic jam.

And Rio dared then, she dared, to flick her eyes up to the rearview mirror and steal just a glance at her queen, sitting at the back of her cab. 

Her heart leapt in her chest the moment her eyes left the road, and she regretted her stupid decision before she even met the stranger’s eyes.

They were already set on her, those eyes, gleaming in the dim light of the car. 

Those eyes, so blue that the sky might be jealous of them. 

They were dark now, dark because of the light –or lack of light, and dark because the stranger was already looking at Rio with something so raw and so powerful that Rio forgot how to breathe for a full minute.

Once Rio had met her eyes in the rearview mirror, she couldn’t tear them away.

She was trapped in the stranger’s dark gaze, and suddenly, Rio wasn’t ashamed anymore, her fidgeting vanished, her twitchiness melted with the stranger’s dark gaze and Rio existed only for her eyes and she could have died now; she could have died and lived a life worth living for having had the privilege to be gazed upon by this queen, this goddess sitting in the back of her cab.

The stranger smirked. 

She held Rio’s gaze and smirked. 

Rio’s heart thumped so loud that it must have echoed all the way into the stranger’s ears and she seemed pleased by that, she seemed pleased by Rio and Rio could do nothing else but look at her and wait for the stranger to release her.

With a simple glance, she had Rio in her snare, and Rio wanted nothing more than to be had by this stranger and she wanted more, she wanted to be had even tighter, even closer. 

Rio wanted the stranger’s attention to feel like a collar around her neck, with a leash that the stranger would tug for everyone to see.

“At the next crossing, take a left turn,” the stranger ordered, her voice so soft, so even.

Rio blinked, “Yes, Ma’am,” she blurted out before she even knew what she was saying and the stranger smiled even wider at that. 

The grin splitting her face sent a shiver along Rio’s spine.

And the stranger didn’t release her, and Rio kept looking at her through the mirror.

Someone behind them honked but Rio didn’t move because the stranger held her still and the honk became more insistent but Rio stayed there, waiting for the stranger to release her.

Other drivers were shouting at her in the street, they were angry and shouting but Rio didn’t move and the stranger held her in her gaze with that dangerous smirk. 

The guy behind honked even louder and shouted even more, but Rio held still.

“Good girl,” the stranger purred and looked away at the street.

Rio’s whole body shuddered and she blinked a few times before putting her foot down on the accelerator and moving, finally moving now that the stranger had released her and sure, everyone on the street was mad at her and glaring at her and shaking their heads, but Rio felt a high like no other.

An incredible warmth entirely unrelated to the temperature consumed her and she could feel it take over her neck and her chest and deep, deep in her belly.

She took that left turn.

Rio took the second right and the traffic lightened.

The cab moved more freely now between the tall façade of the buildings downtown. 

They made their way deeper into the guts of the city, and somehow the night continued to get warmer.

Sweat trickled down Rio’s face, onto her neck. 

The airflow from driving didn’t cool her anymore. 

If anything, it made her warmer.

As they drove further, Rio’s blood seemed to thicken, it felt sluggish in her veins, like lava beneath her already burning skin.

Her breath began to shorten too and as Rio considered rolling up the windows and giving into turning the aircon on, the stranger spoke once more:

“You are tired.”

Rio jerkily nodded, eyes still on the road, sweat still pearling on her skin.

“Pull up here.”

The stranger’s calm voice chased every other thought from Rio’s head, and Rio could only do as ordered, so she parked her car neatly near the sidewalk.

The stranger got out of the car, and Rio would have cried at her sudden departure if the queen hadn’t immediately hopped back inside and settled in the front passenger seat.

“What-”

But the stranger shushed her and laid her gentle hands on both sides of Rio’s face.

Rio could only tense under her touch.

The cool hands grabbed her firmly, and Rio felt her heart thump in her throat.

Unable to do anything else, Rio studied her face, a face so endlessly perfect that she felt she could get lost in admiration.

She looked at the delicate black mascara adorning her lashes, the surprising metal piercing glinting on her eyebrow, her nose that made Rio feel unreasonably warm, her dimpled chin and those full lips painted a dark red wine shade.

“You’re tired,” the stranger stated again, “and I ask so much of you.”

Rio shuddered, but she wanted nothing more than to be allowed to brush her lips against her lips, to feel her skin against her skin, to taste her tongue with her tongue.

The stranger closed her eyes, her blue, blue eyes and Rio, confused, did the same.

She let those hands cradle her face, let the stranger hold her close.

Her index fingers began tracing soft patterns on Rio’s cheeks and the driver felt a sudden sense of coolness take over her.

The heat was no more, only a soft breeze caressing her skin could be felt. 

That, and the stranger’s cool hands.

The woman, the queen, muttered something Rio couldn’t understand, nor hear; something ancient and true that curled familiarly on the stranger’s tongue, like it belonged there before the English language was ever uttered for the first time.

Beneath the foreign words, Rio heard the stranger’s voice in her ear:

Rest, rest, rest, rest.

A sudden wave of calm washed over her, a thousand rocks laid gently on her limbs, heavy, so heavy and the heat didn’t matter anymore.

Rio was wrapped in a blanket of infinite stillness, she didn’t sleep, couldn’t somehow, but as Rio felt every muscle in her body melt, a sharp sense of clarity bloomed in her head: this stranger held an immense power. 

She could use it however she wanted, but she chose to calm Rio.

Without even realizing it, tears spilled down Rio’s cheek.

It was the ocean of calm that broke her, the complete and utter silence.

There were only the stranger’s hands on either side of her face.

A delicate but solid pressure that kept her in place, so much gentler than the previous hold the stranger had on her.

The clarity spread, and Rio could feel the stranger’s power pulse just within her reach.

Warm, electric and maddening.

It was the most beautiful thing Rio had ever felt.

She had been right earlier.

The stranger was a goddess, and Rio felt tiny in her grasp.

She was but a minuscule ant at the mercy of an omnipotent giant.

The stranger spoke again and Rio didn’t understand a thing but knew the stranger was affirming something, a truth that wasn’t true yet but was determined to be so.

It lodged itself in Rio’s mind and all of the sudden became everything she had ever wanted to hear.

Rio could do nothing but agree with the stranger’s unknown words and as Rio was agreeing with her, she saw herself calm her breathing, dry her tears and–

Chapter Notes

Rio woke up at 6pm.

Her alarm rang once and she shut it off.

She blinked, somehow fully awake.

Her sheets were dry and cool even in the insufferable temperature of the room.

Her knees didn’t crack when she stood.

Her mind wasn’t foggy as she crossed her small apartment.

She was slightly hungry and thirsty, so Rio ate and Rio drank.

And as she caught sight of herself in the mirror of her tiny bathroom, Rio was surprised to find that she looked… good.

Rested.

No dark circles under her eyes.

No redness.

No pimples.

Just some rosy cheeks and a confused frown.

Her mind replayed the previous night, a couple of fares and Agatha-

Agatha.

That was her name.

It appeared in her mind like a certainty, Agatha: the stranger, her stranger.

That name was left to her like a gift of great value, proximity, familiarity, access, and with it, the absolute certainty of seeing her again.

And so Rio didn’t dare to think about it too much.

It was like staring into the sun; too bright and too powerful, that name belonging to a goddess, felt odd yet familiar to Rio, like she’d always known it.

Agatha had been in her cab, Rio remembered that.

She had led Rio again, into the city, without giving her directions yet driving so precisely through Rio on the busy streets of the falling night.

There had been an inconvenience then, Rio recalled, slow traffic, honking, angry drivers and Agatha had spoken and Rio had–

Agatha had spoken and Rio had–

What had happened then? 

Rio closed her eyes and tried, she tried so hard to remember, but only a deep sense of stillness came to her and her headache passed in an instant.

The phantom sensation of Agatha’s hands on her face made her shiver.

And Rio could not recall anything else, only her touch, only her voice, only the absolute calm that took over her.

 

 

Her first fares went by smoothly, Rio hadn’t had any coffee yet, but  didn’t feel like she needed it. 

She was focused on the streets, dodging traffic where she knew there would be some, swerving between cars and buses.

And the passengers chatted, seemingly happier too. 

They deemed her open to small talk like never before, and Rio surprised herself by answering, even offering small smiles to the passengers through the rearview mirror.

When the dullness became too much for her to bear, when boredom crept along the edges of her consciousness and pushed a pulsing headache on her temples, Rio would think of Agatha again, think of her hands on her face, of the coolness of her skin and her voice, and it would be a bit easier to focus back on the streets.

But as her night went by, this simple pull towards Agatha became less efficient and Rio would have to actively picture her for the effect to work.

 

 

Soon, she felt jittery again and Rio stopped for her first cup of coffee.

Without thinking, she drove to the usual diner, the one the other cabbies always hung out at. 

It was bright and a bit loud but strangely Rio didn’t feel as overwhelmed when she stepped inside, and when the waitress smiled as she got her order, Rio smiled back.

“You look good.”

Rio turned to face Jennifer Kale, a cup in hand, sitting at the counter, elegant as ever, an eyebrow delicately arched in what appeared to be suspicion.

“Thank you.”

“What happened?”

“Nothing,” Rio frowned, Jennifer Kale tilted her head, a slight smile on her lips, and Rio wanted to smack it away.

What right did she have to comment on Rio’s appearance? Jennifer Kale and Rio were not friends, they were acquaintances at best and not always cordial ones.

Why did Jennifer Kale care about her life, about her face–

“Did you get laid?” Jennifer Kale smirked, thinking she knew everything, so smug and superior to Rio. 

“What if I did?” Rio responded, more biting than was probably necessary, because Jennifer only chuckled and shook her head.

“Good for you,” she said, and Rio regretted her bite; she took a sip of coffee instead, burning her tongue because of how hot it was and cursing herself in the process.

“Anyway,” Jennifer continued, “you’ve heard about those murders, yeah?”

“Murders?” Rio had been too focused on Agatha and her strange fares to know about any murders.

Kale leaned in conspicuously, “they’re finding bodies all over the city,” her voice dropped down to a whisper, “very brutal, I heard, with satanic stuff and shit.”

Rio shook her head.

“The others are worried,” Jennifer continued, tilting her head towards the back of the diner where Rio recognized a few cabbies chatting among themselves, “you know, with all the creeps we meet…”

Rio reflexively chuckled.

“Have you seen any creeps, Rio?” Jennifer asked and Rio felt the back of her neck tingle, the way she was asking felt as though she was accusing Rio of being a creep. 

Rio was no creep, she just drove and kept her head down.

Maybe people didn’t like her very much and that was fine by her, but she wasn’t a creep, or at least she didn’t think she was.

“Where were you last night?” It was sharp and Rio felt her face heat up, and she wanted nothing more than to drop the subject and move on, and Jennifer who normally didn’t give a shit about Rio was being unusually insistent, and Rio had done nothing wrong, she swore she did nothing wrong, she did her fares, meet Agatha and-

And what?

“I was driving-” She tried weakly.

“I only ask, because you weren’t here yesterday morning, and we wondered if your odd stranger might have gotten you,” Jennifer smiled.

Rio blinked.

Jen had been worried.

“The woman who took the bus?” Jen said sharply, as if Rio was a toddler that had forgotten to brush their teeth.

“Yes, I remember–”

“Well?”

“Well no, I’m here,” Rio very obviously retorted, and was immediately embarrassed by her stupid answer to Jen’s brave attempt at connection.

The other cab driver raised an eyebrow but her mouth twitched with the ghost of a smile, she squeezed Rio’s shoulder, “Good.”

With a sip of coffee, a careful nod and a half joking:  “try not to get murdered, Rio,”  Jen left the diner to enjoy her own night.

Why she’d missed dinner was a mystery and why she felt so rested was another, Rio thought as she left her parking spot. 

The rest of the night she drove absentmindedly, every thought focused on Agatha and the last thing she remembered from that night: her touch, cool against her cheeks, her eyes, laser focused on her own and her words, ancient and unknown like a firm caress in her ears.

 

 

The next night, Rio drove with tired relief. 

She hadn’t slept that day, it was still too warm and while her body felt more familiar than it had the night before, there was a persistent  ache in her neck, a buzz under her skin and her usual twitchiness that pushed her through the evening.

Her cab was waiting for her, shiny yellow already pulsing behind her eyelids, leather seat waiting for another night.

But Rio didn’t start right away, instead she went to the carwash and paid for a thorough cleaning.

The huge brushes spinned wildly against her car, a mix of soapy water soaked the metal, her car hadn’t been filthy, she’d washed it just last week, but it somehow didn’t feel clean enough, so Rio sat in her car and watched as the machines made quick work of her yellow cab.

She scrubbed the seats herself, cleaned every crevasse, rubbed a protective balm on the leather, and sprayed a soothing smelling oil in the cabin.

She cleaned, more thorough than she had ever been when cleaning her car.

Through the radio, dispatch sent her to a hotel downtown, Rio didn’t go.

She drove around the city, ignoring people waving her way, stopping for no-one, only driving aimlessly around well-known routes.

The night crept in but the heat remained and Rio still drove around with no purpose beside avoiding clients and dispatch alike.

After a while of the radio asking her to respond, Rio turned it off, she also turned her light off, shutting down everything but her engine, purring gently as she drove along the streets into the night.

There was no destination and Rio drove around in large circles, along loud residential streets to quiet commercial centers, sleepy financial districts and joyous neighborhoods where bars were full and people were wandering around, sweaty and hot, too hot in the damp heat of the city.

The air smelled stale and heavy, like grilled meat and dust and petrol and garbage and as the night went by and Rio didn’t pick anyone up, she looked at the city and the people on the streets, they were bright and alive and standing and talking and fighting and laughing and Rio felt so far away from them.

Rio, alone in her cab, touring the streets, observing the people, felt dead to the world.

A watcher, trapped in her little yellow box, that even if she didn’t drive anyone around, had no will of her own. 

Everyone moved, laughed, cried, traveled, everyone but her, in her car.

Her time belonged to the company, her freedom to her passengers and Rio could do nothing else but watch the world go by. 

 

She circled larger, went to parts of the city she rarely drove by, industrial projects, massive construction sites, closed for the night, lost in darkness.

She felt the coolness of freshly poured concrete, smelled its damp, grounding scent, everything was so empty here.

And in the phantom streets of a city that did not yet exist she saw her.

Agatha.

Standing on the large sidewalk before a windowless building with dark openings like those of a blinded giant.

And she was there, waiting for Rio; alone in the dark, she looked like she commended it, like it was her home and the lights of Rio's cab seemed to dim as she approached.

Rio pulled up to her and Agatha climbed into her cab, in the back seat on the right like she always did.

And everything became clearer to Rio: why she’d been scrubbing her car, why she didn’t take any other passengers, why she had driven so far from her usual routes.

Agatha didn’t speak but Rio felt how pleased she was, and Rio felt whole, she smiled to herself, chest aching because she did good and the queen sitting at the back of her cab was happy with her.

Rio dared then, to glance in the mirror for the sole purpose of looking at her.

Their eyes met and Agatha smiled, so incredibly beautiful even in the dim light.

Her face was like one sculpted out of marble, timeless, regal, a face worthy of adoration, one that could start wars and end empires, one that Rio had the honor to be looking at, in the rearview mirror of her cab.

Emboldened, Rio spoke, a million questions could have left her mouth but only one managed to ring into the car:

“How do I know your name?”

Agatha chuckled, a sound that lodged itself into Rio’s brain and trickled down her spine in a rippling shiver.

“To know another’s name is a gift,” Agatha only said, eyes drifting to the window.

And Rio knew that, it certainly felt that way, so she focused back on driving for a bit, leaving this dark and empty ghost of a city to reach more familiar streets.

“I’m Rio,” she said eventually, because it felt very important then, that Agatha knew her name.

The city welcomed them back, even though Rio couldn’t have cared less.

“Names hold power,” Agatha said, voice almost lost to the rumbling car, “Thank you, Rio,” and the smile, so obvious in her voice, melted Rio entirely.

The streets were calmer now that the night was well underway and a cool breeze made sitting in the cab bearable.

After a few more turns, Agatha spoke again, and her voice held none of the authority Rio had come to expect from her.

She even found some uncertainty coloring her tone, it was so unexpected that it prompted the driver to turn around and glance at her over her shoulder, and she saw Agatha waving a cigarette between two fingers. 

“I don’t have to smoke it-”

Rio winced, “it’s against company policy…” 

But as she was ready to make an exception, Agatha nodded.

“Make a left turn.”

Rio did as she was told, drove the car in a narrow street until Agatha guided her to turn again and they found themselves facing the river on a deserted pier.

The cab, parked in the darkness between the harbour’s empty warehouses, barely managed to light the inlet with its headlights, where inky black water licked at the concrete pillars.

Agatha got out of the car.

She walked to the water, silk skirt flowing around her legs, Rio took a deep breath and followed. 

The first thing that hit her was the silence.

The harbour was quiet, the city, blinking beyond the water, was reduced to a low hum, mostly swallowed by the water slowly flowing by.

Rio leaned against the hood of her car, content to let the night air cool her sweaty skin, Agatha stalked closer, the cigarette still pinched between her fingers.

“I didn’t think you’d smoke,” Rio quipped, “seems beneath you, somehow.”

Agatha shrugged, and with a playful smirk she brought the tip of the cigarette closer to her mouth and softly blew on it, a small flame spurred on the end, effortlessly lighting it. 

Rio blinked and watched, mesmerized at the tiny flame that appeared out of nowhere, dancing in the dark.

“How do you see me, Rio?” She took a satisfied drag, the smoke curling around her in velvety swirls.

Rio thought for a second before answering, letting the words bloom in her mind, twirl in her brain before reaching her lips. 

“Like the first light of dawn when the night seemed endless, or, the darkest night, when the day has been too bright.”

Agatha stepped even closer. 

“I see you as a goddess, bored enough to grace us with her presence.” 

Another step. 

"You're above all: men, women, laws, physics-”

A low chuckle and Rio stopped talking.

“If I’m above all, then nothing is beneath me.”

Agatha took another drag of her cigarette, “if the rules don’t apply to me, then nothing can touch me, can it?”

“No rules apply to you?”

And Rio couldn’t have been ready for the wicked grin Agatha gave her, god– how badly could Rio wish to feel those sharp teeth sink in her throat.

“I’m working on it.”

No, she didn’t understand her now, but in time, Rio would.

Agatha offered her an unlit cigarette from her packet, “Do you want me to show you?”

And the flickering lights in the distance, and the smooth water, and the cold wind, and Agatha’s toothy grin. 

Rio took the cigarette.

“You have to give it your breath,” Agatha explained, her focus now entirely fixed on Rio and that unlit cigarette. 

The cab driver mimicked her, brought the stick near her lips and blew on it.

Agatha shook her head, “Too much, go slower.”

Rio adjusted her breath, but Agatha tutted once again, “Gentler.” 

She placed her hands on Rio’s shoulders and gently squeezed, “Picture a flame, small, like that of a candle. You’re blowing on it but you don’t want to snuff it out.”

Rio frowned but complied, blowing again, softer, slower this time, she pictured that flame, flickering under her breath until she slowed again, until that imaginary flame barely moved anymore, and she blew so softly and so slowly that everything around her disappeared, the city, the car, even Agatha.

And she felt incredibly silly, blowing on an unlit cigarette.

There was the very real possibility that Agatha was just messing with her and would wait for the perfect opportunity to take a step back and laugh at her, but Rio kept blowing gently on the cigarette, slow and steady. 

Agatha’s hands squeezed her shoulders once as a sign for her to continue, but the cigarette remained unlit and Rio’s breath grew short. 

Yet, Agatha held her still, encouraging.

So Rio kept blowing, so softly that it was more like letting the air escape her lungs than actively forcing it out.

She felt every muscle in her body tense, begging her to take a breath, but Rio kept blowing, her chest ached, her blood pulsed in her temples, rushed to her head, heated her cheeks, but Rio kept blowing on the unlit cigarette, wishing for an imaginary flame to offer the goddess that held her close.

As Rio’s head began to feel as though it might detach from her body, as her balance wavered, as her lungs screamed for her to give in and her heart shriveled in her chest, the certainty that nothing would happen started to creep in. 

A promise made and broken once again for the fun of it, but Agatha didn’t seem to be the kind of bully that would embarrass Rio like the others, yet, nothing would happen because nothing could happen, and she silently cursed herself to have believed something so stupid as someone convincing her that she could light a cigarette with only her breath.

And so Rio gave the last of her air, dots appearing at the edge of her vision and playful tingles crept up her cheeks. 

That was it, the moment when she would finally fall forward and pass out.

But the tip of the cigarette caught fire.

A small flame sprung out of nothing.

Agatha laughed, delighted, and Rio finally let herself inhale a big gulp of fresh air. 

She blinked, still dizzy, still bewildered by the flame flickering at the tip of her cigarette, proud despite the wind.

“You did this,” Agatha whispered.

Oxygen flooded her brain again, the sensation second only to the rush she’d only ever felt in Agatha’s presence, but as her eyes locked onto Agatha’s lips, Rio stopped her impulse.

Dark nails scraped her cheek, their sharp points bringing shivers down her spine and Rio melted, existing only in this moment, existing only for her, for her will, for her wants.

Agatha chuckled and shook her head before stepping back to look at the stars.

Rio swallowed and blinked, her senses slowly returning to her, the acrid smell of the cigarette felt grounding somehow, and Rio took a slow drag, watching the woman before her.

The line of her jaw, the inked bird adorning her bicep, her curls dancing in the wind.

Rio looked down at herself, her boring jeans, boring t-shirt still clinging to her despite the cooling night.

Agatha might have been the most beautiful person to ever walk this earth and Rio had never felt so ordinary in comparison. 

But when she raised her gaze, she found Agatha to be already looking at her, eyes burning with an intensity that left Rio breathless for the second time tonight.

“Finish your smoke.” Agatha said. “And let’s go.”
