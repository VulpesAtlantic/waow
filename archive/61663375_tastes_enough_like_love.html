Chapter 1

Explaining what she was doing at that party would necessitate a very long backstory, a whole lot of lying, and a cursory understanding of magick.

Luckily, no one was going to ask her. Probably.

Her purple dress was cut angularly and absolutely stunning if she did say so herself. Her new body was, as Billy had put it, ‘Ta-dow’. Which was nice because it was an exact replica of the old one. Her DNA didn’t deviate.

She was lucky because the party went with the auction and there were plenty of people to mingle and hide behind. She couldn’t just steal the stone though. It was too risky. She was going to have to wait for the new owner to buy it and then intercept it in delivery. That shouldn’t be too hard. She just needed to know who to target.

She was sipping champagne, just enough to blend in, but not enough for it to affect her. She was looking for marks and other people there to steal the stone, though no one should be after it but her. It had been mislabeled in the auction catalogue and far down the list.

She had found a high vantage point on the third story of the museum, overlooking the large open area in the polished entryway. She stuck close to a pillar that would obstruct her from most angles if she needed it to.

She didn’t often need to hide though. A perk of her siphon abilities was that it didn’t leave survivors. She wasn’t infamous except in witch communities which tended to be small and sparse. No one had seemed to recognize her or even acknowledge her more than a cursory politeness.

Her phone buzzed in her handbag. It vibrated her hip from the handbag hanging from the chain that was golden and delicate across her chest. She opened her handbag and pulled her phone out. It was from Billy. I have such a nice suit. I’ll be super nondescript.

She replied with a thumbs down emoji and put her phone back in the bag.

She was about to see if she could enchant her way into the back room when she spotted a familiar face that froze her entire body from her feet to her head.

On the first floor, mingling with people. Mingling with people . Actual people. Wearing a tight green dress that draped elegantly over one shoulder, Rio Vidal. Mingling. With. People.

Last Agatha recalled, Rio hated cities and wealthy, powerful people. She hated dressing up.

She watched as a woman moved up behind Rio, casually placing a hand on the small of her back. When Rio turned around to see who it was, Agatha expected an animalistic reaction, a slice at the uninvited hand on her back. But she smiled and allowed the woman to kiss her cheek.

The woman dropped her hand from Rio’s back and slipped her fingers between Rio’s. She said something to the group Rio had been with and then pulled her away.

Agatha honed in on the loose way their fingers held onto each other. It wasn’t tense or nervous. The way they fit together was familiar. They made their way away from the party and Agatha followed, sticking to the shadows. She found the third floor only followed so far and found some stairs to the second floor to keep following.

She lost sight of them on the stairs and was sure she lost them completely when she saw an emergency door had been propped open with a folding chair. She moved to see out the door better and saw Rio’s hands move from the woman’s waist to her back. The woman had rested her arms on Rio’s shoulders and they were speaking to each other in low tones with smiles on their faces.

The moon and the lights of the city were lighting their private space just outside of the venue. So perfectly lit that Agatha could see the moment their lips met. She watched as the woman closed her arms around Rio’s shoulders and deepened the kiss. She could see the indent that Rio’s fingers made on the woman’s back as she pulled her closer. It was gentle, but needy.

Agatha realized her hands were glowing purple a second before she let off a blast. She forced her magick back down and took a step back, farther into the shadows. She couldn’t watch anymore when the woman broke the kiss to rest her forehead on Rio’s and say something with a smile that made Rio laugh.

Agatha racked her brain for who the woman could be. She wasn’t a witch. That much Agatha could feel. She wasn’t a human either. At least not a normal one.

Agatha didn’t know where she was going. She was so in her head, thinking about Rio kissing someone else. The betrayal that coursed through her body like molten metal, burning and hardening.

She finally stopped and found herself in a bustling kitchen of stainless steel and cooks who couldn’t give a shit less that she was there as long as she wasn’t in the way. She stood there for a moment getting her bearings.

She was over trying to steal this thing stealthily. She just needed a moment to make a plan. After taking another sixty seconds in the kitchen, she made a b-line for the bathroom, needing some kind of container that could hold liquids so that she could make a potion to change her appearance. It wasn’t her usual trick, but there were extenuating circumstances.

She was examining the sinks, trying to decide if they were large enough when the door opened. Agatha became rigid when the woman strode in, a light smile on her face.

Agatha swallowed and turned the sink on, changing course again. She watched the woman examine her makeup in the mirror and run a precise finger under her bottom lip to fix a smudge of her lip gloss.

Agatha could do so many things well and act like she didn’t want to outright murder someone in front of her was one of her specialities. She put on a smile and gushed, “I love your dress.” The woman smiled brightly. She had a kind smile and looked at Agatha through the mirror. “Thank you.” Her dress was a shimming gold with long sleeves that ended in elegant loops around her middle fingers. She pulled the loops from her middle fingers. “Brilliant, but difficult to wash my hands in.” She peeled the sleeves back far enough away from her wrists and turned the water on. She looked at Agatha again. “I love your dress as well. The color is stunning.”

“Thank you,” Agatha forced out.

The woman asked, “Have you been to one of these before? I swear it’s usually the same people every time and I don’t think we’ve met.”

Agatha put soap on her hands and started washing them with a tension on her skin that was unnecessary, but was also more subconscious than on purpose. “First time. I represent a bidder.”

“Ooh,” the woman nodded, eyes moving down to look at her hands. “The auction.” She seemed a bit disappointed. She turned off the water.

“You don’t like the auction?” Agatha probed. She rinsed her hands and turned the water off on her end too.

“It’s unfortunate,” the woman took to carefully drying her hands. “Necessitated by funding cuts to the museum and school.” She sighed softly. “We’ll make due.”

“You work at the museum?” Agatha asked. When the woman seemed surprised Agatha didn’t know who she was, Agatha shook her head. “Sorry, this was kind of a last minute thing. Former-Sokovian royalty are fickle.”

“Oh,” the woman’s eyebrows raised in surprise. “I didn’t realize we’d have a bidder from Sokovia.”

“Former-Sokovia,” Agatha corrected with a curiosity. There was something about the way the woman spoke that itched something in the back of Agatha’s mind.

The woman smiled bashfully and nodded. “Of course. Former-Sokovia.” She took a deep breath and seemed to remember, “I do work at the museum and I teach some classes.” She pulled the loops of her dress back around her fingers and held her hand out to shake Agatha’s. “Sersi.”

“Beautiful name,” Agatha took her hand. “Agatha.”

There was a flash of recognition in Sersi’s eyes that she glazed over with a kind smile. “Ancient Greek name. It means ‘good’.”

Agatha could have fallen onto the floor laughing right there if she hadn’t already decided there was something weird about Sersi and that she was her new nemesis. Agatha didn’t need magick or abilities to know when someone was lying. Or at least holding their cards far too close to their chest.

“It was nice to meet you, Agatha,” Sersi’s smile was still dazzling, but there was something knowing in her eyes.

Agatha withdrew her hand and nodded. “It was nice to meet you too. Maybe we’ll run into each other again.”

Sersi smiled genuinely at that, like it was a joke between old friends. “I’m sure we will.” Then she excused herself to exit the bathroom.

Agatha took a moment to stare after her and then put her hands on the cold marble next to the sink. “Fuck.” She moved over to the bathroom door and waved her hand over the lock to click it into place. Then she checked all the stalls to make sure they were empty.

She pulled out her phone with a shaking hand and grabbed a stack of the really nice towels to shove into the bottom of a sink. She tapped around on her phone, started a call, and put it on speaker.

“Can I come?” Billy asked in greeting. “I’m already wearing my suit.”

“No,” Agatha snapped. “I need a quick glamour potion. Preferably with things that can be found in a women’s restroom.” She turned on the tap and let the towels clogging the drain keep the water in.

“That’s not part of the plan,” Billy seemed confused. “This is recon.”

“Plans change, Teen,” she told him. “Someone here may recognize me. I’m moving up the timeline.”

She could hear Billy moving papers around and flipping through books over the line. Someone outside pushed on the door and it rattled.

“Billy?” Agatha urged.

“I’ve been taking a lot of notes,” he answered. “Uh, okay. A mallow fiber. That’s like… cotton!” He seemed excited to know what it was. “They should be in the ta-tam-”

“Billy, say tampon like an adult,” Agatha instructed.

“Tampon,” he said. “Sorry. So yeah, just like half a tampon.”

“I hate all of this,” Agatha reached into the basket next to the sink and took out a tampon. “Next.”

They managed a potion that worked fairly well. When Agatha looked in the mirror, she wouldn’t recognize herself. She was blonde and her features were less prominent. Her eyes were a bright blue. She used some of her magick to change the look of her dress as well.

“You have like twenty minutes,” Billy explained. “This is kind of…experimental.” “Great,” Agatha pulled the drain on the sink, tossed the wet towels into one of the stalls and adjusted her hair. She really hated the way she looked, but as long as it could get her through the crowd, without being identified, it didn’t matter. “I’ll call you when I have it.” Then she hung up.

Agatha went to the door and waited for the speaking near it to fade away. She unlocked it with her purple and pulled it open. There was a woman and a security guard walking toward it and they both seemed surprised that someone came out.

“That door was locked,” the older woman said.

Agatha made her new face look surprised. She pitched her voice up a bit, “No?”

The security guard looked at the woman. “You can’t lock it from the inside without the key.”

Agatha turned around and left, leaving the security guard to believe the woman was crazy. She took the fastest route from the bathroom to the second floor because she was pretty sure she could get to the floor over where they were keeping the artifacts, blast through it, and fly away before anyone could stop her.

Everything went to spec until she dropped into the room where the artifacts were. The ceiling didn’t have an alarm on it because why would it? The vents, doors, and windows did.

She moved through the artifacts, staying away from the exterior walls, finding them all carefully packaged and tagged. She knew the lot number of the artifact she needed and started moving around the room trying to find it. It took longer than she would have liked and by the time she found the box, pried it open, put the small, intricately carved stone into her handbag, she could feel the potion wearing off.

She pushed herself up out of the artifact room and moved through the second floor, looking for a window. All the windows on the second floor were closed and locked. Billy had read that they were all armed with security sensors so she couldn’t just burst one open.

She finally decided that her best bet was the emergency door that was propped open. It was in a secluded area and Rio and her lady were nowhere to be seen. She didn’t bother with the stairs. If one or two stragglers saw her, she could glamour them fairly easily to forget they saw her.

She made it out of the door and pushed herself up into the air at a speed she didn’t usually like. She just needed to get away. She had the artifact and it was a small part of her much larger plan, but knowing that Rio was in the building behind her… she needed to run.

The loft she and Billy had taken over was half a city away and several blocks before she would have gotten there, she landed inconspicuously on the upper level of the train platform. She walked down the stairs and swiftly moved the rest of the way to the loft on foot.

Billy was wearing his suit when she walked in and she raised her eyebrows at him.

He straightened out the tie. “Just in case.”

She hummed and took her handbag off. She tossed it onto the coffee table. It landed with a cushioned thunk on the glass. “I got it. They didn’t even know what they had. Curator must be an idiot.”

“Great,” Billy walked over to the dining table by the window where he and Agatha had planned the heist. There were still papers scattered and open laptops, but he dug out his notebook and made an overly enthusiastic checkmark next to ‘Soul Tether’.

He looked at some of the notes under his checklist and swallowed. He was going to have to bring something uncomfortable up again. They knew where most things were that they needed. There was one thing that was going to be hard to get, not because it would be hard to find, but because of who had it.

“The thing on the list,” he stumbled. He looked up and saw that Agatha was staring out the window, perhaps not even listening to him. “Agatha?”

She blinked and turned around, one hand crossed around her middle, her other elbow resting on that arm so the back of her hand rested lightly on the bottom of her chin. Billy liked to think of it as Agatha’s thinking pose.

He closed his notebook, not going to bring up the list for the rest of the night. “Are you okay? What happened?”

Agatha shook out of it and threw up a curtain of the woman she wanted to show the world. “We won.” Then she licked her lips. “And I know where to get that thing on your list that you’re always scared to ask me about.”

“Do you know where she is?” he asked. “Or you can find her? Without her finding you?”

Agatha thought it over. “I think so. I have to go back to the museum tomorrow.”

Chapter 2

One bullet in the list in Billy’s notebook said ‘Something of Rio’s’. Neither one was really sure how belonging to Rio something needed to be. They discussed hair. They discussed her portal dagger, which would be much harder to get than her hair. They went into a short philosophical discussion about if all the souls in the world belong to her which Agatha shut down because she didn’t want to remember the kind of power Rio had. Also because Rio would have told them that the souls are their own and Rio is just the guide.

They settled on hair or personal item which Agatha figured hair would be easier. She knew they couldn’t win a fight with her or even pin her down long enough to take a hair. She also didn’t know if she had the fortitude to try to con or seduce her.

Agatha was so tired. It had taken two years to get a body and now they were working on getting Nicky back. And Tommy and Wanda, but her priority was Nicky.

She’d never admit it, but knowing that Rio could be right behind her had always been a comfort. She had never felt such an absence in her life. She knew there was something strange about being a ghost inside of a human body that would make it hard if not impossible for Rio to track her.

Before, it was also equal part annoyance, but something about Rio being there even if she couldn’t see her made her feel safer. Maybe not safer. It made her feel less alone.

And now she could feel the cold space where Rio would have been. She wasn’t sure that even if Rio could track her that she would anymore. Agatha was sure that right before she took Death’s last kiss, she had broken Rio’s resolve or affection. She had finally pushed away the one person she was sure would always be there.

All of this was running through her mind while she sat on the train on the way to the museum.

It was the middle of the week so she only had to breeze past tourists as they meandered about in the large open space that had been for high rolling gala attendees just hours earlier. She took a leisurely walk through a few of the sections. She didn’t actually know where Sersi worked, but if Agatha was good at anything, and she was good at a great many number of things, she was good at getting into places she wasn’t supposed to be in.

She walked through the tall corridors, making a note of all of the employee only doors and the signs pointing to the classrooms. As she made her way deeper into the museum, she was immediately arrested by a carving behind a glass case. It was faded, but it had the unmistakable characteristics of Rio’s Death Crown and her skeleton face she’d used for millennia.

She was in an exhibit of Mississipian culture and it would make sense that art of Rio would be anywhere. She was occasionally seen by regular mortals in places of mass casualties or natural disasters.

There was a bench directly across from the carving and Agatha backed up, not breaking eye contact with the depiction of Rio’s death mask. She slowly lowered herself and leaned back on the wall behind the bench.

Despite looking at an ancient carving of her, Agatha could not get the image of Rio kissing someone else out of her mind. When they got together, Agatha had been the only human - only being - to entrance and fascinate Death so thoroughly that she sought a human face and human pleasures. They had been each other’s firsts in many ways.

There was the sound of heels clicking on the glossy floor coming toward her and Agatha closed her eyes because she knew who it was. The elegant and faint perfume preceded her.

Sersi sat down next to Agatha and crossed one leg over the other. She had a soft sided lunch box with her and unzipped it. “I hope you don’t mind. I usually have lunch here.” There was something in Sersi’s voice that was always reaching out, always trying to make a connection and it was infuriated to Agatha. Something so cordial and so British .

Agatha didn’t say anything and opened her eyes again, looking at the carving.

“I was initially worried when she told me how different you and I are,” Sersi flipped open the lid of her painfully pedestrian lunchbox to reveal several glass containers of food. She took her time pulling one out, opening the first container, then setting the lunchbox to her side away from Agatha. Sersi picked up a grape and paused before eating it. “But it’s a relief now.”

Agatha’s head whipped toward Sersi in annoyance. She wasn’t just going to sit there and be insulted by Rio’s new… pet.

Sersi smiled apologetically. “I didn’t mean it as a slight. I mean that you’re so spectacular at the things you do that if we were similar I would be the pale shadow of you, a two- dimensional being that was a sad reflection.”

Agatha let her anger dissipate a bit. She wasn’t going to let go of it completely. She couldn’t because obviously this woman was her new nemesis.

“Did you take the stone?” Sersi asked casually.

Agatha didn’t see the harm in telling the truth. The stone was currently under the protection of a powerful witch, easily the top ten most powerful witches in the world. Definitely in the top three most powerful Maximoffs. “Yes.”

Sersi nodded sagely and took a bite of a grape. She ate in a mannered way that annoyed Agatha. She was silent and beautiful even when she was eating. Even when she was staring at artifacts and having a conversation with her partner’s ex.

“What are you?” Agatha finally asked. Her voice was gravely from lack of use and she cleared her throat too late.

Sersi took a quick look around. She could have been a smartass and said what her job at the museum was or a bitch and said she was Rio’s new love, but she answered the question Agatha was really asking. “An extraterrestrial.” She took a bite and thoughtfully chewed before swallowing. “But we’ve been here for over seven thousand years so it doesn’t feel like I belong anywhere else.”

“We?” Agatha asked. She wasn’t an idiot. She knew there were several dozen different kinds of extraterrestrials all over the place. If you looked up at night at just the right time you could see the little sparks of them fighting over something stupid among the stars.

“It’s a bit silly of a name and I didn’t come up with it, mind you,” Sersi smiled congenially. “We’re called Eternals, my family and I.”

“You’re immortal?” Agatha asked. She was finally intrigued.

“Not immortal in that we’re immune from dying, but it is very difficult by Earth standards to kill us,” Sersi answered, her eyes on her grapes, carefully picking out a perfect one. She offered the container to Agatha who ignored her. She took it back and set it on her lap.

Agatha hummed. “When did you… see her for the first time?”

“It was some time during the Ionian Revolt,” Sersi explained patiently. “I had been sailing the Aegean sea for Attica when our ship was attacked. It was a merchant ship and a bloody battle.” She shook her head and bit her lip. “It was over so fast, I could barely save anyone.” She sighed. “I saw her move through the water like a ghost, cradling grown men like babies as they took their final breaths.” She exhaled through her nose in a light chuckle. “She looked so confused when she saw me. She had to know I wasn’t human. Then I didn’t see her again for a few thousand years.” She shrugged. “You live long enough, you see a lot of death and sometimes you see Death herself.” She was speaking fondly of the experiences and Agatha was having to hold onto the bench to keep from trying to physically throttle her.

“I never saw her human face until last year,” Sersi replied. “I thought she just took the souls of humans, but she was sitting under a windowsill where an old, gray cat was taking its last moments of life.” Sersi shook her head. “She was so kind to it.” She smiled briefly. “So I spoke to her and asked who she was.”

There was a long pause. Agatha was fuming and wallowing in it. She didn’t even try to speak. She absently rubbed her palm across her sternum.

“She was so sad,” Sersi sighed.

“Why?” Agatha asked, feigning disinterest. She looked away from the carving to survey the rest of the very tall, very wide, very clean room.

Sersi paused. She took another bite of her food and cleared her mouth before answering. “It’s quite heartbreaking to be in love with someone who will always be in love with someone else and knowing that person won’t ever requite their love the same way. It’s like watching someone you love drown over and over again and knowing there’s nothing you can do to stop it.”

The statement was a dagger to Agatha’s heart and an incendiary to her brain. She hated that this person thought she knew so much about her. Agatha hated when she didn’t control the narrative around her own perception. She spouted, “Did you she tell you about-”

“Your son?” Sersi turned to look at Agatha for the first time and her eyes were sympathetic in the slight furrow of her brow and her mouth in the tight frown. She nodded slowly. “I-” she started, but shook her head. “I can’t imagine the-” She shook her head again and settled for, “She told me what happened.”

Another fire of fury ignited inside of Agatha. This woman - this person - knew about her. Her hands flexed of their own volition, sparks of purple arcing between her fingers.

There were hurried footsteps toward them. It wasn’t the heels or loafers of someone that worked there. There was occasionally the squeak of some kind of sneaker. They both looked up when Billy careened into the room, stopping just short of them. He straightened his hoodie and stood up straight upon being observed.

“Hey,” he gestured over his shoulder with his thumb. “Our, uh, Uber is here.” His eyes flickered from Agatha’s face to her hands.

At the glance, she realized she had been leaking some purple and pulled it back in. “I, um, yeah.” She nodded and stood up.

“I love your sweater,” Billy smiled enthusiastically to Sersi.

Sersi smiled kindly back and stood. “Thank you.” She offered her hand to him, “Sersi.”

“Billy,” he shook her hand.

Agatha took a step toward Billy then stopped like she was in a daze. Then her face turned hard as she turned to Sersi, “Tell Rio that I’m going to break down the gates of the hell she guards and let out everyone she’s ever taken from me.” Then Agatha straightened up and strode out.

Billy was shocked at the statement and then looked between Agatha and Sersi, before taking off after Agatha. “Who is that?”

“An obstacle,” Agatha huffed.

“Wouldn’t she have what we need?” he asked. “Like...” He paused trying to find a delicate way to phrase as he was practically jogging to keep up with her. “If they’re near each other, she probably has Rio’s hair somewhere-”

“We’ll find another way,” Agatha barked, interrupting his sentence. She didn’t want to think of Rio and Sersi anywhere near each other.

Billy took a stutter-step and then stopped. “I’ll be right back.” He turned around and jogged back the way they came.

“Hey!” Agatha called after him, trying to stop him, but she realized in trying to get his attention, she got the attention of everyone else in the main entrance. She dithered in place, trying to decide if she wanted to go after him or she was too indignant about Sersi talking to her with her lunch and being so nice about it.

As it did most times, indignity won out and Agatha marched out of the museum and waited for Billy on the windy sidewalk.

He trotted out a few minutes later. He had his hands in his pockets, not wearing a jacket heavy enough to buffet against the wind. “I got a hair.” He put his hand over his pocket. “The problem is, I’m not sure whose it is. Is there a way for you to tell?”

Agatha adjusted her jaw and started walking down the street. “Maybe.”

They made it back to the loft and Agatha picked up her notes. She sat in an armchair near the window that overlooked the street blowing with dead leaves below. She looked at her notes, but she wasn’t reading them. Her eyes had focused on the loop of a letter and then slowly unfocused until it was just a white sky with blue clouds.

Billy could tell something was really wrong and Agatha only got that quiet for one reason. He moved to the windowsill she was facing and sat on it. He crossed his arms and looked at her. “Are you okay? Really?”

Agatha looked up at him and saw that he was deeply concerned. She closed her notes and tossed her notebook onto the windowsill next to him. “I’m fine.” She held her hand out. “Give me the hair.”

He unzipped his jacket pocket and reached inside. He pulled out a long strand of hair and handed it to Agatha. She stood up and pulled the taut between her fingers, holding it up to the light. It was dark brown and wavy. There weren’t really any markers that would make it Rio’s or Sersi’s. She shook her head and set it on the windowsill. “I can’t tell.” She had a hunch it was Sersi’s.

Billy looked down. “Sorry.”

She put her hand on his arm and squeezed it. “We’ll get it. We have a line to her now.”

“Is that her girlfriend?” Billy asked, looking up nervously.

Agatha closed her eyes at the question for a moment. She would never admit how much it hurt to hear that. She nodded. “Yes.”

“You saw her at the auction?” Billy asked. He chewed on his bottom lip waiting for the answer. As much as Agatha hated to be known, he knew her and he was worried.

Agatha nodded mutely. She pushed her hair out of her face. “I should have gotten it there.”

“What if,” he paused. “What if you just ask? I could ask.” He took a tense breath and crossed his arms. “She… You said she loved him too.”

Agatha’s eyes shot to Billy. Her eyes were immediately furious, but they calmed almost immediately. She had told Billy that in a moment of vulnerability. Rio had loved Nicky. She might not have done it normally or correctly, but she had. It might not even be a bad idea to ask. She sighed. “I’ll think about it.”

Billy nodded. He pulled his wallet out of his pocket and opened it. He took a quick calculation of the contents and said. “I’m going to order some food. What do you want?”

Agatha wasn’t hungry, but knew if she didn’t ask for anything he would badger her until she ate something. “Whatever you’re getting is fine.” Then she sat back down in the chair and stared out the window, not having any pretense of study in front of her. She pressed her hand to her sternum.

Billy put her hand on Agatha’s shoulder, at a complete loss for how to help her.

Chapter 3

Across town, someone else was at a complete loss. Sersi watched Rio stare out her apartment window at the city. Her jaw would shift in thought occasionally and eventually, Sersi moved up behind her and wrapped her arms around Rio.

“Is there anything I can do, my love?” Sersi asked.

Rio closed her eyes and leaned back into Sersi. She felt Sersi kiss the back of her neck before resting her chin on Rio’s shoulder.

“No,” Rio answered after taking a moment to bolster herself in the safety of Sersi’s arms.

Sersi closed her eyes, feeling a deep well of pain burning through her body. She was watching Rio sink to the bottom of a murky ocean, letting the ocean take her. She was watching her drown all over again.

Rio moved her arms to rest on top of Sersi’s and gently stroked the skin over her wrist with her thumb. “I have to go.”

Sersi gently released Rio. Rio turned around and unfurled a fist in front of her. A delicate white orchid sprouted from her hand.

Sersi smiled at the flower and took it from Rio’s hand. She ran her finger across the stem and brought it to her nose to smell. “Thank you.”

Rio gently took her cheek in her hand, her palm pressed to Sersi’s cheek. She kissed her and Sersi tasted all of the kisses they’d shared before. Before she could break the kiss or open her eyes, Rio disappeared. All of her warmth was suddenly gone.

Sersi didn’t open her eyes immediately. She stood on the balcony with her eyes closed because tears were gathering under them. For some reason, she felt like she’d already lost Rio.

She slowly opened her eyes and looked out over the city while the flower in her hand slowly turned to glass.

Chapter 4

When Sersi walked into the classroom the next day, she found a much taller person that she expected at the small desks. It was much too early for students anyway.

The young man with curly hair was wearing a much more appropriate for the weather coat and was sitting in one of the desks. His long legs extended far out of the front of the desk and he seemed startled when she walked in.

“Billy,” Sersi set her bag down on the desk. “You’re early if you’re here for primary ancient history.”

He gave her an amused smile. “No.” He drummed his fingers on his desk and looked up at her. “I need to talk to Rio.”

Sersi licked her lips. She straightened up next to her desk, taking a moment to ponder the request. “Why?”

He swallowed and sat up a bit in the desk. The tops of his knees banged into the bottom of it and he winced but tried to play it off. He swiveled his legs and stood. He was significantly taller than her, but they were a good distance apart and regardless, she didn’t seem intimidated in the least.

“It’s…” Billy bit the inside of his cheek trying to decide how much to disclose. “Do you know who Nicky is?”

Sersi nodded.

“It’s about Nicky,” Billy walked across the room and took a piece of paper out of his pocket. He offered it to Sersi. “Please, just give this to her. It’s my number. Agatha doesn’t know I’m here.”

Sersi took the piece of paper and looked at it. For the briefest moment, she had the most selfish thought she’d ever had and thought of turning the paper to dust in front of him. She looked down, her immaculate hair falling into her face.

“Can I ask you a question?” Sersi asked, tapping the paper to her palm.

Billy searched her face. She was the most open book person he’d ever met and he nodded, feeling like he owed her that much.

“Is Agatha still in love with her?” Sersi asked softly. There was a barest hint of a quiver in her voice.

Billy felt so bad about telling her the truth. He could see she was so wounded by it all, but was still being so kind. He cleared his throat that was swelling with second hand emotion. “Yes.” He looked at her like she was a sun, too bright to look at, but the light that would bore into his eyes was guilt that he was demanding so much of someone being torn apart by the situation. “They’re… not good at it. Loving each other.”

Sersi sniffled and wiped at the bottom of her right eye, mostly keeping herself together in front of the strange boy. “I’d say they were spectacular at it. It’s everything else they’re bad at.”

Billy shifted his weight and eventually nodded slowly. “I’m sorry.”

She picked her bag up from the desk and flipped open the top. She slipped the paper into it. “I’ll give it to her.”

“Thank you,” Billy could barely get out and slunk out of the room.

Chapter 5

Billy got himself a milk tea and sat in the shop where he bought it while an overcast sky moved in. He looked at his phone and sighed. No one had texted him recently. He knew that if Agatha wasn’t so distracted by Rio’s new love interest, she would know that he was up to something. But he told her he was going to get some groceries three hours ago and she hadn’t reached out yet.

He hooked his heels onto the bar around the bottom of his stool and stared out the big picture window of the storefront. People were hurrying by and a significant number of them were carrying umbrellas. He’d never been to London before and they were supposed to have been long gone already, but it all seemed like the focal point of their journey now.

Inside the shop was starting to feel so bright compared to the outside. The sky was rapidly darkening with ominous clouds moving in. He took out his notebook and opened it. He skipped the to-do list and his packing list and the spells and potions he and Agatha had had to start making on the fly.

In the back of his book, he had been sketching. He wasn’t great at it, but it was a nice outlet for him. On the last page, he had drawn his mother’s crown, the deep curves, and the long points of the Scarlet Witch. He had drawn his own as he’d seen it the few times he accidentally conjured it. It was a variation of his mother’s, but the points never got as high as hers. He wondered if it all signified something like hers was bigger and grander because she was more powerful. Or as he aged, his would elongate into a blue replica of hers.

He had also started sketching a variation of his own. It was more simple and plain because he didn’t know how to create something like that. He had started reversing his or turning his upside down and enhancing it a bit, but it never looked right. Tommy’s crown was smudged in lines of attempts, erasures, and trying again. It never quite looked right.

It didn’t matter as long as he got them back.

His phone started ringing and scared him. It could be any number of people who would have information or clues as to their next move, but the name on the screen was ‘Mom’. He let himself relax a moment before picking up and answering.

“Hey Mom,” he sighed out. He actually didn’t realize how much he missed her.

“Hey honey,” Rebecca Kaplan answered. There was a smile in her voice that made him smile. “I hope I didn’t wake you up.”

He chuckled, “It’s only four o’clock here.”

“You know how bad I am with time zones,” she told him. She paused and lovingly said, “How are you? Are you having fun?”

Billy rested his cheek on his fist and closed his eyes. He wanted to tell his mom everything. He wanted to tell her about his powers and Agatha and traveling around the world, but he couldn’t. Not yet. So he continued the lie he had made up about visiting colleges with his friends when he and Agatha left the US. His friends were actually visiting colleges that week, even one in Berlin, but he wasn’t with them. “We’re having fun.” He paused. “I miss you.”

“Oh honey,” she sighed into the phone. “I miss you too.”

They talked until it started pouring rain and Billy apologized that he had to go meet some people. She told him to be careful and that she loved him so much. He felt so guilty. Between all of the lying and the look on Sersi’s face and the rut Agatha was in everything was so complicated. He nearly broke down and told his mom everything so he wouldn’t be holding it all on his own.

But he didn’t. He told her he loved her too and hung up.

He hadn’t heard the ding, but there was a text message from an unknown number on his phone. I don’t have a phone. This is Sersi’s number. When and where? It was signed with a skull emoji.

He actually thought the skull emoji was quite cute. He immediately started to text back, but realized that he didn’t have anywhere to meet that was private. He very quickly switched to his browser app and started frantically looking for somewhere to meet that would give some privacy and would be far enough away that Agatha wouldn’t sense either of them.

He found a park on the river and dropped a pin there. He sent it through text. He wasn’t sure if Rio would be able to decipher it, but he was sure Sersi would. 30 minutes.

Chapter 6

The map had said it would take him twenty-six minutes to get there, but he arrived huffing and puffing because apparently crosswalks weren’t taken into account. He had gotten there 30 minutes later on the dot.

It was getting colder and he zipped up his coat, lifted his hood around his head, and started looking around. He watched traffic go by on the road on the other side of the park and looked at every face of every person he could see in the park. Most of the people in the park were joggers because anyone in the park for leisure would have been scared off by the impending rain.

He started to think that maybe Rio was one of the squirrels running up a tree trunk when he heard a voice behind him.

“What are you doing?”

He whipped around and saw Rio standing behind him. She looked so normal that he wouldn’t have picked her out from a crowd if her back was to him. She was dressed very nicely in dark jeans, sensible boots, and a green hoodie under a grey peacoat.

He gestured to the tree, “I-I thought you might be a tree or a squi-squirrel.”

She just narrowed her eyes at him in confusion. The ends of her hair that were sticking out of the pulled-up green hood got caught in a gust off of the river and whipped around.

He shook his head, trying to get himself together. He really should have given himself more time to figure out what he wanted to say. He swallowed thickly and shoved his hands in his pockets. Finally he just spit it out. “I need your hair.”

Rio’s brows sunk into a deeper confusion. “For what?”

He bit the inside of his cheek and added, “It’s for Agatha.”

She searched his face for a long moment and he wanted desperately to know what she was thinking. He started to reach out with his mind to hear her thoughts, but was immediately slapped down by an all encompassing wall of screaming. It sounded like millions of voices all screaming at the same time and he fell to his knees grabbing his ears. It was only a second but it felt like his ear canals had been ripped out with tweezers.

He couldn’t hear and his ears were wet. When he pulled his hand away from his ears, he saw blood.

All he could hear was ringing and in a panic he looked up at Rio. She was looking down at him with pity. She took the few steps toward him and knelt down. She placed her hands on the sides of his face and the ringing slowly started to fade before the sounds of London started getting louder.

His ears were still wet and felt full of liquid, but there wasn’t pain anymore.

He pulled his scarf off of his neck and started wiping the blood away.

“Don’t do that,” Rio answered and stood. “You nearly died again.”

“Was that your thoughts?” he asked, still panicked, but no longer in pain or injured. He scrambled to his feet and continued to wipe out his ears until they didn’t feel full. When he looked at his scarf there was so much blood on it.

Rio didn’t answer. She was studying him still as he tried to clean himself up.

Eventually he decided he wasn’t going to get anymore blood off of his ears and threw the bloodied scarf into a nearby trash can.

“Is this about Nicky?” Rio asked.

He froze in place, giving Rio her answer. She pulled her dagger out of her pocket, separated a few strands of her hair, and sliced them off. She offered the hairs to Billy.

He scrambled to pull a small, clean plastic cup he’d gotten from the tea shop out of his pocket and held it open for her. She dropped the hairs into it and he put a lid on the cup.

“A body is only one step,” Rio warned. She paused, “It’s not the same as what Agatha did. She had possession of her own soul. You need possession of his to get it into a body.”

“Who has possession of his?” Billy asked.

Rio took a deep breath. “He does.”

Billy blinked. “What do you mean?”

“I’m just the courier,” she answered. She ticked her thumb across the ridge on the butt of the handle of the knife. Then she turned the flat side toward her and looked at the blade like she was watching something move inside of it. After a moment, she put it back into some place under her peacoat.

Billy moved his tongue around in his mouth tasting blood there too, but not wanting to examine it further in front of Rio. He swallowed. He felt like he shouldn’t say more about Agatha’s plan, but he had some questions of his own. “Do you know where Tommy is?”

Rio’s curiosity turned to anger. She frowned and stared hard at him until he looked away. “No. But you better find him before I do.”

Billy’s eyes whipped to Rio. “What do you mean?”

“The natural balance is on a knife’s edge,” Rio took a step closer to him. “You and Agatha keep testing it and upsetting the balance.” He took a step back. She took another step forward. “Soon, I won’t be able to hold it together.”

Billy stopped taking steps back. He looked down at Rio. “You’re…covering for us?”

“I’m covering for her ,” Rio’s voice was gravely and agitated.

Billy felt the all encompassing sadness rest on him like blankets that were much too heavy. He looked at the ground and then up at Rio. Everyone around him was so sad and heartbroken.

“What can I do?” Billy asked softly. “To help you. Bring balance back. I want Tommy and Wanda and she wants Nicky more than anything. There’s only three of them that we want back. What can I do?”

Rio blinked and lowered her head. She closed her eyes. When she opened her eyes and looked back up at him, she looked so tired. She looked so beaten down. “Wanda Maximoff isn’t dead.”

Billy’s eyes got wide. “What do you mean? Where is she?”

Rio gestured with her hand out. “Give me your phone.”

He fumbled, getting his phone out of his pocket to give to Rio. He unlocked it for her and handed it over. He didn’t anticipate her turning it over and pulling out her knife. She lightly carved a crude map into the back of it while he winced with every scrape.

Rio handed the phone back. “She’s hiding. That’s all I can see.”

Billy looked at the map. He was sure he saw rivers and mountains. It was a starting point. He checked to make sure that his phone still worked and started to put it in his pocket when it rang. He looked at the caller and found Agatha was calling him.

“Answer it,” Rio instructed.

Billy tapped the green button and put the phone on speaker. “Hey Agatha.”

“Where are you?” she asked. There was a twinge of panic in her voice.

Billy looked over at Rio and found she was looking at the ground, away from the phone. Her eyes were closed and he could swear he saw a tear leaking out from her bottom eyelashes.

“Uh, I went to get tea,” he answered. “I lost track of time.”

“It’s been four hours,” Agatha stated. She knew he was lying. She always told him you can’t lie to a liar.

He swallowed. “I… got the hair.” It was a truth, but it wasn’t the whole truth.

There was a pause and silence. Then Agatha asked, “Did you see her?”

“Yes,” he answered.

There were several short breaths on the other line like Agatha wanted to start a sentence, but couldn’t find the correct words. She finally sounded defeated. “Get back as soon as you can.” Then she hung up.

He looked down at his phone to see that the call had really ended and when he looked up, Rio was gone.

Chapter 7

When Billy stepped into the loft, he had a bag of groceries in his hand because they really did need food. Agatha was standing at the window in her thinking pose. She turned around and looked him over. She cocked her head, seeing something concerning.

“I’m fine,” he put his hand up. “I tried to read her mind, but she healed it.”

Agathe stopped mid-step toward him and then continued. She took his face in her hands and pushed his wet hood back. She pulled him down to examine his ears. “Are you sure she fixed it?”

Billy couldn’t nod the way Agatha was holding him, but he answered, “Yes. This is just… leftover.”

Agatha went to the kitchen and gestured him to sit down. He knew better than to argue, especially when he had been bleeding that much. He set the groceries down on the counter and she got a towel wet under the sink.

He tilted his head to the side so she could clean the blood off of him. “She said Wanda’s not dead.”

There wasn’t a stutter or a pause in Agatha cleaning the blood off of him. “I figured. You Maximoffs were never great at following the rules.”

Billy smiled being lumped in with the Maximoffs. Though as far as rules went, he was the most rule-following of any witch he knew. He pulled the cup and the phone out of his pocket and set the cup on the table. “There’s her hair.”

Agatha stopped cleaning the dried blood out of his ears and looked at the cup. She picked it up and examined it. “She just gave it to you?”

He debated on telling her that Rio didn’t want to do it until he said it was for Nicky and that at some level Rio knew what they were doing. Not telling Agatha everything had never really helped him and she always ended up finding out anyway so he took his time to recount the meeting from start to finish.

Agatha was quiet the entire time and didn’t comment on any of it which was what worried Billy. There was usually a snide comment somewhere in everything and Agatha was taking it in with the stillness of a statue, sitting across from him at the table.

“Are you mad?” Billy asked softly when he had finished the story and Agatha hadn’t said anything for a whole minute.

She looked up at him and shook her head. There was something about the shape of her eyes and the tilt of her eyebrows upward that made her look like she was a sad puppy. “No.” When she looked at him like that, he thought of all of the things he didn’t tell her. It was mostly movements and the look on Rio’s face. How hurt she looked when he talked about Agatha.

Agatha looked at him like she could tell what he was thinking and studied him for a moment.

Billy shook his head and stood up, growing uncomfortable under the study. “I’m going to take a shower. I think there’s still blood in my shirt.” He picked up his phone.

“Leave your phone.” Agatha said, not getting up from the table.

He hesitated and set it on the table.

“I’ll start looking for where this map leads,” she reached over and pressed her hand to the top of it, sliding it toward her.

Somehow he didn’t believe her, but he didn’t want to fight. He slunk off into the bathroom and left her with his phone.

Chapter 8

Sersi had been sitting in her classroom long after the class was over. All the students had left and she had watched the rain gently tap on the window so long her eyes went soft. On the desk in front of her, she touched a rock, turning a small divot of it into water and then back into solid rock.

Her thoughts were interrupted by a soft knock on the frame of the classroom door. She looked up, not really knowing who to expect at that point. She was pleasantly surprised to find Rio.

She turned her chair toward the door and smiled. “Hello, darling.”

Rio leaned on the doorframe, smiling faintly back. She pressed her brow into the frame and held Sersi’s eyes. “Hey.”

Sersi held her hand out and Rio moved to her like she needed to be invited first. She slipped her hand into Sersi’s and Sersi pulled her into her lap. She wrapped her arms around Rio’s waist and looked up at her. “What’s wrong?”

Rio seemed pensive for a moment. “They’re going to bring my son back.”

A smile crossed Sersi’s face and she squeezed Rio. Then she saw that Rio wasn’t smiling. She pressed her mouth into Rio’s sweater covered upper arm. She grew concerned. “Why aren’t you happy, my love?”

Rio leaned her head to the side so that it rested on the top of Sersi’s head. “I don’t think I’ll get to see him.”

Sersi blinked. “Why?”

Rio picked her head up like she just remembered something and looked down at her. She shook her head. “I’m not sure.” She dipped her head down and kissed Sersi.

Rio was always so private and the only reason Sersi knew about Agatha and Nicky because there was just a moment of vulnerability they had together late, late at night while they were both naked and swathed in the sheets of Sersi’s bed. Rio was affectionate and thoughtful, but Sersi knew there were millenia of things she didn’t know about her.

“Let’s go somewhere,” Rio said and stood up.

Sersi stood as well, taking Rio’s hand. She left her bag at her desk and didn’t lock the door as Rio pulled her out of the classroom.

They walked for half an hour in silence, the umbrella Rio had taken from a man in a suit berating a doorman was the only thing keeping them dry as the dim afternoon was turning into a dark night. They moved through the street and Sersi was starting to get an idea of where they were going when they turned down a tan bricked footpath between two massive buildings.

Rio shook off the umbrella before she put it in the umbrella bin by the glass doors. Sersi reached for her and Rio reached back, taking her hand. They took the elevator down into the earth and stepped out into a museum that was hidden beneath the monumental building above.

It was small - only a few rectangular foundations of ancient buildings encased in glass, but they had been many times before. They had both visited when the short rectangular squares were entire stone buildings laid out in the masterfully planned grid of a Roman outpost. They had visited many times in the modern era.

They stood in the middle of it all. There wasn’t much left of the Roman outpost and there weren’t many visitors left as it was close to the museum’s closing time.

“A long time ago, I imprisoned my sister,” Rio started very abruptly. “Not my sister like mortals understand.”

Sersi nodded. She understood.

“After watching worlds rise and fall, we decided that the problem was eternity,” Rio looked at the rocks on the ground. “Beings who live forever have ruined more worlds than I can remember.” She glanced at Sersi who didn’t seem to take offence to what she was saying. “Things lose meaning for them and without meaning there’s just destruction.”

Sersi had seen this first hand. Arishem was using planets as incubators and did not care about the billions of lifeforms he was going to destroy when it was born.

“So she’s tethered to the place the souls are kept and I am tethered to the place where the living are kept. The afterlife is the only true place Eternity can exist.” Rio explained. “But humans…” Rio looked again at the footprint of a city underneath a city. “They don’t live forever, but they understand eternity. Some of them love so fiercely, it’s the most purely eternal thing.”

Sersi didn’t have to ask if she was talking about Agatha. There was something in Rio’s face that was so enamoured with a memory. She’d seen it before when Rio would talk about her.

“And I…” Rio put her hands on the railing in front of them. “Finally, I truly understood how destructive my own eternity can be.” Rio closed her eyes. She was worried that she had been alive for so long, her paradigm had shifted to the place she and her sister had split the realms for. She had been accused of destruction for millenia, but never guilty. Was she about to make the accusations true? For her son?

Sersi put her hand on Rio’s back. “What happens if you help them?”

Rio shrugged. “I don’t know. I’m connected to the process. If I change it…”

“How bad?” Sersi asked softly, stepping closer to Rio.

Rio turned her head to look at Sersi, a streak of actual fear in her face. “I won’t let anything happen to Earth.”

“I’m worried about you,” Sersi replied. She put her hand on top of Rio’s that was resting on the railing.

Rio closed her eyes and turned her head back, facing forward. “Worst case, Eternity gets pissed and kills me for it.”

“I won’t let her,” Sersi dipped her head down and rested her forehead on Rio’s temple. “I might just be some space robot, but I can fight. I have friends.”

Rio smiled a bit. She leaned into Sersi. “Yeah?”

“Yeah,” Sersi smiled. “You’d love watching Thena fight. It’s like a dance.”

Rio took a deep breath. She wasn’t sure Sersi knew who they were dealing with. Rio herself was a talented fighter and one of the two most powerful witches in existence. Unfortunately, the other most powerful witch could want to kill her for breaching the balance.

Chapter 9

“This is impossible,” Billy sighed. He had sketched the carving Rio made in the back of his phone and had spent six hours staring at maps on his laptop, while Agatha was looking on hers.

It was far past midnight and Agatha closed her laptop. “Are you sure she didn’t say anything else?”

“Yes,” Billy was agitated. “I think we need help.”

“From who?” Agatha asked. She was exhausted as well, not used to having to work so long for a solution.

Billy closed his laptop and dropped his head down on top of it. “Would divination work?”

“No,” Agatha pushed up from the table, taking a step away from it to indicate they were truly done for the night. “We need a cartographer or someone who…”

Billy went out on a limb that Agatha was not going to like. He slowly lifted his head, “Has been all over the planet for seven thousand years?”

He was right and the vitriol in Agatha’s stare was nearly enough to make him blast a hole in the wall and fly out of it.

Billy was exasperated. He didn’t know what else they could do, but spend another day or two meticulously looking at maps of the planet on the internet and hoping for the best, “I’m out of ideas.”

Agatha ran her hand over her eyes. She had pushed Billy too hard and she could see it. She softened, “Let’s get some sleep and we can figure something else out in the morning.”

Billy sighed. He stood up as well, picking up his phone. He ran his thumb over one of the grooves before he tucked it into his pocket. He moved to Agatha and looped his arms around her. “Goodnight.”

She closed her eyes and squeezed him, letting some of the danger dissipate with the hug. “We’ll text her in the morning.”

He pulled back and looked at her. “Are you sure?”

She nodded. “The faster we find your mom, the faster we can find Tommy, and get Nicky back.”

He pulled her into another hug. “Thank you, Agatha.”

Agatha couldn’t let him see how doubtful she was that everything was going to work out. Step one of her plan hinged on Wanda not ripping her apart by her molecules upon seeing her for the first time again.

Chapter 10

Sersi was very receptive to helping them. Agatha even came along, lingering near the door of the small map room in the back of the museum.

Sersi looked at the carving on the back of Billy’s phone and studied it for a long moment. Then she pulled out a long piece of paper and sketched it nearly perfectly and much larger.

“I think…” She paused and walked around the map room, looking at the large leather bound volumes of maps. She pulled out two volumes and started flipping through them. After searching for a few minutes with an audience, Sersi looked up with a sigh. “This might take some time. There’s a cafe near the front of the museum. They have coffee and pastries. It’s Tuesday so they’re mostly fresh. You can tell the barista to put it on my tab.”

Billy looked at Agatha who was leaned on the doorframe of the map room. Agatha jerked her head out the door. He raised his eyebrows in surprise. She gave him a neutral nod. Then she gestured to Sersi expectantly.

Billy felt chided that he was nearly rude. “Do you want a… tea, Sersi?” Billy asked.

“That would be lovely,” she smiled up at him.

He smiled back and touched Agatha’s arm. “Tea?”

She nodded.

Billy slipped past her. “I’ll be right back.” His footsteps fading away and the sound of Sersi turning pages in the atlas were the only sounds for a long time.

Finally, Sersi asked, “What happens if the sacred balance is disturbed?” She looked up from the maps to Agatha. “What happens to Rio?”

Agatha was taken aback by the question. It wasn’t that she hadn’t thought about it at all. She hadn’t thought about it in depth though because the natural balance and Rio, currently, were obstacles not concerns. She looked at Sersi and found that the question wasn’t a trap or a way to get Agatha to see reason. It was a genuine question because Sersi didn’t know and was desperate to.

Agatha felt a streak of guilt that she so rarely felt. She had been closing her eyes and running so hard at her goal that she hadn’t been thinking of the consequences. Consequences were always problems for later.

When it seemed Agatha didn’t know or wasn’t going to answer, Sersi went on as she flipped map pages. “I have a few theories but they’re all based on mythology and folklore.” Sersi shook her head. “Which is a foolish way to create a theory, but all I have are artifacts and books because witch culture is very esoteric.”

It struck Agatha how worried about it all Sersi seemed. Like she was sure Agatha was going to succeed and like she was sure something bad was going to happen to Rio.

“As far as I know, Rio is the first witch,” Agatha answered. She felt bad for Sersi and she also hated her, which was a dynamic she seemed to have a lot. “In the modern sense of the term. Modern for the two of you.”

“The Earth came first? Or she did?” Sersi asked, picking up a pencil and making some notes on the edge of the map she drew.

Agatha shrugged. “She always said she was born of the need to keep things tidy.”

Sersi narrowed her eyes at Agatha in thought and concentration. “What comes, I’m not sure how to phrase this, out of a person when they die?”

Agatha took a deep breath. “Humans made the word ‘soul’ and she seemed to think it was an adequate word for it, but it’s not exactly what we think it is.”

Sersi bit her bottom lip and studied Agatha for a moment. “I know…” She paused. “I want to help you, if I can. But I won’t if it will hurt her. Even if she wants it to.” She shook her head. “I can’t.”

Agatha closed her eyes for a moment to push the tears away. Pain and hurt were not words that Rio should ever be able to feel in a meaningful way, but she was afraid she had inflicted both on Rio in the most ruthless way possible.

“Don’t tell her this,” Agatha cleared her throat. “But I don’t want to hurt her either.”

Sersi let out a smile and it elicited one from Agatha.

“I don’t know where you’re staying, but my… sister keeps a flat nearby,” Sersi added. “The library is expansive. She does have an affinity for Sumerian which may help, you know, starting at the first written language. I think if we’re going to learn about Death without asking her directly, which, I’m sure you know is like trying to pull water from a stone, we start as far back as we can.”

Agatha flexed her eyebrows. She absolutely knew how it was trying to get things about the past from Rio. Agatha thought back over times in history where they could gather information from. Granted her formal education was severely lacking, but she was a quick study and always read as much as she could ever since her mother reluctantly taught her to read.

She was so engrossed in trying to piece together everything she knew about Death that she forgot she was working with her sworn enemy.

Agatha didn’t have time to answer about the flat because Billy came careening back into the room with a tray of drinks and a paper bag in his hand. He looked from Agatha to Sersi and back like he was expecting someone’s entrails to be splattered on the wall.

He took a moment to catch his breath with both Agatha and Sersi looking at him. “I got - I got tea.”

He took a tea out and handed it to Agatha. He looked cautiously at her and she just seemed annoyed that he was looking so closely at her. Then he took the tray and bag to Sersi. He handed her a tea. “I didn’t know how you took it so I got some cream and sugar.”

She took one of the cream packets from the middle of the tray and smiled at him. “Thank you.”

“Can I-” Billy gestured to the bag. “Eat in here?”

Sersi nodded. “Just be careful.”

Billy walked over to Agatha and opened the paper bag for her. “I got you a sweet bread since we didn’t eat breakfast.”

It was a kind gesture, especially because Agatha had expressed a fondness for it. She reached into the bag and took it. “Thanks.”

He pulled out a muffin and started breaking it apart. “Any progress?”

“Not really,” Sersi took a sip of her tea and went back to the maps. “I was telling Agatha this might take a while…” She trailed off, seeing something in one of the atlases that held her attention. She set her tea down and picked up the tome, moving with it to the map she’d drawn.

Agatha and Billy both perked up, taking a short step forward to see what she was looking at. After looking for herself for a moment, Sersi rounded the table to show them. “I think it’s… Northern Italy in the Dolomites.” Sersi handed the book off to Agatha and pulled her phone out. She looked up the location on it and frowned. “There’s nothing there. At least that the satellites can see.”

Agatha looked up from the map and stared at one of the volumes on the shelf as she thought.

“Would she… lie?” Billy asked quietly.
