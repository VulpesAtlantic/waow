Agatha is hungry. No, hungry doesn’t quite cut it. She's been hungry before. She’s starved through wars, fled famines, and even once slept in a damp crypt beneath a slaughterhouse, subsisting on nothing but rats and the memory of warm blood. This isn’t that.

 

This is worse.

 

It’s not just the ache in her bones or the way her hands tremble when she tries to hold a glass. It’s not even the heaviness in her chest or the dull, burning throb that’s taken up residence behind her eyes. No, this feels like a kind of unraveling. Like her body is trying to turn to dust from the inside out.

 

She’s run low before, sure. But this—this bloodlessness—is something else entirely.

 

In the past, when hunger gnawed too loud, she’d simply... feed. An animal, sometimes. A human, if they were particularly loathsome and asking for it. It was simple. Efficient. 

 

Clinical, even.

Not since the blood bag system was put into place. Neatly labeled, meticulously filtered, and discreetly delivered. Her donors were anonymous. She never asked questions, and no one ever asked her any either. It worked.

 

Until the law changed. A single clause, buried in a sea of legalese: all donated blood is now prioritized for human use. The logic was brutal in its simplicity—humans need it to survive, vampires technically don’t. And the subtext was clear: if vampires stepped out of line, and reminded the world of what they used to be, it wouldn’t take much to reignite old fears. A witch hunt, Agatha suspects, wouldn’t even need kindling.

 

Now, she’s starving.

 

And her options are laughably limited. Option one: hunt. Find a human, charm them, drain just enough to keep herself upright. It would be easy—too easy. But Agatha isn’t in the mood to chase down her next meal. 

 

Option two: Fangtasia. The Wanda’s blood bar. A hedonistic dive where humans line up to be bitten like it’s foreplay. Loud music, sticky floors, desperate eyes, and too much perfume. She’s been before. More than she is ready to admit. The memory still makes her grimace. She doesn’t want to perform her hunger. She wants to sate it.

 

Which leaves option three: True Blood.

A regulated feeding center. Clean, clinical, consensual. Humans who get paid to let vampires feed. No pretense. No seduction. Just blood.

 

It’s her best option. Maybe her only one.

Their lives are so brief, they cling to every moment like it might be their last. It makes her nervous, the way they feel things so deeply. She’s seen what happens when a vampire gets too close. The mess it makes.

"There’s something I’ve been thinking about," she says softly.

 

Rio immediately tensed a little, setting her sketchbook aside. "Okay," she says slowly, cautiously.

"I don’t want to... overstep," Rio says softly. "Or be, like, too much—"

“I’m the lucky one,” Rio whispered hoarsely against her skin.

And Agatha feels it, her love and Rio’s too burning on her body warmer than the venom could ever be.


"I’ll stay if you will have me"

 

Agatha holds her even tighter, smiling against her hair. “I will have you forever…or how long you want”

 

Rio pulled back just enough to meet her eyes, and the smile she gave, crooked and shining and entirely hers, felt like the sun breaking through centuries of darkness.

 

"Forever sounds good," Rio says.

 

Their mouths met again, but this time there was no hesitance between them, just the warmth of their love.
