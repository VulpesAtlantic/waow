Chapter 1: Silent Night

December 24, 2016

The rattling of the key in the lock felt almost too loud in the quiet apartment. Leon stepped inside, his instincts kicking in instantly. Instead of the usual scents, something entirely different greeted him.

Fresh pine. And toasted spices—the rich, warm aroma of something slow-roasted.

Leon froze in the entryway. He hung his coat on the rack out of habit, his right hand instinctively reaching toward his hip—where his holster usually rested—before he remembered where he was. He was home.

He slowly walked into the living room, and his feet rooted to the spot.

In the corner of the room stood a massive, lush pine tree. Not a cheap, disposable artificial tree, but a perfect, vibrant specimen. The ornaments—deep red, gold, and matte black glass balls—hung in flawless symmetry on the branches. It was immaculate. Because, of course, if Ada Wong did something, it couldn't be anything less than perfect.

From the kitchen came a soft sizzling and the scent of Christmas dinner. The entire space radiated an overwhelming, cozy warmth that felt almost unreal.

"Ada... is that you?" Leon asked, his eyes still struggling to pull away from the tree.

Ada stepped out from behind the kitchen counter. Her usual elegance was a notch more relaxed now, yet she still looked like she had just walked off the cover of a magazine. She paused for a moment, looking him up and down with that lopsided smirk at the corner of her lips.

"No need to look so frantic, Kennedy..." she said softly, her voice cool and caressing.

Leon shook his head, bewildered, and took a step closer.

"Tell me... What's going on here?"

Ada chuckled softly. Her voice was husky, gentle, and entirely devoid of mockery.

"Why do you ask?"

"This is a Christmas tree..." Leon pointed toward the corner of the room, then glanced into the kitchen, where an oven held something baked to a golden brown behind the glass. "...and... Christmas dinner."

"Yes," Ada replied simply, as though having a complete holiday set piece in the middle of their living room was the most natural thing in the world.

"Should I be worried?" Leon crossed his arms over his chest, but the suspicion in his eyes was slowly replaced by something else. Something much deeper. And much warmer.

"I'm just trying it out to see what it's like," Ada answered, turning back toward the tree.

Leon watched her in silence. How long had it been... right, he hadn't celebrated Christmas since his parents died. Until now, the holiday had only meant overtime, empty case files, and lonely bar counters. But now, as the soft lights shimmered in Ada's hair and reflected off the ornaments, a deep, buried knot in his chest began to loosen. He liked it. A lot.

"And how is it?" he asked softly.

Ada paused, tilting her head slightly as she evaluated the finished tree.

"It's not terrible..." she noted dryly, though a strange, soft light glinted in her eyes.

She reached down to a small box under the tree and pulled out the final piece: a heavy, intricately crafted star topper. Turning around, she held it out toward Leon.

"Will you put it up?"

Leon looked at the star for a moment, then met Ada's eyes. He stepped closer, near enough to feel the warmth of her body. He took the ornament from her hands—their fingers brushed, carrying the silent weight of an unbreakable bond. Leon reached up and carefully placed the star onto the highest branch of the pine.

The light from the tiny bulbs reflected off the glass. They stood beside each other in the warm, quiet room—two people who had waded through too much blood and filth not to realize how precious this silence was.

Leon slowly lowered his hand, but he didn't step away. He looked at her profile.

"Are you okay?"

Ada smiled. It was the kind of smile she saved only for Leon: rare, genuine, and a little sublime.

"I don’t know... Yet."

She walked over to the oven, turning her back on him so he wouldn't immediately read what she was trying to hide from his eyes.

Dinner hadn't just turned out well; Leon felt that if he took one more bite, he would be permanently rendered unfit for duty right then and there. The plates were already resting in the sink, and they had moved to the living room.

Leon was sprawled out on the couch, head tilted back, completely knocked out by the food. He loosened his belt slightly, barely able to catch his breath. Ada cuddled up beside him with soft, almost catlike grace, resting her head on his shoulder.

"Just don't touch my stomach..." Leon groaned, placing a protective hand over his gut.

Ada's soft, muffled laugh vibrated against his shoulder.

"Did I pass the Christmas wife test?" Ada asked, lightly running her fingertips along his arm.

"If I survive the night, I'll tell you..." Leon grunted, but a smile hid in his voice.

They snuggled closer. The overhead lights were off; the only light source came from the warm, golden bulbs of the Christmas tree, casting soft shadows on the walls. The room held a sense of quiet and peace that neither of them was used to.

Then, Leon's phone buzzed in his pocket. For the several-th time that evening. With a sigh, he raised the screen. As his eyes scanned the message, a faint, genuine smile flickered across his face. He quickly typed a short reply, set the device down beside him, and pulled Ada back in, wrapping his arm around her shoulders.

"Your friends?" Ada asked, playing it off casually.

"Yeah..." Leon replied, closing his eyes.

Ada's gaze involuntarily drifted to her own silent phone on the table. But only for a single second. Her screen never buzzed for things like this. Her phone meant only assignments, encrypted orders, and danger.

"Christmas wishes?"

"Something like that," Leon smiled. "In their own way."

Ada stayed quiet for a moment. Her head rested on his chest, listening to the steady, comforting beat of his heart. Then, breaking the silence, she spoke in a cool, measured tone:

"Your friends hate me."

Leon didn't rush his answer. He knew who she meant—Claire, Chris, the others who over the years had suspected her shadow on the wrong side far too often.

"They just don't trust you," he corrected gently.

Ada let out a short, mocking laugh.

"That's the polite way of saying they hate me."

Leon didn't answer right away. Because he knew Ada was right. There was no point in sugarcoating it. Silence weighed down on them, but Ada didn't let it rest.

"I didn't think you cared if someone had a bad opinion of you..." Leon finally said, fixing his gaze on the star at the top of the tree.

"I don't," Ada snapped back instantly. It was the truth. The world's opinion had never moved her. But then her voice softened. "But I care if someone hates you because of me."

Leon was taken aback. Ada rarely—or rather, never—talked about her feelings. She didn't use grand words or make confessions. Instead, she lay here in his arms, owning up to her fear in the simplest, rawest way possible.

"They don't hate me," Leon said quietly.

"You risked your career for me," Ada reminded him, a sudden sharpness entering her voice.

"I did."

"Practically your life, too. Just so I could be free."

"I remember," he replied softly.

"After everything—after working for your mutual enemies."

"Pretty much."

Ada let out a small, incredulous laugh. A laugh devoid of joy—it held only the pure bewilderment of facing a logic completely foreign to her.

"And after all that, they don't hate you?"

"No."

"How?" Ada asked, raising her head to look directly into his eyes.

Leon looked at her and gave the answer with such simplicity, as if it were the most natural thing in the world:

"Because that's what friendship is."

Ada didn't answer. She rested her head back on his chest and fell silent. She was trying to process the word.

Friendship.

In her life, relationships had always been transactions: favors, debts, blackmail, and alliances of convenience. There was never anything pure or selfless about them.

Leon was the sole exception. Leon, who was here right now, who had sacrificed everything for her without asking for anything in return.

The silence stretched on too long. It wasn't her usual cool, confident silence—a strange, almost fragile weight sat upon her stillness.

Leon noticed the change immediately. He shifted, searching for her face in the dim light. "Are you sure you're okay?" he asked, his voice dropping to a whisper as he tightened his arm around her.

Ada closed her eyes, slowly breathing in the scent of Leon, the pine, and the warm room. The air caught in her lungs for a second, as if she were wrestling with something, but then she simply leaned loosely against his chest.

"I'm fine..." she replied softly.

That was all she said. Her voice was calm, but her eyes stared into nothingness amidst the twinkling lights of the Christmas tree, hiding from him the suffocating fear that had been consuming her for days.

The bedroom was dark and quiet. Leon woke to the dry, uncomfortable feeling of thirst. He automatically reached out his arm beside him, but the sheet was cold and empty.

He sat up. From the faint, lingering scent on her pillow, he knew Ada had been up for a while. Outside, there was no sound. No gunfire, no sound of shattering glass, yet his reflexes immediately went on high alert. Quietly slipping out of bed, he padded softly down the hallway.

He stopped at the living room doorway.

Ada was sitting on the floor, huddled in front of the Christmas tree with her back pressed against the wall. She had pulled her legs to her chest, arms wrapped around her knees. The faint, flickering lights of the tree illuminated her face: her skin was pale, tiny beads of sweat glistening on her forehead. Her chest rose and fell in rapid, ragged hitches—she was fighting for every breath.

She was completely conscious, eyes open, but staring into nothingness. A quiet, suffocating panic attack. Even this, she handled with silence, dignity, and grace.

A sharp pang of alarm pierced Leon's heart, but he immediately switched into the professional, calm state he had used countless times in the field over the years. He had seen this before. At accident scenes, disasters, failed operations. And with Ada, whenever the shadows of her past caught up with her.

With slow, deliberate movements, he stepped over and sat beside her on the rug. Not too close, but just enough for her to feel his presence. He didn't touch her.

He spoke in a soft, steady, deep voice.

"You know, I'll probably have to run to the convenience store tomorrow..." Leon began, as if merely continuing a conversation they had paused. His voice was calm and rhythmic, like the rolling ocean. "We're out of coffee. And I accidentally bought the kind you hate. You said you'd rather drink motor oil. I thought I'd pick up some of that hazelnut chocolate you like, too. If they still have it during the holidays..."

Ada didn't look at him, but her breathing shifted by a fraction. She was listening.

Leon slowly rested his right hand on the rug, palm up, just inches away from her tense fingers. He left it there. An open invitation.

"And the car needs to go to the shop, too... The front left brake has been making a weird noise ever since it snowed. It's nothing major, but it's bugging me. I can ask the guys at the garage to take a quick look..."

In the quiet, only Leon's steady whispering and the ticking of the wall clock could be heard. Then, with slow, trembling movements, Ada slid closer to him. Leon understood, and he took her hand. Her fingers locked around his palm. Her grip was surprisingly strong, like a drowning person who had finally found solid ground.

Leon responded to her grip with a gentle caress, never breaking the rhythm of his voice.

"...so I might spend the morning in town. But I'll be back by early afternoon. If you want, we can go somewhere after."

Ada slowly closed her eyes. Her chest rose deeply a few more times as she took ragged, shuddering breaths, but the rapid pacing finally began to subside. Despite the cold sweat on her skin, the tension gradually vanished from her body.

Leon watched her in silence. He thought he knew what was happening. He thought he understood completely. Ada had always lived by her own rules—independent, free, a shadow in the world. All of this—marriage, commitment, supervision, this suffocatingly normal life —was simply too much for her. The walls were closing in.

But he didn't bring it up. He didn't demand answers.

Instead, he slowly pulled closer and wrapped his arms around her gently, covering her with his body. Ada didn't resist. She rested her head against his chest, still refusing to let go of his hand. The steady, monotonous rhythm of his heartbeat slowly brought her back to reality.

The suffocating lump in her throat began to ease, but its place was taken by something else: a sharp, driving urge.

Ada's lips trembled. There, in the dim light, wrapped in the safe warmth of Leon, the iron- willed wall she had built over decades to keep herself alive dissolved for a single second. She wanted to tell him. She wanted to speak the words aloud to lift this crushing weight off her chest. It's not because of the commitment, Leon... It's not you I'm afraid of...

Her lips parted slightly. The words hung on her tongue, needing only a single, quiet whisper.

But then reality flashed before her eyes. The danger, their past, an uncertain future. Fear— pure, paralyzing fear that her confession would change everything forever—pulled her back from the edge of the cliff. Her mouth closed. She buried the words deep down, back where they came from.

She didn't dare tell him. Not yet.

Instead, she simply pressed closer against him. Leon asked no questions, didn't push, but simply stood his ground as he always did: a quiet, unshakeable fortress.

And so they remained. Sitting on the cold hardwood floor, holding onto each other under the flickering lights of the Christmas tree, surrounded by perfect, silent stillness.

Chapter 2: The Edge of the Abyss

December 27, 2016

Ada had been pacing up and down the apartment for hours. Her steps were silent as she alternated between the soft rug and the cold hardwood floor—the movements of a caged predator that couldn't find its place. Her body, which had always operated like a perfectly calibrated instrument, was now turning against her.

Her throat was dry, her lungs felt constricted, and waves of dull, sickeningly sweet nausea constantly rose from the pit of her stomach, burning her esophagus.

How could this have happened?

The question echoed in her head like a corrupted line of code in a system. Ada Wong never made mistakes. She didn't miscalculate. Her entire life, her every last breath, her reaction times, and her body chemistry were all dictated by pure, unyielding control. She always saw every outcome three steps ahead. She always had an escape route, a Plan B, a Plan C, spare ammunition, a forged identity. Nothing unexpected was ever allowed to happen to her. Coincidence was a mistake made by amateurs, and unpredictability was synonymous with death.

Yet here she stood, completely vulnerable, carrying a fatal certainty inside her body.

Her instincts—those sharp, ruthless instincts that had carried her through a thousand dangers —had sounded the alarm days ago. She felt the subtle, imperceptible shifts in her metabolism, the unusually sharp acuity of her sense of smell, the alien, inexplicable warmth beneath her skin. A process had begun inside her that couldn't be halted with a command. That couldn't be neutralized with a weapon. That couldn't be overwritten with a program.

She stopped in the middle of the living room, in the shadow of the Christmas tree, and wrapped both hands tightly around her ribs.

She had been trained. Trained since childhood to endure physical pain, mental pressure, and agonizing torment. She knew how to dress an open gunshot wound in the dark without anesthesia. She had learned to operate while starving, deprived of sleep, covered in blood. Her body was a weapon: precise, disciplined, and obedient.

But no one had prepared her for this.

For a time when her body wouldn't have to prepare for death, but for life.

Her stomach suddenly lurched so violently that it took her breath away. Her mouth filled with a sour, acrid taste.

Clapping a hand over her mouth, she bolted toward the bathroom. She dropped to her knees on the cold tile, hunching over the basin. The retching burned her throat, and tears involuntarily welled in her eyes from the physical strain. Her body was in full rebellion against her.

Exhausted and gasping, she leaned back against the edge of the bathtub. Cold sweat beaded on her forehead. Inside her chest lingered that tight, dark sensation—panic. The kind of paralyzing fear soldiers feel when they are surrounded and running out of ammo.

No. Not now.

Ada closed her eyes, drew a deep, slow breath through her nose, and forced her heart rate to slow down. One... two... three... She had to regain control. She had to be the one in control.

And as the physical sickness ebbed, the mental torment struck back all the harder. Because her thoughts inevitably drifted back to him.

Leon.

The man who had changed everything.

She remembered the day she signed the contract. Their marriage, in the eyes of the outside world—and in official records—was merely a legal construct. When Ada was finally captured, the law showed no mercy: her past, her acts of sabotage, and her work with biological weapons meant either a life sentence or immediate liquidation deep within a classified facility.

But Leon had stepped in.

Leon took personal, moral, and physical responsibility for her with the government. He staked his own name, his career, his medals, and his life on keeping her free. But his superiors didn't trust promises. They demanded a rock-solid, unbreakable legal guarantee. A bond that placed Ada under his full supervision, making him her sole guardian, custodian, and warden.

And so, they married. On paper. With cold, dry signatures.

Yet behind the official documents lay the truth. A truth neither of them spoke aloud to the government: Ada hadn't stayed with him because of legal guarantees or the comfort of house arrest. She hadn't stayed because he offered a shield against prison.

She stayed because she loved him.

For the first time in her life, Ada allowed herself the luxury of loving someone. Not that she had wanted to. Leon had simply walked into her life and, by his sheer existence, rewritten the rules.

And once Ada made the choice to stay with him, she swore to herself: she would never betray him again.

Ever since Raccoon City, she had done nothing but wound him. She had deceived him, taken advantage of his good faith, compromised his safety, vanished like a ghost in the night, and broken his heart over and over again. She had watched him transform from a young, idealistic rookie cop into a burned-out, weary, battle-scarred agent—and she knew she had played a massive role in that wear and tear.

When she moved into this apartment, Ada thought she could finally make amends.

That she could offer a home to the man who had spent his entire life carrying everyone else's burdens.

Leon was always saving others. He had sacrificed his own youth, his friends, his peace of mind so that strangers could live.

And now, Ada realized she hadn't given him peace at all.

She had given him another burden.

Her throat tightened. She pushed herself up from the tile. Turning on the faucet, she splashed cold water onto her pale face and looked into the mirror.

Dark circles shadowed her eyes, but the confusion slowly began to evaporate from her gaze. Her mind shifted back into that cool, ruthless mode her trainers had beaten into her decades ago.

Analysis. Assessment. Resolution.

What if this situation were a mission? A contract that needed to be executed?

The answer emerged immediately, sharp and clear. On a mission, if a target or an ally faced a threat severe enough to cause their complete destruction, the agent's duty was to minimize the damage. Remove the source of danger from the field.

And in this scenario, she was the source of danger. Her body, her condition, the secret she carried within her.

She couldn't put him in danger. She couldn't place this crushing, unbearable weight on his shoulders. She couldn't stand by and watch the man she loved slowly fall apart under the burden of responsibility he took on for her.

She would handle this alone. As always. Everything.

It was the one thing she truly knew how to do. Self-reliance. Independence. Fighting alone. She had been taught this; she had been forged for it. She couldn't rely on anyone, couldn't involve anyone else. She had to disappear before the situation became irreversible. She had to leave, far away, to a place where her past couldn't catch up to her, and where Leon would be safe from her.

Because if she wasn't here, he would be relieved of responsibility. No one could hold him accountable for something he didn't even know about. He would be free. Truly free at last.

Ada walked with quiet, measured steps into the walk-in closet.

From the deepest corner of the wardrobe, she pulled out her black, waterproof duffel bag. Its material still carried the scent of her old, dangerous life: gunpowder, gun oil, and synthetic fabric.

She began packing her gear one piece at a time. Her movements were mechanical.

First, clothes. Dark, durable, non-descript items. No red dresses, no high heels. Pure functionality. Tactical pants, thermal tops, black cotton shirts.

Then, weapons. From a hidden compartment behind the wardrobe came her 9mm Glock. Practiced, she racked the slide, checked the barrel, and inserted the loaded magazine. The metallic click sounded suffocatingly loud in the quiet apartment. Beside it went spare magazines, a tactical knife, wire cutters, and a jammer.

Documents. From the bottom of the bag, she retrieved a folder of forged credentials. Four different passports under four different names and nationalities. Each came with a credit card, a offshore bank account, and a fully established backstory. Canadian, Swiss, Singaporean identities. Disguises, wigs, tinted contact lenses.

She had prepared for everything. Calculated every contingency.

When she pulled the zipper shut, her hand trembled for a fraction of a second.

It was done. She could leave. The plan was set, the escape route clear, the timing perfect.

Ada slung the heavy bag over her shoulder. Its weight was familiar, yet it bit into her flesh with unusual sharpness.

She walked through the dark hallway. The flickering bulbs of the Christmas tree cast faint beams of light against the wall. The scent of pine mingled with the smell of gun oil wafting from the bag—a terrible fusion of cold, familiar reality and a fleeting, warm illusion.

She stopped in front of the entrance door.

Reaching out, her fingers closed around the cold, metal handle.

All it took was one decisive motion. Press the handle, step into the stairwell, lock the door, and vanish. Three seconds. That was all it took for solitude to reclaim her.

And then... the movement froze.

Her thoughts spun back to him, willfully and uncontrollably.

Leon.

His face flashed before her eyes. She imagined the moment he would arrive home. Looking for her in the apartment, only to find empty rooms. Seeing the open wardrobe, the empty compartments, and realizing what had happened.

She thought about what he would feel. His heart breaking all over again. Standing helpless in the quiet apartment, before the decorated Christmas tree, teetering on the edge of a breakdown, wondering what he had done wrong. What had he messed up? How had he driven her away? What mistake had he made that caused her to lose trust in him yet again?

Ada's heart squeezed with a pain so sharp it felt as though a blade had been twisted inside her. Her chest cramped. She felt like she was suffocating.

She was trapped. Whatever she did, she would destroy him.

Ada stood at the door, the heavy bag resting on her shoulder. Her hand gripped the metal handle until her knuckles turned white.

The cold, predictable logic that had guided her entire life wavered. She stood on the edge of two abysses, caught between two unbearable choices.

Slowly, she closed her eyes. Her head grew heavy, her forehead coming to rest against the cold doorframe.

"I'm sorry, Leon..."

Her hand still rested on the handle.

And then, finally, she made her decision.

Chapter 3: A Small Problem

December 27, 2016

Classified files were piled high on the desk, and the cold blue light from the monitor cast tired shadows across Leon's face. Holidays or not, the dark side of the world took no break. Although Leon had been assigned almost exclusively to active field ops in recent years, the cross-border, ominous reports had multiplied. More experienced analysts were needed at headquarters—so he, too, was chained behind a desk.

He had his own office, quiet and isolated. Yet he couldn't concentrate.

The lines bled together before his eyes. Instead of reports on threats and illegal arms shipments, his thoughts constantly orbited Ada.

He couldn't shake how strange she had been acting lately. Yes, Ada had always been strange. Unpredictable, infuriating, and with her agonizingly sharp sarcasm, she often tested a person's patience to its limits. But to Leon, she was no longer a mystery. He had figured her out. For years, for decades, he had observed her shadows, and he could associate a reason with every unusual tremor in her demeanor. He always knew precisely when she was lying. When she was up to something, when she was deflecting, and when something was bothering her.

But her current state was entirely different.

Colleagues dropped by one after another, asking questions, requesting approvals. Leon answered. His voice was mechanical, firm, professional, but his mind and soul were somewhere else entirely. Anyone else in his shoes would likely have brushed it off. They would have said Ada was just changing, or finally enjoying the quiet of house arrest and a more settled life.

But Leon knew Ada didn't just change on a whim. She wasn't the type to wake up one morning as a different person.

His instincts, which had kept him alive a thousand times over, were now sounding alarm bells so loud that his head almost throbbed with pain. He felt it in his gut that something was severely wrong. And he was consumed by the urge to figure out what it was.

Rubbing his eyes, he leaned back in his chair and switched into agent mode.

What would Ada do if the situation were reversed, and it were about me?

The answer came quickly: She would try to think with my head.

Fine, he thought. Then think with Ada's head.

A soft, dry, self-deprecating laugh escaped his lips in the empty office.

"Easier said than done..." he muttered into the dim room.

Still, he tried. He began methodically, with agent-like precision, reviewing every possible option. He ruled out threats. He personally checked the channels—no one was actively looking for Ada at the moment, there was no fresh bounty on her head, and she hadn't taken on any external, illicit jobs. She wasn't sick; he hadn't found any suspicious medication in the house. There was no trace of any visible danger.

And then he reached the final possibility. An option that made his heart suddenly tighten in his chest.

Ada is afraid.

Leon swallowed hard and slowly straightened in his chair. His gaze cleared, the exhaustion evaporating in the blink of an eye. It was as if he had stumbled upon a fresh track in the dark.

What could Ada Wong possibly be afraid of? What existed in this world capable of keeping a woman like her in terror—and something she wouldn't dare tell him, no less?

The puzzle pieces his memory had involuntarily recorded over the past few days suddenly began to lock tightly together.

Ada had been unusually distant and dreamy these past few days. More than unusual... She was never dreamy. Ada was always, at every moment, scanning her surroundings with flawless, razor-sharp focus; her vigilance never wavered for a single second.

She slept in later than usual. Yet every morning, she always woke at the exact same minute, by an internal clock, without an alarm.

Speaking of mornings: she had "forgotten" to drink her coffee multiple times. She would just stand over the untouched cup or leave it to go cold on the counter, even though she previously couldn't imagine starting her day without it.

She talked about feelings. Unprompted. She brought up topics like friendship, loyalty, and disappointment—things that previously couldn't have been dragged out of her even with extortion.

And then there was that look. Leon had caught her several times in recent days staring at him. Long, silent, with a deep, searching intensity in her eyes. But the moment he turned toward her, she immediately looked away.

And the Christmas tree.

Ada had set up a pine tree and cooked dinner. Yet she had always said Christmas was something only children truly enjoyed.

"I'm just trying it out to see what it's like..." her words echoed in Leon's mind. As if she were trying out what it would be like if...

Leon's pulse spiked. A distinct, hot wave washed through his chest. At first, he just laughed softly, incredulously, and shook his head.

"Come on..." he whispered. "That can't be."

But the realization, as if waiting for this exact crack in his armor, crashed down on him ruthlessly.

She didn't drink wine at dinner. She rarely touched alcohol anyway, but lately, she hadn't accepted a single sip. She pushed the meat further away from herself at the table, as if the smell of the roast bothered her.

And that panic attack in front of the tree at night... That wasn't the kind of attack that happens when memories of the past and ghosts come haunting. It wasn't battlefield trauma. It was the silent struggle of someone carrying something alone, something she was deathly afraid of.

Leon slowly buried his face in his hands. Resting his elbows on the desk, his head felt heavy as stone. He would have considered this as the absolute last possibility in the world, but now, as he laid out every tiny detail, the picture was clearer than anything else. It was more logical than any other explanation.

"My God... Ada."

He desperately tried to convince himself otherwise. He thought these signs could mean a thousand other things. Stress, exhaustion, a rough patch spanning a few weeks.

But every fiber of his being, every beat of his heart, screamed that he was right.

He knew Ada. He knew her better than anyone else on this planet. Ada did nothing without a reason. She was acting stranger for a reason. She had brought Christmas into the apartment for a reason. She had a panic attack in the dark for a reason. She was this vulnerable for a reason. And she was afraid for a reason.

She's pregnant.

Leon felt as though the ground had been pulled out from beneath his feet. Falling. A pure, dizzying freefall into nothingness.

His first thought wasn't that he was going to be a father. He didn't think about his own life. Instead, it was that terrible, gnawing awareness that the woman he loved was carrying this fear alone. That she was fighting the unknown by herself, consuming herself, while he should be standing right beside her.

Why didn't you tell me? the question screamed inside him.

But as quickly as the question arose, the answer hit him instantly. Because he knew her like the back of his hand. He knew how Ada's mind worked.

She feels she's putting me in danger with it. She feels she'll become a burden to me. She thinks she has to protect me from it.

And in that instant, pure, paralyzing panic bit into his chest.

Because Leon knew exactly what Ada Wong would do if she felt her presence posed a danger to him. What she would do if her instincts told her she had to protect Leon from herself.

Leon bolted upright. His chair slammed against the wall behind him with a loud crash. At a breakneck pace, he gathered his keys and coat, leaving behind on his desk the half-finished, classified reports, the open files, and the running monitor. "My lady?" he addressed the baby girl with a serious face. "If I may, I recommend this vintage. Excellent. The yield that year was of supreme quality."

Ada pressed her hand over her mouth to stifle her laughter. Leon was simultaneously incredibly sweet and endlessly ridiculous. Somehow, it was always this duality that gave his character the essence Ada loved so much.

"Sir?" Leon turned to Aiden. "Is this to your liking?"

In response, a giant spit bubble popped on the boy's lips.

"A splendid choice," Leon nodded, then dropped into a cross-legged position, aligning the bottles into the little ones' mouths at the same time.

A slow, peaceful silence settled over the living room. Only the rhythmic suckling of the babies and the dull thud of snow blocks occasionally sliding off the eaves broke the quiet.

Ada didn't move. Leaning against the doorframe, she watched them.

As she observed Leon, the day from one year ago suddenly flashed into her mind.

The moment when, in the cold light of the bathroom, she had seen the two firm lines on the test.

She remembered that paralyzing fear with complete clarity.

The thought of what a child would do to Leon—what an unbearable burden of worry it would place on this man.

On the shoulders of a man who was already carrying far more than his share.

And she remembered what she had thought about herself back then.

How she had tried to convince herself that she could be a good mother, that she could have a real family and protect them.

Then how she had disassembled that hope in a matter of seconds, rationalizing to herself why she would fail.

That Leon couldn't possibly want any of this.

And then she remembered the moment when, clutching the door handle, she had realized: she couldn't give it up.

This picture right before her eyes—the image that had lingered behind the fears poisoning her soul even back then.

Her eyes and nose began to sting. She quickly stepped up behind Leon and placed a hand on his shoulder.

"Managing?" she asked softly, though the answer was obvious.

"Yeah," Leon said, looking up at her with a smile.

It was true.

Because Leon was a father.

Precisely the one he was meant to be.

Leon woke up at dawn to the fact that he hadn't woken up to anything.

His body had become so accustomed to the constant night interruptions that silence itself felt suspicious. He opened his eyes instinctively and turned toward the nightstand.

The baby monitor wasn't there.

He rolled to his other side: Ada's spot was empty, and the little ones were missing from the co-sleeper too.

He scrambled out of bed. His stomach still felt heavy from Ada's fantastic dinner. He drank a glass of water, then stepped out of the bedroom, searching the dark hallway for the light that might lead him to the others.

Halfway down the stairs, he heard it.

It was a pleasantly deep, humming voice. As he walked down, the melody came into clearer focus.

Ada was curled up in the living room's giant armchair, the two tiny bodies resting on her chest.

She was humming.

A slow, silky-toned lullaby that made the glowing lights of the Christmas tree feel even more intimate.

Leon stopped beneath the archway.

Something remarkably deep, an overwhelming warmth spread through his chest. The feeling was unlike anything else.

The scents, the presents under the tree, the silence.

The lullaby.

Aiden and Lena.

And Ada.

He stepped closer slowly. He minded his every move, as if fearing that if he stepped too loudly, the spell would shatter like a dream.

Ada looked up at him.

"I didn't mean to wake you," she whispered barely audibly. The little ones were breathing deeply and peacefully on her chest.

"It's fine," Leon replied just as softly, then carefully lowered himself onto the arm of the armchair. "What are you humming to them?"

After a short pause, Ada shrugged almost imperceptibly.

"I don't know..." she whispered. "Somehow I just... remember it."

She looked down at the children. "But I have no idea where from."

Leon gently smoothed a stray lock of hair away from her face, tucking it behind her ear. When he went to pull his hand back, Ada gently pressed her cheek against his palm, silently telling him to stay. She leaned into his large, protective hand.

"Are you okay?" Leon asked.

Ada didn't answer immediately.

She just looked at him, and in that look was their entire lives.

The past that had nearly destroyed them, the present, and the quiet future. The countless nameless moments in which these two people had saved each other over and over again.

They had saved each other.

In every possible way.

"I'm fine," she said finally.

It was true.

Because Ada was a mother.

Precisely the one she was meant to be to finally, truly find herself.

He'd buy her flowers. Take her dancing after dinner. Or to the movies. Had Ada ever even been to the movies? And when was the last time he himself had sat in a theater or in front of a cinema screen? His heart ached at the thought of holding her to the rhythm of slow, soft music, surrounded by the familiar, elegant scent of Ada's perfume. He sighed deeply behind the wheel, as if the breath could pull the image closer to him.

He was torturously desperate for them to finally be alone, where the outside world ceased to exist. He almost felt guilty about it—their kids meant everything to him—but he craved Ada. He craved the chance for them to be just two carefree people for a single night; two people from whom fate had stolen their youth.

When he pulled into the driveway, he saw the faint, ghostly glow of the nightlight burning in the nursery window. He quickly killed the engine and walked into the house with the quietest steps he could manage. He kicked off his boots, threw his jacket on the hook, and immediately headed upstairs.

Ada was asleep in the large armchair in the middle of the nursery, situated exactly between the two cribs. Her head was tilted onto her shoulder, one arm hanging limply over the armrest.

Leon stepped up to the cribs and carefully touched the twins' foreheads, one by one. Neither of them had a fever. The invisible weight that had been pressing on his chest all day finally lifted.

Then he stepped over to Ada.

He dropped to his knees beside the armchair and took her hand with infinite gentleness, making sure not to startle her. Ada slowly opened her eyes.

"Hi, gorgeous," Leon whispered.

Ada blinked a few times, trying to clear the fog of sleep.

"Hi, handsome... You're finally home."

Leon smiled, taking Ada's warm, sleep-creased face into his palm. "I'm taking over the night shift," he said, then stood up and offered his hand to help her up. "Go get some sleep."

Ada stared at him groggily for a few seconds, then slowly stood, leaning on his solid frame. But instead of letting go, she pulled him along. "You come too," she said, her voice still raspy and sleepy.

She pulled Leon all the way to the bedroom, and as she collapsed onto the bed, she still didn't let go of his hand.

"I haven't even showered yet..." Leon protested weakly. "I'm sweaty, and..."

"And?" Ada asked.

She pulled him down onto the mattress, snuggled tightly against him, and rested her head on his chest. She anchored her legs around his, like someone who wanted to tie herself down forever.

"I missed you."

Then she fell back asleep almost instantly.

Leon wrapped his arms around her, held her close, and tried to breathe evenly so he wouldn't disturb her rest with a single sound. He gently stroked her hair and pressed a soft kiss to her forehead.

Nothing compares to this, he thought, breathing in the faint baby scent radiating from Ada's hair.

At the end of every single day, he gave thanks for this quiet.

Because he was home. Because he had a home to return to.
