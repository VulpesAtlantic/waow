Chapter 1: Bizarre dream

Far away from those men whose hearts were filled with greed, Ada allowed herself to limp all the way to the hotel room where she was staying. She couldn’t find a place very far from Wesker or the men in the helicopter that had flown her away from that city—a city she would never forget. Everything that had passed before her eyes, everything she had heard, everything she had done.

And Leon.

She would never forget him.

However, she could save her useless weakness for another time. Right now, she needed to reach the door without fainting. She needed to keep going. She needed to avoid thinking about the blood starting to soak through her bandages, but it was also essential to remain fully aware of the toll. Ignorance is stupidity, she knew that all too well. Balance, she reinforced to herself as she opened and then locked the door. Ada was always a hostage to the need for balance and caution, never peace; for her, it was only ever a precise, calculated metric.

She dragged her injured leg across the floor. Sitting down on the bed, her vision blurred at the edges before turning dark. She couldn't pass out like this. For a second, she feared she would bleed to death on some random hotel bed right after surviving a turbulent ride through the depths of hell. Perhaps she deserved it.

She promptly shook her head and stood up from the bed. She would never allow herself the miserable luxury of self-pity and self-deprecation; time was, and would eternally be, her most petty enemy. One second of weakness and she would lose her life. Ada moved heavily across the room, forcing herself to change the bandages and staunch the bleeding while her eyelids fluttered threateningly. Her heart pounded with a mix of the fear of death and a rising urge to vomit.

For the sake of her self-treatment's progress, she chose not to panic when she couldn't see anything out of her right eye the moment she removed the old bandage. She didn't think, she just acted. Piece by piece, she stripped away the filthy clothes that were in the way. There were only the new loops of the bandages, only the medical knowledge she possessed, and the simple need to keep breathing.

She was in nothing but her undergarments. Wounded, bloodied, she smelled of gunsmoke, corpses, and filth; disgusting, pure and simple. As she wrapped the final turn of the bandage around her injured knee, her muscles gave way to overwhelming exhaustion, and her mind gave up on her. Ada collapsed onto the bed and lost consciousness, her chest heaving, proving she was still alive, despite everything.

A throbbing migraine pulsed, shattering every coherent thought she could try to gather. She hadn’t even opened her eyes yet, and she was already feeling the full weight of her injuries and blood loss. Still, she was alive. Battered, broken, vulnerable, and profoundly exhausted, but not dead.

Ada didn't move for a long time, focusing entirely on adjusting to the headache and the fierce, burning ache shooting through her entire body. She was certain she had woken up with a fever. A thin, cold layer of sweat coated her skin despite being mostly unclothed. But just for this brief moment, there was no rush to get up.

She had completed the mission; now she could mentally prepare for the hardest part of all: dealing with the consequences of remaining a human being. Feeling, questioning, remembering. As if the sensation of having every muscle and bone brutally crushed wasn't enough, it came with an excruciating, suffocating tight grip around her so cold, trained heart.

She could already feel tears of fatigue and remorse burning behind her eyes. She wouldn't allow herself to cry just yet, she needed to at least… what did she need to do? What excuse would she use as armor to avoid the inevitable here, completely alone, destroyed, in the dark? She shook her head and forced her blurry eyes open.

Her heart skipped a beat. That was not the ceiling that had faded from view before she blacked out.

Ada forced herself to sit up as her heart hammered violently against her ribs. The room was entirely unrecognizable. She had never been here before, of that, she was certain. She checked herself first—she was exactly as she had been when she passed out, down to every detail, including the fact that she was half-naked. That realization offered very little comfort.

Wesker. He had surely tracked her down and taken her. Or perhaps the other men who were with him. Maybe he had sent someone else. Her mind raced with possibilities, leaving her with only one absolute certainty: she had been captured while weak and unable to fight back.

She bolted upright, all her physical pain instantly locked away. Her wide eyes scanned the room. The curtains were drawn, the bed was massive and soft, and the overall aesthetic was comfortable—the color palette was a welcome relief to the eyes, but none of that mattered. Ada searched frantically for weapons, syringes, needles, or hidden cameras.

She couldn’t take even a single step before the door opened. A chilling premonition ran through her entire body. She adjusted her expression to look calm and threatening, or at least she tried to.

A man stared back at her. His eyes were wide, and his jaw had practically dropped to the floor. He was holding two mugs, dressed in casual clothes, almost like sleepwear (?!). Ada had rarely seen a more confused expression in her entire life. As his gaze swept over her body, his expression worsened. He didn’t seem bothered by how bare or exposed her state was, but rather by her numerous injuries.

Within a span of just a few seconds, she thought: A tall man, pure muscle. She didn't let herself falter. Was he armed? No. Ada could try to run. Where to? He blocked the way with his broad shoulders. There was the window, but she had recently fallen from a great height, she wouldn't survive a second fall. She mentally cursed herself for not checking which floor she was on.

There was another door inside the room.

Ada bolted.

“Hey- wait a minute!” he raised a hand, but it was too late.

She ran with wide, undeniably desperate strides into the other room. She slammed the door shut, locked it, and leaned her weight against it. She could hear the man's hesitant footsteps approaching the door.

She found herself inside a large, comfortable bathroom without it being overly luxurious. A wide bathtub sat in the corner. There was a rug that felt pleasant to the touch, also in a muted color. Next to her was a large, clean mirror, and underneath it was a marble sink where various expensive beauty and makeup products rested, brands she had never heard of.

Two towels hanging. Two toothbrushes by the sink. Two laundry baskets side by side.

Where the hell was she?!

The environment was nothing like a threatening laboratory or a war zone, but that did the complete opposite of putting her at ease.

Ada didn't move away from the door, even when she felt the man's presence right behind it. If he tried to break it down, he would succeed. She needed more weight, but she couldn't find anything other than the laundry baskets.

Her eyes narrowed as they targeted a window right above the baskets.

Perfect.

She heard two reluctant knocks on the door, her entire body froze with fear.

"...Hey... Ada, it's you, right?" His voice was deep, but so careful that it only frightened her more.

He knew her name. Or, at least, the name she had used on her most recent mission to secure a sample of the G-virus in Raccoon City.

The man fell silent when she didn't answer, but then, "Ada, please come out of there. I have absolutely no idea what happened, or why you look like this, but I'm not going to hurt you."

Oh, sure. Like she would ever fall for that.

"I'm serious, I'm not here to hurt you. Talk to me, what happened? Do you remember anything?"

Ada was already on the other side of the bathroom, examining the window. She unlocked it cautiously and looked down.

It was a penthouse.

Her breath hitched.

"...If you're not going to answer me, I'm just going to stay right here until you talk to me."

Ada didn't mind the sound of the stranger leaning against the doorframe. At least he wasn't trying to force his way in, but that was just basic decency. One she didn't often see in kidnappers or torturers, but still.

It didn’t take long for her to compose herself. It was a penthouse? Well, that meant she was closer to the roof. She could try jumping from one rooftop to another, or be more reasonable and slowly climb down floor by floor. Perhaps she could find a balcony where she could break through the doors and infiltrate the building from the inside. She smiled slightly to herself at the thought of managing to reach the elevator.

She was already sitting on the windowsill. She studied the absurd distance between herself and the ground with a cold, analytical expression. Ada had just escaped Raccoon City by braving massive heights with a hookshot, and she had been in almost the exact same physical condition she was in now. She could escape this place too.

The difference was that she had a fever and no hookshot. She lost her balance slightly as her foot found leverage on the ledge. Her hands were sweaty, her vision blurred.

The man didn't seem to like the silence. Perhaps he had looked under the door, or perhaps he had heard some of her movements, she didn't know what had given her away, but she heard him catch his breath in horror.

"Ada!!" he knocked on the door, finally. "Ada, come out of there!! Get back inside, you're badly hurt, you're gonna fall! Please, please, just get back inside!!" He continued to pound on the door.

His actions didn't affect her much, nor did the noise. But the excruciating migraine, the fever, the pain, and the sweat on her hands made her falter as she attempted to step onto the narrow ledge. She was sitting in the window, already almost entirely outside the building, the bright light only causing her to wince further.

"Fuck, tell me you're still in there!" The stranger lost his patience and forced the door open, which only increased her urgency to flee. "Wait, wait, please..." He took slow steps forward and raised his hands, trying to make himself look harmless. "I know what this looks like, but I swear you don't need to do this, okay?" the stranger assured her in a calmer, gentler voice. Ada looked down at the asphalt again. For an instant, she considered it. But Ada Wong didn't have the luxury of instants; her legs grew weak and her hand slipped at the exact same time, causing her to lose her balance.

The man let out a thunderous "No!!!" and practically threw himself the rest of the way.

His arms locked around her waist just before he fell backward, caring little about the thud of his own body against the bathroom floor. Ada gasped and fell splayed across his chest.

He was panting, his eyes shut. Ada couldn't make sense of his desperate reaction, and she certainly didn't intend to stay half-naked in the arms of her potential kidnapper. Yet, she couldn't have pulled away even if she tried. There was simply no strength left in her. Besides, the man's muscular arms were crushing her against him, almost like… a hug? She didn’t do hugs. He might have just saved her life, but who the hell was this man to hold her like that?

"Holy shit…" His head hit the floor with a thud that echoed through the bathroom. "That's another six months of therapy," he muttered, but Ada caught it.

Her eyelids grew heavy as her body finally gave out on her. "Let… me go…"

Surprisingly, he backed off a bit, but kept his hold. "I'm carrying you to the bed."

"No…"

"You’ve turned down my help way too many times already." He stood up effortlessly, as if Ada weighed no more than a bundle of clothes. "Look at you…" He shook his head, a heavy sadness in his voice. "I'm not leaving you like this, Ada."

"Who do you think you are… Let me go, I don't need-" She forced the words out.

"Oh, I can see that," he sighed, his voice slipping into something close to tenderness. "Just sleep. Nothing's going to happen to you. You always find a way out of everything."

The soft comforter welcomed Ada’s broken body like a warm embrace. She melted into it, letting the fever completely take over. Still, she refused to fully drop her guard, forcing her eyes to stay open just a fraction longer.

And up close, she finally saw him.

The man tucking her in, murmuring about her wounds, clothes, and taking care of her—the man who had been strong enough to pull her back from the edge this time.

Despite the signs of aging and the beard… Despite the furrowed brow… Despite the completely different physique… and the lack of a uniform…

"…Leon?" she whispered.

He stopped checking her injuries and froze. Then, his eyes softened.

"Took you long enough, huh?" A faint smile touched his lips. "Go to sleep, Ada. Don't mind good old me."

Hoping that all of this was nothing but a bizarre dream, Ada let go and lost consciousness once more.

Chapter 2: Make yourself at home

The first thing Ada noticed when she woke up again was that it wasn’t as freezing cold as it had been when she fell asleep. Her throat swallowed dry; a terrible, foul taste flooded her mouth, but the discomfort at least helped force her eyelids open.

She looked down at herself. She was dressed in comfortable clothes that, strangely enough, were exactly her size. Every single bandage on her body had been changed, one by one, and her skin felt relatively cleaner, too. A bucket of warm water and a cloth rested beside the bed. Mortification hit her like a train, faster and more intense than any sense of gratitude.

It hadn’t been a dream, which made absolutely no sense.

Perhaps her fractured mind had been playing tricks on her in that instant the man placed her in bed. Perhaps it was just a wishful illusion born from her heavy conscience.

"…You doing okay?"

A chill ran down her spine. Ada looked toward the other side of the bed. At a safe distance, sitting in a chair, there he was. Worry bled from those blue eyes, and his hair was still styled almost the exact same way. But it wasn’t him. It couldn’t be.

Logic was Ada's only ally in any situation; she couldn't afford to abandon it at such a critical moment. She stared at him like a fierce panther about to attack.

The stranger sighed and crossed his arms. "I almost forgot how stubborn you can be. Almost."

Ada blinked a few times. Her mouth opened and closed. She didn't know what expression to make.

This didn't sound like the sweet, naive rookie cop she had met at the start of her mission, but it didn't sound like the resentful, betrayed man who had tried to save her life despite her lies, either.

The man let out a softer breath, his muscles relaxing as a tiny smile tugged at the corner of his mouth.

"You must be incredibly confused. I don't blame you." Slowly, he stood up from the chair and walked around the bed. "I'm not exactly having a normal moment here either."

Ada's heart raced. Mechanically, her features turned cold and expressionless, her posture stiffening. He stopped right in front of her and knelt down. Her fingers flexed against the comforter.

"This injury right here looks like you really forced your leg, even after you’d already hurt it." He looked down at Ada's bandaged knee, his tone bordering on a reprimand.

Ada furrowed her brow. "You took a real close look, didn't you?" she scoffed.

He didn't seem bothered by her tone or her glare. "It was kind of hard not to. It looked pretty bad and deep. If I’d known you were going to mess up your leg this much and that you wouldn't let me carry you back then, I would’ve put a little more effort into making it tighter."

Ada stared at him.

He, Leon, stared right back.

"What? I’m serious. I was in a bit of a rush, but-"

"Stop." Her voice cracked, and she turned her face away.

"-I could have done a better job. The way you bandaged my shoulder was way tighter-"

"I get it, Leon." Ada fixated on staring at the comforter between her trembling fingers.

A shaky sigh escaped her lips. Her headache had every right to start bothering her again after the wave of emotions that had just crashed over her.

Leon tilted his head, trying to bring himself back into the line of sight of her unbandaged, hazel-brown eye. Ada refused to look at him.

How?

How, after everything she had done in name of her job, after lying about absolutely everything to him during their time together, after using him to get the G-Virus, after pointing a gun at him—how was this man, after god knows how many years, kneeling by her wounds all over again, insisting on helping her?

She wasn't going to cry. She refused with everything she had to let tears roll down her cheeks in front of anyone, even if it was the goddamn gentlest man she had ever met.

Silence hung over them for a moment. Leon sighed and gave her less-damaged knee two light taps.

"Look, I don't know how this happened. You're a few years early..." He finally succeeded in catching her line of sight. "And this is only going to work if you talk to me."

"I don't know what happened," she shot back, stubbornly defensive.

"I know. I could tell," he said, trying to crack his neck as if to ease his own tension. "Just don't try to run away again, that's all I ask. We're going to figure this out, alright?" Ada gritted her teeth. She felt like a child being talked into eating less ice cream, which only soured her mood further.

"Fine. I get it," she said, straightening her posture again, forcing any trace of vulnerability out of her demeanor.

Leon didn't look the least bit surprised. His hand moved away from her knee as he stood up. Like the choreography of ocean waves, his approaches and his touches came and went, strangely aware of her emotional barriers and the silent boundaries she had established.

"Since you get it, I also hope you understand that you're going to have to stay here for a while."

"I won't. I can manage on my own," she shot back instantly.

"I know. That doesn't mean I'm going to let you walk around hurt."

"You don't need to let me anything. I-"

"Ada, when we first met, I was twenty-one. Want to guess how old I am now?" His expression didn't change, just as his tone remained completely serene.

Ada knew exactly what he was doing. If, due to some rupture or anomalous flaw in the timeline, she was in the future, it meant she was in uncharted territory. It came down to the fact that she no longer knew the cities like the back of her hand, she no longer had the same employers, maybe the hotels she knew didn't even exist anymore… and the worst of all.

That maybe, she would have to rely on someone.

She had no one like that in her life. It wasn't a melancholy fact; it was convenient and easy to have no attachments to anything or anyone. But right now, in her current state, in the place she found herself, and above all, given who she was dealing with…

"Come on, don't worry about hurting my feelings. How many years do you think have passed? You're going to walk out of here, and then what?" He shook his head. "Stay as long as you need to. I'm not going to lock you up, of course not, but I can't take that risk. I'm staying with you."

Something tender stirred in her chest while something unsettling twisted in her stomach. Leon remained just as unyielding about protecting her as he had always been. Her brows furrowed slightly, echoing his gesture as she shook her head.

"You haven't changed at all," she whispered.

"You think so?" he murmured, his deep voice carrying a lightness that Ada was certain she would never receive from him again.

"Just tell me how much time has passed already."

"About twenty-eight years."

"Oh."

"Yeah."

Ada fought hard not to let on just how completely thrown off she felt. "Alright."

"Alright, huh?"

"Yeah."

Leon turned his face away, trying to suppress a smile. He crossed his arms and whispered something that escaped her hearing. Ada's attention caught on something bright and striking that became visible as he moved.

A wedding ring on his ring finger.

Connecting the dots wasn't difficult. All the pieces fell into place. The room she was in was far too large for just one person, it had a double bed, and the place was impeccably decorated. There was a sublime yet efficient vanity table on the wall next to the bathroom. The bathroom itself had already provided enough evidence: the towels, the makeup, the baskets.

There was a walk-in closet on the wall beside her, too.

The room belonged almost more to her than it did to him.

A bitter, painful feeling washed over her, but it was diligently locked away. "You said I could stay here," she finally looked him in the eye.

"You can. What, are you going to try to pay me rent?"

"Won't your wife mind?" she went straight to the point, in no mood to return the joke.

Leon took a deep breath, a smile threatening to break through. "Uh, of course you noticed, right." He dragged a hand over his mouth, trying to wipe the grin away.

Ada didn't find it the least bit funny. "Won't your wife be angry?"

Leon shook his head and scratched behind his ear. "She’s… a pretty gentle soul, when she wants to be. A very logical woman." He forced himself to stop smiling like a goof. "I'm sure she’ll understand."

"I see." Ada suddenly stood up and took a step forward.

"Where are you going?" Leon stopped himself from trying to help her right away.

"I'm going to take a real shower, since you won't let me leave," she grumbled as she walked past him.

"Oh, good,"

Ada felt the grip of his hand close around her wrist. Before she could react or pull away, she already had one arm slung over his shoulders. One of his hands rested right above her waist.

"I can walk...!"

"Limping isn't walking," he replied, unbothered.

Ada scoffed. "What the hell happened to you over the years? You weren't this bold before."

"You want it in chronological or alphabetical order?" he shot back in the same unimpressed tone, opening the bathroom door.

Ada tried to disentangle herself from him, but the warmth of his body lingered for a moment longer. Leon only let go of her when she was standing right in front of the shower.

"I'll go get a chair for you."

"You absolutely will not, no way. I can stand up. I'm not completely broken."

"Got it. I'll get the chair then."

"Leon, I'm serious," she glared daggers at him.

"Are you gonna try to escape through the window again?"

She gritted her teeth. "No."

"Okay, then no chair," he walked toward the bathroom door.

"What kind of logic is that?" Ada huffed.

"It's not about logic. You handle this better than I do. When it's time to change your bandages, call me."

Ada didn't answer.

"...Get comfortable, make yourself at home." Leon smiled, as if he had told a joke, one that Ada didn't catch, which only made her more irritated at the wording.

The sound of the door closing echoed through the bathroom. Ada felt the tension in her shoulders give way. It wasn't a sense of relief, but rather one of latent exhaustion. She undressed piece by piece. She refused to believe that Leon had dressed her in his wife's clothes. She stared at the top with a certain disdain and regret.

Of course he was married.

Someone like Leon, so caring and genuine, deserved a life like that. A life shared in a large, comfortable house that illustrated welcoming and warmth. He deserved the constant presence of his partner, and maybe he even had children with her. He was worthy of everything he had. Everything Ada could never even dream of providing for him.

She shook her head to banish the thoughts and dropped the clothes into the basket. What did that information matter to her, anyway? Good for him. The bandages fell from Ada's body and snaked down to the floor. He deserved to be happy, and he seemed to be doing very well, as if the trauma of what they had gone through together had healed over.

Ada stepped under the shower and let the water burn her wounds. She closed her left eye and tried to see with her right, but darkness greeted her once more. Her black hair was now soaking wet; she watched the path of the water dripping from it hit the floor.

In her life, there was no room for commitments of that scale. Amidst weapons and disguises, wedding rings and white dresses didn't fit. Certainly, husbands didn't fit, nor did close friends, or even Leon, the only person with a purely good heart who had ever crossed her path.

Maybe that was what they meant to each other: just a crossroads in the middle of the journey. She knew that; she had known it from the moment she laid eyes on him.

So why were those same eyes burning so much now?

She swallowed dry. Her hands worked quickly as she rubbed the body wash over herself. No tears, no red eyes, no wretched sounds of pain, no time wasted.

She stumbled out of the shower; the towels were different from last time. She didn't think twice before grabbing one and drying herself off. She just needed to sleep, to close her eyes and pretend a dream was playing inside her head.

There were fresh bandages and a change of clothes waiting for her on top of the sink, because of course Leon would think of that. Ada’s features faltered, a knot forming in her throat before she resolved it all with a sigh. Her objective was clear: get dressed and get back to bed.

And, once in bed, she immediately set to work re-bandaging her wounds.

"Ada?" Leon knocked on the door twice.

"...It's open."

Ada didn't look at him when she heard his footsteps approaching, simply continued to treat herself with a solemn expression. Leon calmly sat down in front of her and kindly took the bandage from her hands. If she were being honest, she didn't have the strength to fight him anymore.

So softly and gently, his warmth emanated close to her again. With patient, delicate movements, his hands bandaged what was left to be done.

Ada closed her eyes and accepted it, just this once.

It wouldn't happen again.

“Did you close this yourself?”

Something back there that changes everything, he said. Ada’s thoughts raced over one another, each arriving with a possibility more inconsistent and doubtful than the last within a matter of seconds.

“My vision isn't what it used to be.”

During the night—while Leon was still sleeping in the guest room after a lot of stubbornness—she gathered the clothes she had already worn and looked for a washing machine inside the house. The first ones were stained with blood, the second pair she had worn until she couldn't anymore, and now the ones on her body. What kind of cop was that?

"...In the middle of the night.”

Ada Wong, in all her status as a spy and reputation as a mercenary… sitting beside a washing machine and a dryer, sipping tea and eating crackers next to Leon S. Kennedy, who leaned against the wall watching the rain fall through the window, devouring more than half of the package by himself, but taking his time to drink his tea. “Sounds good.”

“But what if I wanted to go to the mountains? Some of your requirements would be violated.”

“Then I’d go with you anyway.”

“So we’ll go to the beach.”

“Because I’ve been good?”

Ada rolled her eyes, but there was amusement on her face. “Enough.”

“And do you have to carry me in your arms?”

“You agreed to that earlier. I’m trying to be romantic. Can’t keep up?”

“Hmph, carry me then. I think you’re just showing off now that you’re healed and strong again.”

“What do you mean, ‘again’? When was I ever weak?”

She tried to resist, but Leon caught the hint of a curve on her lips. It was true—he’d do anything to make her smile.

X

At some point during the night, the cold set in. They were forced to get dressed before curling up in bed again. Amid whispered conversations, naps, and a few more tender touches, morning arrived. Leon was the first to wake up; he was surprised when he saw the sunbeams streaming through the curtains. He tried to follow Ada’s lead and go back to sleep, but gave up. With great effort, he slipped away from Ada without waking her, which was almost impossible, but he kept trying. If she hadn’t woken up, she must have been truly exhausted.

He automatically made his way to the kitchen and brewed coffee for them. He could figure out what to make for breakfast once Ada was fully awake, or perhaps after a hot shower.

Once the coffee was ready, Leon grabbed the cups and walked to the bedroom. The backs of his fingers burned against the hot ceramic, but he was glad that at least he could feel his fingers, unlike the harsh numbness the Syndrome had caused.

He managed to open the door even with his hands full and looked at the bed, where Ada should have been curled up in the sheets.

But she wasn’t sleeping. Nor was she wearing comfortable clothes. She didn’t even have the same haircut. But she was wearing makeup, and she was dirty, and…

He must have been thinking too much about the past and was now seeing things. Except that, no, his mind couldn’t have conjured up the authentic, precise image of his wife exactly as he’d first met her, covered more in bandages than in clothes.

Ada, looking younger, broken, bloodied, and as if she’d walked halfway around the world. There were remnants of her red dress and black tights clinging to her body, but very little. The bandages were numerous, in several distinct places, which meant there had been several separate injuries.

His stomach sank. He couldn’t even react, or move any of his damn frozen legs. Ada stared at him with the fury, terror, and confusion of a caged animal, and that only made him feel worse. Before he could say a single word, she ran off at a speed that doctors would probably not recommend for someone in her condition.

“Hey—wait a minute!” he raised a hand, almost dropping the mug, but it was too late.

The bathroom door slammed shut with a loud thud. Leon could only stare at the surface in astonishment.

What the hell was going on?!

He shook his head and set the cups aside. He didn’t know what to do, but hesitantly walked over to the door.

He tried to reason with her as best he could, as much as his stunned state would allow. His mind raced: how was he supposed to deal with the most distant possible version of Ada? He tried to consider the possibility that it was her in the same body, but… with a younger appearance? But no, the injuries proved otherwise. And she was afraid of him, which proved that she didn’t remember anything.

And it was too quiet on the other side of the door. Leon licked his lips and tried to listen carefully.

A draft. She had opened the window on the top floor of a building.

“Ada!!” He knocked on the door. “Ada, come out of there!! Get back inside,you’re badly hurt, you’re going to fall! Please, please, just get back inside!!” He continued to pound on the door.

He forced the door open and tried to talk to her, but Ada wasn’t in any condition to make that effort. When she slipped, Leon’s heart raced as if he were about to lose all the years of his own life since he’d first met her.

He lunged toward the window, raised his arms, and miraculously pulled his wife away from certain death—this time. The pain of falling backward onto the floor barely registered; all his muscles relaxed except for his trembling arms, which held Ada tightly against him.

“Holy shit…” His head hit the floor with a thud that echoed through the bathroom. “That’s another six months of therapy.”

Despite her protests, Leon carried her to the bed. Her skin was feverish; she seemed willing to fight back but lacked the strength to do so. He covered her up to her stomach with the sheet and began to examine her numerous wounds and bruises.

“Damn… what happened? There isn’t a single part of you that isn’t injured,” he couldn’t help but mutter. For a moment, he didn’t know what to do. He had to change her bandages, clean her up, get her dressed…

“…Leon?” she suggested in a faint whisper. Leon stared at her blankly for a few seconds. Oh, it was true. She didn’t even know that this strange man was someone she’d met before. But if this version of Ada had already met him… It could only mean that these injuries came from…

“Took you long enough, huh?” he forced a faint smile to touch his lips. “Go to sleep, Ada. Don’t mind good old me.”

As her eyelids drooped, her defensive posture also crumbled. Leon’s gaze swept over the bandages; some of them were undeniable evidence. One wrapped around her leg—the one he himself had wrapped there while avoiding looking at her thigh so many years ago. One around the shoulder that Annette Birkin had shot right before his eyes, leaving him so distraught and furious. One around her head, likely from self-treatment after that unforgettable scene of Ada hitting the ground and lying there motionless while a pool of blood gathered around her.

“...” A shaky breath escaped his lips. He stood up and quickly grabbed more bandages, a bucket of water, and a cloth.

One by one, the soiled bandages were removed from Ada’s warm skin. Drawing on everything he’d learned about cleaning and treating wounds over the years, Leon combined care with expertise with each bandage change, with every wipe of the cloth across the blood.

Once everything had been properly sorted out and checked, Leon grabbed a chair and placed it on the other side of the bed, right next to the spot on the mattress where Ada-his-wife usually slept, but there was a cruel empty space there. In this young Ada right here in front of him, there was no trace of makeup left; the dark circles under her eyes highlighted them rather than the shadow, and her serene, exhausted face was pale.

The pieces fell into place in his mind, and he began to swing his leg. If two plus two equaled four, and if the young Ada who had been in Raccoon City was in that room, and the current Ada had vanished…

Leon ran his hands over his face. It was his fault; he shouldn’t have spent so much time dwelling on the past in such a short span of time. Or maybe he was just starting to get in a sour mood simply from imagining his wife alone during a time when she had no one to confide in.

Everything always went to hell when he traveled—or even just planned to travel. He should have been suspicious of the calm.

X

Hours passed. Leon remained seated there, lost in thought. It wasn’t going to kill him, but it certainly wasn’t making him any calmer either. At some point, he got up and went to the kitchen, grabbed some peanuts to munch on, and surveyed his home.

Travel photos? Gone. One by one, the photos and travel picture frames were all in his hands. He wandered from the living room to the guest room and shoved everything into the first drawer he saw. Inside the guest room itself, next to the bed, there was a photo of him and Ada, a little more recognizable than the others. It didn’t take him long to shove it face-down into the bottom drawer.

In that room, he would do everything in his power to keep Ada out. Everything else in the house that might hint to Ada that they were married—with the sole exception of what was in their bedroom—Leon hid it all, just as if he were sweeping dirt under the rug.

Ada was way too smart; she’d definitely find out if he left any trace behind, and if she did… Leon didn’t know what would happen, or how she’d react. And God knew that was one of the last things Leon would ever sacrifice in his life.

The chair next to the bed held him once again; it felt more like a waiting punishment until she woke up. His leg bounced. He pulled his cell phone out of his pocket and unlocked it. The home screen was a photo of the two of them together; Ada’s face was much more visible than his.

“…I might as well.” Reluctantly, he changed the wallpaper; Ada’s beautiful face was replaced by a dull, pretentious photo with flowers and confusing blue lights.

His eyes grew heavy; he was sure he’d dozed off a few times, but he could barely remember it. As his mind wandered somewhere between “What if Ada’s condition suddenly worsens, or maybe she tries to run away again and gets hurt, and I’m not here? I can’t get up from this chair,” and “I’m wasting time; I need to find a way to locate Ada,” and “What a pain, thinking about both of them with the same name is so confusing,” the so-called patient woke up.

She sized herself up first, and Leon knew it would be hard to convince her, but not impossible. Even though she was looking at him like a fierce stray cat with three injured paws and years of abuse weighing on its shoulders. They exchanged heartfelt words; Leon tried not to lie to her.

“Just tell me how much time has passed already,” she demanded. Oh, how nostalgic that tone was.

“About twenty-eight years,” he replied, trying not to sound presumptuous.

Her eyes widened slightly. “Oh.”

“Yeah.”

She tried to hide her expression, but he’d already seen it. “Alright.”

“Alright, huh?”

“Yeah.”

Leon turned his face away, trying to suppress a smile. “Always so sure of yourself, it’s almost cute.”

He cursed his own tongue immediately when she didn’t respond. Had she heard him? If so, maybe she’d try to push him away. Or, in the worst-case scenario, maybe she’d piece everything together and reach the one conclusion Leon feared. Her analytical eyes fixed on his wedding ring, which only made him more tense. Damn it, how could he have forgotten to take that off?

“You said I could stay here,” she finally looked him in the eye.

“You can. What, are you going to try to pay me rent?”

“Won't your wife mind?”

Leon stared at her. He blinked once, twice; the corners of his lips trembled. A “Tell me, does this bother you?” and other responses got stuck in his throat.

Instead, he took a deep breath. “Uh, of course you noticed, right.” He ran a hand over his mouth, trying to wipe the grin away.

“Won’t your wife be angry?” she retorted.

Leon shook his head and scratched behind his ear. “She’s… a pretty gentle soul, when she wants to be. A very logical woman.” He forced himself to stop smiling like a goof. “I’m sure she’ll understand.”

Out of Ada’s sight after leaving her in the bathroom to take a shower, Leon headed to the kitchen. The sun was rising again in the sky, which explained just how exhausted he was. Ada had been unconscious for an entire day and night. He needed to make something for her to eat—and maybe for himself, too. He’d asked her to call out for him, but he knew she wouldn’t respond. Perhaps the time it took him to prepare something was the time it would take her to bathe.

He was proud of himself. He’d let the perfect joke slip out and managed not to laugh at the situation. But given the circumstances, he figured he’d done no more than the bare minimum. Not-so-secretly, he was afraid of how this timeline thing worked.

He stopped dwelling on it. As expected, Ada hadn’t called for him as he’d asked. He set the sandwiches on the table and instinctively headed to the bedroom. Ada was lying on the bed, stubbornly bandaging her own wounds by herself. Leon took over the rest of the process, though he hadn’t expected her to fall asleep in the middle of it.

Leon gently laid her down, covered her with the sheet, and left the room. Ada already seemed more stable, even though she hadn’t eaten anything. He made a mental note to get her to eat as soon as she woke up, but for now, Leon had to do something—anything—to try to find his wife.

He left the two sandwiches on the table and slipped into the “guest room.” Truth be told, Leon and Ada didn’t even have anyone to host. The room was one of the reasons they bought and renovated the place. It was perfect for storing all their equipment, spacious enough and located at the very back of the house, it was ideal and convenient for moving the walls around.

At the end of what was now the room’s hallway, hidden beneath the weapons, were the high- tech computers—one for each of them. Leon grabbed the best of the computers and got to work. He took the ring off his finger and connected its frequencies to the computer.

Of course, since Leon and Ada each had risky jobs that involved dealing with dangerous people, going around making it obvious that they were important to each other wasn’t exactly ideal, he admitted. Except that the wedding ring wasn’t just an explicit symbol for the world to see, but a way for them to find each other if anything happened.

Ada was probably already sick of hearing Leon’s jokes about how possessive that might seem. And, once again proving itself useful, the ring was his only hope of finding any trace of Ada.

Or maybe not so useful after all, because Leon had already been staring at the computer screen for hours. How could he track something that hadn’t existed twenty-eight years ago? Was the ring even still on her finger?

As customary, Leon was treating her as if she were, well, Ada. But when she pointed out that she was 1. Wearing his wife’s clothes and 2. Sleeping in the master bedroom, he didn’t have the nerve to even try to argue with her. Clothes? Sure, he could handle that. Now, as for the bedroom… no. Absolutely not.

That evening, right after dinner, he made his way to the guest room as usual. As expected, Ada knocked on the door not long after. He opened the door just a crack.

“What?”

“Don’t ‘what’ me, move,” she tried to force her way in.

“No, wait.” Leon gently let her open the door, but didn’t step out of the way. “You’re already in the other room; you can stay there. It’s no problem.”

“Are you serious? It’s no problem for you, but it is for me.”

“Why? Is it uncomfortable in there?” he played the concerned host.

Ada wasn’t buying his act. “You bet it is. Get out.”

“Ada, my room is closer to the kitchen; it’s too far for you to walk all the way over here.”

She crossed her arms. “Far? You’re just making excuses.”

He was. “No, I’m not. You can sleep there, seriously. I sometimes sleep in this room anyway. It’s no sacrifice.”

Ada’s expression changed; it was practically visible how she processed that information as a piece of fact. “Why do you sometimes sleep there? Do you two fight?”

Leon opened and closed his mouth. “… maybe.”

That was a lie. Yes, like many couples, Leon and Ada argued from time to time, but nothing serious enough to warrant sleeping in separate rooms—they’d already fallen asleep without each other far too many times in their lives. And even if it were a nasty fight, he wouldn’t sacrifice sleeping next to her. Leon only spent the night in the guest room for its technological purpose, when it was unavoidable to bring work home.

He wasn’t sure if it had been a good idea to confirm that. Maybe it would give the younger Ada some ideas, and in the future, she’d kick him out of the bedroom whenever they fought. The thought was as ridiculous as it was depressing. A truly good night’s sleep only came when he was crushing her or being crushed by her, with no other option.

“I’m not asking about your marital problems,” Ada retorted, her voice low and steady. “I want to sleep, and you’re going to do the same over there in your bed.”

“I’m already going to fulfill half of the demands you made earlier, I’m going to buy your clothes,” he decided to appeal. “Can’t I stay here? Are you going to kick me out? What a demanding guest.”

Ada stared at him, looking as if she’d been caught off guard. Leon knew that look; he’d won. “…You’re very stubborn.”

“Thanks, good night.” He tried to close the door before she changed her mind, but Ada put her foot in the way to stop it from closing. Leon saw this and stopped; the door didn’t even brush against her foot, he was still afraid of hurting her.

“Don’t think I’ve given up. Tomorrow I’m sleeping there.” She pointed her finger at his face.

“I get it. Do you want help getting to your room?” he offered, hoping it would shoo her away —which worked. Ada, still limping a little, turned her back on him and went to sleep.

Or so he thought. In the early hours of the morning, while Leon stared at the “Unable to connect” message on his computer screen as if it were to blame for his back pain, he heard a noise in the distance. He frowned, shut down the computer, and stood up.

He turned off armory mode and headed toward the noise. His eyes were aching from exhaustion after staring so long at his “progress” in breaking through temporal barriers on screens; his vision was blurry, but the image of Ada standing next to the washing machine wasn’t his vision playing tricks on him.

Since he hadn’t convinced her to go back to sleep, he went to make some tea. Staring at the kettle, his mind finally processed the situation. He chuckled to himself and shook his head. Ada was so stubborn, insisting on being independent to the very end. But doing laundry in the middle of the night? That was a first. He had to tell the present-day Ada about this when she got back—it would be pure entertainment for him.

He exchanged a few words with Ada. The rain was falling heavily outside the window.

“I could have done the laundry, you know? I’m a very helpful host.”

“And I’m an even more helpful guest,” she replied without looking at him.

Leon shook his head, still watching the rain through the fogged-up window. Ada studied him from head to toe.

“You’re such a light sleeper,” she remarked, but her tone was laced with curiosity. Ha, how bold of her to assume he was sleeping at all.

“Do you usually stay up until dawn?” He finally looked at her again as he retorted in the same tone.

“When necessary.”

“It sure seems very necessary.”

“You can go back to sleep if you're bothered.”

“But I’m already here, so I’ll help put things away.” Leon crossed his arms and looked down at his feet. “You really should get more rest. You exerted yourself a lot that night. How are you supposed to get better like this?”

She straightened up, rolling her eyes. “I have no problem with physical exertion. And, for your information, I recover really fast—faster than this. She pointed vaguely at herself.

Leon made a sound of agreement. “I can imagine. Practice makes you get used to it,” he muttered.

Her gaze grew more intense as she looked at him. It was as if he could physically feel Ada trying to dissect every layer of his behavior.

“…That doesn’t seem to bother you anymore.”

“Hm?”

“Never mind.”

“You can’t start a sentence and then drop it. I’m curious.”

“Don’t you feel like it’s wrong? Doing what you’re doing for me.”

It took Leon a moment to understand. His brain was sluggish from sleep deprivation. He tried to piece together what they were talking about. Oh, she was referring to her work, probably. About seventeen years ago? Yeah, maybe it did bother him, a little. But Ada was like a part of him that he couldn’t let go of, and he’d made peace with that.

“Why would I feel that way? It would be wrong if I could do something and didn’t,” he replied honestly.

“We’re even, Ada. Just know that.”

It had been a long time since Leon had felt such an urge to laugh. Damn, he did a terrible job of hiding it! But he could hardly blame himself. Seeing Ada try to annoy him with the clothes shopping was also unexpected. He couldn’t say for sure why, but he knew something was bothering her enough to make her try to elicit a reaction from Leon. And seeing that defiant, sullen face was just too much. Her reaction when she didn’t get what she wanted? Priceless. Worth more than ten paychecks.

He had to tell Ada about that once he managed to bring her back!

After many years of marriage, he and Ada used to eat out wherever and whenever they felt like it, but not always at expensive restaurants. For example, at that very moment, Leon was very happy with his steak and mashed potatoes, but what wouldn’t he give for a huge hamburger? Still, he decided—almost unconsciously—to treat Ada as best he could. Her reactions were a bit angry, but she shouldn’t be that bothered, right?

In any case, he had given up on the idea of trying to figure this out on his own. He’d tried, really tried, but made very little progress. He’d learned to handle any situation with tactical calm, but a temporal loop that could cause irreversible damage to the person he loved most wasn’t exactly just another Thursday.

“Stay here, I’ll be right back.” He stood up from his chair with his cell phone in hand.

“Where else would I go…” Ada muttered, her eyes fixed on her plate and a small, pensive pout on her lips. Classic.

Leon smiled and nodded before walking away. He dialed the number and waited, hoping for the mercy of an answer.

“Leon?”

“Sorry to call during lunch, and while you’re on leave.”

“You’re not bothering me, Jake’s still finishing up the food. He decided to take a chance and start cooking different things. Can you believe it?” he heard a noise, probably Sherry settling in more comfortably on the couch.

Leon simply dropped Ada off at home before heading out to meet them. He didn’t bother locking up or hiding anything else; he was sure Ada would search the house. Two knocks on the door sounded as she finished her sentence; all of them looked toward the door.

“Yeah, you’re right. Sorry, we must be making the situation worse.” Sherry straightened up and rested her hands on her lap.

“You’re right, Hunnigan.” Helena nodded and resumed a solemn expression.

“...” Leon bit his cheek. “I’m going to need you guys not to ask any questions again.” Without changing his expression or showing a single trace of embarrassment, Leon slipped the wedding ring off his finger and handed it to them.

“I can imagine,” Sherry nodded emphatically.

“Whoa,” Hunnigan stopped him, managing to elicit a confused look from him. “We’re still going to need that. You’ll have to leave it here.”

Caught red-handed. She never missed an opportunity to tease him.

He picked up the remote to turn off the TV. His eyes narrowed when he actually looked at the screen. The movie was paused, nowhere near halfway through.

“...” He slowly turned off the TV. His features stiffened, and he swallowed hard.
