Many fans after Requiem: "Ada could never be Leon's wife, it would be so out of character for both of them." "The Government wouldn't allow it." "Ada is just a criminal." "Ada is not wife material." "Leon can't marry her." Me: Hold my beer. I got this.

What happens between the cutscenes, the bio-crises, and the blank pages of history? Spanning from 2003 to 2026, this story uncovers the invisible threads binding Leon and Ada together during the years the games left in the dark. This journey explores not only their stolen moments, but the heavy consequences of their feelings for one another, all while unearthing the dark truths behind Ada’s origins. What sacrifices are they willing to make when everything is on the line? Can two separate, fractured worlds ever truly become one? A character-driven epic that stays as close to the official canon as possible, filling the gaps with the raw, uncompromising drama of two survivors. While carefully honoring the established lore, it takes minor, necessary detours to unearth the stories that were never meant to be told.

Chapter 1: An Agent is Born

February 18, 2003

The windows here at the training facility never open. Sometimes, Leon would find himself staring at them, realizing the air was always exactly the same: filtered, sterile, utterly impersonal. There were no times of day here. Time wasn’t measured by sunrise or sunset, but by never-ending drills and bureaucratic reports.

Leon had learned the rules quickly: you don’t ask unnecessary questions. And most importantly, nobody cares about the past.

But letting go was hard for him. He just wasn't built for it.

The images of Raccoon City refused to fade. Not the way the psychologists or his superiors said they would. It didn’t get "easier with time." Instead, things had just… rearranged themselves in his head. Certain details had worn away, while others became terrifyingly sharp.

And there was one face stubbornly burned into his mind.

Ada Wong.

At first, he didn’t even try to explain to himself why he thought about her so much. Too much had happened there, in too short a time. When you're trying to survive, you latch onto anything you can—it’s only natural. At least, that was how he comforted himself.

During the first few weeks, it was just pure instinct. Whenever he gained access to a new database, whenever a classified file crossed his desk, or a list of names scrolled down his monitor, his eyes would automatically search for her. Not openly. Not conspicuously. It was just there, in the corner of his eye, like a constant, silent command:

Ada Wong.

But there was never a match. Or what did come up was too old, too incomplete, or completely irrelevant. It was as if she had never even existed in reality.

That was what infuriated him the most. Because he had seen her. He had heard her voice. He had touched her skin. She couldn’t just be nothing.

As the months bled into one another, the search stopped being an instinct and became a conscious choice. He learned how to bypass different systems, which intelligence reports were worth digging into, and where information managed to slip through the strict filters. He spent nights sitting in front of the monitor, the blue glow of the screen casting sharp angles across his face in the dark, silent room.

Images, reports, names. And a whole lot of nothing.

Sometimes he would stop. His hands would hover helplessly over the keyboard as a question pierced through him: What if she really is dead?

The thought wasn’t new, he just hadn’t allowed it to get close enough until now. After all, he had seen her fall. He had heard the laboratory explode. And yet… there was something about the puzzle that didn't fit. Ada wasn't the kind of woman who would just let herself die. But facts are stubborn things, and according to official records, she didn't exist.

One evening, months later, he finally called off the search. It wasn’t a grand gesture, and there was no dramatic flair to the moment. He was just… tired.

Another empty results list blinked on the screen, the cursor flashing impatiently in the corner. He leaned back in his chair and just stared into the darkness for a long time. Then he closed his eyes, and the toxic thoughts crept in.

What if…? What if she hadn’t fallen? What if none of it had ever happened? What if I had stayed with her?

They were dangerous thoughts, and he knew it. Because there were no answers to them, only the image of a world that never came to be. Slowly, he exhaled, leaned forward, and closed the search window.

"That’s it, then," he said softly into the silence, mostly to himself.

He didn’t make a rigid declaration; he didn’t make any promises. He just… let it go. At least, in that exact moment, it felt that way.

Time moved on. Mission after mission. He learned how to shut off his brain and stop thinking. How to act, how to survive. And somewhere along the way, he changed too. The look in his eyes grew harder, his movements more precise, his decisions much sharper.

The past retreated into the background.

Then one day, after a long, brutally exhausting mission, he returned to headquarters. The office noise was standard: the monotonous clatter of keyboards, hushed conversations, ringing phones. A sort of constant white noise that you stop noticing after a while.

He was right in the middle of typing his report when a single sentence suddenly cut through the background noise from behind him.

"I'm telling you, that woman isn't wrapped tight."

His hands froze on the keyboard for a fraction of a second. He didn’t look up. He didn’t move.

"She slipped right through our fingers again yesterday," his colleague continued nearby. "It's like she knew exactly where we’d be beforehand."

"Who is she?" the other asked.

"We have no idea. No name. No proper profile. The only thing for sure is that she’s after bio- weapons. And she always works alone."

Leon’s heart hammered in his throat, but he slowly, mechanically resumed typing, acting as if nothing had happened.

"Did you at least catch a glimpse of her?"

"Just for a second. Black hair. A red dress. Then she was gone."

It was a simple sentence. But everything inside Leon tightened. On the screen, the words blurred before his eyes.

A red dress.

He took a deep breath and swallowed the thought down. No. It doesn't mean anything. The world is full of black-haired women. The world is full of mercenaries. The world is full of damn coincidences.

He stayed late that night. His report had been finished hours ago, the office had cleared out, and the lights had been dimmed. Yet, he still sat there. Doing nothing, just thinking.

It couldn’t be her. And yet… the description was too accurate. Not even because of the physical traits, but because of her methodology. She works alone. Always one step ahead. Leaves no trace behind.

She was exactly like…

Leon let out a soft breath and rubbed his temples.

"Stop it," he warned himself.

What would change anyway? If it was her, she wasn't the same woman he had met back then. And if it wasn't, he was just chasing a ghost. The whole thing was pointless.

The next day, he forced himself to stay away from the terminal. He didn’t look her up for days. He went on more missions, trying to block it out of his head. He didn’t ask anyone, didn’t investigate. He just did his job, like always.

But one evening, when everyone else had truly gone home, he sat back down in front of the computer anyway.

His security clearance was on an entirely different level now compared to the old days. He knew exactly where to look and how to bypass the restrictions. He didn’t hesitate. A few quick commands, a security code, an illegal detour through the internal network, and the highly classified files materialized on the screen.

He paused for a second, his hands resting on the keyboard. He made a deal with himself:

This is the last time.

Then he started typing. This time, he didn’t search by name. He searched by description. By operational methods. By profiling patterns.

And the system answered.

He didn’t get a full profile, nor a name. Just fragments. Observations, excerpts from military reports, half-sentences:

"Unknown female operative.""High-level combat and infiltration capabilities.""Bio-weapon related targets.""Frequent red attire.""Identification: Failed."

He read through the whole thing slowly. Once. Then once more.

There was no photo attached. Her name wasn't there. He had no tangible proof in his hands.

And yet, he knew instantly, without a shadow of a doubt. Not from logic, not from the data. But from that visceral, gut-wrenching feeling he had carried inside him for four years—the one he was utterly powerless to explain.

"It's you..." he whispered to the monitor.

His voice sounded strange, foreign in the echoing silence. As if speaking it aloud were a sin.

He felt no relief. He felt no joy. Only a crushing, heavy physical pressure building in his chest.

She’s alive.

That was his first clear thought.

The second one was far more complicated, but the core of it remained the same: she hadn’t asked for help, she hadn't looked for him, she hadn't left him any kind of trail. She simply existed in her own dark world.

He leaned back in his chair and stared at the ceiling.

He had found her.

And yet, he had found nothing. He wasn’t any closer to her; he didn’t understand her secrets any better. He just knew she was out there somewhere in the world. And that… was simultaneously infinitely comforting and terribly unnerving.

On the screen, the cursor was still flashing impatiently. Slowly, he turned back around and closed the file with a single keystroke. He didn’t save anything; he left no digital footprint. It was as if he had never been there.

But deep down, nothing was the same anymore. Because now, he wasn’t searching for an absence in the dark. He was searching for a very real, flesh-and-blood shadow.

And he knew—he knew with absolute certainty—that sooner or later, they would cross paths again. Not because he would go looking for her. But because their fates, their very natures, simply gravitated toward one another.

A faint smile tugged at his lips in the dark office. Tired. Resigned.

And somewhere deep, deep down… waiting.

Chapter 2: The Red Spy

November 5, 2003

Ada pressed her back tightly against the cool concrete pillar. She didn’t peek out into the room; it would have been an unnecessary risk. Instead, she relied on her hearing, observing the three agents from the furthest, darkest corner of the room. She even held her breath, making sure not even the faintest rustle would give her away. She didn’t recognize two of the agents from their profiles, but the third… she would have known his voice out of a thousand.

"Are you sure there's nothing here?" Leon asked the partner standing beside him.

"Absolutely nothing..." the other grunted in disappointment. "I'm telling you, someone played us!"

Leon and the third agent fixed their eyes on the speaker.

"Care to elaborate?" Leon’s voice was deeper than Ada remembered. More soldierly. More assertive.

Before his partner could reply, Leon studied the monitor for a few more long seconds. The way he concentrated was a familiar motion.

"I think the intel was bad. Someone brought us here on purpose."

His partners, of course, were skeptical. They didn’t see the whole picture. They couldn’t.

"Why?" the other agent asked. "Wouldn't they have ambushed us when we got here then?"

Instead of the operative who was still scanning the monitor, Leon answered. He saw the flaw in the logic perfectly.

"Not if we aren't the target."

"What are you getting at, Kennedy?"

She smiled in the dark. Smart boy. She knew Leon was trying to think realistically, but in this twisted world, reality rarely helps. As government agents, they weren't used to being sent to tentative, uncertain research sites. Most of the time, they were given concrete, already identified targets. This current operation was an anomaly.

"Maybe they just wanted us here, and not somewhere else," Leon said.

A heavy silence settled over the room for a few seconds. Finally, one of the agents broke the quiet, complaining loudly.

"Well, I think someone just yanked our chain. Even tactical training would've been better than this."

His partner snorted bitterly beside him.

"You said it, brother. Come on, let's head back to base. We aren't escaping the paperwork either way..."

She heard their footsteps as they headed toward the exit, but the clicking of Leon’s boots lagged behind. He didn’t move.

"I'm going to run through the system one more time," he called out after the others. "Go on ahead."

Ada exhaled in relief. That was exactly what she had hoped for. Or, if she wanted to be honest with herself… she knew exactly that he would do that. She knew him.

"There's nothing there, brother," the others urged from a distance. "If there was something, I would've found it."

"Still, it won't hurt to give it one last look."

She heard their muffled sighs.

"Kennedy never gives up. See you at the helipad."

The sound of footsteps slowly faded down the corridor. Leon was left alone. Ada heard him step closer to the terminal.

"Come on, what are you hiding?" he whispered to the monitor, his voice echoing softly in the room. "Why are we here?"

Once Ada was certain that Leon's partners were far enough away, she carefully, inch by inch, peered out from behind the concrete pillar. Leon stood half-turned away from her, the blue glow of the monitor illuminating his profile. She could see his face clearly.

She knew he wouldn't find anything on that computer. The entire server, the whole trail that led them here… was just bait. Her bait.

She rarely resorted to such convoluted, amateur methods just to catch a glimpse of someone. In fact, if she were to be entirely honest, she had never done anything like this in her life. It was out of character, and it was dangerous.

But this situation was different. Five years. Five damn years had passed since Raccoon City. She hadn’t seen him since that hellish night.

Slowly, methodically, she sized him up from the shadows, like a scanner trying to absorb every last scrap of information about the target. He had changed. He had filled out. His posture, his shoulders… he was no longer the rookie cop she had left behind in the collapsing laboratory.

So, you really did become a government agent, Leon. She thought.

For a single fraction of a second, she wavered. The urge to step out into the light and walk up to him was so strong. Her leg almost moved, but her common sense immediately yanked her back to reality.

Come on, Ada, what would you even say to him? There’s no point in letting him know you’re alive.

She had no idea that Leon had already found her shadow in the Pentagon's classified files long ago. She thought he still believed she was dead. She thought it was safer for both of them this way.

Come on, Ada. Get out of here. Leave while you still can.

Yet she stayed. For another long, heavy minute, she just watched him from the gloom. She tried to rationally analyze her own reactions, to understand why she cared so much about this man. Why she cared… at all?

She knew many men in her line of work. Politicians, mercenaries, CEOs. But she never let anyone get close to her. She trusted no one. She just deceived and manipulated them as part of her job. And they never gave her sleepless nights; they were never difficult to handle.

But Leon… he was in an entirely different category. Around him, she felt something she had never felt with anyone else before: She regretted deceiving him. And she didn’t understand why.

She continued to study his form in the dark. Like someone hoping the answer was written on him somewhere. But it wasn't.

She shook her head to clear this useless sentimentality from her thoughts. Stupid… She thought to herself.

She wanted to slip away, to leave the whole thing behind before she got completely flustered. She was just about to turn around when Leon suddenly lifted his eyes from the monitor. He snapped his head up like someone who could feel, with absolute certainty, that someone was watching him from the dark.

Ada slipped back into cover instantly, completely soundlessly. She heard the heel of Leon’s boot click on the concrete. He looked exactly where Ada had been standing just a moment ago.

Don't come over here, Kennedy.

But in the next second, his footsteps made it clear: he was heading toward her. There was no time to deliberate. Ada made her decision in a single heartbeat and, cat-like, slipped soundlessly into another of the room's covers, shrouded in deeper darkness.

From this new vantage point, she watched Leon look behind the pillar. Right where Ada had stood seconds before. She saw his tense, suspicious face as he looked around in the gloom, his hand resting on the grip of his gun. But the darkness hid her well. After a few tense moments, he shook his head, his suspicion replaced by disappointment, and walked back to the machine.

"Looks like it really is nothing," he said aloud, as if trying to convince himself.

He pulled the data drive from the terminal, pocketed it, and walked with purposeful strides after his partners.

Ada didn’t move from the dark. She stood there motionless until the sound of the helicopter, revving up in the distance and then slowly fading away completely, confirmed it: they were gone. Leon was gone.

Only then did she step into the light, right where Leon had stood moments before, and the muggy, empty room suddenly felt even colder than it had before.

Chapter 3: The Silent of the Sea

May 27, 2005

Out on the open sea, the night silence carries an entirely different weight. It’s not like on land —it is deeper, darker, almost infinite. Standing on the deck, Ada watched the moonlight cut a silver ribbon across the water. The gently swaying sailboat felt as though it were drifting at the very edge of the world, utterly isolated from everything.

She stared at the silver ripples on the water and waited. She had been waiting for far too long.

Maybe he won't come, the thought slipped through her mind, and in truth, she couldn't even blame him if he didn't.

She despised lacking action, hated when excitement was missing from her life. In moments like this, during times of stillness, the silence became too loud. Not around her, but inside her own head. And that was when the thoughts she preferred to bury deep always crawled back to the surface.

Thoughts like Leon S. Kennedy.

She was almost furious with herself for caring this much. Yet, it wasn't Leon's fault. Ada had left "accidental" breadcrumbs behind, banking on Leon’s famous agent instincts.

She shook her head in annoyance and inhaled the cool sea air. Why am I doing this? Why don't I just let it go?

But she knew the answer all too well. Something invisible, an irrational magnet, had drawn her to Leon from the very first moment.

Alright. There’s nothing deeper to it, she tried to reassure herself, slipping on her usual cynical mask. You just desired him, plain and simple. It’s just your body playing games, Ada, don't make a big deal out of it. Even you are allowed a little pleasant distraction from time to time.

Yet no matter how hard she tried to force her feelings into a rational box, even her own internal voice rang hollow.

It was already past midnight. Ada thought it best to concede defeat and accept that Leon wasn't coming. Perhaps it was better this way. This whole idea was a massive mistake. She turned to head down to the cabin, but just then, cutting through the soft whisper of the sea, the wind carried another sound from the distance.

The low rumble of a motorboat.

An involuntary, triumphant smile flashed across Ada's face. So she had been right after all. I know you, Kennedy.

Leon’s motorboat cut its engine softly, almost imperceptibly, alongside the sailboat. Ada watched him remain behind the wheel for a moment, tensely scanning the deck, wary of a trap.

Finally, Leon climbed aboard. For a while, neither of them said a word. They just stood there in the dark, the only sounds on the sea being the soft clatter of the rigging and the lapping of the water against the hull.

Ultimately, Ada broke the silence. Her voice was like silk. "I wasn't sure you'd show."

Leon slowly took in Ada's silhouette. Every fiber of his being was taut. "I might still regret it."

Ada offered a faint smile, then turned toward the water. It seemed as though she suddenly couldn't bear to look into Leon’s eyes.

"Why am I here, Ada?" Leon asked, stepping closer to her. He wanted to know the rules of engagement.

"Because you're a good tracker."

Leon wasn't in the mood for flirting. Not tonight. "Don't play games. You wanted me to find you."

"That is a slanderous accusation," Ada purred playfully, with that signature, nerve-wracking confidence of hers.

Leon stood his ground. "Don't waste my time."

Ada let out an barely perceptible, heavy sigh. Leon didn't mind that she was starting to realize he was no longer the naive, wet-behind-the-ears rookie she could pull by the strings at her whim in Raccoon City. He wasn't so easy to get past anymore.

"Spain..." Ada began, her voice suddenly dropping into a low, sultry register. "You said that after six years, you expected a different kind of greeting than a gun to your back. Perhaps now… you can have it."

She smiled at Leon beguilingly. He looked away toward the water, offering no reply, but he felt his pulse skyrocket. She had hit a nerve.

"And surely you have questions," Ada continued. "Perhaps I can answer a few."

Leon let out a mocking laugh. It tasted bitter. "Why, are you going to tell the truth? That wouldn't be like you."

Ada didn't flinch. She was enchanting, as always, and that only made Leon angrier. "The things I would lie about anyway, I simply won't answer," Ada said with her devastating charm. "For instance, if you're interested in my employers, then..."

"I'm not interested," Leon cut her off.

"No?"

"No. It’s better for both of us if I know nothing about your motives." That was the truth. If he had to report this to the White House, neither of them would come out of it well.

"What are you interested in, Leon?" Ada finally asked, her eyes practically burning into his skin.

Leon hesitated for a few seconds, but then spat out the core of the matter. "What the hell do you want from me, Ada?"

Ada didn't answer right away. She just looked at him. Leon’s operative brain kept chanting for him to turn around and leave, but catching Ada's gaze, something fractured inside him. There was a sadness in her eyes, the kind carried only by those who have seen far too many horrors in life. The same brokenness lived in both of them. Perhaps that was their only common ground in this godforsaken world.

Finally, Ada answered in a soft, clear voice: "You interest me."

Leon laughed incredulously. "Me, or what I know?"

"What you know, I can find out from elsewhere," Ada countered instantly.

"Oh, so you have absolutely no need to use me anymore?" Leon mocked, though his voice was losing its edge.

"I know you're angry. And it really suits you," Ada said. She paused for a moment, then suddenly, as if tearing it out from behind her walls, she asked, "Don't you want to get even?"

Leon ran his eyes down her figure. God, you have no idea how much I do, he thought. But instead, he turned back toward the water, fighting to regain control. "Seduce you, then leave you behind? I'm not like you, Ada."

The woman smiled gently and stepped even closer to him. They could practically feel the heat radiating from each other's bodies. "Yet you came. I wonder why?" she asked, her voice barely a whisper.

Leon didn't answer. A fierce, bloody battle raged within him between reason and desire. But fight as he might, it was futile. They wanted the exact same thing. The tension vibrating between them practically sparked in the air. They stood like that in the silent darkness for a long time.

Then Ada offered a choice. A final out. "You can leave if you want to, Leon. I won't blame you."

She turned to go below deck, but in that exact split second, Leon’s brain completely shut off. He reached out, grabbed Ada’s arm, and firmly pulled her back toward him.

"I'm not done with you yet," he said, leaning in dangerously close. His voice was raspy, foreign.

In any other scenario, Ada’s instincts would have kicked in instantly, delivering a defensive kick or a wrist lock. But not now. This wasn't a mission. This wasn't a fight for survival. It was just Leon, and her.

She looked down at Leon’s hand tightly gripping her arm, feeling the searing heat of his palm against her skin, then looked up into his face.

"Don't think I've forgotten who you are," Leon warned darkly. He seemed to be warning himself just as much as Ada. Especially himself.

Ada offered a fleeting smile. She loved it when Leon was this dangerous. "You'd be wise not to."

For a few seconds, they drank in each other's gazes in silence. Finally, Ada pressed herself fully against Leon’s chest, feeling the warmth of his body.

"I wouldn't mind if you stayed a while," she whispered so close to his lips that her breath brushed his skin. "But I understand if you leave. Because I can't promise anything more than what I can give tonight."

Leon didn't answer. He couldn't. He caught the intoxicating scent of Ada, and all of his resistance evaporated.

Eventually, Ada turned and walked down into the sailboat's cabin with slow, hip-swaying strides.

Inside, she leaned against the small kitchen counter and waited. Her heart was beating uncharacteristically fast. For a moment, she felt an inexplicable surge of anger at herself for allowing her defenses to drop like this. Since when do I let anyone get to me like this? Especially a man who, by his very profession, poses the greatest danger to me? she thought.

At that exact moment, Leon stepped into the cabin.

As they looked at each other in the dim light, words became entirely redundant. Both knew that in this very minute, they had lost a battle against themselves. Leon slowly walked over to Ada and stopped a mere inch away.

"I'm going to regret this," he said softly, his voice a low rumble from deep within his chest.

"Maybe it will be worth it," Ada whispered, looking up at him.

Leon then reached behind his back, slowly drew his service pistol from his belt, and placed it onto the counter with a heavy thud.

Ada let out a short, soft laugh. "You brought a gun, Kennedy?"

"Just playing it safe," he said calmly, his eyes tracing Ada's lips. "Just like you."

With that, his hand traveled downward. With a slow, deliberate motion, his palm slid against Ada’s thigh, sliding her black silk dress up inch by inch. At his touch, Ada shivered, goosebumps breaking out over her skin, which only fueled Leon’s fire further. He reached into the holster secured to her thigh, gently slid her hidden knife out, and carelessly tossed it onto the counter next to his pistol.

They were disarmed. Both of them.

"You really have become a professional," Ada noted, her breathing quickening.

"Or maybe I just know you too well."

"That’s your downfall..." she whispered, their faces now only a breath apart.

"No doubt," Leon said, and in that moment, he finally kissed her.

His kiss was consuming. It started out tender, but was instantly overtaken by the stifled fury born from Ada deceiving him years ago. It carried the raw, wild desire that had been straining his body ever since, and that immense void that had gnawed at his insides for six years.

They kissed for a long time, hungrily, desperately in the cramped cabin. An unfamiliar, elemental warmth washed through Ada, unlike anything she had ever experienced before. The sheer closeness of Leon, his skin, his taste, belonged to an entirely different world from the one she was used to in life. This wasn't a transaction; it wasn't manipulation. This was something… real. And she knew with absolute certainty that this was something that would be agonizingly difficult to walk away from in the future.

Eventually, she slowly pulled back from Leon, resting her forehead against his broad shoulder as she tried to catch her breath. "We are making a huge mistake," she whispered into his neck.

Leon reached under her chin, his long fingers gently but firmly tilting Ada's face back up toward him. There was no regret in his eyes.

"Too late," he said, and kissed her again. And this time, neither of them tried to step back from the edge of the abyss.

In the morning, Ada woke first.

The heavy, sensual scent of the night's passion still hung in the air of the cabin. Leon was covered up to his waist by the light sheet. For a few minutes, Ada simply allowed herself the luxury of letting her eyes linger over his muscular, flawless body. Then she studied his face. Leon was sleeping deeply, peacefully. Perhaps for the first time in years, he hadn't jolted awake at a sound.

So different from the rest. In every way, Ada thought.

She had never encountered a man quite like him, and no one had ever been capable of exerting such an influence over her. But in the next second, the professional within her took the reins. She slipped out of bed beside him soundlessly. She needed to vanish before they both ended up in danger. She had already lingered in the light for too long. If she wasn't careful, she might get used to this warmth.

No. That cannot be part of the plan.

"Ada..." Leon stirred, his voice thick with sleep, his eyes cracking open slightly.

"Go back to sleep, handsome," Ada whispered softly, leaning over him for a brief moment.

Her voice soothed him, and Leon immediately drifted back into a deep slumber.

Ada dressed in absolute silence. She didn't want to wake Leon and send him away. She would rather be the one to disappear; after all, it was her trademark. She looked around the cabin, checking to make sure she had gathered everything. Her gaze drifted to the small kitchen counter, where Leon’s service pistol and Ada's knife still rested side by side.

Ada looked at the peacefully sleeping Leon and smiled faintly. She fished a protein bar out from the very bottom of her bag—the only edible thing she had on her—and quickly jotted down a few lines on a scrap of paper:

"Next time, maybe you'll get a proper breakfast."

She placed the bar on top of the note next to Leon's gun, then stepped out of the cabin, closing the door behind her carefully without letting it click.

Up on deck, she looked around as the morning sunlight began to illuminate the horizon. Her eyes settled on Leon's motorboat. She had to get to shore somehow without waking him. The wind and the currents were heading toward the coast, and untying the boat was no great feat.

You can't even complain, Kennedy. I'm leaving you the sailboat.

She could already picture Leon's annoyed but resigned face when he woke up and realized what she had done. A soft laugh escaped her throat. In the next instant, however, her features hardened. Once again, she was Ada Wong, the ice-cold professional.

She climbed down into the motorboat, untied the lines, and once she had drifted a safe distance away, she started the engine without hesitation. She steered toward the shore, speeding away from the sailboat—from the place where, for the first time, she had left behind a genuine, unmistakable piece of her soul.

When Leon opened his eyes hours later, the first thing he noticed was an incredible, almost frightening sense of calm.

He didn't bolt upright. He didn't instinctively reach for his weapon. His heart wasn't pounding from a nightmare. He was simply… well-rested. For the first time in years, perhaps since the Raccoon City disaster, he had woken up like this. For a second, he didn't even understand this strange peace resting in his chest.

Then suddenly, like a broken dam, the memories of the night flooded over him.

Ada’s face in the half-light. That intoxicating, spicy scent of hers that still clung to his skin. Her beautiful, naked body on the sheets.
