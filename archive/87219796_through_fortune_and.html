When Agatha hit a clearing that dipped into a valley, her heart started to thunder in tandem with horse hooves. There was a battle going on.

 

The sides were murky; who was wearing whose colors, if they were on horses, it was a blur. It was then Agatha realized she was dreaming, though she could not control it: she was simply a voyeur. This happened to her, sometimes. She would have a dream that made no sense and then wake up with an immense headache.

 

So Agatha was already not looking forward to the morning.

 

While she could not determine what the fight was about, she could see a singular knight sifting through its clanking enemies like a scythe through wheat. Somehow, the figure knew the exact angle to get between joints and cracks of armor to strike bone or even organs.

 

Somehow, in the depths of her subconscious, just like her lungs knew to breathe, Agatha knew the knight was Rio Vidal.

 

And it was certainly different from what the stories said. If it was up to Agatha, the Harbinger of Death would be wearing black or even charcoal gray armor. She would be imposing. But she was the same color as the other figures, and if anything, on the shorter side.

 

So how did Agatha’s dream know it was the queen?

 

⚔︎

 

Agatha heard a knock on her door and startled awake. She knew it was probably one of the overnight maids performing her final duty of waking the morning staff. Agatha touched the back of her neck, cold with sweat, and blinked groggily. Her head felt like it would before it’d start to pound. Fabulous.

 

She looked out of her window. It was just before dawn; she had not gotten a full night’s rest, but she’d certainly had worse. She quickly got dressed and almost jumped out of her clean clothes when she opened the door and there Al was, grinning.

 

“Morning,” Al greeted cheerily, but Agatha was still not awake enough to give him a proper response.

 

They went to Her Majesty’s room. It was empty. Al showed Agatha how to make the bed properly and make sure the queen’s items were stocked.

 

“I do not dress her?” Agatha asked.

 

“Rio tends to dress herself unless it’s a fairly complicated outfit,” Al explained. “Partly because she’s stubborn, partly because she wears clothes more tailored to men and there’s no need to waste hands on clothes she can put on without difficulty.”

 

Agatha had noticed that. When Al had given her a tour of the rooms last night, Agatha had caught a peek of the queen’s wardrobe, and it had mainly consisted of shirts and pants: casual, fancy, nightclothes, it didn’t matter.

 

Al continued through his spiel. “You will be providing her with clean rags and a whetstone for her sword, though.”

 

“Does she not have a squire?”

 

Al made a ‘so-so’ motion with his hand. “Billy was just knighted, so for now, the duties are being split. At least you are not the one assigned to the horse stalls.”

 

Agatha did not even try to hide the disgust in her face. She was used to cleaning up after smaller animals, like chickens or rabbits. Once, very briefly, she’d had a cat as a child. But a horse? The amount of labor seemed horrible. And very smelly.

 

As Agatha walked into the edges of the courtyard shaded by an awning, she remembered Al’s instructions: put out the queen’s materials for when she done with her morning practice and then get started on taking her dirty clothes from the day before to the laundering room. Luckily, Agatha did not have to scrub at the queen’s clothes herself; Agatha found that a lady-in-waiting mainly constituted of low-labor, low-skilled menial tasks. It made sense as to why they tended to choose those of noble birth to do it. It was seen as an honor, basically.

 

And, truly, Agatha was going to give a quick in and out at first. Being near the queen made the baby hairs on the back of her neck stiffen. But then she got a glimpse of the woman practicing.

 

Rio Vidal in the battlefield was her namesake. She was smooth and knew exactly where she was going. Her hair was still in its braid, though it looked slightly more slept in than last time Agatha had seen it. She was wearing a billowing, thin linen shirt and brown breeches. Simple clothing, though Agatha was sure it was of a quality befitting of a monarch. When the queen moved, Agatha was standing close enough that she could see pools of sweat bleeding through the other woman’s shirt: under her arms, at the small of her back.

 

Agatha’s mouth, on the other hand, was embarrassingly dry. Parched, really. She felt the muscles of her fingers loosen their grasp, and the realization that the wash rags she held in her hand were about to fall startled her into tightening her grip until her knuckles paled.

 

Female knights were not banned, but they were certainly not common. They usually had to work twice as hard to gain the same results as their male counterparts. That’s the little Agatha knew about it, anyway. She could still remember the first female knight she’d met: her body was just beginning to go through its changes and a group of knights had stayed at their village inn on the way to a border patrol just a few leagues away.

 

Agatha had probably looked embarrassingly starstruck at the one female knight of the bunch. She held her helmet in the crook of her arm and her hair was in a long, blonde braid. The knight had taken a sweeping glance at Agatha and then muttered, “The commoners are worse than usual,” loud enough that Agatha had been able to discern it.

 

It had made her stomach hurt, and Agatha didn’t know until years later that she had felt the ache of desire.

 

Her Majesty looked so put together maneuvering a sword it was as if she’d been out of place in the other situations Agatha had seen her yesterday. She was fighting a knight Agatha did not know the name of— she wondered briefly if she would ever even learn the names of everyone in the castle— and Agatha had not even understood she’d presumed that whoever was on the business end of Rio’s two-handed long sword would be holding back to save his monarch’s face until Agatha watched the man grunting and sweating even harder. He was giving it his all.

 

Agatha found herself subconsciously licking her lips and then mentally smacked herself upside the head. A fellow maid servant, sure. A cook, a baker, a gardener, any staff member, absolutely. But the fucking queen? The one that Evanora had told sordid tales of during Agatha’s childhood, Agatha going to bed afraid to let her toes out from under her blanket?

 

It made total sense this woman had been considered a harbinger of death. Though she fought differently than she had in Agatha’s dream. Though why Agatha had decided her dream must have been some sort of vision and not a figment of her imagination was lost on her. In her dream, the figure she was sure was Rio had fought almost mechanically, like she knew her next move before she’d even begun to perform it. But here, she was a little more relaxed. She was smiling through the sweat dripping from her brow.

 

A clattering noise and the knight befell to Rio Vidal, his groaning echoing through the sparring arena. The queen walked over to him and almost immediately stuck out a hand. The man took it and Agatha watched as Her Majesty pulled him up effortlessly. She couldn’t hear their conversation that followed, but it seemed amicable enough. The man talked with his hands; the queen kept hers on her sword, even after she’d already sheathed it.

 

Rio Vidal walked over to where Agatha was and Agatha rushed to look busy, setting down the wash rags and jumping a bit theatrically to look like she hadn’t heard the queen coming up. “Oh, you’re here.”

 

Agatha turned around to see that the queen’s eyes were not just brown, but there were flecks of green and gold in them. Hazel. She said, “I am, in fact, fulfilling my duties as your lady’s maid.”

 

The queen grinned, and Agatha realized too late that she enjoyed how Agatha was staring directly at her. Agatha dropped her gaze to the gap in the queen’s front teeth. A safer place to look: more respectful.

 

“I was going to whet my blade,” the queen spoke, and Agatha subtly and mentally shook herself out of her harrowing thoughts. “Join me.”

 

It was not a question. Though Agatha wanted to make sure she completed her chores in a timely manner.

 

“If Al gets onto you about duties, I’ll talk to him,” the queen said, as if she were reading Agatha’s mind.

 

“Okay,” Agatha decided, though she knew she probably had little choice in the matter, even if personally, her choice was to play a bit of hooky and follow the queen to… an armory. Though it was fairly small.

 

“This is my personal one,” the queen piped up, as if she could read through Agatha’s thoughts. “A few of my suits, some jousting lances or bows and arrows if I wish to partake.”

 

But there was an entire table dedicated to the care of the queen’s sword. A whetstone, a pail of water already set out for her. Agatha noticed how the queen had several of most things in her personal armory: archery bows, knives, hell, even multiple spiked maces. But she only had one sword.

 

Her Majesty dunked a cloth into the pail of water and then wrung it out on the whetstone until it was, presumably, properly lubricated. “Have you been enjoying the book?” she asked.

 

Did the literal ruler of this kingdom want Agatha to follow her to ask her about a book of all things? Though she answered honestly, as it only revealed that Agatha found magic to be interesting, which was as embarrassing as revealing two plus two is four. “I have been.”

 

“Have you gotten to the green magic chapter?” the queen then continued. She did a test glide of her sword over the whetstone, and clucked her tongue to herself, a single click, before adding a little more water.

 

“No,” Agatha chirped, and then pure mischief beat in her chest as she added, “Why, is there anything worth reading in there?”

 

The queen only looked up at Agatha, an eyebrow quirked, as she seamlessly tested her blade against the stone again. She did not need to look at her movements, and from what Agatha could tell (which was admittedly not much), the form looked just about perfect. The reverberation of the blade on the whetstone bounced around the armory.

 

“I think—” and Agatha would be lying if she said her tongue did not feel thick in her mouth while saying this, but Agatha did not mind a white lie or seven— “that if you truly disliked what I had to say, you would have punished me by now.” She sucked on her teeth to alleviate the feeling in her mouth. “Why haven’t you?”

 

Her Majesty simply shrugged. Even though Agatha could tell the queen was acting nonchalant, there was a sort of ever-present tenseness in her shoulders that Agatha saw was still there. “You remind me of Jennifer, I suppose.”

 

Agatha’s lips puckered slightly. “Jennifer does not like me very much.”

 

The queen gave out a genuine laugh at that. It sounded nice. “That is part of the reason why she may dislike you. When blunt people meet other blunt people, they view it as competition.”

 

“You do not see yourself as blunt?” Agatha asked, having noted the use of third person. She was reminded of when the queen dressed down that women-hating dickhead in Agatha’s first court meeting.

 

“I’m more of a tool,” the queen said. “But I suppose I do not mind, as there are worse things to be.”

 

And that admission made Agatha’s head spin. First off, it did not back up the queen’s own claims that she was not blunt, as blunt and honesty were siblings when it came to descriptors, and telling Agatha such things felt pretty honest. Unless she was lying, to get in Agatha’s good perceptions?

 

“Do you share this with all of your lady’s maids?” Agatha tried.

 

“Only the smart ones,” Her Majesty answered, her lips having morphed into a ghost of a smirk. “And besides, pretty basic court politics. I rule the tangible. Honesty is a rare currency when everyone fights to be in one’s good favor.”

 

The even you went unspoken. And Agatha did not deny it. There was a reason the tightrope she’d found habit in walking existed to begin with.

 

“Careful,” Agatha warned her. “Your words are starting to sound fairly blunt, and a little birdie told me that was not an accurate description of the queen.”

 

The queen held up her sword. Even in the armory, a low-lit room compared to the field they had just been standing in, the weapon glistened. “Sharp is more like it.” The queen lightly tapped her finger on the edge of her sword; a droplet of blood instantly seeped from the digit.

 

A sick part of Agatha wanted to put it in her mouth.

 

“If that stains, you will be finding another maid to clean it up for you,” she spoke, instead.

 

“Of course,” the queen remarked. “I am sure you have quite the list of your own duties to attend to.”

 

A subtle dismissal. Agatha took it before her thoughts festered into something she did not desire. “Good day, then, Your Majesty.”

 

⚔︎

 

The next few days went by quickly, Agatha learning so much that she felt like an entirely new person. She had originally thought, on the carriage ride here, that all the new knowledge would exhaust her. But it has exhilarated her, really. Meeting new people who, while a bit distrusting when they heard her last name, were still kinder to her than the villagers she grew up next to; the layout of the castle, of which Agatha was starting to get the hang of in the few spots she knew she’d need to visit daily; the book she had borrowed from the library, which she read several chapters of a night.

 

Agatha decided to read the book in the order it was written, despite the queen’s questioning, and was not surprised to see the queen’s scribbles in the margins for the chapter on green magic. Though Agatha used “scribbles” loosely, as Her Majesty’s handwriting was incredibly neat and looked like the product of a childhood under royal teaching. Agatha read the first passage:

 

Green magic is about the cyclical essence of Life and Death. One cannot exist without the other; the life green magic is able to muster comes from the nutrients and shapes of the life that existed and was ended before it. Green witch Constance Thornden names one of the biggest hurdles of a green witch’s magic is forgetting the more “dreary” side of the coin. A witch who enjoys partaking in botanical practices, for example, must remember that not only do flowers bloom, they wilt.

 

“For example,” Thornden states, “when planting flowers or herbs, use items such as crushed egg shells or bone dust to help nourish the buds. Sure, your flowers may bloom nicely without them, but remembering what came before helps current life reach its full potential.”

 

Agatha glanced over to the queen’s notes in the margins: The bone of meat-eaters tends to work best, as their marrow is usually fattier.

 

Appetizing.

 

Most of the queen’s notes were of the same tone— they were actual notes. Best practices for a flower depending on if it bloomed once or multiple times, if it needed outside help for pollination, if it was picky about temperament. She very much detested a green magic scholar by the name of Buck Delaney. Agatha knew that, as a world inhabiting magic, superstition tended to hold at least a small bit of truth to it, but Delaney seemed to take it much too far. Every time Agatha saw the man’s name mentioned in the test, there was usually some sort of insult next to it: stupid, the bane of green magic’s existence, asinine. One of Delaney’s spiels about using gold-tipped clippers for more precise shaping had Her Majesty writing, I wish this man was still alive so I could revoke his scholarly status.

 

Agatha quickly thumbed through the rest of the book, the chapters on spirit and divination magic, and found nothing, which meant this was the only section the queen had written in. A devilish idea planted its seed within her mind, and she got up from her admittedly comfortable spot in her bed to sit at her writing desk for just a moment.

 

There was half a blank page at the end of the chapter on green magic, as that is where the prose had naturally ended. Agatha dipped her quill in her inkwell and wrote: Has one considered Buck Delaney’s strategy involving molding petal shape?

 

She blew on the ink lightly, and when it was dry enough, Agatha snickered and closed the book before going to sleep.

 

A few nights after Agatha had finished the book and returned it to the library, and after her chores were done and she’d finished putting Rio’s bedthings out (she’d been able to do it fully by herself, now, Al having told her the night before she most likely did not need to be checked anymore), Agatha realized she was hungry.

 

Now, Agatha was almost always hungry. Such was life. Though lately, the last week or so, Agatha had gotten a bit used to feeling fuller. Which meant that her hunger now felt that much more painful, like her stomach had become spoiled and now detested being empty.

 

Agatha had spent her first few nights wandering the castle, mainly to look in the library, but tonight, she had other plans. She had a good guess of where the main kitchens lay; it wasn’t too far off from where the serving dining quarters were— that made sense, as Agatha figured they did not want to travel very far to feed the staff.

 

Agatha made her way towards where she’d just been an hour ago to eat dinner: that night had been barley and poultry and vegetables in a broth. Most things were in a broth, probably because it did not take much watching over to prepare. Agatha was close to the dining quarters when she smelled something. It was sweet.

 

She followed her nose and a few turns later saw that she’d come across the main kitchens. The door was cracked, and Agatha couldn’t help but walk in curiously.

 

There was only one person in the room: a woman around her age kneading and shaping dough. It took a good half-minute for it to happen naturally, but when the woman looked up, she practically jumped at seeing Agatha watching her. “Um, how long have you been there?”

 

“Not too long,” Agatha half-truthed. “What are those?”

 

“I don’t know,” the woman admitted. Her hands were stained red, a similar color to her hair. “It’s kind of a puff pastry, I suppose, and there are raspberries involved. But I fear I may be wasting dough. I’m experimenting.”

 

“Why?” Agatha asked.

 

The woman shrugged. “It’s my night off, and I wish to get better at baking. You are the new lady’s maid, correct? What’s your name, again?”

 

Oh, so people were talking about her. Agatha blamed the warmth blooming on her face from the way she walked closer to the wannabe pastry chef, and therefore close to the hearth that housed a pot, presumably where this dough would be placed to cook. “Agatha.”

 

“I’m Wanda,” the woman clarified. And then she looked at her hands and sighed, mostly to herself. “This is not the shape I wanted,” she moped. “But if I knead the dough any more, it will toughen.”

 

“I would say shape has no matter if it tastes nice,” Agatha offered.

 

“Not if I want to serve these at royal banquets.” Wanda’s shoulders slumped. “I’ve only been the head chef for two seasons. I know Her Majesty is not a fan of hosting events, but on the chance that she wants one, I would be failing her.”

 

“Who cares what she thinks?” Agatha said. Agatha certainly did not care. “Where I hail, I was lucky to be fed. Food comes out the same way regardless of how it looks on the plate.”

 

“I know you mean to be consoling.” Wanda defeatedly brushed her hands on her flour-peppered apron and opened the hearth’s pot, already warm on a bundle of crackling wood. “But food is an art for me. I want it to look nice not to impress others, but more to feel like I can accomplish something that takes real effort. Anyone can throw vegetables and meat into a bowl and serve it, but I want to do something that people can look at and know takes skill.” When Wanda was done putting her half a dozen palm-sized dough pieces into the pot, she threw her hands up in exasperation, as if she were relieved her hands did not have to dexterously deal with baked goods for a spell. “But Red’s recipes are barely that. A handful of sugar. A sprinkle of nutmeg. By the stars, baking is precise! And what she’s left behind is anything but.”

 

“I’m sure you’ll get there at some point,” Agatha tried to comfort with. And then her stomach became louder than her brain. “If you want, I can try your food and see how it tastes.”

 

Wanda laughed, like a pebble striking stained glass. “I do not worry about how my food tastes, but you are welcome to have some when it finishes baking. If you’re willing to wait about twenty minutes, you can have some of whatever the hell I just put on the hearth.”

 

Agatha did not have to talk much after that. Wanda seemed like the type of person who hated silence and was always willing to fill it, and Agatha learned a lot about the castle just from Wanda’s ramblings, something she even admitted to doing at some point—

 

“I hope I’m not steering the conversation,” Wanda admitted.

 

“I want to know more about the people here,” Agatha had told her.

 

Agatha learned Billy, the squire, was actually Wanda’s younger brother; he had a twin somewhere, but she said Agatha would not know they were even related, they looked so different. She also ended up learning the culinary favorites of many in the castle. Some she only knew the names of, but the ones that stuck out to her were from the people she’d already met: Al liked things so sweet they’d rot your teeth off; Jen enjoyed a sweet and salty combo; the queen enjoyed many types of fruit, but her favorite was mulberry.

 

“She’s apparently loved mulberries since she was young,” Wanda explained. “They’ve always been associated with her birthday treats. She named her familiar after them, even.”

 

“I have yet to meet a familiar,” Agatha admitted. “There are not many magic users from where I was raised.”

 

“I had one,” Wanda said. “His name was Vision: a fox.” Her lips turned down. “There was an accident a few years ago, a loose boar in the town square while I was collecting things at the market. He was killed protecting me.”

 

“My condolences.” Agatha found herself really meaning it. “Is it too much for me to ask what that feels like?”

 

Wanda shrugged. “No, I would rather you admit you were curious than lie. I do feel like a part of me is missing. I think, if another animal came up to me and was interested in being my familiar, I would want to turn it down. At least right now. I’ve lost many things, though, that were worse. So some of it may be that.” The noise of a tack falling, then. A candle had melted enough that the tack had been exposed and dropped. “That would be the pastries.”

 

As Wanda started to uncover the pan and set it on a wooden cooling spot, Agatha asked, “What’s your favorite?”

 

Wanda shot her a bemused look. “What?”

 

“Sorry, I just mean you mentioned what everyone else liked. What do you like?”

 

Wanda tapped her lip. Agatha could feel the heat from the hearth as well as the pot, now inches from her. “I really enjoy desserts with vanilla bean in them, though they are quite expensive to get, so it is rare to bake with.”

 

Without thinking too hard, Agatha pulled one of the pastries from the hearth. It was golden brown and flaky. She held onto it just long enough to drop it on the table in front of her, shaking her hand harshly after. Her fingertips stung from the burn. “Ouch.”

 

“No one forced you to do that,” Wanda teased.

 

Agatha tried not to look forlornly at the steaming and flaky dessert. “It looks good,” she offered.

 

And Agatha waited all of about three minutes before tasting it. Of course, she felt the scalding of her tongue, but she did not care. She nodded as a testament to her previous statement. “Good,” she repeated.

 

“You are allowed to chew with your mouth closed,” Wanda told her.

 

Agatha covered her mouth before explaining her reasoning for mouth-breathing the pastry down her throat. “S’hot!”

 

A bit of nerves showed on Wanda’s face before she asked, “You truly find nothing wrong with it?”

 

Agatha at least had the decency to swallow before answering. “I may not be the high-regard opinion you seem to seek, but I did enjoy it.”

 

“If it were not so late, I’d try another batch,” Wanda admitted. “But I also think I am tired enough that I may accidentally burn myself.”

 

“Let me know when you do,” Agatha said. “Make another batch, that is.”

 

“It was nice to meet you,” Wanda said, and Agatha nodded as if to answer Likewise, as she now had the rest of the pastry in her mouth. “I think I will leave the rest out for the morning staff.”

 

Agatha held her finger up. “One more?”

 

Wanda smirked. “Well, if you liked them that much.”

 

Agatha nodded before snatching up another one straight from the pan. Thankfully, this one had been given more time to cool down. “I would argue that I am being incredibly selfless by not taking the rest to my quarters, actually.”

 

“Okay.” The word left Wanda’s mouth as a cackle. “You should head out, now, so I can clean up my mess and rest myself. Before I push you out.”

 

“I doubt I would mind.” Though at Wanda’s glare, Agatha added, “Okay. Good slumber, then.”

 

Agatha finished her second pastry before she even made it to her room.

 

Chapter 3: three

A few nights after she’d met Wanda, Agatha decided to spend at least half of her evenings chatting the woman up and getting a treat in return. And if Wanda was learning how to make sweets, there was surely a chance they may be less than perfect, and Agatha was willing to risk the chance to martyr herself and make sure they tasted okay.

 

Over the past week or so she had been here, Agatha had begun to recognize parts of the castle. Every hallway was decorated just differently enough that Agatha could pinpoint the subtleties. Sure, every part of the castle was draped in green and black, but some hallways had vases, and those that did contained vases that were different patterns.

 

A fortnight after she had start to call the royal bearings her abode, Al came up to her and dropped a small burlap sack in her palm.

 

"You'll get paid semi-monthly," Al had told her.

 

I get paid? was Agatha's first thought, though she had the wits about her to keep it an internal realization. "Yes, thank you." She was sure she sounded very natural and not surprised in the slightest. Her next idea she voiced aloud. "I am allowed to visit the town square and spend my wares, correct?"

 

"As long as you avoid trouble," was Al's answer, and Agatha, too impatient to wait, had chosen to skip dinner in favor of visiting the evening market.

 

Wanda had caught her on her way out. "Could you grab some vanilla bean for me while you're out?"

 

Not one to disagree with pretty women, Agatha said, "Of course."

 

Agatha had not expected she'd ever had the sort of freedom to go to the market, much less the time. Not even a month had passed since Agatha had made her way here— barely even half a month. Before, she had been restricted from going to the market without her mother's permission. She had been able to add a few stops to her errands once she was smart enough to realize she could, but she tended to pay for her adventures in bruises, especially if she took too long.

 

Sometimes she would receive those bruises without adding her own stops to her mother's whims, so at some point, Agatha would do what she wished, anyhow, if the consequences were going to remain the brutal sort of same.

 

But this market was nothing compared to what Agatha was used to. Salem's Peak boasted a market of a handful of vendors and a sprawling audience.

 

Westvista City's market made Salem Peak's market look like a droplet of water in the type of lake that could hide a sword.

 

Al had told Agatha of the servant exits to the town square, and Agatha had barely managed to wave to the two armored guards at the door of the castle wall before someone was pushed into her.

 

Thankfully, Agatha had spent an entire childhood having to keep from getting knocked down, so she just muttered, "Give a girl a warning," before making her way into a crowd that somehow got more and more packed in the more she entered it.

 

Agatha kept the burlap sack of coins close to her chest as she looked around for vanilla beans. The passive stroll she had imagined was certainly not feasible here, and maybe Agatha should have acted a little less impulsively on assuming what a capitol city's market would fare like— though she wasn't going to admit that to anyone but her own conscious.

 

She had spent a good quart of an hour looking for vanilla, or any sort of spices, when something shiny caught her eye. Agatha couldn't help but get a closer look. Of course, to go against the tide of screaming people, she had to elbow her way to the vendor's stall. It seemed to hold mostly secondhand items, though Agatha made her way to the section of the booth that held jewelry.

 

On a second inspection, Agatha realized what she'd been eyeing was a necklace on a silver chain. A figure of three women carved in some sort of marble or ivory on a background of mosaic blue. Before she could even clasp it in the palm of her hand to get a feel, a man's voice barked at her, "Three silver pieces."

 

First, Agatha did not like the tone of that voice. She looked like an obvious castle staff member, her maid's gown a sage green. Secondly, Agatha only had four silver pieces, and she was betting vanilla beans to be anywhere from one to two. "Am I not allowed to look before I decide if I would like to buy?"

 

The man was non-descript: like any man to ever exist, really. He may have been bald under the cap he wore, but Agatha chose not to spend moments of her finite existence staring at his face or anywhere near it.

 

Agatha twirled the pendant in her hands. "In no way would anyone in their right mind pay three silver pieces for this."

 

"You have a problem, you can go somewhere else," the man told her gruffly.

 

Agatha started to pick at the silver lining of the necklace with her nail. "This is rusting! Did you not polish it when you acquired it to sell?"

 

"Of course I did!" the man protested.

 

"Then if it's coloring, it must have been sitting here quite a while," Agatha reasoned. "I'll take it off your hands for one."

 

"Silver piece?" the man sputtered. "You're out of your mind."

 

"Two silver pieces and you give me back a bronze," Agatha haggled. "And I won't tell my fellow maids up there—" in this moment, she pointed to the looming castle at the end of the square, "—about the horrors of your wares."

 

Agatha could practically see the man thinking, he was so obvious. Gritting his teeth and whatnot. "Damn, fine, the thing is a piece of shit anyway."

 

Agatha just tossed him her coins and a shit-eating grin before snatching her change. "Have a good evening!"

 

She put the necklace on after she was out of sight of the man and his ridiculous stall. She probably would not go there again. She had never really owned a piece of jewelry before— a statement made ironic now that she worked in a place full of them. But this one was hers, and it rested nice against her sternum. Agatha tucked it under her collar.

 

By the time she found the vanilla beans and made her way back up to the servant's entrance, the sun had past set now that the days were growing shorter. Agatha had to hurry to not only give Wanda what she had asked for (and very cutely said her gratitude for, so score one, Agatha) but she had to run around to complete her nightly chores. The queen was, of course, not in her chambers: she rarely was when Agatha prepared them. By the time Agatha was done, she was heaving for air.

 

While the market had been nice, Agatha would not want to go often if it meant having to rush like that.

 

As Agatha walked to her own quarters, she felt her stomach rumbling. “Red?” Agatha guessed, remembering Wanda’s ramble from their first encounter.

Wanda had already been the second-in-command, but being the person in charge is different.” The woman stopped, and Agatha did so as well, to match her pace. Though she couldn’t help but glance in the direction of the kitchens before the woman continued. "Being the final say in something can be a daunting task."

 

"I… am sure it is." Agatha truly had just wanted to grab some pastries.

 

"I have a feeling that, soon, we will be snacking on quite a lot of lemon cake as that is Rio's favorite for her birthday," The woman mentioned.

 

"Birthday?" Agatha echoed. "When is it the queen's birthday?"

They were walking again, thankfully, on the way to where Agatha's stomach pointed like a compass to true north. Agatha said, "The shortest day of the year. That was at least true, and could be corroborated by Al.

Agatha’s blush deepened at that and she stuffed the rest of her treat in her mouth as an excuse to not be forced to speak. "I hope that is not the only quality you know of me. I can promise I understand what restraint is."

Agatha’s mood soured a bit at the mention of the worst person in the entire world. “In some manner, I suppose, though I can tell at least a fraction of what I was taught had a certain bias.”

“Hello, little one,” Lilia greeted. “You are quite a ways from your witch at the moment.”

 

Agatha then realized she knew this creature. “Is this Mulberry?”

“I feel grand,” Agatha lied.

 

Al was holding a chunk of bread that had been dipped in soup. Like a steaming kettle, Agatha let it out. “It’s hard to believe that with what happened to her last lady’s maid.”

 

Al blinked. “What do you mean?”

 

“You told me she died!” Agatha hissed.

 

Al’s piece of bread slipped from his fingers and plopped back into his soup. “What? I did not say such a thing!”

 

“She’s in a better place?” Agatha questioned, throwing Al’s words back at him. “That is what you tell a child whose dog had to be struck down because of a lame leg.”

 

“Um.” Al looked a bit sheepish. “That was certainly not what I meant.”

 

Agatha looked at Al expectantly then, her food forgotten.

 

“I forget you don’t know,” Al admitted. “I, um, was not born the man I am now. I just… recently, I guess, realized I am a man. And before then, I was Rio’s lady’s maid.”

 

“You were the queen’s lady’s maid,” Agatha repeated, and Al nodded.

 

“Like I said, everyone here knows. We rarely get new people in the castle. You are the first in, what, five or six years?” Al picked up his bread again, though it was all soppy now and Al’s fingers got sticky as he ate. “When I said the lady’s maid was in a better place, I simply meant… Well, I do feel like I’m in a better place.”

 

“Why do people rarely move into the castle?” Agatha asked, curiosity taking over her.

 

And Al just chuckled at that. “What have you heard about the queen?”

 

“Sure, but the castle provides free food for what I’m sure is not that much labor,” Agatha explained. “And you get a bed. And people here could certainly act worse.”

 

Al just looked at Agatha, and the look growing in his eyes was not one Agatha wanted to be on the receiving end of. “Agatha, if you think living here is so much better than living with your mother, that says something about your upbringing, not others’.”

 

Great. Agatha detested pity.

 

She chewed off a bit of her lip, her plate having been empty for a few minutes now, and pretended that there was something out the window to take a peek at. “Well, it’s not about me. I was more just being logical about the situation. I guess I should help the queen tame her supposed rat’s nest.”

 

Agatha practically knew the directions to the queen’s chambers by heart now, especially considering most of the journey was also the same journey she took to go to her own private quarters.

 

The queen was reading from some sort of manual and inspecting a trough of flowers that she had set upon her dresser. There was dirt on the dresser and the floor. Agatha’s face soured for a second before she immediately cooled it. She was going to have to clean that up.

 

The queen noticed her and immediately said, “Sorry, I’ll use a cleaning spell when I’m done.”

 

“I wonder why you or others do not use spells for the entirety of the castle, then,” Agatha pondered, though she dared a peek over Her Majesty’s shoulder. There were only a few flowers. They were all bright yellow. “To give underlings duties to fulfill, I suppose? But those look nice.”

 

“They are meant to be orange,” the queen muttered, and then she took a glance at Agatha. “I forget you are not as aware of magic etiquette.”

 

Agatha tried not to make that look like the blow it was. She did not need multiple reminders a day from different people on just how outside of everything she was.

 

(And yet, she still felt less outside of everything than she did where she used to call home.)

 

“Magic takes energy. It takes force.” Rio took her fingers and pinched right under the petals of the flowers until they were beheaded. “Those who use it want to use it carefully. “Some would do it for the time allowance,” Agatha argued.

 

“Maybe,” the queen admitted, “but you would end up using all of your free time to sleep and rejuvenate that energy. Humans are not endless wells. The queen then asked, “Do you think you have enough?”

 

“Time?” Agatha clarified. “I have more than I did with my mother, I suppose.” Agatha glanced down, all the flowers topless. “You killed them?”

 

The queen’s eyebrows furrowed. “What? No. Killing them would be on the opposite end, with their root support. My plan is to graft them. I should get more from my garden.”

 

As the older woman was about to leave, Agatha said, “Your Majesty, you need to sit down. It’s getting dark out, and I need to take care of your hair.”

 

The queen’s face went into this shape… like one of a sad pet. “I have too little time, I'm afraid.”

 

“What happened to the people in the castle having plenty of it?” Agatha fought back.

 

“I…” And that was the only word the queen could muster up. She then sighed and plopped herself into the chair at the vanity dresser, which Agatha was already standing beside.

 

“I do appreciate the cooperation, Your Majesty.” Agatha was nice enough to not let too much smugness creep into her voice.

 

The queen only looked up at Agatha through defeated lashes. “Your Majesty is very formal. I do not mind if you call me Rio.”

 

“Whatever you order, Your Majesty.”

 

The queen had a particular tell when Agatha was able to successfully poke the bear a tiny bit; her tongue moved around in her mouth like it was sweeping away whatever words had impulsively formed against the back of her lips. Sometimes she’d suck on the gap of her teeth.

 

The queen then looked at Agatha through the mirror. “You can simply brush it,” she decided. “I will put it into a ponytail in the morning and that should last me a few days.”

 

Agatha raised an eyebrow and continued to get out the necessary supplies. The hairbrush was carved from a thick bone and some sort of animal hair for the bristles— Agatha figured probably something stiff like boar’s.

 

“It is easier to sleep with the hair already styled,” Agatha said, treading a bit carefully. “It will stay in longer. Fewer times you have to sit still to bear it.”

 

Agatha watched as the queen’s head lowered; she’d slumped into the chair. Pouting like she was an ornery teenager and not somebody who ruled a vast kingdom. “Or I could just sleep now. I am very exhausted.”

 

“It can be your choice,” Agatha said, “if you want to look like you rolled out of bed when seeing a crowd.”

 

The queen looked up through her lashes to meet Agatha’s eyes in the mirror. “You still have that mouth on you.”

 

Agatha chewed on her lip, hiding a grin when she saw a curious look in Her Majesty’s eye instead of an angry one; that made her feel more emboldened. “Well, I must eat somehow.”

 

“Hmm.” When that was all which left the queen’s lips, Agatha went ahead and took the comb. She began to slowly pass it through the ends of the queen’s hair.

 

And then the queen asked, “Why do you start with the bottom?”

 

Agatha raised her eyebrows. “Did your last lady’s maid not do that? It helps with tangles.”

 

“Um, not really.” The queen squirmed in her chair as Agatha broke a particularly nasty knot. She’d put the teeth in the middle of the tangle before brushing it out, making it easier to smooth. “I either convinced my maids not to brush my hair, or when I was younger, they practically strapped me to the chair and got it done as quickly as possible. So starting at the top.”

 

Considering the length of Her Majesty’s hair was similar to Agatha’s, there were certainly not as many knots and tangles. Agatha’s hair was worse after she’d bathe in the river near her home. (Well, her old home, she supposed.)

 

When Agatha reached over to the vanity to grab the vial of oil to massage into the queen’s scalp, the monarch’s posture straightened as she asked, in shock, “Wait, you already finished brushing it?”

 

The vial was tipped just enough that a few droplets had fallen into Agatha’s waiting palm. “Yes?”

 

“It didn’t hurt,” the queen murmured.

 

“It’s not supposed to,” Agatha said softly. “Not if you do it correctly.”

 

Agatha rubbed her hands together and made sure most of the oil was at her fingertips before she began to massage the queen’s scalp. She saw some red at the base of the parting in Rio’s hair and, curious, started to temporarily part other, smaller sections to see that Rio’s scalp contained some irritated skin, most likely from rarely being taken care of— either from lack of care or from being too rough with the little care it received. That, and Agatha suspected Her Majesty was tender headed.

 

It would explain the possible upset the queen would make at having her hair brushed. Before Agatha had convinced her mother that taking care of her daughter wasn’t necessary, and Agatha could pick up the slack, so to speak, Evanora had brushed her hair. And it had been fairly awful. Evanora did not care if the tangle she was brushing through was painful, especially because Agatha had loved playing outside, and Evanora would insult Agatha’s state of appearance while tugging at her waves. That tugging hurt, and Agatha would not consider her scalp to be particularly sensitive.

 

Though Agatha was sure Rio’s experience was more about those brushing her hair not knowing she was tenderheaded than not caring. But a part of Agatha was relieved; that part of her was relaxing her posture, loosening her lips now that she’d figured out that, at least in this particular instance, the dragon was simply a common lizard. “Was that why you throw a tantrum every time your hair must get brushed?”

 

And the queen pouted at that. Stuck her bottom lip out, perturbed, and everything. It made a bubble of a giggle rise in Agatha’s throat, though she was able to pop and suppress it before it escaped. There was something particularly enticing about annoying a revered warrior and living to tell the tale. “I cannot help if it is not enjoyable,” the queen then muttered. “I have been in many a battle. I am no stranger to pain. But the pain of brushing is different.”

 

Done with the massage, Agatha began a simple hairstyle: two French braids. “Everyone has their personal tolerances,” she lilted.

 

“It sounds as if you are mocking me,” the queen accused, though her voice was not cold enough for Agatha to truly take it seriously.

 

“No,” Agatha said, and it was maybe half-true.

 

“Well.” The queen broke eye contact through the mirror, choosing instead to stare at the floor. “I appreciate your patience.”

 

Agatha did snort at that. “People don’t generally choose to pair that word up with me.” She finished up the braids, tying them off with a pair of black ribbons.

 

“Wonder why.” The queen smirked when Agatha paused her movements, and Agatha knew she was jesting. “You may go.”

 

Before Agatha fully slipped out the door, she said, “Have a good night, Your Majesty.”

In the past couple of weeks of living in the castle, Agatha usually woke up a bit startled. It was the job of the night maids to go around and knock on the doors of the day staff, and so that was what would pull Agatha out of her slumber.

 

Surprisingly, Agatha did not have many nightmares. Dreams, sure— but that was to be expected. The only odd occurrence is when, about once a week or so, Agatha would have the dream of herself in the field again. The dream itself was as terrifying as a mouse, but Agatha would always wake up with a baby of a tension headache and spend a few minutes massaging her temples to make it go away.

 

Meanwhile, when Agatha had lived with her mother, her mother would complain of Agatha whimpering or even screaming in the night.

 

Maybe it was just a product of living with such a horrible demoness, Agatha mentally figured.

 

And the raps on the door the small rotation of maids would give on Agatha’s door felt like birdsong compared to how Agatha would jump awake if her mother had been in a particularly foul mood. Which had been more often than not.

 

So far, Agatha would take a couple of minutes and sit up in her bed first thing, picking the sleep out of her eyes and getting her bearings. Then she would get dressed and begin the day. Her first orders, unless Al or Jen or someone of a higher ranking came in to tell her otherwise, was to go into the royal chambers and straighten things up. Though now Agatha knew why the queen’s rooms tended to be in a fairly tame manner most mornings; and so far, Agatha had not bumped into her during this time, which meant Her Majesty was awake and off before Agatha herself was.

 

While Agatha would surely rather be holed up in the library, as she usually registered two books a week at her current pace, the trade off was well worth it. Others seemed surprised at how Agatha talked about free time in the castle, though with most of them being born here, they seemed to take it for granted.

 

Jennifer was one of the few who gave Agatha credit for her perspective. “Unlike most people here, I was born in one of the neighboring provinces,” she’d told Agatha one day. Agatha was scribing the queen’s notes into something more presentable— something the queen usually did, but a duke had traveled three days to speak with her on something apparently very urgent, so Her Majesty was preoccupied.

 

And Jennifer, of course, had felt the need to watch over Agatha and make sure things were done correctly. Though at least she gave good conversation while distrusting Agatha’s ability to string words together.

 

“I was considered lucky, as my father was the Count of the land we lived on,” Jennifer continued. “But even then, I had my list of duties to attend to, and I saw how tirelessly others were forced to work. Not because my parents were cruel, but because there was that much work to do. We had our own crops and livestock.” Her lips turned down. “I do not miss waking up when it was still dark to attend to the cattle.”

 

“My rooster would find his way into my room and peck at my feet if I slept in,” Agatha supplied, and she tried to hide her bright expression when the anecdote actually made Jen laugh. “What got you here?”

 

“I was the oldest, and I only had sisters. So I got an education.” Jen paused for a second, presumably thinking about her family. “I still see my sister and mother sometimes. I write to them. My father passed years ago. One of my sister's husbands mans the properties now.”

 

“My condolences,” Agatha gave, knowing that if it were the other way around, she would be having a fucking celebration. And then she sat back in the chair she’d taken up to take quill to parchment. “That’s all, I believe.”

 

Jennifer cleared her throat and peered over Agatha’s shoulders and quickly read what Agatha had written down. “Your handwriting could use some work,” she said, “but you have remembered an adequate amount of information.”

 

Agatha knew hearing adequate from Jennifer was the same as hearing fantastic work from Al, so she took the praise for what she knew it was.

 

“By the way, you will be washing some of the queen’s clothes today,” Jennifer mentioned, and Agatha tried to keep her groan inside of her. It was so big she practically felt bloated. “Three of the girls are out sick today and the rest of the laundry staff are busy.”

 

Well, Agatha figured working with wet clothes for a living was bound to make someone more prone to colds, especially as the weather started to prepare for winter. Or maybe one of them had gotten something and they’d all kissed with tongue. Yelena looked like she would be someone that would happen to. Agatha had not asked yet only because Yelena was not her type.

 

Agatha had only had to give clothes to the launderers the entire time she had lived here, but now, a handful of weeks in, she has been instructed to go back to it. But Agatha had not missed washing clothes. Putting clothes away, stripping beds, dusting curtains, even brushing the queen’s hair were all things she didn’t totally detest doing. But truly cleaning clothes got your hands all red and cracked, and if it was particularly cold or dry, they would bleed as well.

 

Agatha was sure the main reason she had avoided the harsher chores until now was because of her status; even with Evanora being a disgraced viscountess, Agatha still had higher standing than many of the staff members who were either born of a lower rank or with no rank at all. (She was pretty sure Al was the only one of the staff who ranked above her, and now that made even more sense than it had before.) The thing was, even if Her Majesty explicitly despised her— which, to Agatha’s best abilities, she did not— it would look horrible for someone with noble blood to be, say, mucking stalls. It would make the inner workings of the castle look desperate for staffing. It would make the loyalties to the Vidal kingdom look fickle. Because if Agatha was on chafed knees bleeding into the stones of the castle, the other counts and earls and even dukes would be wondering who was next. Not them, necessarily, but their children? Was that a queen worth vowing oneself to?

 

But Agatha was not going to complain. Verbally, at least. If she had to spend an average of one or two days a month doing what she was raised to do every day, she would keep her disdain limited to her thoughts.

 

The laundry room was loud and humid. Agatha was immediately able to taste the air, and it smelled of lye.

 

A brunette Agatha could not remember the name of said, “You here for Her Majesty’s items?” When Agatha nodded wordlessly, the woman added, “Her personal clothes are washed in the smaller room over there 'cause the stuff in there is nicer.”

 

The “royal” laundry room, so to speak, was half the size of Agatha’s bedroom, with washing boards. Only a couple of items were in the dirty pile, but Agatha recognized Her Majesty’s riding clothes as a part of the pile.

 

Might as well start with the worst of it, especially if she was going to have to wash it again.

 

Her Majesty Rio Vidal usually went riding across the perimeters twice a week. She was not Captain of the royal guard— that title fell to Wade Wilson, one of the few men in this place that could make Agatha genuinely laugh— but it was considered a royal duty for a knighted monarch to walk around and make sure things were okay. Agatha wondered, for just a moment, if all of the queen’s duties were written down, how long the parchment would be.

 

Agatha grabbed the riding pants first. Even with the brown pigment, she could still see dirt stains on the knees. Which meant the queen had gone in her garden for a bit after her session.

 

Agatha realized she had never actually visited the queen’s garden, but she also was unaware of how public it was. The queen seemed to have quite the opinion on green witchcraft, and it wouldn’t surprise Agatha if Her Majesty was closed off with what would be such a personal space for a green witch. And she refused to ask anyone about it, because that would reveal a type of curiosity that Agatha preferred to keep to only herself.

 

She scrubbed the pants until the water bled clear. Stupid Rio Vidal. Did she know how her actions affected other people? What would it be like to hold so much power that you could accidentally, unintentionally, wipe out an entire town of people? Say no to the grain stores being emptied, and that was surely what would happen, no? Agatha’s thoughts raced right into the hands of The Harbinger. Sure, some of what she heard as a child must be myth— the queen did not particularly seem like the blood-sucking type, especially once Wanda had told her in secret that the woman would gag on pasta if it was overboiled— but myths are borne from a little truth, even if the resemblance is barely there. What kind of blood was actually on the queen’s hands?

 

Agatha liked speaking with someone who seemed to enjoy her sharp words, but it was hard to forget how sharp the queen’s sword also was. And at the end of the day, Rio Vidal was smart because of her upbringing.

 

Agatha was smart despite hers.

 

Linen shirt next. There were sweat stains, again, though they were a bit faded. They must be fairly fresh if Agatha was still able to parse the outlines, though. An uneasy feeling gurgled in her stomach, and she thought back to watching the queen practicing her swordfighting. Knowing how the sweat and dirt had seeped into the clothing she now held had Agatha’s fingers feeling numb, as if she had been white-knuckling them.

 

It took her a moment to realize she had, in fact, been gripping onto the linen shirt too tightly to make logical sense, and she loosened her grip. Her face was already warmly pink from the work she had been doing, but now she knew her face felt heat for another whisper of a reason.

 

And Agatha was used to dirt. She grew up in a village boring enough that she repeatedly had to chase preteen boys off from throwing stones at her chickens— she had honed the habit of finding freedom in the woods long before she had considered she could find it in something in stone and mortar.

 

But as she let the curiosity get the best of her and bring the shirt to her nose to take a sniff, her eyes watered at the intensity of soil and salt. The type that embedded itself into clothing when one spent the entire day rolling around in a field and sweating. Riding horses, digging for roots in the dirt, whirling around with a sword.

 

Agatha scrubbed the soap and then the shirt against the washboard before she could partake in a second and more stupid action.

Agatha slowly but surely got into the hang of things. She woke up early, and she went to bed late, but somehow she felt the most rejuvenated she had in a while. Maybe it was actually having something to do, and having the ability to converse with people with more than just an empty stack of hay for a brain. Maybe it was the freedom of being away from her mother.

 

Her schedule was forming into something more concrete. She’d be woken and do her morning duties as she always had. After her morning duties, she would either go to the kitchens and sweet talk Wanda into giving her something warm and fluffy to break her fast, or she’d eat the gruel in the serving kitchens.

Polishing armor, taking clothes and bedspreads to the laundry room as well as bringing the clean ones back up and putting them away, preparing the queen’s bath for her. Sometimes she would handle correspondence, where Her Majesty would receive a letter and have Agatha transcribe it for her. Agatha found her list of chores fairly random and not always becoming of a lady’s maid. They were also still looking for a squire— if Agatha was not filling in the duties, it was Al or Billy, Rio’s old squire.

One of the figures made its wispy way past Rio and slammed into Agatha before grabbing her torso. For a figure that Rio was unable to cut down with her blade, this thing was entirely too strong to make sense. There was no way Agatha was able to get free. Against her, the figure felt cold and surprisingly slimy.

 

"Are you crazy?!" Agatha screamed so loudly Evanora could hear it crisply over the noise of fighting. "And how the hell did you get unbound?"

 

"Do you not think I have connections? But I was unable to use them until I got back into the city. My rightful home. And it would have been our rightful home, but you squandered your chances." Evanora tittered around, walking primly up to her daughter to stare at her nose to nose. "I figured, with the way you were writing to me, that you would have no issue helping me take down this corrupt kingdom. But it seems you inherited my ability to hide truths."

 

Agatha turned to see Rio wrestling with two shades holding each of her arms. Her legs swung far, but they did nothing to free her. Agatha's felt her eyes grow and her face drop.

 

"I see the way that irreputable woman looked at you when my shadows stopped you." Evanora took a nasty side glance at Rio before sliding her eyes back to her daughter. "You figured your new way of life could be a royal's whore?"

 

"How dare you!" Agatha roared antagonistically. "I am a woman of virtue."

 

Agatha could see the slap coming, but she was incapacitated and unable to stop it other than close her eyes in preparation. She squeezed her eyes shut as the sting of the slap struck her cheek and then reverberated throughout the rest of her face.

 

Rio started kicking so high her foot reached above her head. "You are a terrible excuse for a human being!"

 

Evanora sneered. "Rich, coming from you. I should have you know—"

 

Suddenly, a figure— a human figure, that was— materialized out of the blue and a candelabra whacked Evanora right in the head. She doubled over onto her knees and howled in pain, clutching her head, and the shadowed figures instantly vaporized.

 

Agatha was forced to fall to the floor yet again. Her tailbone already ached.

 

"Al!" Rio shouted. "Circle to Agatha."

 

Agatha quickly sat up as she saw Al, the perpetrator of the hit, run in the same direction as Rio: right to Agatha. The second the three were close enough together, Al immediately drew a dagger and started carving a circle into the floor, orange sparks following the grooves in the hardwood floor.

 

The second the circle closed, Rio turned to Agatha. "We only have as long as Al can hold the circle, and we cannot drain him too badly."

 

"Thanks," Al huffed out sarcastically.

 

"How much do you trust me?" Rio asked.

 

Agatha could hear her mother get up and start throwing a fit as her targets sat in a magical protection. While the circle was complete and working, nothing could cross it. The shadow figures had come back, though they were slightly weaker than before, most likely due to Evanora feeling drained from her head injury.

 

Agatha answered honestly. "So totally and utterly completely that if you mess it up now, you will never see my face again."

 

"You need to make your mother hit you with her magic," Rio said.

 

That had been the last thing Agatha expected. "Is that not the exact opposite of what we would like to happen?"

 

"I hate to not side with you on this one, Rio, but you sound like a bunch of bananas," Al commented.

 

"Agatha, please." Rio held Agatha's face in her hands. "You love baiting people and getting them to get angry. And I love that very much about you. You need your mother to hit you with her magic. I promise it will make sense later."

 

Something bubbled in Agatha's gut uncomfortably, but she knew that if she died, Rio and Al were surely goners, too. "Okay."

 

"You can take down the circle now, Al."

 

The second Al quit pumping magic into the protection wards, chaos ensued. Rio sprung up like a human-sized hare and immediately got back to what she had been doing with the shades beforehand. Al did the same, though he was much closer to the shades, as all he was carrying was his dagger.

 

"Mother," Agatha started simply, "why are you even doing this? You could have run away to Disclus or some other place. Why choose to stay here?"

 

"Because this is my home. It is my city. And that psychotic queen's parents are why my city is crumbling!" Evanora threw her hands up, and her shadow figures danced in response. "We were only trying to fight against corruption."

 

"Corruption?" Rio called out. "What in the devil are you talking about?"

 

"Your parents forced my family, among others, to start paying more," Evanora spat. "And why? We paid our dues. We were the reason the coffers stayed full. And what did your parents do with that money? Well, they certainly did not ask my father, who they made give ten percent more of his income. Or his brothers. Or his friends. Because instead of celebrations and property updates, our money paid for things others did not work for."

 

Agatha squinted her eyes in confusion.

 

But Rio's voice cracked in frustration when she screamed, "The entire time, this horribly damned mess has been about TAXES?"

 

"That is what your parents called it," Evanora affirmed. "But what it was, was theft."

 

And a shadow figure rammed into Rio. Agatha yelped as Rio crumpled like a doll and fell on the hard floor. Agatha's brain racked through an encyclopedia's worth of insults, remembering Rio's advice.

 

"Can I ask you something?" Agatha simpered.

 

Evanora turned towards Agatha. "Yes, daughter?"

 

Agatha put on a face of pure innocence. "Well, as my mom, you never really gave me the talks I saw all the other kids getting. How is it babies are made again?" Agatha shrugged as best she could while being restrained. "Because I think I got some misinformation without your expertise to correct it. Rio and I keep trying and anything has yet to come to fruition."

 

Bingo. Evanora's face wrinkled into an expression of pure rage. "Do you need to be punished, Agatha? Is that it? You were away from me for too long, and you have forgotten how to act."

 

"Not really," Agatha said. "I acted like this in Salem's Peak, also."

 

"Why do you push and push?" Evanora asked. "I swear I went gray early all because of you. You do not know how to stop. That is why we are in this mess to begin with. How easy could it have been to just forgive your mother and do your familial duty?"

 

"Ha," Agatha cackled. "You said duty."

 

Evanora growled angrily as her hand shot out. The shadow's… tentacle? Tail? wrapped itself around Agatha even more tightly, until its round nothing of a paw stroked Agatha's face. She yelped in a pained surprise when she actually felt it cut her; she had not expected something with no edges to dig deep enough for her to bleed. Her hands were restrained behind her back and they were gripped so firmly Agatha's shoulders screamed at her.

 

"I wish I could say it pains me to do this," Evanora sighed, "but you deserve it. You are bad, Agatha. There is something inherently wrong with you, and I have felt it since the moment you were born. Before that, even."

 

"Oh, what a load of horse shit," Agatha seethed. "I know everything. The only reason you can stand here and talk nonsense to me is because I had taken enough root inside you when they found you. And I know who my father is, too."

 

"Agatha, I have tried so desperately to be a good mother to you," Evanora reasoned, "but all you do is let me down. How can you expect me to prioritize you when you cannot even prioritize me?"

 

"You were supposed to prioritize me first!" Agatha snapped. "Because that was your damned job! And you tell me you were a good mother." At that, Agatha cackled. "You were barely a mother at all. I had hens that were more maternal than you, and I still managed to butcher them at the end of the day."

 

Evanora finally yelled, "Quiet, you insolent child!" and threw her hands in Agatha's direction, magic pouring directly from her hands with the goal to slam right into her daughter.

 

Agatha tensed, unable to move. She surely expected death. The tarot card Lilia pulled for her flashed in her mind; maybe it had been a literal death, after all.

 

She hadn't expected it to feel so… nice. Like finishing up your first goblet of wine and feeling a nice buzz. But then it got even better. Agatha felt as if she were reaching towards some sort of ecstasy. Agatha had never really believed in an afterlife, but maybe she had been good enough to get into it. Maybe things would be easier.

 

Agatha realized she was definitely not dead when she heard Al's voice go, "Holy shit! She's a spirit witch!"

 

When it was all over, Agatha gracefully floated down the several inches she'd been from the ground until both of her feet gently touched the ground. The buzzing in her chest had become an ocean's worth of wasps. "What just happened?"

 

The shadow figures were gone. Agatha turned until she saw a shriveled up corpse wearing her mother's dress. she walked slowly towards it— and that's what it was now: an object, not a person. Agatha even poked it with the tip of her shoe before backing away. It gave her the creeps.

 

"This is what I saw." Agatha turned to see Rio standing a few feet from her. "But only when your mother had me in that shadow figure. I connected the dots when Al showed up."

 

"I read about spirit magic," Agatha said. "I actually know what it is."

 

Al looked around at the mess the council room had been. "I… am going to go talk to people to fix this room so it ceases looking as if a storm ran straight through it." He took a glance at Agatha and Rio. "You can, uh, have your little conversation without me."

 

"I cannot verbally express my gratitude enough," Rio said. "We would not be here without you."

 

Al cracked a smile. "Hey, it's my job, is it not?"

 

"Best protection witch ever." Rio shot Al a thumbs up.

 

But her face grew more solemn when Al was far enough from earshot. "I hope you can forgive me."

 

"For what?" Agatha asked dubiously.

 

"I saw your mother's corpse," Rio reiterated, "and I did not tell you."

 

"You had no time to explain it," Agatha said. "Just like how Al would waste time explaining to us that he planned on putting on a cloaking spell and trying to distract her."

 

"Knocking your mother out is different than killing her." Rio averted Agatha's gaze.

 

"You did not take my mother's life," Agatha corrected. "I did. I chose to follow your advice knowing it would probably not end well for her. I maybe did not connect the same pieces you did, but I knew that much."

 

"I only fear you will regret it," Rio voiced. "And me."

 

"Rio." Agatha waited until Rio would meet her gaze. "I… I have never felt so relieved in my entire life."

 

It was then the weight of what happened fully hit her. As her knees failed her, Rio seemed to catch the instant change in Agatha's face before her body could catch up and immediately went to catch her in the form of a bear hug. They both fell on their knees as Rio held Agatha in her arms.

 

"You're okay," Rio murmured. "And you are so good."

 

Agatha bit back a sniffle. "In what way? I told you I'd read up on spirit magic. They have to kill people to store their energy."

 

"So I know that means you read the part where it talks about new research findings." Rio pressed her lips to the crown of Agatha's head. Her favorite crown, despite everything, if she were to choose. "And with the two of us on it, we will figure something out."

 

Agatha tentatively wrapped her arms around Rio's stomach and nuzzled her face into Rio's neck. "If you say so."

 

They sat there together in silence for a few minutes. Agatha liked that she could be silent with Rio. It was something about sharing nothing with each other that meant everything in the long run.

 

Of course, the metaphorical spell was soon broken as Al came back with Lilia and Jen.

 

"Damn." Jen whistled. "You guys sure did some damage."

 

"Eat rats," Agatha mumbled snottily into Rio's chest.

 

Rio just rubbed small circled on Agatha's back. "Well, there's a sign she's feeling better."

 

"You two go to the royal chambers," Lilia said, though Agatha could tell it was a kindly worded order. "I was regent long enough I know what to do for an afternoon. And I can tell someone to bring you aid for your injuries."

 

"No need," Rio said. She slowly helped herself and Agatha get up. "We have many bruises and some cuts, but I cant ake care of them."

 

And she did. When they got to their chambers, Rio took strips of old linen and dampened them before blooting the cut on Agatha's face.

 

"It's not too deep to scar," Rio decided, "which is good. You would not want the reminder."

 

"Is that how you view your scar?" Agatha asked. "The one on your back?"

 

Rio's lip curled up in a sad sort of smile. "I was riding a horse when I was fifteen and she bucked me. I fell into a ravine and sliced my back open. They were only able to find me because Lilia saw Victoria running around pasture without me on her back."

 

"Wait, Victoria was the one to buck you?" Agatha said in surprise. "You still ride her all the time. She's your favorite horse."

 

"Maybe I like it when girls are mean to me," Rio replied playfully, and she brushed her fingers right over the blush that bloomed on Agatha's cheeks. "Either way, it was not her fault. A deer had spooked her. She was simply scared."

 

After Agatha tended to Rio's own small cuts, mainly from being tossed around, it was an unspoken agreement to get into their sleeping clothes and lay in bed together, even though it was still hours until dinner.

 

"I cannot even fathom what I will do now," Agatha pondered. She had her head on Rio's chest, listening to the monarch's steady and strong heartbeat.. "She was the person that knew me the most. The longest. And no one comes close."

 

Rio tapped Agatha's jaw so that she could look up into Rio's eyes. "I can promise you that there will be a day where you have known me longer than you ever knew her." Rio then tucked a stray hair behind Agatha's ear. "I will draw up the math and hand you the calendar date myself. We can make a celebration out of it."

 

Unshed tears began to appear in Agatha's eyes. "You are ridiculous."

 

Later that night, when Agatha wanted some fresh air but was too drained to fully walk outside, she opened the window of Rio's room and stuck her head out. Looking to her right, she saw a nook of bricks that fell in such a way that a bird's nest had been built there.

 

"A nest!" Agatha exclaimed. "It would be cute to see baby birds come springtime."

 

Rio, who was reading in bed, perked her ears up. "Oh, no, if you're looking right outside the window, that's Mulberry's little cubby stash. Corvids like collecting things."

 

In the light of the early sunset, Agatha strained her eyes to see that Rio was correct; the next contained a myriad of small, shiny objects.

 

And that was when a sun beam hit just right that Agatha saw a flash of blue.

 

"Rio." Agatha turned and shuffled hurriedly towards the bed. She pointed out the window. "That nest has something that's mine."

 

"Why don't you practice moving objects and collect it with magic?" Rio suggested.

 

"Baby, please." Agatha jutted out her bottom lip. "It's my necklace and it is very special to me. I want to make sure it does not drop."

 

"The locket?" Rio closed her book. "Well, I know how much you were missing that thing. Let me give it a whirl."

 

When Rio was able to magically snatch her cameo necklace in no time and Agatha had it in her hands again, the robber herself perched on the open windowsill, flapping her wings.

 

"You," Agatha pointed, "are a little shit."

 

"Hey," Rio protested. "She was only doing what is her nature to do. It would be like if you got upset that Scratchy chewed on your shoes."

 

"Scratchy would never do that," Agatha insisted. "I am his mother and he is a gentleman."

 

"He chews on my shoes," Rio muttered.

 

"You leave them scattered about the floor," Agatha pointed out. "So he thinks they are toys."

 

Rio grumbled instead of giving an answer, because Agatha was right. Instead, she said, "Maybe we should find a good, safe spot for that necklace."

 

Agatha immediately put it around her neck.

 

"Well," Rio amended, "when you are not wearing it, that is. How do you feel about dinner in bed?"

Agatha glanced up at Rio and traced her moonlit profile with her eyes. "What do you mean?"

 

"It means…" Rio bit her lip. "That while I do not have a plan, that whenever I ask you to wed me, I would hope you say yes."

⚔︎ Four Years Later… ⚔︎

 

"He's teething."

 

Rio looked up from the work on her desk. "How do you know?"

 

Agatha held their seven month old up. Nicholas was chewing on the handle of a spoon.

 

"That has got to be uncomfortable buddy." Rio had barely even tugged the spoon towards her when their son started whimpering. "Okay, never mind."

 

"Aww, he's just like his Mama." Agatha turned him around and bounced him as she sang, "Do you give Mami puppy eyes and she does what you want? Yes she does, yes she does."

 

"He gets eating objects from his brother," Rio accused.

 

"I do not know why you keep this farce up that Scratchy secretly terrorizes you," Agatha said. "He is literally perfect."

 

"My shoes get holes in the toes, Agatha. And I put them up now!"

 

Agatha notched baby Nicky on her hip and peered over Rio's shoulder to the spread of journals on the desk. Her eyes ticked up to Rio's, a worriedly soft look reflected in them. "Do you even get any sleep?"

 

Agatha and Rio had around the clock care for their infant, but they still chose to put Nicky's crib next ot their bed so they could comfort him if he woke up in his sleep. They usually took turns, but even on nights where it was Agatha's night, Rio tended to stay awake regardless, putting all her available effort into her research. And considering most of her day was taken up by the duties of an involved monarch, it meant sacrificing sleep.

 

"You feel weak and restless. You can barely sleep. Spells you could do with one hand tied behind your back just a year ago you sometimes struggle with now." Rio frowned. "Your magic needs to be recharged. It being such a core part of you, and the fact that it was restricted from you for so long… I have to make this work."

 

Agatha's eyes grew sad. She would be lying if she told Rio to not worry about it, and they both knew that. Short of finding someone they deemed worthy enough to kill, Agatha was depleting. It was not fast, but it was happening. About two years ago, Rio had found out a way through analogue magic to transfer magic from user to object to spirit witch. But it was not a sustainable amount of magic for the average witch, much less Agatha. So Rio was trying to figure out a way to multiply that amount to a useful degree without basically blowing everything up.

 

And while Agatha had no problem with her mother being her victim the first time, any possible next time was off the table. Going through the traditional methods of keeping spirit witches magic-infused— while morally wrong, of course— was not something Agatha could get away with doing. Not with the eyes she and Rio had on her. Not with their child. Agatha would make sure nothing got between Nicky and his mothers.

 

"Someone could always hire a magic mercenary to take me out," Agatha joked. "And then when he hits me… Boom. Recharged again."

 

"Agatha," Rio chastised. "Actually, I was just double checking my work. I have something in the garden for you. Want to bring Nicky?"

 

In the research garden, there was an orange tree Rio had been working on for the better part of three months. That was the difficult part of this method of research; each experiment took so long, Rio would have to conduct multiple at once. But even as powerful of a green witch as she was, Rio did not have enough essence inside of her to consider that. So what she usually did was work on multiple theoreticals at a time, and whichever one had the better math and statistics was the one she physically trialed.

 

Rio stood on her toes to pluck a fruit from the newest orange tree. Rio broke the seal of the peel easily and began to tear it off with ease, having done it countless times now for Agatha. She handed Agatha the peeled orange in a singular piece. "Milady." And then she held her hands out.

 

"That's Queen's Consort Milady to you." Agatha gave Nicky to Rio for her to hold and then took two sections of the orange at once to pop into her mouth. As she chewed, a peculiar expression formed. She ate two slices at a time thrice more until the orange was gone.

 

"How do you feel?"

 

Agatha rubbed her hands together and then raised one hand up and flung it out. A comically large ball of magic shot into the sky, as high as the clock tower, before it exploded into shards of color in the sky.

 

Rio smiled wide. "Yeah?"

 

Rio's grin was contagious. Nicky looked up at his Mami and mirrored him. "Guh."

 

"It definitely isn't the same amount of fuel an entire human would give," Agatha admitted, remembering the intense rush she felt when her mother had blasted her, "but… it's something. It might even be sustainable." Her arms flopped to her sides. "Sometimes I wonder what I did to even deserve you."

 

"Okay, first, you do deserve me, you are just emotional about the breakthrough." Rio shifted Nicky on her hip. He was getting heavy. "But also, love is not about what you do or don't deserve. Do I deserve to breathe? No, I just do. I love you in the same way."

 

Agatha rolled her eyes, but they were teary. "You sap."

 

"You know what this means?" Rio waggled her eyebrows. "It means I can have enough magical energy for the fertilization spell again."

 

"Okay, scratch that. You are a pig." But then Agatha pursed her lips into a smirk and shrugged nonchalantly. "However… while I would want to wait until Nicky is walking and talking on his own… I'm not saying no."
