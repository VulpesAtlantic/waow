The first time he had gone up into the ducts was to clean them out, the honest truth. The Quadrant had been a mess, and Rocket couldn’t resist proving how much more responsible and capable he was than anyone else onboard. Getting to bitch about how sore and greasy it made him and having an excuse to use up all the hot water were nice, too. “Whatever’ll keep you off the controls,” Peter had mumbled, still pissed off about the Milano , and Kraglin had been cool letting Rocket do as he pleased if it meant less work for him. So, with free range of the tools and equipment onboard, Rocket worked his way up into the ducts, humming along to the music that echoed through the ship and pilfering whatever scraps he could repurpose.

It had been a few hours since Rocket had been at it, past Groot’s bedtime, probably, and the movement in the ship was slowing down. Rocket had been perched on a particularly wobbly row of filtration grates overlooking the cockpit with the intention of tightening the screws when he overheard voices below. The music was pretty low, so the voices must have been even lower, ‘cause he couldn’t make out who was down there. That was fine with him. This song was a funny one, and he liked singing along, even if he couldn’t understand a word of it.

“Oogum, oogum, boogum, boogum

Boogum buh-baby, bah-cassinyaspella-onnn me

Nah-nah-nah oogum, oogum, boogum, boogum

Boogum buh-baby, nah-cassinyaspella-ahh- hey!"

Someone had turned it off. Rocket clambered over his little scrap pile toward the main vent, fully intent on hollering to the idiot on shift to put his song back on, when an unfamiliar, slower one kicked on. Closer to the vent, his ears perked up at recognizing the voices. It was Pete and Gamora, talking. Well, shit. He growled in spite of himself.

Peter and Gamora had been getting closer since Yondu’s funeral. Rocket hadn’t noticed at first, was still grieving the loss of his short-lived friendship, but it was getting pretty obvious now that the two of them were spending more intentional time together. Sure, he and Pete still had time, but as soon as Gamora passed by looking even slightly unoccupied, boom. Pete was gone, fast. And Rocket couldn’t tell what irritated him more, that Pete would just up and leave, or the fact that Gamora frickin’ let him. Let him follow her around, attended to his stupid jokes and big smile and bragging in ways she never woulda before. Peter had run his mouth to Rocket and Drax in the beginning about all the hot women he’d screwed around with, and it didn’t sound like it took much effort to find them or snag them, so why he had to target Gamora, the most hardened and most opposite of any of those others, all to get off, Rocket couldn’t guess.

Rocket growled again, properly irritated now. Frickin’ whatever. As if he cared enough to try and unpack whatever was happening there. At least he knew that when Pete inevitably got shot down, he’d come running back to his friend. The nasty part of him wanted to shout at Peter about the song, start some shit just to feel something, but Gamora being pissed off with both of them was more trouble than it was worth, and he was trying not to steal any more batteries ever since they had to recover his latest friend’s frozen body from the vacuum of space. His thoughts were derailed abruptly by the sound of laughter, and it wasn’t Pete’s.

Peering down through the grate, Rocket’s eyebrows had raised at the sight of Gamora, laughing softly as Peter’d fumbled with the Zune on its perch. Huh. That was a new sound. More surprisingly, Pete had been able to sashay over with that stupid puppy smile on his face and pull the Zehoberei woman into his arms without getting a blade through his gut.

It was like Rocket’s heart skipped a beat, and that was weird, because that only happened when there was a threat too big to laugh off, and there was no threat in sight. It was just his friends, hugging and swaying. But he’d never seen Gamora really dance before, if that’s what this even was. She looked so soft somehow. Hard eyes and lean, sharp angles, curled up in Peter’s big arms like she could sleep there. Peter had raised his arm then, and Gamora spun out from beneath it, made it seem natural, before spinning right back in. And she was smiling, almost as wide as him.

There was a sneer tugging at Rocket’s lips. What the hell had Peter done to break her down? Must be a damn good feeling if the assassin daughter of Thanos was getting into it. That, or Gamora was not as wise as he thought. He knew it wasn’t true before the thought had even fully formed, but it made whatever was going on in his chest sting less.

As Rocket had stared and mulled over seemingly hundreds of ways Pete coulda lured Gamora into that kind of closeness, a sensation crept up around him, hard bark and foliage. Not the same kinda closeness, but similar, maybe? Safe?

The memory of Groot’s smile distorted through a film of tears bled into Rocket’s vision and the feeling soured fast. No, it was something else. Different, somehow.

Shit. Rocket outta go, if not for self-preservation, because what he was doing was unfair. For sure Peter’d be pissed off if he knew their privacy had been violated, and that ain’t a big ask on a ship so small, some basic privacy, right? But it was like the longer he stood there, the more impossible it was to move or breathe. He could still feel the branches of yet another dead friend suffocating him and splinters of smoking wood slipping between his fingers.

And then it was the plushness of matted white fur that assaulted him next, enveloping him on the floor of the cage as he drifted to sleep, and warm hugs and mechanical limbs-

It was like every nerve in Rocket’s body finally slammed into gear, screaming at him to run. Metal went clattering to the floor around him as he willed the shaking to go away, and he was vaguely aware of his claws digging into his arms ‘til the pins in his knuckles locked up.

What had happened, Rocket didn’t have a clue. All he knew was that it hurt all over, and every time he blinked, he could see every one of them, adrift in the dark. That kinda closeness… nope. Just no. He fell all crumpled up onto the floor amongst his tools. It was cold and uncomfortable, but felt too hard to get up and find his hammock. So he stayed where he was, swallowing for air, and swore up and down that he would never let himself nose into that kinda torture again.

But now, there he was a few months later, curled up at the same grate as Peter and Gamora rocked slowly beneath him with a curtain of stars all around. The ducts didn’t need cleaning, and the vent cover wasn’t squeaking anymore. Sure weren’t those things that brought him up there, but it wasn’t like the echo of slow music encouraged him either. She buried her face into the crook of his neck. Her hair cascaded down his arm, and it was beautiful, the way the brown and pink shimmered under the light. “No, it still sounds nice.”

Pete smiled, just a little. “Yeah.”

A lot of shit had changed in what felt like a short span of time, was pretty evident in the way the two of them were glued at the hips. And then there was the bug lady. She wasn’t a looker either, and she had the audacity to put her hands on him. So, what? If it wasn’t being cut open or getting the crap knocked outta him, it was being pet like a dog?

He’d rather be called a rat than be pet like a frickin’ dog.

Rocket tightened his arms around himself as that fun fact sank in. No point wasting his time watching or imagining that maybe, just maybe, he could ever just get close someone as he was if the only options were “pet” or “hurt”. Instead, she slowly pushed onto the tips of her hind paws and let her eyes flutter back and forth from Peter’s eyes to his mouth before pressing her lips rigidly to his. Rocket could not tear his eyes away from the prettiness. They had mouths that worked right. He was vaguely aware of his claws tracing the metal implants in his cheeks, hidden now under his fur, and the only reasons he could manage speech in the first place. Their lips were like when they drank from cups, and he couldn’t move his like that. Couldn’t kiss with them if he tried.

With a long sigh, Rocket let himself sink the rest of the way down before curling away from the grate. Hugging his shoulders, he gazed at the darkness further down the duct where he ought to be headed. But the air was warm, and the song was vibrating through the metal that his ear was pressed into.
