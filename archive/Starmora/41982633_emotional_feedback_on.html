Chapter 1: Bare-Hand the WMD

Through one of the bubble-shaped windows in the Benatar’s lower level, the surface of the planet Muphurn shimmers with iridescence in the scant light of its only far-off star. They’re making their descent; the planet’s greenish white mountains and its bluish white oceans have begun to lift out of obscurity as they approach, and the ship gives a tremor as the gravitational field takes hold.

Loki leans back from the window, peering up the stairs toward the Benatar’s upper level, and he asks Mantis, louder than is strictly necessary:

“Do you think Quill’s flying with his feet again?”

Upstairs, Rocket lets out a barking laugh, and Quill shouts, “I heard that, you dick!”

“I’m aware!” Loki calls back.

“And this baby’s making a perfect landing,” Quill doubles down, speaking over Rocket’s heckling. “Friggin’ textbook smooth atmo-entry. I’d like to see you do any better!”

He hears, vaguely, Drax mentioning something about how, actually, the use of magic would likely improve the smoothness of a ship’s landing, which is followed immediately by Quill arguing that Loki doesn’t know how to fly an M-class ship in the first place, and then by Rocket loudly claiming that he is the only one here who’s capable of making a quote-unquote textbook smooth atmo-entry on a planet this size.

This, of course, devolves into a heated debate up in the cockpit that makes deciphering individual words impossible, and Loki smirks, stepping away from the window and approaching the exit ramp door.

Despite all the chaos upstairs, they should be touching down any minute now.

“Hopefully he’s managed to bring us somewhat near the target this time,” he says offhand to Mantis, leaning his hand against the door and drumming his fingers against it. “I’d rather we didn’t have to walk across half a planet to get there.”

He closes his eyes, focusing on the warm metal beneath his fingertips and the hum of energy from the planet below, but whatever’s radiating from the weapon they’re supposed to be collecting is still difficult to pinpoint. There’s unmistakably something magic about it—and specifically a magic that’s akin to Loki’s own, he can feel as much even from the upper reaches of the planet’s atmosphere, so the Nova Corps hadn’t been wrong or lying about how old this artifact was, at least.

But the energy is… diffuse. Low power, wide range. It’s anyone’s guess where on the planet it might be, at least until they get closer.

“Ah, well,” he sighs, dropping his hand away from the door. “It might be nice to stretch our legs for a bit. The planet looks fair enough.”

It occurs to him then, a bit belatedly, that Mantis hasn’t said a single word to him.

She’s been sitting on a crate of fuel pods for the last ten or so minutes, idly kicking her legs back and forth while they wait to land, and naturally, she has not been listening to a word he’s been saying. She’s still sitting on the same crate, not ten feet away, but she’s staring into space with a sort of far-off look on her face as if she’s quite literally gone somewhere else.

Loki clears his throat. She doesn’t notice.

“Mantis.”

Still nothing.

The look on her face is… It’s more than just far-off, he realizes. Eyes wide, brows pinched in the middle. Loki frowns, tilting his head to the side and watching her as he slowly crosses the distance between them, and then he reaches over and snaps his fingers in front of her face.

That works, at least. She jolts as she comes back to awareness, like she’d forgotten he was here at all. Or like she’d forgotten she was here at all.

“You alright?” Loki asks her. “You seemed a bit—” he waves a hand vaguely in front of his own face— “distracted.”

It’s as if he can see the way her thoughts reassemble themselves, clarity swimming back into her eyes like something physical. She blinks, shakes her head, smiles.

“Oh! Yes, I was distracted,” she says with a nod, smiling wide. Then, using the exact phrasing that Quill uses to describe it, she tells him, “I zoned out again. I didn’t mean to.”

“Mm-hmm,” he says, heavy on the skepticism.

It goes over her head, or she chooses to ignore it. It’s difficult to tell with her sometimes. “Have we landed on Muphurn yet?”

Loki opens his mouth to answer, but at that precise moment a muffled boom reverberates through the ship as it smacks into the ground with all the grace of a several ton Bilgesnipe. The floor quivers under his feet as the ship groans and scrapes its way across the ground for several drawn out seconds—he distinctly hears a box of metal scraps toppling over and clattering across the floor in the level above—before, finally, the ship slows to a rocking stop.

After another beat he says, “Apparently, yes.”

Their communicators click on, and Rocket’s voice comes through half a second later, midway through a sentence: “—veryone better make fun of Quill for that landing—”

His voice on the communicator is overlaid with the sound of his voice echoing from above in the cockpit, and immediately interrupted by Quill’s, both on the communicator and above as well: “I only had any trouble with it ‘cause you were being a douchebag, and you know it!”

Gamora steps down into the lower level, with Drax and Groot right behind her. She reaches up to tap her ear and turn off her own communicator, and she levels a bored glare at Loki.

“You did that on purpose,” she tells him.

“I have absolutely no idea what you’re talking about,” Loki says, offering a hand to Mantis to help her down from the crate, which she doesn’t need but takes anyway.

“I am Groot.”

“I do not goad them into arguing for my own entertainment, thank you, and quite frankly, I’m offended that you would think otherwise.”

Drax nods. “It is often very entertaining.”

“Isn’t it?” Loki asks.

“Okay!” Quill says as he gets into the lower level, doing a quick test-deploy of his helmet and then retracting it to be sure it’s in working order. While he’s checking his blasters, he asks, “Everyone all set for some ancient artifact hunting?”

“Honestly, it shouldn’t be much of a hunt,” Loki tells him. “We’re not even off the ship and I can already feel it.”

“Oh, good. Magic radar’s up and running?”

“It’s not radar,” Loki reminds him for the hundred-thousandth or so time, rolling his eyes. “But… yeah, more or less.”

Gamora asks, “Any thoughts on what it might be?”

“It’s certainly something with magical properties,” Loki says, crossing his arms and shrugging one shoulder. “Something with magic that’s at least distantly related to my own, from what I can tell. Could be of the same source the Aesir draw their magic from. Possibly. Conjecture’s about all I’ve got so far, though, aside from what we’ve already been told.”

“Which wasn’t much,” Gamora reminds him, and that’s true. The Nova Corps had only known what they’d heard secondhand through scouts and agents; namely, that whatever was being hidden on this planet was very old, that it was very valuable, and that it could be very dangerous in the wrong hands.

“I am Groot.”

“Yeah, we’re gonna find out,” Rocket agrees, coming down into the lower level and already preparing a weapon the size of his entire body as if they’re about to face a gargantuan beast the moment they step off the ship, “and maybe I’ll get a chance to use this baby, too.” “Let’s hope not,” Gamora says, and Mantis nods in agreement.

There’s a deep clunk as the exit ramp door begins to open, metallic plates shifting around each other in a staccato rhythm to reveal a wider and wider gap through which the bluish airlock forcefield and a vast whitish green plain beyond that is visible.

And when the forcefield begins to open—

Wait.

Something’s wrong.

Loki hears the telltale hiss of air rushing out of the ship and mixing with the new atmosphere outside, which itself is nothing at all unusual, except something—magic, instinct, nerves, paranoia, he has no idea what but something —registers as deeply, deeply wrong. A jolt of fear runs through him. His shoulders tense up. Seiðr ripples out of him to form a new forcefield, inches behind his own back, shimmering green and airtight from one end of the Benatar’s lower level to the other.

Silence reigns for the span of a heartbeat, then another.

He waits for something catastrophic to happen, and oddly enough, it doesn’t.

“Uh,” Quill’s voice comes from behind him, on the other side of the forcefield along with everyone else. “Loki?”

What—

What the hell had he sensed? What was wrong? Something was wrong, he can still feel it now, so what…?

He catches a glimpse of his own hands in his peripheral vision, and he immediately gets his answer. Oh, for God’s sake. He huffs, rolls his eyes, drops his hands. The forcefield remains exactly where he cast it, unwavering, keeping the utterly freezing air outside from reaching any of the others.

Utterly freezing, Loki can only assume, because his skin’s gone entirely blue.

“Did anyone,” Loki asks, turning toward them and planting his hands on his hips, “anyone at all, think to check the temperature on this planet before we’d landed?”

By turns, they all look toward Rocket, who drops his weapon and throws his hands up in the air. “What? Muphurn’s a temperate planet. What’s the problem?”

Quill gestures at Loki—blue-skinned, likely red-eyed as well if he had to wager a guess—and he says to Rocket, “Dude! What do you think?”

“I am Groot.”

“Yeah,” Quill says, “I’m gonna take a shot in the dark and say it’s probably a little colder out there than we were expecting.”

“A little?” Gamora asks, wrinkling her nose.

“It must be very cold,” Mantis says.

“I am Groot.”

“Why would I bother checking the frickin’ weather,” Rocket doubles down. “What, you morons can’t handle a little…” he glances past Loki at the terrain, and uncertainly finishes with, “… snow?”

“I can,” Drax says.

“A little snow,” Quill repeats under his breath, and he deploys his helmet again, peering up through the open exit ramp door past Loki’s forcefield. It takes him a second, reading through all the data that pours through that little contraption, and then he says, “Huh. Weird. It’s reading eighty-five.”

Rocket makes a face. “No way. You’re readin’ it wrong.”

“Oh. Wait,” Quill says. “That’s negative eighty-five. Terran Units, so that’s—” he taps another button on the side of his helmet— “two-oh-eight K.”

Gamora winces.

“Temperate, hm?” Loki asks, directing a pointed look at Rocket.

“Wait, wait, wait, hold on,” Quill speaks up again. He’s still got his helmet on. “It’s going up. We’re at, uh… 210… It’s going up about a Kelvin every thirty-some-odd seconds.”

“I am Groot?”

“Yeah,” Rocket says. “The hell kinda planet changes its temperature that quick?”

Loki frowns. He’s still got his left hand on his hip, and he lifts up his right, inspecting the back and front of it, the Jotunn claws at the tips of his fingers. It’s an odd sensation—made odder, still, by the fact that the effect seems to be fading. His skin’s begun to lose some of its blue hue, the nails already growing less black.

He drops his hand.

“It’s because this artifact we’re looking for isn’t Aesir,” Loki tells them, more certain of it now that he’s voiced the suspicion aloud. “Or Asgardian. This is a Jotunn weapon.”

It makes sense. He’s seen this before. The glamour’s only ever fallen away without his permission once, and that was the first time, the first time it ever fell at all, in the presence of the Casket of Ancient Winters—a weapon that could easily plunge even the most temperate of planets into its own ice age. There was at least one weapon capable of such a thing; stands to reason there might be another, somewhere, and that it would draw the attention of the Nova Corps at one point or another.

“That’s… inconvenient,” Loki sighs. “I suppose I’ll proceed alone from here, collect the weapon, and—”

“Nope,” Quill cuts him off, prompting Loki to roll his eyes again. “Nuh-uh. You know the rules, man, buddy system or nothing.”

“I’m sorry, are any of you capable of surviving temperatures this cold?”

“Uh, yeah, we are, thank you,” Quill argues at the same time that Drax grunts an affirmative.

“Really.”

“Dude,” Quill says, “you were literally right there the last time I was in temps this cold. And I was… you know. Fine.”

“Yes,” Loki deadpans. “Fine is exactly how I would describe it.”

“Seriously, man. Even Terrans can manage this kinda cold for a little while if we’re bundled up enough. And it’s still going up pretty quick,” he says, retracting his helmet. “At this rate we’ll be at breezy fall day weather in, like, ten minutes.”

“Ten—” Rocket says, shooting a look at Quill. “Who taught you math?”

“Whatever’s happening out here,” Loki speaks over both of them, ignoring the brand new asinine argument that that’s going to start, “it’s almost certainly been caused by this weapon being detonated on the planet, and for all we know, we could have miles to traverse before we reach it. What happens if it goes off again before we do?”

Quill shrugs. “Like I said, we’ll bundle up.”

“If that were to happen,” Gamora cuts in before Loki can object, “would you be able to do this again?”

She waves at the forcefield, and Loki hesitates, because— well, yes, technically he could. A forcefield is hardly a complicated spell. Hell, he just conjured this one on what amounted to sheer panicked muscle memory alone, and before he’d even realized why he was doing it in the first place.

And yet, if it happens while they’re out there, if he stops paying attention for even a moment…

He heaves a sigh. These idiots are coming with him no matter what he says, he knows that, so there’s not much to be done for it. Just don’t stop paying attention, he thinks, steadfastly ignoring the way that thought pokes at the tangled ball of nerves sitting against his ribs. Easy enough, right?

“Fine,” he relents.

And, since his skin’s nearly back to its usual color and it’s probably more-or-less safe, he releases the spell, allowing a gust of freezing air to rush into the Benatar’s lower level. Groot and Rocket both shout at him for it. Mantis lets out a startled little eep and plasters herself to Drax’s side. Quill tugs his jacket collar up to cover his ears and mutters oh holy shit. And Gamora winces again, though it’s likely out of sympathy for the others; she should be essentially unaffected by it.

“Yeah,” Loki says, directing a particularly pointed look at Quill, who’s already backing up toward the cockpit and looking about ready to rescind everything he said about how well he can manage cold weather. “Bundle up.”

All things considered, Peter thinks Muphurn isn’t actually half bad.

Sure, the air’s cold enough to hurt his lungs a teensy bit every time he inhales. And sure, he’s getting a little bit of déjà vu from the way Jotunn-generated cold feels just a little bit off compared to the real deal, less like regular cold weather and more like the vacuum of space when your suit’s a little on the wrong side of too cheap. But the effect of that weapon keeps on wearing off, and it’s not windy at least, and Peter’s a hell of a lot better dressed for the cold this time around than he was before.

“It’s kinda pretty, isn’t it?” he asks Gamora, nudging her as they make their way over a snow- dusted hill, and she hums in agreement.

“Course Gamora frickin’ likes it,” Rocket complains from where he’s sitting hunched in on himself on Groot’s shoulder, all wrapped up in a Rocket-sized coat that’s even fluffier than Peter’s is. “I’d probably like it too if I had bionic frickin’ skin.”

“I am Groot.”

“Seriously? You, too?”

“I also like it,” Drax says, looking around at the wide open glittery white fields and the snow- capped trees peeking over every horizon. The place they’re headed for is in plain view just a hundred yards or so ahead, a towering castle of crystalline blue-and-white built into the side of a mountain—which, actually, now that Peter’s looking at it and they’re getting a little closer, that may or may not actually be part of a building.

If that’s the detonation spot for this weapon they’re looking for, then all that ice is probably as unnatural as the rest of this snow.

“Sort of looks like…” Peter says, miming a little explosion with his hands, “I dunno, like an ice bomb went off.”

“That is likely exactly what happened,” Gamora says.

“It is pretty,” Mantis admits, tugging her hood tighter around the sides of her face. She keeps having to adjust it when her antennae knock it back.

Gamora nods. “It is.”

“I am Groot.”

Drax grunts. “It is indeed aesthetically pleasing. I only wish I did not have to wear this coat.”

“Oof,” Peter wrinkles his nose. “Nipples bothering you again, bud?”

Gamora groans. “Can we please not talk about that again?”

From the back of the group, Loki adds, “For what must be the tenth time, Drax, you were more than welcome to remain behind on the Benatar.”

“I could not do that. This weapon could be dangerous.”

“It is dangerous,” Loki corrects him as they come up on the cave’s entrance, and—

Ooh, yeah. Definitely way less castle-y than it looked from a ways back, and much more like a bunch of ice erupted at random out of the cave entrance. The ice radiates out from that central spot like the points on a massive crystal star, and the steam gently wafting out is cold enough that Peter can feel it from outside.

He lets out a low whistle. Goddamn.

“Which,” Loki goes on, apparently unimpressed, “is why this planet’s entirely changed climates, and why this cave is entirely frozen over, and why you all look like you’ve fallen into a vat of feathers and fur. But a bit of discomfort is far better than freezing to death, I’d imagine.”

“Man,” Peter says, shaking his head, even though he’s not really looking at Loki anymore, too busy taking in their new surroundings as they step inside. “It’s like you don’t even care about Drax’s nipples.”

“I’m not sure the words exist to describe just how little I care about Drax’s nipples.”

“That’s fair,” Gamora agrees.

“I am Groot.”

“Wow,” Mantis says. “Look at this place.”

“Yeah, you’re telling me,” Peter says.

It’s not all that big of a cave, probably not even wide enough for the Benatar to fit inside, but the inside of it is friggin’ gorgeous. Bright blues and purples and greens run like ribbons through the trippy fractal reflections all over the ceiling and walls and the floor. Peter can see four, five, six, seven repeats of all of them in teeny tiny images all over. It’s bright as all hell in here, too; it even, at certain angles, looks like Mantis’ antennae are lighting up.

Gamora, apparently reading his mind, asks, “Where is all this light coming from?”

“From outside,” Loki answers, sounding distracted. “Ice can do strange things to light.”

Yeah, apparently. Peter squints, wrinkling his nose. With his eyes he follows the path of the ice, which isn’t difficult since it all seems to radiate out of the same central point right at the back of the cave, and he stops short.

“Eugh,” he says, wincing. “That’s a dead guy, isn’t it?”

Loki steps up beside him, like he’d been intending to walk straight through the group and put them all behind him on his way to investigate, but he stops, too. He’s staring ahead at the same thing Peter is, the same thing they’re all staring at now, some twenty or thirty feet away toward the back of the cave. It’s a roughly person-shaped mass of ice— very roughly. It’s covered in so much of the stuff that Peter hadn’t even registered it as anything separate from all the rest of their surroundings at first, the crystal shards of it fanning out from his back and his head and his arms like massive porcupine spikes.

“It is,” Loki answers. He doesn’t look away from it. There’s still just the littlest bit of red showing in his eyes, Peter notices, but that’s about it as far as the Frost Giant-ness goes right now.

Drax frowns, tilting his head. “It does not look like a dead guy.”

“That’ll be all the ice, bud,” Peter says. “Froze himself up good.”

“Oh, no,” Mantis says, antennae drooping.

“Yeesh,” Rocket says, and he hops off Groot’s shoulder, shuffling over with a little device in his hands that keeps beep beep beeping faster and faster the closer he gets to the guy— at least until one of Loki’s magic shields goes ahead and shimmers into existence right in his path, and he smacks right into it.

“Ow,” he yells, rubbing his nose with his free hand, “you krutackin’ son of a—!”

“Don’t go walking right up to it, you idiot,” Loki practically hisses at him, the shield dissipating as he steps past Rocket.

“Why?” Rocket yells, waving at it. “It’s just a dead guy! We’ve all seen a dead guy.” “Not him,” Loki says, and he’s right up on the guy now. “What he’s holding.”

Gamora asks, “And that would be…?”

“Yeah,” Peter agrees, squinting again. With all the ice, he can barely make out the shape of the guy at all, let alone anything he might be holding. “What is it?”

“Is it the weapon?” Drax asks.

Rocket, apparently having forgotten that quick about Loki making him run into a magic shield, says, “It’s a weapon alright, and it packs a frickin’ punch.” He’s looking down at the device in his hands, then he holds it up in the air like a camera. That thing might as well be spouting off specs in a secret code; Peter can’t make heads or tails of what it’s picking up, but Rocket sure can. “Oh, yeah. I could make one hell of a bomb out of this.”

“We’re being paid to recover it, Rocket, not scrap it,” Gamora reminds him. “Loki? What is it?”

Loki’s busy inspecting the dead guy from a little closer up. Without turning around, he asks, “Are any of you Frost Giants?”

“We are not,” Drax answers.

Peter shakes his head. “Yeah, last I checked, you’re still the only one here.”

“Well, then I suppose it’s a weapon of mass destruction that, if any of you were touching it while it detonated, would freeze you from the inside out down to… probably just about Absolute Zero within the span of a few seconds, judging by our friend here,” Loki says, waving vaguely at the dead guy. “And then, if I had to wager a guess, it would result in an unstoppable cascade that would freeze everything around you for quite a large radius, freezing anything in its path and rendering all life moot.”

“Oh, wow.”

“Rocket, please try sounding a little less turned on by that,” Peter tells him.

Gamora asks, “And if we were Frost Giants?”

Loki tilts his head, still inspecting the dead guy. “Then I imagine it would make quite a lovely paperweight.”

“So, we’re… assuming it’s safe for you to touch it?” Gamora asks.

Peter puts hands on his hips. “I dunno, dude, maybe you should let Rocket run a few tests before you— and he’s grabbing the ice bomb.”

“Loki!” Mantis shouts.

“I am Groot!”

“Relax,” Loki says. He’s already rolled up his sleeve, and now he’s working at prying the little hourglass-shaped thing out of the dead guy’s frozen fingers. The guy must have been holding it up in the air, because it’s right around at Loki’s eye level. “Now that I’m this close to it, it’s far less dangerous to any of you. If it goes off, I’ll simply banish it to the same pocket dimension where I keep all my weapons.”

Peter throws his hands up. “What about you, man? I mean, why not banish it there now instead of just grabbing the damn thing?”

Loki pauses, looking over at them. “Well. Because my weapons are in there.”

“And?”

“And I’d rather not risk damaging them except as a last resort,” Loki says, like that should have been obvious. “Some of them are quite valuable.”

“Right,” Peter says. “So you can’t risk your fancy knives, but you can bare-hand the WMD no problem.”

Loki nods, giving the thing one final tug; the dead guy’s fingers break off with a crack that would be a lot less gross if it wasn’t, y’know, a dead guy’s fingers. Loki doesn’t pay a whole lot of mind to that, though. He turns the weapon over, inspecting both sides of it—his hand has gone full on Frost Giant now, all deep blue with cold steam wafting off his knuckles. The skin of his arm is just as blue all the way up to where he rolled his sleeve up, and his sleeve’s crackling with a bit of ice, too.

That’s about where it ends, though. His face and the rest of him is still a hundred percent Asgardian.

“There, see?” Loki says, flashing them a smile. “Easy.”

He waves the thing in demonstration. It sort of looks like it’s made out of glass, if glass was jet black and a little concerningly glowy, but it doesn’t explode or render them all into icicles like Mr. Porcupine over there, so Peter guesses it must be fine.

“That actually was pretty easy, yeah,” Peter admits.

Drax says, “It is a shame this frozen man was not a Frost Giant, too.”

“I am Groot?”

“I dunno,” Peter shrugs. “I guess he just accidentally set it off, kiddo.”

“I am Groot?”

“Whaddaya mean, why’d he have it here in the first place?” Rocket asks. “It’s probably worth more than the frickin’ Benatar. He must’ve stole it.”

“Perhaps he lived here,” Drax says.

“In this empty cave with no furniture in it?” Gamora asks, and Drax nods.

“He was obviously hiding it here,” Peter says. “Safe spot to keep it until he could come back and sell it.”

“I do not know,” Mantis speaks up.

All of them look toward her. She’s suddenly started walking toward Loki and the frozen dead guy, but her eyes stay fixed curiously on the latter as she tilts her head, looking him over from the tips of those icicles shooting out of his back to his newly emptied, outstretched hands. In the weird light, it still sort of looks like her antennae are glowing, and that’s probably why Peter thinks for a second that she’s gonna reach out and touch the guy.

She doesn’t, which is good, because Peter has no idea whether she’d instantly freeze to death if she did.

“He does not look like he was trying to hide it,” Mantis says, pointing at his outstretched hands. “He looks like he was trying to use it.”

“Use it?” Rocket asks. “What, like he was testing it out?”

“That… doesn’t seem likely,” Gamora says. “Most people don’t set off incredibly powerful bombs for no reason other than to see what they can do.”

“Most people don’t know how to have a good time.”

“I mean, she’s right, though,” Peter says. “The guy was literally holding the super ice bomb in his hands, so why would anybody…?”

He trails off, because just then, there’s a faint tremor in the ground under their feet. It echoes a bit, a deep rolling rumble like distant thunder that goes on for several seconds and which sort of sounds like it’s coming from somewhere beyond the back of the cave, somewhere deeper inside the mountain.

Well. Shit.

Peter sighs, dropping his hands and warily eyeing the back wall of the cave. “This nice easy mission is about to get a whole lot harder, isn’t it?”

“Why do you say that?” Drax asks.

“‘Cause I got a weird feeling that whatever our frozen dead guy was trying to use the ice bomb on…”

“We’re about to meet it,” Gamora finishes for him, already reaching for Godslayer.

“Yeah,” Peter nods, “so maybe we should—”

The rumble amps up, and there, right at the back of the cave where Mr. Corpse-sicle was pointing his ice bomb, a massive crack splits through the ice from floor to ceiling. “— RUN!”

ON A PLANE OUTSIDE OF TIME…

When the crap hits the fan at the TVA, B-15 catches on to what’s happening before Mobius does.

’Cause the thing is, it starts slow. Nebula starts standing. “If you tell us he’s back already—”

“Uh, nah,” Kraglin says, peering down at the console. “Nah, he still ain’t here. But we got a planet comin’ up.”

It was a rhetorical question, apparently, since he’s already flipping a switch on the console to check for himself.

“I mean, it’s breathable, I guess,” Peter says, tipping his head from side to side as he thinks. “Might not be too bad, but I was kinda hoping for a planet with, y’know, people on it.”

It will be another twenty minutes before the Quadrant’s systems are able to see that, yes, there are in fact heat signatures on the planet they’re approaching. It will be another five minutes after that before the ship’s systems are close enough to make a very rough estimate at the planet’s population: Based on those heat signatures, somewhere between one million and ten million. Small, for an entire planet, but still nothing to scoff at.

Mantis, sitting alone on her bunk half a ship away from the others, already knows this now.

Somehow.

She feels odd. She’s felt odd, for days and even weeks now, and she thought—hoped—that it might pass, but instead it’s only gotten worse.

She just wishes she knew what it was. All she knows is that it’s an awful, tense feeling in her stomach that’s steadily grown like a black hole sucking up everything around it and getting heavier with each passing day, with each passing hour. It’s a nervousness that sends her heart thudding, skipping, far faster than it ever should. It’s not so much an ache in her head but a… humming? A humming. A buzz. It’s like— It’s like the way solar energy ripples over the windshield of the Benatar when they skirt too close to a star.

The way light bounces. That’s how her brain feels.

A soft knock jolts her from her thoughts, and when she looks up, she sees Loki standing in the doorway. He’s leaning one shoulder against the doorframe, arms loosely crossed, his eyes on her.

He looks worried— but that makes sense, because he is worried. They all are.

“Are you alright?”

It is the same question he asked when they were approaching Muphurn, and this time, Mantis doesn’t have it in her to lie. This time she only shrugs.

She watches as, slowly, like he’s trying not to but he can’t quite help it, he lets his eyes drift just slightly up to her antennae. She knows they’re glowing. She can see the light glaring off the ceiling. She has no idea how to stop it.

“Right,” Loki says, carefully.

He wants to say something else, but he’s not sure what to say, she can tell.

“Can you—?” Mantis starts to ask him. “Can you… feel anything? Anything… off?”

She knows immediately that the answer is no. A look of sympathy—guilt, maybe—comes over him, and he lets out a quiet breath, his shoulders dropping with it. He shakes his head. “I’m sorry,” he says, “but what I can do isn’t anything like what you can do. If what you can do even is magic, then I’m afraid it’s nothing like any magic I’ve ever studied.”

Mantis nods. She expected that, but she needed to ask.

“Can I come in?”

Mantis nods again. She uncrosses her legs so she can pull her knees up to her chest and give him space to sit. With one hand she reaches up to tug at her right antenna, but that does nothing to stop it glowing. It just hurts, so she stops.

Loki steps away from the door frame and makes his way toward her, turning and sitting slowly on the edge of her bunk.

After a moment he says, “I don’t suppose you have any idea what it is that’s bothering you.”

Mantis shakes her head.

“Hm.” He wrinkles his nose. “I thought as much. Can you describe it?”

She can, probably, if she really tries to. She shakes her head anyway.

Loki seems to take that well enough, and he asks, “How long has it been going on?”

“Um…” Mantis says, thinking back. “I don’t know. Months?”

That startles him. His eyes widen, and he does an almost funny double take. Almost.

“Months? That long? Since before Urunia? Before Thanos?”

“No! No, not that long. Not before Urunia, I don’t… I don’t think,” she says, frowning, because it’s so difficult to know when exactly she started feeling so odd. “It wasn’t bad when it started. It’s only the past few days that it’s gotten worse.”

The startled look fades somewhat. He nods, slowly, then says, “And that’s why you’ve been so distracted as of late.”

She nods.

“Alright. That’s— troubling, but… alright. We’re approaching a planet now,” he tells her, and she refrains from telling him that she already knows they are. “Quill’s hoping we’ll be able to take you to see some sort of medical professional, either there or at the next planet or the planet after that, but to be perfectly honest, I think it’s a bad idea. Pointless, anyway.”

Again, Mantis nods. For a brief, ridiculous moment, she feels like that one doll that Kraglin superglued to the Quadrant’s dashboard, the one with the orange hair and the big plastic head that bobs up and down every time they hit some turbulence.

“I’m wondering…” Loki says, but he trails off, hesitates, like maybe he thinks he shouldn’t tell her what it is he’s wondering after all. Then, apparently, he decides to just come out with it. “I’m wondering if there’s something I can do.”

“You— what?” Mantis perks up. “Do you think there is?”

Loki squints and tilts his head to the side. Maybe, that gesture says, but maybe not. He turns one hand up, his right, and he gazes pensively down at his open palm.

“There’s a spell,” Loki tells her. “It might help, but I won’t pretend to have any idea whether it truly will or not. If anything, I think it might… help me understand a bit more, and that knowledge might in turn put me in a slightly better position to help you. I suppose that’s better than no one understanding or knowing how to help you at all, which of course is the position we’re in right now, but—”

“Yes,” Mantis says, already frantically nodding. “Do it.”

Loki raises an eyebrow at her. “Are you—?”

“Yes! I’m sure! I don’t know what’s happening to me, and I’m scared, and—”

“Alright,” he says, gently cutting her off, both hands up in surrender. He slowly lowers his hands into his lap. “Alright. If it might help, I’ll try.”

He pulls his legs up onto the bunk, too, turning to face her.

“I should warn you,” he says. “The last… well, every time I’ve used this spell, I’ve used it as a weapon. Once against Drax, when we first met, and if you remember, that unnerved him to the point that he refused to go anywhere near me for quite a while unless it was absolutely necessary. And the time before that, the person I used it on was so angry when it was over that she immediately punched me in the face and knocked me unconscious.”

“I won’t punch you.”

Loki huffs a laugh, smiling. “I appreciate that.”

“And you’re not using it as a weapon this time,” Mantis says, because that’s obvious. “You’re using it to help.”

An odd look comes over him then, but only for a moment. Mantis can’t figure out what it means before it’s gone. “So I am,” he says. “But Mantis, what I’m saying is that regardless of my intent, this spell can be… unpleasant. Unsettling.”

“It’s alright. I trust you.”

“Wonders never cease,” Loki says, again with that odd look on his face. He takes a slow breath and huffs it out as a sigh. “You’re absolutely sure?”

“Yes.”

“Then here goes nothing, I suppose,” he says. “Brace yourself.”

She does. Physically, anyway. She’s not sure how to mentally brace herself, but she straightens her shoulders, fills up her lungs, tries to relax each muscle from the tips of her fingers down to her toes, tension releasing. That’s all she can do. There’s nothing to be done about the big ball of tension in her stomach, or about the buzzing in her brain. Not yet. He lays his palm against her forehead like he’s checking for a fever, and for just a split second, all she gets is the perfectly normal surge of another person’s emotions swelling to overlap with hers.

This much, at least, is familiar. For nearly as long as Mantis has known him, Loki has always felt like this, like a soft background hum of concern and fear and nerves underneath everything else, because he worries for them, all of them, far more than he likely will ever admit aloud. Far more than he would likely be alright with her knowing about.

But in this split second, she can feel that the background hum has surged up to nearly swallow everything else. She can feel that he hasn’t been able to set it down, this worry, these frayed nerves, since she went under the ice on Muphurn however many hours ago.

Then the split second is over, and—

“Oh, Mantis.”

It’s warm. Warmer than the Quadrant ever is. Uncomfortably warm.

Her vision has gone fuzzy, and when it clears, she’s standing near the edge of a cliff that overlooks a beautiful stretch of rolling hills, green-blue-purple-orange grass rippling in the breeze.

Beside her, Ego stands and watches, too.

He looks like he’s admiring the view as well, but that wouldn’t make sense, would it? Then he would only be admiring himself.

That makes perfect sense, she thinks for reasons she can’t identify, and her heart skips a little faster, and her veins feel a little colder. She is afraid of him, of Ego, and she cannot remember why. He’s practically her father, isn’t he? He’s been raising her all her life, hasn’t he?

“Mantis, Mantis, Mantis. You were just a lonely little larva when I picked you up,” Ego says. “No one around that wanted you, no one left that loved you. And now look at you, huh?”

He turns, directing a half-smile at her that’s all charm and all teeth.

“Time flies,” Ego says, turning back toward the scenery, toward himself, and Mantis suddenly knows what’s coming next, she knows what’s going to keep coming next for years and years and years and years, and she wants so badly to stop it but she can’t and—

“Guess we better head out, kiddo,” he says as she begins to panic, but he can’t see it. He’s only a memory. “Time to see if that spark got passed on somewhere, huh?”

Piles and piles of bones filling up the caves beneath the planet’s surface—

No, no no no—

Please, not again—

There’s a tug beneath her diaphragm, and Ego’s gone.

She’s sitting on the steps with Drax and he’s telling her about his daughter, he’s saying she was like you, she was innocent but Mantis isn’t innocent, she’s not, and the love radiating off of him makes her so inexplicably sad—

A million lifetimes—

Cataclysmic chaos—

She’s in a massive circular room, a library, an office maybe, with high vaulted ceilings and a single desk directly in front of her, and it’s so dark she can barely see except by the blue- violet-black light coming in through the windows (space? is that space?) and there’s someone else here but he’s—

It’s okay it’s okay this is Loki’s magic it’s just Loki’s magic it’s just—

That’s what the feeling was, that’s what the feeling is, they’re scared, they’re terrified, and that feeling sinks into her veins and shrivels her lungs and sends her brain buzzing buzzing buzzing and screaming and—

The return to reality is so instant that it’s disorienting.

It takes a moment for her to realize it’s real.

Her heart thudding away. Her own breath coming in and out in heaving gasps. Her bunk. Her room. The dim light from the hallway fanning in through the open door. The stars outside her porthole.

And Loki, directly in front of her, his hand hovering between them and a look of dumbstruck shock on his face. He’s too pale. His eyes are wide. He’s out of breath, too, every bit as much as she is. His hand trembles until he finally drops it, but he does not look away from her.
