The familiar scent of antiseptic lingered in the air as she waited, looking at the clock on the wall that seemed to tick away far too slowly, a countdown to when the door would open and the doctor would walk in with her embryo and transfer it in her.

Rio tried to keep her focus, but her thoughts kept drifting back to all the times she had been told that motherhood wasn’t in the cards for her. She experienced the disappointment and felt the sting of failure when the adoption process hadn’t gone through, when being a foster parent only led to constant heartbreak. It wasn’t easy, but it wasn’t entirely unfamiliar either. Growing up in the system herself, she had spent most of her life coming to terms with dreams that didn’t pan out and learned to temper her expectations early. She had learned a lot over the years and has let go of so many things rolling with the punches — but the dream of motherhood? That had never changed. It had always been there, tucked inside her, waiting.

Though, Rio had never imagined it would come down to this: a single embryo, the final one in her collection, a last chance. This embryo held the promise of what she had always wanted.

Today would be the day, she thought. Or at least that was the energy she was manifesting out in the world.

“Good morning, Ms. Vidal. My name is Dr. Ralph Bohner and I will be the one doing the procedure today.”

Nothing. Not even an eye roll. Instead, Agatha’s jaw flexed again — like she was holding something back.

Rio gave a sigh and tested how much weight she could put on her without seeming like she was leaning too much when they took the next step.

Another step.

Another pause.

The uncomfortable sensation was no longer just from her ankle but was starting to build in her somewhere in her chest. There goes another thing to add to whatever list of reasons why Agatha was pulling away.

Agatha’s jaw worked for a bit before she spoke. “I didn’t mind.”

You can just take my car.” Rio freed her car keys from her keychain and offered it to Agatha. “I’m obviously not going to be using it for the next few days.” Rio raised her foot to emphasized her injury. “Plus, it’s the least I can do for you for all your help. I’d rather you take the car than you pay for a ride home.”

The aroma of Italian spices released combated the smell of the mountain of flowers next to it. She served Rio a slice on plate before getting one for herself. Soon, they were each nibbling on their own slice of pizza. They underestimated their hunger, practically inhaling their initial slice without much conversation at first.

“Personally, I still wouldn’t wait in a long line for food. But, man, this is a good pie.” Rio said, reaching to grab another slice. “You’re forgiven for the lack of pineapple.”

Agatha preened, pleased that she was getting a return for the time she sacrificed in line in the form of Rio enjoying the meal. “They'll probably ban me for even ordering it considering how much of a New Yorker Pizza Poppa is,” Agatha said in between bites, “but, sure, I'll beg them to include it next time since you're so adamant in asking for it.”

“Too bad for him we’re not in New York.” Rio critically shook her head. “I don't think it's worth giving patronage to a business that bans you over such a small thing— especially one with a long ass line. We can go look for another place t—” Rio was interrupted by the chime of the shop door opening, mouth clamping shut as they turned their attention to the entrance.

Alice entered armed with a plastic bag carrying a brace and rolls of bandages. The look on her face an indication that she was prepared to scold her oldest friend for being on her feet still tender from injury instead of resting and elevating her leg as she should. She comically halted, leg half-raised, just inside the doorway at the sight of her friend’s company.

“Oh, uh, I didn't realize someone else was joining us.” Alice raised an inquisitive brow at Rio. She continued walking in, seeing the greased paper plates when she approached them. “...I see I’m late to dinner.”

Rio straightened at once, setting her slice down on her plate and gestured quickly between her two guests. “You’ve already met in passing, but Agatha, this is Alice. A close friend of mine. Alice, this is Agatha. She’s...” She glanced at Agatha as if checking for permission.

“I brought pizza.” Agatha said, meeting Alice’s curious gaze neutrally urbane. “Would you like a slice? There's plenty left.”

“Maybe later.” The amusement on Alice's face at her casualness was clear as day. “Don’t mind me. As you guys were.”

Alice placed the bag of stuff next to the pizza box and pretended to busy herself unpacking it and setting the health supplies aside. She relished the opportunity of being able to have a glimpse of their dynamic, making an attempt to blend to the background like a fly on a wall and keeping a subtle eye on the pair.

The exchange of words between Agatha and Rio began again with a little less effort than before.

Alice considered them both. Noticing how Agatha leaned slightly forward toward Rio when listening. How Rio’s voice softened when speaking directly to Agatha, the same way it would when she spoke to Lilia or any of Rio’s previous foster children she still kept in touch with. How neither of them was cognizant of their instinctive adjustment to each other’s pace of pausing to take a bite and trying to keep conversation. There was the careful breadth of counter separating them, and their words were not intimate, but a thrum in the air and an invisible thread stretched taut between them existed.

Up close, she was beginning to understand the fragility Rio mentioned about Agatha. But not in the way she expected.

It was the smallest of looks. The way Agatha’s attention clung to Rio’s mouth when she spoke. The way Agatha tried to swallow a faint, coy smile when Rio made a commentary about battle scars. The way the tiny muscles tightened in Agatha’s jaw every time Rio grimaced when she shifted her foot.

Oh. There was something there. It wasn't straightforward, but it was unmistakably present — like the show of tiny bubbles in heated water before it rolled into an intense boil.

A certain serenity settled over the flower shop, a sort of warm exhaustion that came after something unexpectedly good. It was gentle despite the oscillation of the gravitational pull and retreat that ensued between the two particular persons.

Suddenly felt akin to an intruder, Alice decided to excuse herself now that her hands had nothing to do and let the delicate whatever is happening in the midst of the two to proceed in private. “Well, you guys continue enjoying the 'zza.” She pointed at the supplies on the counter. “I'm going to leave those there for later and start setting up the cot.” Alice rounded the counter to walk behind Rio and disappeared to the back of the shop.

“I thought the only room back there was an office?” There was a smidge of concern the moment realization dawned on Agatha. “Don't tell me you're planning on staying down here for the night?”

“There’s enough room to set up a cot,” was Rio's relaxed answer.

“What about when you need to use the restroom or take a shower?”

“You know it’s not like I can easily move up and down the stairs, Agatha.” Rio snorted as she waved to her foot. “Besides, it's a flower shop. I can just hose down here near the watering station and place an ‘out of order’ sign outside the customer restroom. I’ll keep it locked so only I can access it.” She gave a lopsided grin when Agatha's face remained stuck on disbelief. “It’s not the first time I’ve had to live like this. I had to do the same thing while the upstairs unit was being renovated into a living space. The cot's actually a lot more comfortable than you think.”

“I highly doubt that.”

“You're free to lay on it and have a feel if you don't believe me,” Rio offhandedly suggested.

“It's not the cot that I'm worried about.”

Agatha thought Rio looked stupefied, as if to suggest her statement was bizarre and something so out of left field. Her chin tensed and the corners of her mouth going downwards as the earlier sense of offense returned. Was her concern really that unexpected?

Rio’s face quickly composed, giving her a short nod. “...I'll be fine. Thank you, Agatha.”

It felt kind of like a polite ending. Agatha could accept the gratitude without pressing further, but she was unsatisfied. Their conversation had been on its way to leaving the awkward territory, but it’s now going slightly wrong and she didn’t want this to be its conclusion. She sighed, long and unsteady, and said the first thing that came to her. “We’re good, right?”

After today's events, the question made Agatha feel like the biggest hypocrite — especially considering it was as if she was in the middle of conducting an ambush with the intention of making sure Rio still thought of her positively after today. Agatha wished she could take it back the moment it left her mouth, but it had already found its mark. Rio had gone still, blinking as her brows pulled together slightly.

For a heartbeat, Rio didn’t answer. Then, “...is there a reason why we wouldn’t be?”

Agatha had no idea what she expected her answer to be. She opened her mouth to respond, but it felt strangely dry and nothing came out.

Rio's voice had gone careful, measured as though she was tiptoeing on a frozen lake that threatened to crack under her with every step forward. The idea of Rio being uneasy because of something she had done or failed to say made its way back to the forefront of her mind. It was accompanied by another: the possibility Rio might eventually decide Agatha was too inconsistent and too prickly, requiring too much effort and too difficult to keep close.

Her fingers itched to clutch at something to distract herself from the image of Rio pulling back, the tiny threads of doubt continued to creep in like an invasive vine.

But then Rio’s eyes met Agatha’s. Really met hers.

It was a direct and steady gaze that stopped the thoughts cold. They scanned her with that careful, deliberate attention she always had. There was no sign of offense in Rio’s eyes, just a gentle search to try and understand. As if Rio was waiting — though, Agatha didn’t seem to know exactly what she was waiting for.

Her instinct was to avert her eyes, but something intuitively told her not to. Agatha closed her mouth, swallowed, and then opened it again. There was no reason to over-think this. She told the truth in the least vulnerable way as possible: “No. I’d say we’re good.”

Rio held Agatha's gaze for a bit longer, checking for a catch hidden inside her reply. For the second time that evening, something unspoken by Agatha was accepted and the tension in Rio’s face relaxed enough and her eyes seemed to brighten a bit. “That's good,” she said softly.

The sight of it warmed Agatha and a sense of relief washed over her and leaving her feeling a bit lightheaded. She felt her mouth curve upward and had to look down before her face could betray too much of that feeling, passing it off as a nod. “Yeah,” Agatha agreed as if everything was settled. “Good.”

There was still a perceived distance, but it was no longer the slow, separating drift it had been earlier in the day.

As though she had been biding her time, Alice returned from the back and easily slid in to stand beside Rio. Her reappearance acted as a signal that their time together was nearing its end, expressions failing to hide their disappointment. Alice had a laugh to herself and nudged her friend with her elbow. “Cot’s all set. Did you need anything else upstairs aside from clothes and toiletries?”

While Rio pondered on her list of demands, Agatha worked to clear the counter of the remnants of their dinner. She consolidated the uneaten crusts onto one plate before stacking the other underneath and dumping it into a trashcan located at the end of the counter. When she turned her attention back to the two, the smile Alice flashed at Agatha seemed obnoxiously all-knowing, making her feel like a child caught doing something they shouldn’t.

Agatha ran a hand through her hair, squaring her shoulders. “I should probably get going. Sounds like you two have lots of things to do,” she said as soon as there was a break in their discussion.

Rio looked at an analog clock hanging on the wall. “I didn’t realize it was already this late. You should go. We had a pretty early start to the day.” She said in agreement.

Agatha nodded, standing and brushing off some crumbs that fell on her lap. She stopped patting near her pockets, feeling something in them. “Oh, right. I should give these back to you.”

She reached into her pocket and pulled out car keys, earning Rio yet another brow raise from Alice.

“I thought you were going to return them tomorrow.”

Agatha shrugged and said matter-of-factly, “I’m already here. Might as well return it now.”

Alice didn’t miss the way Rio seemed to deflate at the sight of Agatha placing her car keys on the counter. She stepped forward and brightly provided a compromise. “How about I drive you back to your place?”

Agatha shook her head immediately. “No, it’s okay.”

“I really don’t mind.”

“I know, but it’s out of your way.” Agatha hesitated, glancing between them. “You already seem pretty busy helping Rio out with getting her settled down here.”

“The main parts have already been done,” Alice insisted. “Plus, this way you don’t have to try to fit it in your schedule to return Rio’s keys to her. Saves you the trouble of having to figure out how to get back, too. Rides can take a while to get here sometimes at this hour.”

Agatha could tell she was fighting a losing battle. She couldn’t think of any further rebuttal that would have been reasonable.

“That settles it.” Alice grinned when she said nothing, grabbing the keys off the counter.

“If it makes you feel better, Alice drives like a grandma. You’d be very safe.” Rio lightly said to ease Agatha who appeared like she was sulking as if she had just lost a trial.

“I’m right here,” Alice muttered. “I’ll have you know, most of my traffic stops have been the elderly so I take offense to that comparison. I’m just cautious on the road. You can never know what the other person is going to do. There’s nothing wrong with being a careful driver.”

“Still doesn’t mean you have to barely drive the speed limit. Flow of traffic is a thing you know.” Rio reached for her cane where it leaned against the counter, getting ready to stand.

“Nope, you stay.” Alice placed a firm hand on Rio’s shoulders, forcing her to sit back down. “We don't all need to go.” She smirked wryly, interrupting Rio before she had the chance to object. “It's about time you rest. Why don't you stay here and use those to wrap your foot?” She looked pointedly at the supplies she had laid out next to the pizza box. “I’ll be outside to start the car.”

They gawked at her as she began to walk out of the shop, tossing the keys and playing catch with it.

A soft tickled huff came from Rio the moment Alice walked out of the door. “Sorry about her, she means well.”

“I know someone she would get along with.” Agatha snorted and corrected herself, “Two people actually.”

“I have an idea who one of them may be.” Rio gave a half-suppressed laugh as a wiry young man came to mind. She grabbed the cane, rose slowly, and managed to maneuver herself from behind the counter and motioned to the door. “We should go before she starts using that horn. I don’t want people to come knocking about the noise.”

Agatha’s eyes flicked to Rio’s swollen ankle, to the cane, then quickly back up to avoid seeming like she was staring. She clasped her hands behind her back, almost shy. She stayed close behind, ready if needed but not stepping in unless asked as Rio hobbled towards the shop entrance, steadying herself with her cane.

They made their way outside together, greeted by the cool and crisp air of the night and streetlights flickering on one by one.

Alice was already inside the car, seated behind the wheel. She lowered the window and stuck her head out to purposely shake it in displeasure at the sight of her friend not following her instruction. “Seriously, Rio? What did I say?”

“Relax. This is the farthest I’ll go.” Rio made a show of positioning herself by the shop entry, resting both hands on top of her cane and leaning against the doorframe.

Alice rolled her eyes, determining not to waste her breath and brought her head back inside to adjust the mirrors instead.

Agatha continued walking toward the parked car during their exchange. Once she stood by the passenger door, she paused and turned to face Rio, in no rush to move even if she had been the one to suggest she should go. “I’ll text you when I get home.”

It was one of those throwaway statements one would say as a goodbye. A formality of sorts. But, after tonight, somehow, it felt like it was too much for what it was.

Rio adjusted her grip on the cane, settling into a comfortable stance. She titled her head slightly, voice easy. “Sure.”

“You’ll be fine by yourself, right? You don’t plan on injuring yourself again in the next hour or so?”

Rio let out a soft laugh. “No promises.”

Agatha gave a small nod, almost to herself, then opened the car door and slipped inside.

The car pulled away from the curb soon after seatbelts were fastened. A faint smile crossed her face as Rio watched the duo drive away in her car. Once she could no longer see her car down the street, she supported her weight on the cane and pivoted, ready to head back inside.

The sound of another car engine starting nearby startled her. Rio looked around, catching a glimpse of a red car pulling out of a parking spot just to the side of Vida Bloom before it sped off.

This silence in the car — as Alice did, in fact, drive with the steady caution of a grandma — was the kind of quiet that made Agatha hyper-aware of the frequency of her breathing and the blow of the air-conditioner resembling the roar of a plane. Alice looked perfectly comfortable with the silence, a juxtaposition to Agatha who sat stiffly in the passenger seat, hands neatly folded in her lap, bracing for impact for when Alice finally decided to strike conversation.

Every so often, Agatha would sneak a glance her way, teeth mulling over her bottom lip, trying to find the perfect thing to say in response to the questions she knew were coming. Each passing streetlight lit the inside of the car in soft flashes, reminding Agatha of warning lights that often accompany an alarm — not really a comforting sign.

“So,” Alice finally spoke once they rolled to a stop at a red light. Her attention stayed on the road, hand resting loosely on the steering wheel.

“‘So,’” Agatha parroted.

Alice turned her head slightly to look at Agatha. It was fascinating how everything about her seemed to discourage conversation, but Alice could only snort and talked anyway. “It was thoughtful of you to bring Rio dinner.”

“It was just pizza.”

“Still a nice gesture. Didn’t realize how close you two have gotten.” Alice said lightly, pleasantly entertained at the fact that Agatha actually looked faintly embarrassed at the comment.

“I don’t— Rio, she—” Agatha stopped, exhaled, and then restarted, more composed. “I wouldn’t say the word to use would be ‘close.’”

“...right.” Alice chuckled softly and drummed her fingers on the steering wheel. “Because of the baby contract.”

Agatha’s eyebrows shot up sharply, surprise flickering across her face before she quickly masked it. She had forgotten about the fact that Alice was aware of their situation. Her lips pressed together like she wanted to say more but couldn’t. Instead, she simply nodded and repeated after her, “...the baby contract.”

A moment passed, neither of them attempting to continue filling the silence. It was a pause in a conversation that hadn’t quite figured out its next sentence yet — Agatha welcomed it. She turned away to look out the window, resting her elbow against the door and propped her head in her hand. The traffic light sure was taking its sweet ass time to turn green even though the intersecting street had no cars going through. Next to her, Alice hummed and leaned into the driver’s seat like she was waiting for Agatha to be the one to start the conversation again.

The notion became hard to ignore the longer the car stayed idle. Agatha gave a surrendering sigh and proceeded with small talk. “Have you and Rio known each other long?”

“Since we were teens,” Alice replied with a quirk of her brow.

“That explains a lot.” The thought in Agatha’s head verbalized without permission.

“Oh? Like?”

Agatha hesitated long enough to make it seem like she was choosing her words carefully. “The way you two interact. It’s very... established.”

Alice smiles wistfully. “That’s one word for it.”

There was another stretch of silence as the light finally turned green and the car was on the move. Alice let a few blocks pass before she spoke again.

“You know what’s funny?” At that, Agatha shifted her head to look at Alice’s direction. “Rio always goes on about how good she is at reading people like a book. But, if you were to ask me, I’d say she’s actually pretty shit at it— especially when it comes to people she’s close to.”

Agatha gaped at her, incredulous. Her impression of Alice heading towards negative the longer she spent time with her. It was peculiar, this sense of feeling offended on Rio’s behalf. Whatever Alice said may have been the truth considering she knew Rio longer than the couple of months of moments they spent together here and there. But, “That hasn’t been my experience,” she found herself muttering. Rio always hit the mark when it came to her. She didn’t even try to hide the disdain in her voice. “Aren’t you two supposed to be best friends?”

“Wait, hear me out.” Alice laughed at the sour expression she caught on Agatha’s face when she turned the wheel. “It’s precisely because we’re such good, old friends that I can say so with certainty. Growing up the way she did, I think it’s more her filling in the blanks and making up her own conclusions on things because she’s so used to accommodating and anticipating what others need.”

“Why are you telling me this?”

“I wasn’t going to say anything, but Rio...” Alice had never been one to jump into delicate matters, but something deep within her felt like she had to step in after what she saw of her friend's reservations tonight. “She doesn’t know how to stop trying to earn her place. Even when she’s already seated at the table. Because— no matter what she says— she believes that she doesn’t know how to want and not lose it. That if something is meant to stay, it will. And if it’s not, reaching for it will only make the act of leaving worse. So, she would rather miss a chance than make the wrong move when it comes to people.”

They reached another stop at a red light and Agatha found her back nearly pressed to the car door, trying to create some distance. Alice turned and awkwardly smiled. There was a well-meaning nosiness pushing at the edges of her gentle tone. “I believe you’re the same— in some way— and I’d really hate it if you guys miss something just because you’re reading the wrong pages of each other’s books.”

“If you’re trying to imply something—”

“I’m not trying to imply anything.” Alice corrected. “I was merely stating my observations.”

Agatha frowned and crossed her arms. “You think there’s something going on, but there’s nothing to observe.”

Alice hummed. “Alright.”

The quick surrender threw Agatha off and her arms slackened. “That’s it? You’re not going to argue?”

“I don’t need to.” Alice shrugs a shoulder as she continued to drive. “If there’s nothing, then there’s nothing.”

“There is nothing.”

“Of course.” Alice nodded thoughtfully.

Agatha huffed a breath, growing tired of this conversation. She looked intently at Alice, searching for a trap. There wasn’t one — that might have been worse.

As a beacon of hope and salvation, the familiar brick of her apartment building came into view of her periphery. Agatha was quick to free herself from the seatbelt, unlock the door, and step out into the sidewalk the moment the car rolled to a stop and Alice finally put the car in Park. She managed to mutter a word of “Thanks” and nothing else before she shut the car door and began to walk to the entrance of her apartment building.

There was the sound of a car window rolling down behind her. “Anyway, I hope you do like her.” Alice called out after her.

Agatha whipped back around. “Excuse me?!”

“Since you guys are having a baby together and all that.” Even from a distance, she could see Alice’s lips twitching, like she was fighting a smile. “It’d be nice if you guys at least tolerate each other.”

Agatha could only watch in disbelief, shocked at her audacity, as Alice bid her a merry “Good night, Agatha!” before driving away.
