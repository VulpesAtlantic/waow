Lilia held court, as usual, with Jen her right-hand man. Pepper mostly sat silently, her hand on her pregnant belly and her gaze on whoever was talking, so mostly Lilia and Jen. Janet was silent for the most part, happy to observe and only interject if someone was being particularly nippy. Talk was easy, talk was cheap. The members of the Family Calderu currently present all rubbed along well together, unless Jen decided they wouldn’t. But even in her foulest moods the family was a sickeningly happy one.

And the happiest of them all was still yet to arrive.

“Where’s the prodigal child?” Peter asked, signing rather than speaking, a habit he and only one other member of the family had. But she wasn’t here yet.

“On her way,” Lilia said with a dreamlike smile, signing fluidly with her words. “She’s stuck in traffic.”

“She had something to shoot in the city and wasn’t sure when she’d get away,” Lilia explained, her fingers moving through the air with a grace that none of the others possessed. Then again, it was hardly as if they could have inherited it, being all adopted.

“She got a girl yet?” Billy asked, speaking without signing.

“Several, I hear,” Jen said, earning a laugh from Lilia and a disapproving look from Pepper.

“She’s not that bad,” Pepper responded, signing quickly with one hand as Morgan beside her tried to mimic the movements. She was still learning; seemingly picking up the swear words faster than anything else.

Jen only shrugged in response and it was as if the movement of her shoulder triggered something. The front door opened and a shout of “hello! Merry Christmas!” sounded throughout the house.

Lilia was first to move, because of course. Peter next, tapping Billy’s shoulder to alert him and dragging him behind. Then Pepper and Morgan, who ran ahead to beat even her grandma. Jen trailed up the rear.

“I miss anything good?” She asked the little girl, shooting her brother a wink as she did. “Auntie Jen pissed anyone off yet?”

“She said a bad word to Uncle Billy.”

“I passed them on the highway. Would recognise Wanda’s bimmer anywhere.” Rio offered to the group as Peter hurried around to kiss her cheek and help her out of her black puffer jacket. She shivered, despite wearing a maroon sweater beneath it.

“Come in,” Lilia encouraged, waving her little family into the kitchen again. They all dutifully followed, as they would if their mom were leading them through war or famine or flood.

“Catch a glimpse?” Jen asked, taking her seat again at the island and popping a grape into her mouth as Rio settled back against the counter with her arm draped around Billy’s shoulders.

“Brown hair, cream suit.”

“Cream?!” Jen barked, making Rio show her teeth in an amused grin.

“You’re trouble,” she accused, pointing at Jen and then signing for Billy’s benefit too.

Everyone but Billy looked up when the ancient door bell could be heard. No one had used it in decades which meant the noise was a surprise for two reasons; the other being that the door was open, and Wanda was family, not to mention she had a key anyway.

It was Lilia again who moved first, laughing as she passed Jen at the expression on her face. Everyone followed after and Rio grabbed Jen’s arm as she did.

“Hey,” she whispered urgently, “take your fangs out. It’s Christmas.”

And after the house began to swell with things, it soon began to swell with memories. Because they added kids to the mix.

First came Pepper, cute and blonde and sensible. Then Jen, beautiful but with an acid tongue. Then Wanda, emotional but easily swayed. Next was Rio, who could charm the hind legs off a donkey with her quirky and observant nature. Rio couldn’t see through the heads of her beloved family as they gathered around the now closed door but she was smiling all the same. She’d just been speaking about her, so it was ridiculous that she should so soon forget. But forget she had and so the effect of a beautiful woman in a cream suit with snow in her hair was more than Rio could handle in the moment.

Wanda moved forwards to give her a somewhat stilted kiss to the cheek and Rio blinked as she was met with blue eyes that bore into her own.

“Rio,” Wanda said with an unusually tight smile on her face, “you managed to find time in your busy schedule for us?”

Rio’s gaze snapped back to her sister and she realised suddenly that her sister was in a crisp white shirt, just visible beneath her camel coat.

“Dressed for a board meeting?” She asked, tilting head as she surveyed the redhead. “Nice.”

“Shut up,” Wanda retorted, shoving Rio’s shoulder as she moved to focus more on Jen.

But Rio’s attention was elsewhere again, instead on the stranger in their midst. Janet, obviously, had swept in. She was holding the stranger’s one hand in both her own and just as Rio was taking a tiny step closer she was pushed back.

Billy had been the one to do it, wanting a word with his sister apart from the others. The word in question? “Hot”, he signed to Rio, keeping his hand close to his chest and a glimmer of a smirk on his face.

Rio rolled hers but signed right back as introductions were being given in the crowded hallway: “Looks pretty cold actually.”

Billy barked a laugh and everyone turned to them, including the stranger.

And Lilia, dragging her because she would not yet follow, pulled the stranger towards Rio, the last of the lot to introduce herself.

“And my youngest daughter, Rio.”

A hand was placed in her own and Rio blinked as she looked across at the woman now standing stiffly before her with snow in her hair and ice in her gaze.

“It’s a pleasure to meet you,” the stranger said, pulling her hand away the moment they’d done a double up-down and no more.

“Agatha,” Rio called the stranger, “welcome to the mad house.”

Agatha smiled a little, a twitch, maybe. Perhaps a flicker of amusement, but nothing concrete, nothing Rio could latch on to.

Over Agatha’s shoulder, Billy signed again “hot” and Rio couldn’t help but agree.
