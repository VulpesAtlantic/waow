“Could you please get it into you stupid, fucking, moronic, fucking-…?!”

Jen threw her arms up when there was a knock at the door. She growled in frustration, storming away from Agatha’s desk just to get some distance, just to not see her stupid fucking stubborn face.

How she agreed to take Agatha on she had no idea. Her younger self had perhaps been more open, more optimistic.

No, she thought. Her younger self had been the same as her older self: aware that Agatha would make it big, make bank.

She sighed and shook her head as Agatha barked for whoever it was to come on in. In turning Jen saw the only other adult in the house, Lucy, the nanny.

Or was it Lily?

“Sorry to…” Lucy or Lily glanced briefly at Jen before turning back to Agatha, “interrupt. I’ve just been speaking with Nicky and… he’s a little upset.”

Oh shit.

If Jen had thought she’d be able to get through to Agatha by the time the evening was through, now she knew she didn’t stand a hope in hell. Nicky would come first and Jen would be left having to bite her tongue.

“What happened?”

She looked to Agatha, saw her expression was changed now. She was no longer angry, she was worried. Jen only felt a little guilty that she’d put her needs ahead of Nicky’s. Only a little.

“Apparently one of the older boys at school called him names.”

“Names? Which boy?”

Jen rolled her eyes, and decided now would be a good time to interrupt: “Is he hurt?”

“No, I, he-…”

“Where is he?” Agatha asked to no one in particular, already storming from the room to find her son.

Jen just sighed and began to stride after her, offering only a cursory, “Thanks, Lil.” As she stepped past the nanny, named Lucy.

She followed the furious mother through the quiet house until stepped out through the open glass doors and the sound of a ball being bounced could be heard echoing through the large backyard. Jen grimaced. That sound always set her teeth on edge.

She rounded the corner a few seconds after Agatha and saw her client already knelt down before the little boy with the long hair. Nicky was a good kid, she knew that. Would she have preferred Agatha hadn’t had him when she did? Yes.

Did she have a strange affection, almost love for him? Also yes.

So when she saw his hang-dog expression and his little pout her hand flew to her chest without her even being aware of it.

“Honey, Lucy says-…”

“I’m fine, mama.”

“Baby, remember what we discussed? How talking about your feelings helps? It’s just me,” and without looking back around to even check Jen was there, Agatha added, “it’s just Jen.”

Nicky looked up, sent his frown in Jen’s direction, and Jen felt a stirring in her heart that made her edge closer as Nicky looked back to his mom.

“Jackson stole our ball and I asked him-…” Nicky’s little fists raised to nudge into his eyes and Jen watched as Agatha held his sweater a little more firmly. She stepped closer, slowly crouching down in her Gucci skirt.

“I-I,” Nicky continued as he sniffed and the first sign of tears appeared in his dark eyes so unlike his mama’s, or his father’s. “I asked him nicely. I said he could play and I-I… I even said he could be in my team if he wanted a-and he pushed me away and called me stupid a-and…”

Jen sucked in a breath, unable to not look at the heavy tears falling across flushed cheeks. She could already feel the coiled cobra that was Agatha Harkness beside her. She was trying to be soft for her son, trying to calm the fury that she knew was bubbling away inside her.

Where she crouched beside her she placed a steadying hand on her knee. To calm her as well as to help keep her balance. Four inch heels were not conducive to crouching for this long.

“Honey, what else did he say?”

Agatha’s voice was calm but the hands that held her son were pale, a brilliant white, her knuckles whitest of all.

“The other boys… they said it-it was bad but I… I don’t know what it-… he said I was gay, mama. A-and I’m not. I’m no g-gay or stupid o-or,” Nicky sniffed heavily, and Jen would have grimaced for the sound of it but the whole picture was too sad, too tragic to worry about little things like snot.

So long as it stayed away from her Gucci skirt.

“You’re neither of those things,” Agatha hushed quietly as she gathered Nicky in for a tight hug.

Good, Jen thought, snot in Agatha’s hair was better than on her skirt.

Jen reached to tentatively rub Nicky’s back, frowning as she watched him just cry and Agatha, mega-bitch who’d just been protesting about doing a fucking roundtable, became the soft mom she’d seen before.

It was shocking really. Then again, she always supposed actresses needed to have their feelings all so close to the surface. It just so happened that her actress always seemed to be boiling over with them.

They all stayed crouched for a while, Jen soon feeling a bit like a spare part in the little trifecta, until Nicky was hiccuping and pulling away to wipe at his face with his sleeves.

Kids were so gross.

“I’ll talk to the principal, okay? I’ll make sure-…”

“N-no, no don’t! I don’t want to be called g-gay again.”

Jen opened her mouth and closed it quickly, and Agatha glanced at her for just a second before she looked back around to her son. “He won’t call you gay again if I just-…”

“Nicky, sweetheart,” Jen said cautiously, speaking for the first time since she’d arrived, “baby, do you know what ‘gay’ means?”

Agatha turned to look at her then and suddenly mega-bitch was back, and she hadn’t even mentioned the fucking roundtable!

“Jen. No.”

Jen raised her brows at her reaction, watching her pale features for a second as they fought to fend off their annoyance, their… fear? What the fuck?

“He’s old enough, smart enough. It isn’t a big deal.”

“No, Jen. I-…”

Nicky sniffed obnoxiously, making both adults turn to him in surprise. He blushed a little and wiped his nose with his sleeve.

Jen took her chance, “Nicky, I’m gay.”

“Fucksake,” Agatha muttered, the word coming out a lot more venomously that Jen was prepared for. Plus, when was the last time Agatha cursed in front of Nicky?

She stared at her for a second, watched the way she kept her gaze down, her hands still gripping Nicky’s sweater. But without any further interruptions she looked up again, softened her features and quietly, as calmly as she could, she explained: “Baby, you know how your mama and daddy love each other?”

Nicky hesitated, toying with the ends of his snotty sleeves. Jen waited for a reaction but when she saw she wouldn’t be getting one she tried again: “Well, okay. Do you remember Susan?”

Nicky reacted this time. He nodded, his deep, older-than-his-years frown in place.

“Well, she wasn’t just my friend, she was my girlfriend. We were dating.”

She watched as Nicky’s eyes remained on her, remained steady, and wondered if he even really knew what dating was. And then: “You loved her?”

Agatha beside her looked up then, was watching her son with an unreadable expression. Jen glanced at her, saw something that looked a lot like fear in her friend’s profile. But she turned back to Nicky and smiled a little, “Yes, sweetheart. I did. Being gay just means that some boys love boys and some girls love girls. Now as you get older we can talk a little more about what gender is but-…”

“Jen.”

“Right.”

Jen cleared her throat, “I am a girl and I love girls, okay? Sometimes I am lucky enough to cuddle the girls I like and sometimes, sometimes I kiss them. Like your mommy and daddy do.” It hadn’t been Nicky who’d reacted to this, it had been Agatha.

Her head had snapped around and Jen almost lost her balance at the suddenness of it. Agatha looked… boy, she looked mad. It almost made Jen smile.

She turned back to Nicky, cleared her throat, “And boys who love boys are also gay. There’s nothing wrong with it. Nothing bad or scary or shameful. So if this kid called you gay then it’s because he is stupid. Because it’s not an insult, alright? Would you consider me, your fun, beautiful Auntie Jen horrible because I like girls?”

Nicky sniffed again, but shook his head, reacting much quicker than he had before. Jen’s heart fluttered a little in her chest. She ignored it.

“Good. So this kid is just an idiot, alright?”

Nicky nodded. He sniffed.

“Are… are there lots of people who are gay?”

“Oh? In Hollywood?! Loads! Let’s see, there’s me, there’s this pretty girl Alice I hope one day you’ll get to meet. There’s her friend Rio, I hear she likes basketball-…”

“Jen.”

Agatha was standing now, clearing her throat and moving around to hold Nicky by his shoulders. Jen looked up at her from her painful crouched position and raised her brows. By the cut of Agatha’s jaw she knew she was mad again, knew something had set her off.

She stood slowly, wincing at the pain of it.

“I think we all understand well enough what ‘gay’ means.”

“What the fuck is your problem?”

But before Agatha could answer the worst happened. Nicky had thrown his arms around her waist and was currently pressing his snotty, disgusting face against her Gucci skirt.

“Nicky! What have we discussed about my clothes?!”

Nicky pulled back almost instantly and looked sheepish which made Jen feel guilty, but only a little guilty. Because she now could feel a damp patch against her hip and she rubbed at it with the heel of her hands.

“Sorry,” he mumbled, reaching back up behind him to seek his mom’s hands.

Jen huffed as Agatha bent over him to kiss his head and mumbled something into his ear that Jen didn’t quite catch.

“What was that?”

“Oh, just that Auntie Jen can be cranky sometimes.”

Jen glowered across at Agatha, not at all amused that she was now smirking, now looking incredibly smug again as she held her son against her. She couldn’t scream at her anymore like she had been earlier but she could still make her opinion known: “This roundtable is good for your image. You go in there, you ham it up with your fellow actresses, you even sidle up to the gay one and you will secure that Oscar nom, maybe even the win.”

Agatha’s smirk had fallen somewhere in her little speech, she just hadn’t pinpointed the word upon which it had disappeared. But she looked furious now, the brilliant white of her knuckles was now a metaphorical flame about her whole person.

But it was Nicky who interrupted, hiccuping as he asked: “W-which is the gay one?”

Jen threw her hand up, dismissive of him now he was getting in the way of her shouting at her client. “Vidal. Uh, Rio. Nicky, why don’t you go play? I need to speak to your mama.”

“Are you going to shout again?”

Agatha sighed quietly, “No, baby. Jen won’t shout or Jen will get fired. Isn’t that right, Jen?”

Jen narrowed her eyes, knowing Agatha would never fire her because no one else would put up with her. But she took the bait anyway, for Nicky’s sake.

“Fine. No shouting.”

Nicky nodded slowly, eyeing up both Jen and his mom for a second before he reached a sticky hand out for Jen to shake. Jen looked curiously at his little hand for a second and then, in a quiet voice, Nicky explained:

“Because we can’t hug, Auntie Jen.”

Agatha raised her brows at her, daring her to refuse her son. But Jen never would. As much as she wanted to. She lost, at least, on that battle. But she’d get Agatha to that damn roundtable if it killed her.

“But I can kiss you. C’mere.”

She ducked down, pressed a firm kiss to Nicky’s sticky, warm cheek, and made him smile as she gently wiped her lipstick from it. He looked nothing like his mom, but he had her charm.

So much so that she didn’t even mind when he reached his sticky fingers up to touch her cheek.

She might not have wanted him to have come along when he did. But, fuck, was she pleased he’d been born.
