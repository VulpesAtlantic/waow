Agatha Planned a Date, Not a Crisis

by LabNotesAndFiction

Summary

It’s supposed to be a quiet, simple dinner. Agatha cooks, Rio arrives exactly on time, and everything goes… mostly according to plan. Until it doesn’t. When the evening veers unexpectedly, Agatha is left to navigate something deeper than she expected. But maybe that’s what intimacy is—choosing to stay, even when things get complicated.

Notes

Hey everyone! This one’s been building for a while—Agatha and Rio’s first proper date, complete with nervous prep, quiet tension, and all the things neither of them quite knows how to say. It’s soft, a little awkward, and far more vulnerable than either of them planned for. There's a moment in here where things don’t go perfectly (you’ll know it when you hit it), but it’s handled with care, and above all else—this is about love in progress. I hope you enjoy this one! Agatha had made a terrible, terrible mistake. She had asked Rio out. On a date. At her apartment. Where there would be no distractions, no third-party buffer, no escape. What the hell had she been thinking? Phase One: The Initial Panic At first, she had been fine. She had asked. Rio had said yes. No big deal. But then— The realization hit. And the moment it did, Agatha’s entire brain went into full-on meltdown mode. Her entire internal monologue became: Oh God, it’s real. Oh God, it’s happening. Oh God, what if I say something weird? Oh God, what if I make it weird? Oh God, what if Rio is just treating this like an efficiency experiment and I’m the only one losing my mind over it? That thought alone sent her spiraling. Because she knew how Rio worked. Knew that she approached everything with precision, with calculation, with a logical structure. Which meant Rio had probably processed this as a straightforward agreement. A simple, neutral social interaction. Not… Not whatever the hell Agatha was experiencing. Which was full-body panic every time she thought about it for more than ten seconds. She was not going to mess this up. If Rio processed everything like an algorithm, then fine. Agatha would be flawless. She would execute the perfect dinner date. And if she had to spend hours researching what qualified as an optimal dining experience? Then so be it. She refused to half-ass this. Takeout? Too impersonal. Something store-bought? Absolutely not. Something she wasn’t confident making? A risk she would not take. She needed something she could make effortlessly. Something that didn’t look like she was trying too hard, but also showed she put effort into it. Something Rio would actually enjoy. Which meant— She had to strategically select a dish that met all efficiency standards. Nothing too messy. Nothing too elaborate. Nothing that required too much assembly time, because Rio would probably question her time management choices. She went through at least twenty options before finally settling on one.

She spent way too long cleaning. Her apartment was already clean. But suddenly, every single thing looked out of place. The bookshelves? Reorganized. The coffee table? Rearranged. The couch? Fluffed, straightened, adjusted again. At one point, she actually stood in the middle of her living room, frowning at her furniture placement. Did this look too casual?

Too formal? Too much like I’m trying to impress her? She adjusted everything three more times. Just in case. Step Three: Herself. …She hadn’t even thought about what to wear. Which meant she was already behind schedule. She didn’t want to overdress. She didn’t want to underdress. She needed something that said: “Yes, I look good, but no, I didn’t put extra effort into this because of you.” Which was a blatant lie. But Rio didn’t need to know that.

And yet, despite all of that, she still found herself standing in the kitchen ten minutes before Rio was supposed to arrive, second-guessing everything. What if this was a mistake? What if Rio wasn’t actually thinking of this as a date? What if Agatha had miscalculated everything? She actually reached for her phone. Considered texting Rio. Something vague. Something neutral. Something that would give her an out. And then— A knock at the door. The knock on the door came exactly on time. Not a minute early. Not a minute late. Exactly. On. Time.

Because of course it did. Agatha had no idea why that made it worse, but it did. It was so perfectly, ridiculously Rio. There was no casual “show up whenever.” No “I lost track of time.” No margin of error. Rio had calculated her travel time down to the minute. Like this was a goddamn business meeting. Agatha took a sharp inhale, mentally coached herself to act normal, for once in her life, and walked to the door. Her fingers hovered over the handle. Just for a second. She could still cancel. She could still claim she had a work emergency, or that her oven had exploded, or that she had suddenly developed a deathly allergy to social interaction. But she didn’t. Instead, she swallowed all her ridiculous anxiety, forced her expression into something resembling normalcy, and opened the door. Rio stood perfectly composed. Like she had optimized her existence for this exact moment. Her hair was neat. Her shirt was tucked. Her posture was absurdly straight. Like she had spent exactly five minutes ensuring every detail was calibrated for maximum efficiency. Agatha had never felt like more of a mess in her life. She tried not to stare. Tried not to notice the way Rio’s sleeves were pushed up just slightly, revealing forearms that— No. Stop that. This was fine. Totally fine. She could be normal about this. She could—

…Why was she still staring? Say something, idiot. “…Hey.” Rio nodded. “Hello.” Agatha stepped aside. “Come in.” Rio did. And just like that, she was inside Agatha’s space. Inside her apartment. Where there was no buffer of a public setting. Where it was just the two of them. Alone. Agatha’s brain was already overheating. It was weird. She had spent days spiraling about this. She had gone through every possible scenario. But now that Rio was actually here, standing in the middle of her apartment— Agatha felt like she had lost control of the situation. She wasn’t sure why. She had invited Rio. This was her apartment. This was her plan. So why did it suddenly feel like Rio was the one who had the upper hand? Why did it feel like Rio was completely unbothered, while Agatha was having an internal crisis? Why was Rio just standing there like she hadn’t completely altered Agatha’s reality simply by existing? Agatha cleared her throat. Focused. “So,” she said, forcing herself to move. “Make yourself comfortable, I guess.” She gestured toward the couch, hoping Rio would actually sit down and stop being so composed. Instead—

Rio nodded. But she did not sit. Instead, she did the worst possible thing she could have done. She started scanning the room. Analyzing. Assessing. Agatha swore she saw the gears turning in real time. She resisted the urge to grab her by the shoulders and yell, ‘DO NOT PROFILE MY LIVING SPACE LIKE A CRIME SCENE.’ Instead, she asked, “…What?” Rio’s gaze drifted. “You have a lot of books.” Agatha felt her soul leave her body. “…Yeah?” Rio tilted her head slightly, like she was calculating something. “How do you organize them?” she asked. Agatha blinked. “I… what?” Rio gestured toward the bookshelf. “Your system. Is it alphabetical? By subject? By frequency of use?” Agatha opened her mouth. Closed it. Because of course Rio would ask something like that. Not ‘Oh, nice collection.’ Not ‘Do you have a favorite?’ Not ‘I didn’t know you were a book person.’ Just— What’s your filing system? Agatha blinked at her. Then, very slowly— “…I put them where they fit.” Rio visibly frowned. “You don’t have a structured system?” “No, Rio, I just—put them places.”

Rio’s frown deepened like this was physically painful to her. “I assume you don’t rank them, either?” “No, Rio, I don’t—” Agatha stopped. Exhaled. Refocused. She narrowed her eyes. “You already know I don’t rank my books.” “Yes.” “Then why did you ask?” Rio shrugged. “To confirm.” Agatha dragged a hand down her face. She should have known. She should have anticipated this. She was losing this battle already. She turned toward the kitchen—quickly, before Rio could profile anything else. “Whatever,” she muttered. “Dinner’s almost ready.” Rio nodded. Like this was all perfectly normal. Like she hadn’t just set Agatha’s entire nervous system on fire.

Rio's POV Agatha’s cooking was good. Objectively. Rio had analyzed the composition—balanced, well-seasoned, efficiently prepared. She had also noted Agatha’s technique while cooking. The way she moved through the kitchen was structured, methodical. Not messy, not chaotic. Every action served a purpose. Rio had respected that. She had expected to respect that. What she had not expected— Was to be completely, utterly distracted.

Not by the food. Not by the process. By Agatha. Which was a problem.

Rio was very, very good at compartmentalizing. She did not let irrelevant information interfere with important tasks. She could filter unnecessary input out. That had always been a strength of hers. But right now— She was struggling. Because Agatha was sitting across from her. And Rio was noticing things she should not be noticing. Things that served no analytical purpose. The way Agatha rolled her sleeves up before eating. The way she curled her fingers around her fork, casual, effortless. The way she licked sauce off her thumb without thinking. Unacceptable. Rio refocused. She needed to fix this. Her thoughts were messy. Which was wrong. Her thoughts were never messy. But tonight, they were. They were scattered, non-linear. It wasn’t just what Agatha was doing. It was how she was looking at Rio. She wasn’t studying her, exactly. But she was watching.

Not in the way most people watched Rio—like they were trying to figure her out. Not like she was a puzzle to solve. Agatha was watching her like— Like she already knew her. Like she had already figured her out. And she just… liked what she saw. Rio’s fork paused halfway to her mouth. That realization hit something deep. Something she did not have a name for. Something that had never been part of any analysis. Her pulse was off. Not fast, not panicked—just… off. She felt strangely warm, despite the neutral room temperature. Her breathing was steady, but she was aware of it in a way she normally wasn’t. This was wrong. She needed to recalibrate. She needed to redirect her attention. She cleared her throat. “The meal is efficiently prepared.” Agatha paused mid-bite. Then snorted. “Jesus, Rio. Just say it’s good.” Rio frowned. “That is what I said.” Agatha smirked. “No, you said some robot shit about efficiency.” Rio exhaled sharply. “Your cooking is satisfactory.” Agatha raised an eyebrow. Rio adjusted. “It is above average.” Agatha’s smirk widened. Rio recalibrated again. “…It is enjoyable.” Agatha grinned. Rio immediately felt worse.

Incorrect move. New variable introduced. Unacceptable. She went silent, trying to stabilize her internal systems. Agatha leaned forward slightly, balancing her elbow on the table. There was something in her expression. Something Rio could not define. It wasn’t just amusement. It wasn’t just teasing. It was something else. Something that made Rio’s heart rate shift again. “…You good?” Agatha asked, tilting her head slightly. Rio should have been able to answer. She had analyzed thousands of scenarios. She had prepared for every possible conversational outcome. And yet— Her brain offered nothing. Blank. Empty. Just Agatha. Her voice. Her expression. The fact that she was so close across the table. That was a problem. Because Rio did not get stuck like this. She did not get distracted. She did not experience reaction delays. And yet— “…Yes,” Rio said, voice a little too even. Agatha raised an eyebrow, like she didn’t quite believe her. But she didn’t push.

She just took another bite of her food, nodding slightly. “Good.” Rio focused on her plate. Tried to process the data. Something was wrong. And she had no idea how to fix it.

Rio had been monitoring her reactions all evening. Tracking variables. Assessing deviations. Adjusting where necessary. It had been manageable. Until it wasn’t. Because at some point— Something snapped. It wasn’t one big thing. There was no singular catastrophic event. Just a series of small things. Too many inputs. Too many unknown variables. Her senses had been dialed up all evening. And now— Everything hit at once. The sound of Agatha’s voice—casual, amused, warm. The clink of silverware against ceramic. The soft ambient hum of the apartment, the faint creak of the chair as Agatha shifted her weight. The heat of the room—not excessive, but enough to feel off. The way Agatha looked at her. And then— Then Agatha reached for her hand.

Not a big movement. Not even an intentional one. Just a brief touch—casual, nothing significant. But it was enough. Enough for everything to crash at once. Her body locked up. Not tense. Not stiff. Just—blank. Her brain disconnected. Everything flattened. Her ability to react, to engage, to function— Gone. Like someone had flipped a switch. Like someone had unplugged her from reality. She stopped processing. Her breathing didn’t change. Her expression didn’t change. She was still sitting at the table. But she wasn’t there anymore. Her mind had gone completely, utterly silent. She wasn’t sure how long she sat there. Seconds? Minutes? Long enough for Agatha to notice something was off. “Rio?” No response. A shift in Agatha’s tone—concern creeping in. “Hey. You okay?”

Still nothing. Agatha leaned forward slightly. “Hello? Earth to Rio?” Still nothing. Then— A small pause. And then Agatha’s voice—softer this time. “…Shit.” Agatha had seen it. She knew. She knew something was wrong. Somewhere—distantly—Rio became aware of one fact. Agatha cared. Not in the broad, impersonal way most people did. Not in the way people were taught to care out of politeness. Agatha cared about her. Specifically. Individually. That was a problem. Because Rio cared too. Her chest felt tight. Her thoughts were not organizing the way they should. And the reason why— The core issue, the root cause, the thing she had been refusing to process— She liked Agatha. Not in a neutral way. Not in a she is an interesting person way. Not in a we are good conversational partners way. In a way that made her pulse jump at the smallest interaction. In a way that completely derailed her thought process.

In a way that she could no longer ignore. She had been wrong about the variables. She had been wrong about her own reactions. This wasn’t just data. This was something else entirely. There was a disconnect. The world was still happening. She was still sitting there. She was still aware of things, distantly. She knew Agatha was talking. She knew Agatha was worried. She knew she needed to respond. But she couldn’t. The words wouldn’t come. Her brain wouldn’t restart. She wasn’t panicking. She wasn’t scared. She was just—stuck. Like a program caught in an infinite loop. No input. No output. Just… nothing. Agatha’s voice changed again. She wasn’t pushing anymore. She wasn’t asking questions. She was just there. She didn’t touch Rio again. Didn’t try to shake her out of it. Instead—

She just talked. Not demanding a response. Not expecting one. Just talking. Her voice was low, even. “…Okay. You’re okay.” A pause. “I mean, I don’t actually know if you’re okay. But you’re safe.” Another pause. “I think you’re probably overwhelmed. And that’s fine. I mean, not fine-fine, but—you know what I mean.” Her voice was steady. Familiar. Real. And somehow— That helped. It didn’t fix anything. But it gave Rio something to hold onto. A thread. A connection. Something that told her she was still here. Still in the world. Still grounded to something. And slowly—very slowly— She started to come back. It was gradual. Not sudden. Not instant. Just… slow.

Her awareness crept back in. The sound of Agatha’s breathing. The feel of the chair beneath her. The way the lights weren’t too bright anymore. The way the sounds weren’t as sharp. Her muscles unlocked. Her hands curled slightly—testing movement. Her breathing deepened. And finally— Her first real, conscious action. She blinked. Agatha immediately noticed. She didn’t react big. Didn’t start asking questions. Didn’t demand answers. Just— “…There you are.” Soft. Relieved. Like she had just found something she thought she’d lost. Rio swallowed. Tried to speak. Failed. Agatha nodded like she expected that. “It’s fine,” she said simply. “You don’t have to say anything.” Another pause. “You want water?” Rio hesitated. Then—slowly— She nodded.

Agatha stood without hesitation. Moved to the kitchen. Let Rio sit with herself for another moment. Because Agatha—somehow, impossibly—understood. And Rio— Rio needed to figure out what the hell she was going to do about this.

Agatha's POV She was a journalist. She handled information. Facts. Events. Stories. She dealt with tangible things. Things with clear beginnings, middles, and ends. Things she could document, dissect, and present in a structured way. But this? This wasn’t structured. This wasn’t a story she could write out neatly, analyze, and hand in to an editor. This was Rio. And Rio wasn’t responding the way Agatha expected her to. She wasn’t brushing it off. She wasn’t making some dry, efficient statement about recalibration. She wasn’t even snapping back with her usual, precise logic. She was just— Gone. Silent. Still. Like a program that had just crashed mid-execution.

And Agatha had no idea how to fix it.

Step One: Identify the Problem (Except She Couldn’t.) Agatha was used to figuring things out quickly. She could read a room, a situation, a person—quick, sharp, effortless. But this wasn’t effortless. She had never seen Rio like this. And she had never felt like this. Like she was completely unprepared. Like she was one wrong move away from making everything worse. Like she wanted—desperately—to help but had no idea how. That feeling was unbearable. Because Rio had never needed help before. She was the most self-sufficient person Agatha had ever met. She didn’t get flustered. She didn’t freeze. She didn’t—shut down. But she had. And Agatha didn’t know what to do. Step Two: Try the Usual Tactics. Her first instinct was to talk. That was what people did, right? They talked through things. They asked questions. They figured things out. But after the third "Are you okay?" went unanswered, Agatha realized— That wasn’t going to work. Rio was in there, somewhere. But she wasn’t coming back yet.

Not with words. Not with questions. So Agatha adjusted. Step Three: Don’t Make It Worse. She took a slow breath. Backed off physically. Didn’t touch Rio again. Didn’t try to force her to snap out of it. Didn’t rush her. Because that wasn’t what Rio needed. Agatha wasn’t sure how she knew that. She just—did. Because Rio didn’t get stuck like this. Which meant if she was, something was really wrong. And forcing her out of it wouldn’t help. So instead of pressuring her to respond, Agatha just… existed next to her. Step Four: Talk Without Expecting a Response. Not at Rio. Not demanding anything. Just… talking. Low. Even. Something steady in the silence. “…Okay. You’re okay.” A pause. “I mean, I don’t actually know if you’re okay. But you’re safe.” Another pause. “I think you’re probably overwhelmed. And that’s fine. I mean, not fine-fine, but—you know what I mean.” Still no reaction. But Agatha kept going.

Because she had a feeling Rio could hear her. Maybe not actively. Maybe not in a way that registered yet. But the words were there. And she wanted Rio to have something to hold onto.

Agatha didn’t rush. Didn’t fill the silence with useless noise. Just… waited. Let Rio take the time she needed. And slowly—very slowly— She started to come back. It wasn’t dramatic. There was no big gasp, no sudden movement. Just— A small shift in posture. A slight deepening of breath. And then— A blink. She didn’t react big. Didn’t start peppering Rio with questions. Didn’t demand an explanation. Just— “…There you are.” Soft. Relieved. Like she had just found something she thought she’d lost. Rio swallowed. Tried to speak. Failed.

Agatha didn’t push. She just nodded, like she expected that. “It’s fine,” she said simply. “You don’t have to say anything.” Another pause. “You want water?” A beat. Then—slowly— Rio nodded. Agatha moved deliberately. Not too fast. Not too slow. She stood. Walked to the kitchen. Poured a glass. Came back. Set it down within reach—but didn’t force it into Rio’s hands. Just… let it be there. Because Rio would reach for it when she was ready. And Agatha—somehow, impossibly— Understood that. Neither of them spoke for a while. Rio was still recovering. Agatha was still processing. She had never seen this happen before. Had never thought Rio was capable of shutting down like that. And—somewhere deep down— That worried her more than she wanted to admit. But Rio was still here. She had come back.

And somehow— That was enough. For now.

Agatha didn’t rush. Didn’t push. Didn’t force the moment forward. She just waited. Waited for Rio to reach for the water. Waited for her to drink it. Waited for her to fully come back. And eventually— She did. It was small. Barely anything. But Rio’s fingers moved first. A slow, hesitant motion—like she was testing her control. Then, carefully, she reached for the glass. Lifted it. Took a sip. And Agatha finally let out the breath she’d been holding. It took a while before Rio spoke. And when she did— Her voice was quiet. Not shaky. Not weak. Just… low. Measured. Like she was being deliberate with every word. “That… was inefficient.” Agatha snorted.

She couldn’t help it. It just—escaped. Because of course. Of course, the first thing Rio said after recovering from a full-system shutdown was a complaint about its inefficiency. Agatha shook her head, rubbing her face. “You absolute nightmare of a person,” she muttered. Rio blinked at her. Did not take offense. She just exhaled slowly, still gripping the water glass like it was an anchor. Agatha leaned back, watching her carefully. “…You good?” A pause. Then—Rio nodded. It wasn’t a confident nod. It wasn’t a sure one. But it was real. Agatha would take it. Neither of them moved for a while. The apartment was too quiet. The weight of what just happened hung in the air. And Agatha— She didn’t know what to do with it. Because this was the first time she had seen Rio like this. And it scared her more than she wanted to admit. Because Rio wasn’t supposed to break. She was sharp, composed, in control. She was the kind of person who didn’t falter, who didn’t crumble under pressure. And yet— She had.

Right here. At Agatha’s table. And Agatha had been the only one around to see it. Agatha could ask. Could demand an explanation. Could try to pry open whatever had just happened inside Rio’s head. But she didn’t. Because she knew, instinctively, that Rio wouldn’t be able to explain it yet. She had spent the last few minutes stuck. Frozen. Shut down. And now? Now, Agatha could tell she was still coming back. Still recalibrating. Still processing whatever the hell just happened. So Agatha just sat with it. Didn’t make a joke. Didn’t fill the silence. Didn’t act like this was normal. Because it wasn’t. And that was fine. Eventually, Rio set the glass down. Pressed her fingers against the rim. Didn’t look up. Then, after a long pause— “…I did not anticipate that.” Agatha huffed a small laugh. “No shit.” Rio finally met her eyes. There was something different in her expression. A weight.

Something she wasn’t saying. Agatha narrowed her eyes slightly. Didn’t push. Just—waited. And then—quietly, cautiously— “…Thank you.” It was so soft. So unlike her usual clipped, certain tone. So genuine. Agatha swallowed. Felt something shift deep inside her chest. She nodded. Didn’t make a big deal out of it. Didn’t tease. Just said— “Yeah. Anytime.” Rio exhaled. Not relieved. Not dramatic. Just settling. And Agatha realized— She had never seen Rio like this before. Not just the shutdown. Not just the overload. But after. After a moment of vulnerability. After being seen. And now? Now, she was still here. Still sitting in Agatha’s kitchen.

Still drinking water from a glass with faint condensation on the side. Still processing whatever the hell just happened. But she wasn’t running. She wasn’t trying to cover it up. She was just… Existing. And for some reason— That meant something. Agatha didn’t know what. Didn’t have a name for it yet. But she would figure it out. Eventually. For now— She just sat there, across from Rio. Still. Present. And Rio let her.
