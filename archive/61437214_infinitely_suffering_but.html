Chapter 1: did some force take you because i didn’t pray?

“Mami, stoooop.”

Rio bit back a smile at the sound of the whine. She had been trying to wake her son up, but the boy was a deep sleeper, just like his mama. She had started by gently calling his name, then playfully poking his legs, hands and cheeks, before finally resorting to lightly pinching his nose when she saw his eyelids flutter.

“Wakey, wakey, sleepyhead. We’re going to surprise mama today, remember?” Rio sang- songed, prompting the boy to sit up urgently as he rubbed his eyes. His cheeks flushed when he remembered their plans.

“What’s the time now, mami? Can we still make it to mama’s office before her lunch hour?!”

Rio ruffled her son’s hair, enjoying the softness and the way he leaned in a little more to her, allowing the action and Rio secretly thought he looked so much like Agatha then, since her wife also liked it whenever Rio stroked her hair. It felt like it was just yesterday Agatha had given birth to him and Rio had held the little bundle in her arms, but time flew so fast and before long, they’ll have to drop him off to his first day of college and Rio really wasn’t ready to let him go.

They still had some time before that, so she would enjoy her time with her son as much as she could.

“It’s still early, Nicky, but you have to get ready soon; it looks like it’s going to pour and if we leave too late, we’ll be stuck in a traffic jam and that’ll ruin the surprise.”

“Got it!”

Nicky pushed his blanket aside and jumped off the bed, getting ready to sprint to the bathroom when he suddenly braked and reversed his track.

“Good morning, mami!”

He pressed a quick kiss to Rio’s cheeks and rushed off again, the sounds of his little feet leaving the room had Rio cackling in place. She shook her head and left Nicky’s room, packing up their lunch and the rest of items they might require for the rest of the day, all the while keeping an eye out of the windows to check on the weather situation. It had been raining a lot lately, and now might not be the most suitable time to have a family day out, but both Agatha and Rio had been really busy these past few weeks and barely had time to be home as a family of three.

Rio had been on a series of outstation trips to different states in recent months since she had to track for a runaway serial killer which required her to leave the house for a few days every two weeks or so, while Agatha had been stuck working on a high-profile case that could make or break her career, so they had been taking turns accompanying Nicky, with the help of their closest friends. It was an important period for both of them in their careers and Nicky had been so patient and understanding throughout it all, acting very mature for a child his age. Rio and Agatha both felt guilty and immensely grateful for Nicky and their friends, vowing to return the favour at some point.

Now, Rio finally got a few days off from her work as an FBI agent and she had planned for a surprise family day out for Agatha with her accomplice, Nicky. She was supposed to send Nicky to school a few hours earlier, or at least that’s what she told her wife, but Rio had already called the school to let them know that Nicky would be taking the day off and already plotted with Agatha’s secretary to clear her afternoon schedule; they were all set.

Initially, Rio had thought about taking them for a picnic at the beach or at the park, or maybe spent the day at the zoo or the theme park but she was forced to change her plans due to the bad weather. Instead, she decided to take them to the museum to watch the latest exhibit on witches through the ages and had a homemade meal there. Agatha had always had a fixation in witches and occultism and after more than a decade of being together, Rio also developed a casual interest on the subject and they both passed it down to Nicky. She had checked the brochure for the exhibition and they had a kids-friendly section, so it was a great time to bring Nicky along as well.

Rio reached for a wrapped bouquet of white roses that she had finished earlier before waking Nicky up. It wasn’t the flowers she normally picked for Agatha but a few weeks back, Agatha had worn a white suit to the court and she gave a powerful interview to one of the leading news networks in the nation. Rio had gone back to her hotel that night, drained and could barely open her eyes after days of running around collecting evidence interviewing witnesses. She had switched on the TV for background noise and her sleepiness evaporated once she got a glimpse of her wife on the cheap, janky TV. Agatha was wearing a white suit with a deep V-neck cut and a fitting that perfectly complemented her curves. Rio stood there, frozen, and perhaps slightly drooling as she greedily took in the sight of the woman she had missed everyday while she was away. The interview barely ended when she pounced on her phone.

That night, Rio spent a few long hours on her phone and in the shower. She couldn’t even bring herself to regret it when she had to wake up at the crack of dawn the next day and spend another long day sleepily running around chasing ghosts. Her partner, a junior agent had teased her for being a little spacey and smiling randomly the next day, contrary to her usual poker face, but she couldn’t give a shit about it. All she cared about was to quickly finish their tasks there so that she could go back home and kiss her wife.

The white roses were a reminder for that suit and that night, and she knew Agatha would understand. If she was lucky, Agatha might even be persuaded to put it on again that night and maybe this time, Rio could put her ‘interrogation’ skills to use and interview Agatha…

“Mami, I’m ready, let’s go!”

Rio blinked, focusing again on reality and took the sight of her son in his checkered blue shirt and khaki trousers, looking proper and ready for a day out at the museum. She couldn’t help but to pinch his cheeks and drop a kiss on his forehead.

“Let’s go, amigo. The witches await!”

Nicky carried a backpack containing his drink and snacks, cheerfully munching a chocolate granola bar to tide him over until lunchtime since Rio had decided they had no time to sit down for a proper lunch. In one of his hands, he had his stuffed toy rabbit, Scratchy, whom he declared would like to see the witches as well today. Rio had no objection to that. She quickly transferred the flowers and basket of food into the car, frowning slightly when she saw the heavy clouds heading their way.

Rio then helped Nicky to his booster seat behind the passenger’s side of the car and got into the driver seat, switching on the music to play ‘The Ballad of the Witches Road’ by Lorna Wu which Nicky insisted was a staple if they were going to the museum. Rio rolled her eyes when she heard that, making a mental note to let Lorna know of Nicky’s adoration since Lorna was Alice’s - one of Rio’s oldest friends’ - mother and Rio, Agatha and Nicky saw Lorna regularly a few times a year whenever she was not on tour or stuck in the studio.

Not long after they left the driveway of their house, the rain poured with a vengeance and Rio sighed, slowing down her driving speed despite her desire to reach Agatha’s office as soon as possible. She would never take any risks whenever she was with Nicky, despite having garnered a reputation as an adrenaline junkie at work, mostly because of her bull-headed determination to not let criminals get away.

They entered the highway and Rio managed to convince Nicky to add a few more of Lorna’s songs to the playlist. They sang along to the songs, chatting in between as Nicky pointed out the blurry figures he could still make out despite the heavy rain and Rio giving a preview of what to expect when they were at the museum later.

Both mother and son were also gleeful at the thought of Agatha’s face when she saw them showing up unannounced in her office later.

And then, there was a sudden bright light and a deafening sound and suddenly, Rio was no longer driving.

They never saw it coming.

Pain.

Pain everywhere she could feel. Her head, her shoulders, her chest, her arms, her back, her stomach, her legs.

Her throat felt dry, even if she felt chills all over her body. Something wet was sticking to her…blood? She couldn’t move her muscles, but she forced her eyes to open slightly before hastily closing them again when bright lights seared her retina.

She opened her mouth next and rasped out a name. “Nic..ky...”

The place was loud with chatter and yelling and she didn’t know if anyone could hear her, but Rio made out urgent footsteps and suddenly, Agatha’s voice was right next to her.

“Rio, baby, I’m here. Oh god, oh god, Rio. Where’s Nicky? Was he with you?”

Agatha sounded hysterical and Rio couldn’t blame her, Rio felt the same way even if she wasn’t in the condition to show it right now. She tried opening her eyes again, slower this time, squinting as she adjusted to the lights and she caught sight of her wife’s face that was streaked with tear stains.

She opened and closed her mouth, not producing any sound. She didn’t know what to say. Didn’t know if she could. Then a police officer appeared as if conjured, his expression apologetic and Rio had a foreboding sense of doom even before he started speaking.

“I’m so sorry, ma’ams. Your son, Nicholas, didn’t make it. When the paramedics arrived, they did everything they could to bring him back, but they were unsuccessful. They believed it happened upon impact.”

Agatha’s face crumpled and she fell to her knees and Rio’s ears were buzzing and everything hurt but she heard someone screaming and was that her? Or was it Agatha? She didn’t know, didn’t care because she couldn’t wrap her mind around the words that the stranger had just said because how could Nicky be gone? He was just there with her. He woke up from sleep in his bed, safe and sound and warm earlier and she had hugged him and kissed his little face. Oh god, when was the last time Rio told him that she loved him.

Seconds, hours, or perhaps centuries had passed when Rio suddenly registered Agatha’s pained face hovering before her and she saw Agatha snarled and Rio couldn’t do anything, didn’t want to do anything when she felt a harsh slap landing on her face; she could barely feel it.

But she definitely didn’t miss what Agatha screamed next.

“You! You took my son away from me! I hate you,” thick venom dripped from her voice, “Rio Vidal, I don’t ever want to fucking see you again.”

She saw people rushing to restrain Agatha - were they the hospital staff? Or maybe they were her friends. She didn’t know, she couldn’t register anything anymore and then, she blacked out.

They said it was a truck driver who drove under the influence and his blood alcohol content (BAC) was almost three times the legal limit.

They said the road was slippery from the heavy rain.

They said he was breaking the speed limit when he swerved off to the wrong lane at the wrong side of the road.

They said that the passenger’s side was hit with high impact and whatever happened would be over in an instant.

They said Rio was thrown out of the car, practically flying for a few feet and rolled around, landing harshly on her back.

They whispered that Rio was lucky to be alive, to get out of such a wreck with cuts and bruises and concussions and minor spinal injury and a broken leg.

Rio didn’t feel lucky, not for a second.

Lilia cried the nights when she stayed over by Rio’s bedside, when she thought Rio was asleep.

Rio caught a glimpse of a stuffed rabbit and white roses covered in blood in the news before Alice hastily shut it down and Jen started talking loudly about the lunch menu that day.

Rio vomited in the bucket beside her bed.

Agatha never visited.

The divorce happened in the blink of an eye.

She was served with the papers while she was still bed-ridden with a cast on her leg, recovering from her injuries. Lilia, one of her oldest friends who was also a mother figure to her and the one who encouraged her to chase after Agatha during the early days of their courtship was also the one who handed her the papers, looking much older from her actual age as regret, pain and sympathy were etched deeply on the lines of her face. When Rio accepted them, she felt a bone-deep exhaustion she had never felt before in her life, not even when she was pushed to the brink while training to be a federal agent.

Her hand was trembling as she signed her name to the dotted lines - not because of her injuries, but out of the visceral pain in her heart. The content of the papers was straightforward enough: Agatha would let go of the house they shared to Rio and the money in their joint account would be split equally between them. They would get to keep any assets and cash in their own names. No talk about custody, no, because Nicky was no longer around for that.

Rio shut her eyes tightly, taking a deep breath to stop herself from screaming, from crying, from begging Agatha to come back because she could not lose her wife, not after she had lost her son. She would never stop blaming herself for causing her son’s death and also for hurting the only family she had left in this entire world.

Now, Rio Vidal was all alone.

Tears fell down from her eyes despite her best effort to stop them and an agonized sob came out of her throat. She didn’t know how to do this, didn’t know how to wake up every day without listening to Nicky’s laughter and hearing Agatha’s voice. Not sure how to go on without getting one of Nicky’s tight hugs and Agatha’s loving kisses. She couldn’t go through the nights without tucking Nicky to sleep and being cuddled up to Agatha. Not sure how to live loving them with her whole heart but having to live with empty spaces shaped like the two most important people to her in her life.

Rio grabbed the blankets by her side and punched them, wanting to do something, anything so she could forget about what she’s lost, so the pain in her heart could be replaced with something more tangible rather than something that festered inside her entire brain.

She felt careful arms wrapped around her body and even with her eyes closed, she recognized the scent of sage and sandalwood that belonged to Lilia. She let herself break down then, mourning it all, accompanied by one of her oldest and closest friends.

Lilia tightened her hug, mourning alongside the girl she had considered like her own daughter. Her voice broke when she spoke.

“It’ll be alright, piccola. You’ll be alright.”

seven years later.

Rio wiped the sweat gathered on her forehead with the back of her palm. She had gathered a handful of potatoes, carrots, spinach and some fruits for the big dinner tonight at Lilia’s house. Lilia said she had invited a few friends and wanted to show off Rio’s home-grown vegetables to them, so she had requested (read: ordered) Rio to bring some tonight and she would plan the menu based on whatever Rio had. So, Rio got digging, working up a sweat despite preferring not to on the day they were planning to have their big ‘Pre-Halloween Week’ dinner, whatever that was.

If it had been Alice or Jen’s turn to host and they asked her the same thing, she would have laughed at their faces, but Lilia was like a mother she never had, and Rio could hardly ever say no to her. She needed to be at Lilia’s house in half an hour and had no time to clean herself up if she wanted to be on time, so she decided to just take a shower there instead. Half sweating and still in the outfit Jen had dubbed as her farmer boy’s overalls, she shoved the vegetables in a produce bag and grabbed a change of clothes, walking as fast as her feet would allow her and headed to her car.

Once inside, she threw the items she was holding below the passenger seat and started the car engine, taking a moment to do the breathing exercises she learned from her therapist to calm herself down before she started driving.

It had been seven years since the accident, since Rio lost her whole family. This year marked the year when Nicky had been gone longer than the time he had been in this world and Rio had increased the frequency of her trips to her therapist and even the catch-up dinner with her group of friends or her ‘coven’ had gotten more frequent as of late to twice a month, because of course they knew, especially since they had set a dinner gathering on one particular date in November every year for the past six years.

They used to have dinner perhaps once every once or two months, which somehow almost always coincided with major holidays, and it was clear they didn’t want Rio to be alone. The dinner attendees would rotate depending on people’s availability but Lilia, Alice and Jen would never miss it and sometimes, Lorna Wu, Sharon Davis (one of Lilia’s sweet neighbours who had just lost her husband), the Jimmy Woo (Alice’s D&D buddy), Dr. Olsen- Danvers and her family (Jen’s coworker) and other various characters would also show up to the dinners, always at the invitation of one of the others.

Rio just showed up alone.

She didn’t know who would be showing up later, but she would plaster on a smile and be polite and spoke as little as possible as she normally would; it was always easier that way rather than diving into the deep tragic backstory of her pathetic existence.

She caught a glimpse of her house from the rearview mirror. If one didn’t know, they would probably think that there was a very happy family living in the house considering how well- taken care the house was, with the greenery and flowers surrounding the empty land around the house. Only when they got closer would they realize the haunting silence that the house emanated – no rising conversations or peal of laughter, no scattered belongings and slamming doors, only the quiet breathing of its single occupant and the timely whirring of the automated watering system when they went off.

Aside from that, Rio had made sure the coat of the paint for the house stayed fresh and did regular maintenance so that nothing was broken or went into disrepair, keeping the house’s condition top notch. Six years ago, the house had been transferred to her name after her divorce with her ex-wife had been finalized. Since then, she had quietly transferred the deed name back to her ex-wife and made sure it was ready to be lived in, anytime she wanted the house back. She had moved out of state once they settled the divorce and Rio had no idea where she was and didn’t pry for an answer from her friends, knowing she had lost the right to do so.

Rio had all her important documents and some outfits thrown into a luggage bag, ready to move out at a moment’s notice if her ex-wife ever came back to claim her house. There was nothing else left for her there, after all.

Rio would probably feel sorry to leave her flower garden and her vegetable patch. Those were there since they first lived in the house, even before they were blessed with Nicky. Rio had a green thumb and enjoyed gardening and once Nicky was old enough to walk, he would often be in the garden with Rio, chasing after small insects or helping to water or plant saplings or picking any fruits or vegetables that were ready. They had plenty of space for expanding the garden back then, since Rio barely had time to work on it and she was hoping that Nicky would want to claim a patch of land as his own garden, but since both were no longer true, Rio had expanded her garden over the years and now, it was becoming more like a small farm.

After the accident and the divorce, Rio had gone back to work for a few months and was stuck behind a desk job. It got a little much after a while and Rio decided to resign and thankfully, she qualified for pension due to her exemplary service. Between that, her savings, investments, and the compensation and insurance she got after the accident, it was enough for her to never work again a day in her life if she didn't splurge.

Still, she went to a farmer's market under Lilia’s insistence and sold her produce, some fresh and dried flowers and some handicrafts. If there was any extra produce after her own consumption and the sale, Rio would donate the rest or send them to her neighbours and friends.

She reached Lilia's house and found that the house was already bustling with activities. Jen was tidying the living room and setting up the tables while Alice was tasked with scooping out and preparing the pumpkins for Halloween decorations. Rio exchanged quick hellos with them and walked into the kitchen to find Lilia, Lorna and Sharon in the midst of cooking while singing and dancing to the music on the radio.

“Veggies delivery.” Rio announced and dropped the produce bag on the counter. She made her way to kiss Lilia's cheek, gave Lorna a quick hug and nodded at Sharon. The women were all busy with their own tasks of preparing the main dishes and desserts and Rio knew she'd be roped in as well once she cleaned up.

“Thanks, Rio. These look amazing.” Lorna was the one who took out the produce and got them cleaned and Rio shrugged, but pleased with the compliment, nonetheless.

“Good season. I have to take a shower, but I'll come down and help out. How many more people are we expecting today?”

Rio saw Lorna and Lilia exchange a glance, but she didn't think too much about it. Lilia stopped stirring her stew and moved closer to them. In hindsight, Rio should have noticed then that there was something wrong with the careful way Lilia was speaking, but at the moment, she didn't think anything was amiss.

“Just waiting for four more guests. You can get cleaned up, but there's no need to rush, we got this.”

“Alright, I'm going to make pumpkin flan later; I've already asked Alice to set aside some for me and she'll help me out.”

Lorna laughed at the thought of her daughter being in the kitchen and Rio grinned, expecting the reaction. Lilia shook her head and groaned, muttering what Rio was sure to be Sicilian prayers and she waved to them before heading to the second floor where the guest bedroom and bathroom was located.

Once she was done showering, she headed downstairs again, ready for her kitchen duties. She was walking past the front door when the doorbell suddenly rang, so she yelled a bored, “I’ll get it” so no one had to leave whatever they were doing, barely hearing the panicked “Rio, wait!” from Alice and the rushed footsteps when she pulled open the door.

Rio froze.

Standing before her was a figure she hadn’t set her eyes on for years, who didn’t appear surprised to see her, but still looked resigned and had a forced smile on her face.

Rio thought she was hallucinating as she took in the sight of Agatha in a long brown coat, white turtleneck sweatshirt and a pair of blue jeans. It had been seven years, but she didn’t think her heart would ever not quicken at the sight of the woman she had loved the most - even if her heart might have different reasons to do so now.

Was she really here?

Rio was lost in thoughts, not realizing that the other occupants in the household had rushed over and were standing behind her, whisper-yelling to each other in a low volume. Her eyes were locked with Agatha’s blue ones, and it felt like an eternity had passed when an unfamiliar voice broke through the growing tension.

“Agatha? Is everything alright? Can I call the boys - oh, hello! I wasn’t expecting such a grand welcome at the doorstep.”

A beautiful brunette with a bright smile walked from behind Agatha and just like that, the spell was broken. Rio let out a breath she didn’t know she was holding and stepped behind the others, letting someone else take the rein because a whirl of thoughts were crashing together in her mind.

She knew that her friends had kept in touch with Agatha after Agatha moved away, but she wasn’t expecting them to invite her tonight, although the suspicious actions earlier were starting to make sense now. Rio felt a surge of anger at being ambushed like this, but the feeling quickly dissipated to make way for exhaustion, her constant companion that had been with her for the past seven years.

Rio barely registered when Lilia stepped up to welcome the newcomer, Wanda Maximoff, into her house and she had apparently brought her 12-year-old children as well to the dinner, Tommy and Billy Maximoff. Rio blankly stared when Agatha started introducing the group to Wanda and her children. Agatha easily pointed out Lilia, Lorna, Alice and Jen, but stayed quiet when it came to Sharon and Rio.

Sharon was all too happy to introduce herself to them, unoffended since Agatha had never met her before. Her cheery disposition matched Wanda’s perfectly and they traded some light conversation of the travel and the weather before Wanda switched her attention to the only person in the room who wasn’t introduced yet.

“Rio.”

Rio offered in a hoarse voice when she saw Wanda’s enquiring gaze and didn’t miss it when Wanda threw a quick sidelong glance to Agatha who pointedly was looking at everything and everyone that wasn’t named Rio. Rio felt a pang of pain that quickly turned to numbness when she remembered she had expected this and worse if she ever met Agatha again. Wanda, to her credit, took everything in stride and reached out to shake Rio’s hand, despite Rio’s less than enthusiastic return. Rio couldn’t even muster a weak smile to keep a polite facade. She did notice that Wanda had emphasized that she was Agatha’s colleague and friend, as if that information was relevant to Rio. Clearly Wanda knew something about her history with Agatha, if the way she acted was any indication.

Things got worse when Wanda’s twins came running from the car and Rio got reminded of how Nicky would be around their age if he was still around, so Rio quickly retreated, mumbling an excuse about needing to prepare the pumpkin flan or else it wouldn’t be ready on time and practically flew to the kitchen alone.

Once there, the familiar choking pressure around her neck increased and she gasped for air. There was a growing pain in her chest, and she coughed, her chest heavy and slow as she tried to work on the steps that she learned in therapy to overcome an anxiety attack. She focused on the senses exercise, eyes roaming over the myriad of cut vegetables and naming them in her mind, touching the smooth granite of the kitchen counter and the soft fabric of her own clothing, listening and smelling the boiling stew left on the stove, and finally, recalling the taste of the warm cookies she had stolen earlier before going to shower, working on taking slow, deep breaths in the meantime. She was gradually coming down from it when she felt a light touch behind her back.

Rio turned around and sure enough, she saw Jen’s worried face. Her friends knew that when she was going through an anxiety attack, gentle and minimal touch could help to ground her, so they usually would reach for her back or her arm, silently being there with her to let her know she was safe. Rio smiled and weakly nodded at Jen to thank her and let her know that it was okay to let go.

Now that she had calmed down and her senses returned to normal, Rio could hear the sound of music in the living room and the noisy conversations going on. All the adults in the room probably had an inkling of what was happening here, and Jen was the one sent as their scout to make sure Rio was fine. Rio tapped on the counters, letting the dull, repetitive sound further soothe her.

“Are you feeling better now?”

Jen looked at her cautiously and Rio just nodded, not trusting herself to speak just yet. Her eyes flickered to the living room direction then back to Jen, the unspoken questions clear in her eyes. Jen let out a sigh.

“I’m sorry we didn’t tell you earlier that Agatha is coming tonight. Alice and I were supposed to let you know once you get cleaned up, but they reached earlier than expected. Apparently, Wanda wanted to come here sooner - Wanda’s the named partner and the owner of the law firm she’s working in right now - so they just bundled up the twins and started driving. I don’t know what exactly happened, but Agatha just called Lilia out of the blue and said she’s coming back here, so Lilia invited her to this dinner.”

Rio bit her lower lip, hesitating before she spoke, still in a hoarse voice. “Visiting or staying?”

“None of us knows.” Jen replied. “I think Lilia asked her to come around so she can interrogate her further. I’m really sorry you had to find out this way, Rio. We didn’t mean to hurt you.”

It did hurt, but Rio understood. Agatha was also their friend, and they were trapped between a rock and a hard place, especially Jen, who had known Agatha the longest. In a way, it also explained why the dinner tonight mainly consisted of the core members of the coven, because they knew Rio would be even more overwhelmed if her breakdown was witnessed by casual acquaintances.

“I’ll get to work on the flan.” Rio pushed herself from the kitchen counters and paused. “After that, I think I’ll just leave.”

“Absolutely not.” Lilia walked into the kitchen and stood in front of Rio. “Please stay, piccola, you are my daughter and will always have a place in my house. I know this will be hard, but if after tonight you never want to see her again, just let me know and I will do my best to keep you away from each other whenever she is around.”

Rio’s eyes reddened, her heart seized with fondness when she heard Lilia called her as a daughter again although it wasn’t the first time she had heard that. In fact, she had heard that since she was in her early 20s when random circumstances, or perhaps a fortuitous fate had led her to become roommates with Lilia, who was renting a broken flat temporarily while her own house was damaged in a freak burst pipe accident. Rio was struggling to juggle between studying and working part-time during the time, but she still made time to chat and listen to Lilia whenever she could, cooking for the woman whenever her schedule allowed it. The much older woman grew to become fond of Rio, so much so that when she moved back to her house, she even took Rio along, taking minimal rent so Rio could focus more on studying instead of working and worrying about rent.

That was the first time in her life that Rio knew the warmth of a mother and for Lilia, who never had a child of her own, Rio was the closest thing she had to a daughter.

When Rio met Agatha during her final year of university, Lilia was also the one who encouraged Rio to go after Agatha when Rio voiced her hesitance about having grown feelings for an uprising junior attorney from an established law firm. She didn’t feel like she was good enough for the young upstart, but Lilia quashed her doubt and told her that she would regret it forever if she didn’t even try, and she was right, as always. Lilia was also one of her biggest supporters when she decided she would apply and train to become a federal agent, and although Lilia never said anything, Rio knew she was worried for her safety and prayed more frequently whenever Rio was gone on a tough mission.

Aside from Agatha, Lilia was the person who knew her the best in the world, the one who had given a second chance to an orphan like her when no one else would. So, when Lilia promised her something, she knew that the woman would try her darndest to keep it. She also knew that Lilia only wanted the best for her, so she resolved to follow the rhythm of her mother figure.

“Alright, I’ll stay.” Both Lilia and Jen looked massively relieved, which almost prompted a smile from her. “Can you please call Alice here? She can’t escape just because I had a dramatic moment.”

“I’ll get her.” Jen volunteered, clearly not wanting to stay much longer in the kitchen, lest she be roped in to do more chores. “I’ll call Lorna and Sharon too and I can stay in front and entertain our…guests.”

Rio shrugged and turned around, starting to pull all the ingredients required for her pumpkin flan. Minutes later, Alice was dragged in by her mother and she assisted Rio with the flan, quickly escaping once they were done and the flan had been left to cool. Rio could have tagged along with her, instead, she chose to stay in the kitchen and sent Sharon to the front, leaving herself to work with Lilia and Lorna, who she worked seamlessly with, having spent many years cooking together with them.

“Rio, Agatha mentioned that you're an FBI agent, that sounds interesting.”

“I was an agent, but I resigned a while back. Now I mostly work on my home garden - well, I would say it's a small farm at this stage.”

Agatha appeared shocked and turned her attention to their mutual friends, who suddenly seemed to find their meal very interesting as they pointedly didn't look elsewhere aside from their plate. Wanda and Agatha exchanged a quick glance, and Rio took a sip out of her apple cider, acting like she didn't know how the news affected her ex-wife.

“A farm!” Billy shrieked. “Can we visit Ms. Rio's farm? Mommy, please?”

“Urm,” Wanda paused, looking over at Rio. “If Rio doesn't mind…?”

Rio gave a genuine smile this time, probably her first one tonight. She liked it when people, especially kids, had an interest in her farm so she readily agreed. Jen seized the opportunity to mention to Wanda that many of the vegetables on the table today were from Rio’s farm, making Wanda, Tommy and Billy ‘oohs’ and ‘aahs’, praising Rio over the produce’s taste and Rio shyly accepted them, explaining more about the produce she provided. Agatha was poking her plate, her expression looking a lot gentler as she purposely picked up some vegetables to her mouth. They made eye contact temporarily and both broke it away quickly.

The topic of conversations kept moving after, Wanda and the twins fitting in the group like they had always belonged and for Agatha, Rio imagined it felt a bit being invited to a school reunion with the people she genuinely liked, and she was pleased that Agatha got to experience this once again.

Lilia asked them if they were visiting or thinking of staying around here, in a joking tone but Rio knew she was just representing the others’ curiosity, including Rio’s. Wanda diplomatically said that she was visiting but would be staying for a while, since the twins had had a rough year (she didn’t elaborate further), but Agatha didn’t say anything. Tommy whined about leaving his Nintendo Switch behind because Wanda had rushed them for the trip and Rio wondered why Agatha looked vaguely embarrassed at this. Wanda soothed Tommy by promising she’d get his Switch delivered with her secretary’s help. Lorna then invited them all to her concert on Halloween night, which got a rowdy cheer from everyone because apparently Wanda had mentioned to Lorna earlier that the twins were huge fans of hers, but Rio stayed quiet since she knew she probably wouldn’t be able to make it to the concert as she had started to feel sharp pains in her injured leg - those would usually last for a few days, maybe a day, if she’s lucky, but Rio didn’t believe in luck. On the plus side, Rio’s flan was a hit when they started on dessert.

Dinner continued and Rio got even quieter than at the beginning, sweats started forming on her forehead from the pain. It was a relief when Sharon announced that she would have to leave soon. The others jumped to their feet to help clearing out the table and when Rio stood to do the same, she was stopped by a sharp glare from Lilia, who had been monitoring her since earlier.

The woman called out for Alice, asking her to bring Rio back in Rio’s car. That prompted a curious look from Agatha, which Rio regretted, because she really didn’t want Agatha to know that there was something wrong with her. Alice took one look at Rio and easily accepted, pretending to playfully wrap her hand around Rio’s shoulders when in reality, she was truly holding Rio up. Lorna chipped in to say that she would pick Alice up from Rio’s later so they could go home together, to which Alice agreed, since they came to Lilia’s house together anyway.

Alice and Rio bid a quick goodbye to everyone, and they rushed outside, as fast as Rio’s leg would allow her while still trying to appear as if she was walking normally on her own. Once they reached the car, Alice helped Rio out to the passenger seat and hovered, worried when she heard Rio gasping in pain.

“Painkillers?”

“Dashboard.”

Rio managed through her gritted teeth and saw Alice quickly reaching out for the medication and holding one out to her. It was a practiced routine with her group of friends at this point. Rio swallowed the medicine dry while Alice closed the door and made her way to the driver’s side to start the car. They waited a few more minutes for the medication to take effect and once Rio let out a sigh of relief and thanked Alice, she nodded and started to drive away.

“You guys didn’t tell Agatha about me at all through the years, didn’t you?”

Rio stared out the car windows, taking in the Halloween decorations in the neighbourhood. If it had the additional advantage of avoiding the look Alice was probably giving her, that was just a coincidence.

“We knew you wouldn’t want us to…and she didn’t ask. We would just tell her that you’re doing well in the passing if your name ever came up every now and then, but that didn’t happen much. We know it still hurts for the both of you to hear each other’s names.”

Rio laughed, hollow and broken, something breaking within her to hear how much her found family cared about her and Agatha, despite everything that happened.

“Thanks, Alice.” Rio considered. “I should probably thank the rest too; it can’t have been easy for you to take care of the crippled old me and tiptoe around whatever the fuck that is going on between me and Agatha now.”

They stopped by the red lights and Rio felt Alice’s firm hands holding her shoulders, pulling Rio to look at her.

“Hey, don’t say that. We, I, love you and Agatha. Both of you are family and family sticks together, alright? We might have our rough patches and dislike each other sometimes but the love stays. I thank God that the dinner earlier wasn’t terrible but even if it was, I would still have your back. Through hundreds more terrible dinners if needed. Me, my mom, Lilia and even Jen, even if she can be such a witch sometimes.”

“You still have a little crush on her, though.”

“I really do, but shut the fuck up about that for now, I’m trying to be a good friend here.”

They laughed and when the traffic lights turned green again, Alice let go of Rio and resumed driving.

“Let’s hope there won’t be any more awkward dinners. Perhaps Agatha and Wanda would leave soon, and I can just avoid seeing them until then.”

Rio mused out loud when they got to the house.

With those parting words, it was no wonder she jinxed herself because when she opened her front door the next day, Agatha was already standing there, looking right at her.

Today was a bad day.

Rio woke up early in the morning with an agonizing pain in her left leg. She tossed and turned in her bed and screamed into her pillows before the pain subsided marginally. She knew it was only a quick moment of reprieve before it came back in full force, so she quickly took a painkiller from her bedside that would help with the chronic pain and knew she would have to keep it by her side at all times for the next few days.

Rio sighed, lying down in bed with her limbs sprawled as she waited for a few minutes, making sure the medication was in effect before she got up and started moving around for her morning routine. She prepared a cup of tea for herself and reheated some leftover dishes from last night, which Alice had sneaked into the car at some point while she was distracted by her pain.

Under Lilia’s arrangement, she had to show up at the market once a month. While at first Rio wasn’t too happy about it, she had gradually learned to enjoy it and realized how beneficial it was for her. She got to meet and socialize with other people that way, which actually helped a lot with her mental health.

Once she checked the plants and catalogued which ones she would take with her next week, she would probably sit down and make some bookmarks or work on some resin necklaces since her stock was running low. Those were big hits with students and tourists and relatively simple to make without her having to move a lot, so Rio found great joy in making them.

She ran the to-do list in her mind, humming to herself. Once she was done with her tea and meal, she heard door knocks, catching her off-guard.

She wasn’t expecting any visitors today and while Rio mainly kept to herself, it wasn’t unusual for some neighbours to visit her house asking to buy some produce or flowers, so Rio wasn’t exactly surprised either. It could also be one of her friends following up with her after last night’s dinner. Rio grabbed her walking crutches and headed to the door, opening them with practiced moves.

“Hi Rio! Hey, sorry for the unannounced visit, but I was just around in the neighbourhood and I thought, actually, I just wanted to apologize, I mean, on behalf of Wanda, if you felt pressured about the twins asking to visit your farm - which was beautiful by the way, I saw it outside and oh my god, are you alright? What happened, sweet-”

Rio and Agatha froze, knowing full well what Agatha was about to say, given that the pet name used to flow easily between them once upon a time. Rio cleared her throat while Agatha coughed. An uncomfortable silence fell between them and Rio reflected on Agatha's demeanour earlier. She hadn’t seen a nervous, rambling Agatha in a while, though she had seen plenty of that version of Agatha during their earlier days of dating.

Agatha had told her that it was a bad childhood habit that she had mostly gotten rid of, but it still appeared at times when she was particularly nervous. In her adulthood, she had only gotten that way during her first day in court and when she was going on her first few dates with Rio; both were situations she thought she absolutely couldn’t mess up. Rio used to find it devastatingly cute.

It was dangerous to be caught up in those days again. Rio tried to clear the thoughts from her mind and found that Agatha had also calmed down, the only evidence of her loss of composure earlier was in her flushed cheeks.

“If you don’t mind, would you like to come inside for coffee?”

Rio offered hesitantly, not knowing if Agatha would like to step in the house which housed so many memories of them, of Nicky. Something flickered in Agatha’s expression then, which Rio still recognized as sadness and loss, so she didn’t push or say anything else, just letting Agatha decide where she would like the conversation to happen. Honestly, Rio preferred for it to be inside the house where it was warm and comfortable because cold weather really didn’t agree with her bad leg and when Agatha glanced at her leg and crutches, Rio knew Agatha sensed that too, so she wasn’t caught off guard when Agatha responded.

“Sure, if you don’t mind. Sorry for the sudden visit.”

Agatha repeated and Rio just shook her head, not really minding it, especially since it was Agatha. She went straight to the rarely used coffeemaker in the kitchen, working it with ease the way she used to back then, when Agatha woke up in the morning and desperately needed coffee. Her ex-wife had never been a morning person, but a good cup of coffee always helped, so Rio learned how to make it just for Agatha. Even to this day, she kept the brand of coffee Agatha liked in the pantry, even if she personally didn’t drink coffee.

She avoided Agatha’s eyes as she placed the mug of coffee and grabbed warm water for herself before taking a seat at the opposite side of the table.

Agatha’s eyes were looking at crutches again.

“From the accident.” Rio pulled a wry smile, gesturing at her crutches. “Most days, I don’t really need these but if I overexert myself or the weather gets too cold, it gets…painful. Those are the ‘bad days’ and I’ll need my best buddies on those days.”

Rio chuckled, although there was no humour in the situation. ‘Painful’ was a light way of putting it. Sometimes, she would just lie in bed crying because the pain felt like it had enveloped all of her until there was none of her left – just pain, or there would be days where she seriously contemplated cutting off her leg but those were probably too much to dump on one’s ex-wife. Agatha looked aggrieved.

“I... I didn’t know. They didn’t say anything to me.”

A bitter, angry part of Rio wanted to reply with how it was supposed to be Agatha’s job to know back then, as her wife, and not find out from someone else, but the other part of her that still carried a huge amount of guilt reined her back.

“It’s alright, it’s not something I advertise to the public.” Agatha flinched at the implication behind Rio’s words. “Most people don’t really know I need to use crutches at all. It’s just something I have to live with every now and then, which is fine because it’s what I deserve anyway.”

Oops, getting into too dark of a territory now. Rio quickly tried to switch the topic when Agatha looked like she had been slapped. “Is there something I can help you with? You mentioned Wanda and the twins, earlier? Don’t worry about it, I don’t mind people visiting. I work hard on the farm anyway and I do want to show off a little, sometimes.”

Rio threw in a light joke, but Agatha didn’t bite. Rio would be frustrated if she didn’t know Agatha’s stubbornness was also one of the reasons she fell in love with the attorney in the first place. Rio leaned back in her chair, resigned. Of course Agatha would press on, she knew how much Rio loved her job. The thrill of chasing criminals and working under deadlines, the puzzles she had to put together and solve, and of course the feeling of bringing justice and peace to the families who desperately needed it - Rio loved all of that and more. She enjoyed every second of it, aside from the times when she had to part for too long from her family.

“Yes. After I recovered, I went back and worked the desk for a few months. I hated it. I would have done a million things differently if I could.”

Rio hesitated, before adding in a whisper. “Some days, I wished it was me instead, or that I too, died on that day. Maybe that would be better off for everyone.”

“I don’t…I don’t wish it was you, ever. I don’t want it to be Nicky, but I also would never want anything to happen to you. Losing him was my worst nightmare coming true and if I lose you too, I don’t think I can survive that.”

Nicky wouldn’t have wanted that for us.”

“He would probably sit both of us on the sofa and give us the mother of all lectures.”

Rio didn’t even realize she had started to cry at some point during the conversation.

“He would. You did so well with him, Rio. He was Rio junior, all the way through.”

Remember when he used to sit Scratchy and me down for mock trials? Somehow, I always ended up guilty. I guess that’s what happens when the prosecutor and judge was the same person.”

They both chuckled through their tears.

“We raised a sweet boy.” Rio breathed out.

“We raised the sweetest boy.” Agatha agreed. “I could never have done it if I didn’t have you as a partner.”

Something about that scratched Rio’s heart in the wrong way. She pulled away her hand from Agatha, pretending to sip her drink, finishing it and wiping her mouth with the back of her hand.

“Seems like you got some really good therapy while you were away, the retainer fee must have been astronomical.”

Rio cracked a joke, shifting the attention somewhere else now that the mood had slightly lightened. There was still lingering heaviness between them, and Rio wasn’t sure of her own complicated feelings towards Agatha and where they currently stood, but it felt like a step forward.

“That’s for Wanda to worry and for me to never find out.”

“How is it, working with Wanda? She seems like a great person.”

“She’s been a great boss and a wonderful friend.” Agatha began. “She lost her husband, Vision, last year and then her brother, Pietro, passed away earlier this year, so it had been a rough period of time for her and the twins.”

Rio was surprised to hear the news, since she never would have guessed that from the woman’s easy smiles and laughter.

“I can’t imagine how hard it must have been for her, she’s a strong one.”

“She really is. She has been dealing with a lot, so I try to help out whenever I can. Her firm - where I’m working at right now - is called ‘WandaVision’ since she founded it with Vision, so it has been a rough transition period. They both made me a senior partner months before he passed, so a lot of things also fell on my plates after his passing. I owed both of them much for trusting me enough to hire me seven years ago when I showed up in front of their firm with my resume and my luggage, asking them to give me a chance and to bring me into the firm.”

“I’m sure it’s no hardship for them, considering you had handled so many high-profile cases at that point.”

Rio’s eyes softened, her heart clenched as she thought of Agatha running off to the other side of the country without even a fixed employment, just so she could run away from her ghosts here.

Away from Rio.

“I did, but unfortunately, they didn’t handle criminal law since they were mostly focused on corporate and financial laws. When I joined, they immediately made me a partner and expanded their area of practice to include criminal law. They’re good people.”

Rio thought it wasn’t fair that so many good people had to be subjected to the pain of losing their loved ones, but that’s just the natural order of all things. Sometimes, fathers fell ill. Sometimes, accidents happened.

Sometimes, boys died.

“How long will you be sticking around town?”

Agatha seemed hesitant to answer the question, and Rio allowed herself a brief moment to question the reaction, but ultimately ended up pushing it to the back of her mind.

“Most likely until after Thanksgiving, though Wanda will take the boys back next weekend since they can’t miss school for too long. I have plenty of days off saved up since I rarely used them over the years, so I’m taking the chance to splurge now.”

“Would you like to stay here?” Rio blurted out and immediately regretted it. At Agatha’s widened eyes, she quickly added her reasoning. “I mean, you’re staying in a hotel now, right? And of course you can afford to stay for the whole month, but why waste money when we - I mean this house has plenty of guest rooms?”

It was too natural to let slip that Rio still thought that the house belonged to her and Agatha sometimes, though that couldn’t be further from the truth. Agatha looked at her face for slightly too long, making Rio squirm in her seat. She broke into a smile soon enough and reached out to squeeze Rio’s hand before pulling back (and Rio pretended she wasn’t missing the warmth of Agatha’s hand).

“That sounds like a nice idea, let’s visit it again next week, closer to Wanda’s departure date?”

Rio returned the smile and nodded in agreement.

Perhaps, today wasn’t all bad.
