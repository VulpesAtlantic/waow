Summary

She throws a pointed look over her shoulder before starting to make her way up the stone steps. The heavy velvet jacket artfully draped over her shoulders, empty-sleeved, sways elegantly as she moves. There’s something familiar about the silhouette of it, the way it hangs around her, like a cape, or maybe even a shroud— “Let’s go solve a crime.”

Chapter 1

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for notes

 

Agnes O’Connor works alone.

 

Always.

 

That’s kind of her whole schtick, actually. One of the few conditions that she’s staunchly refused to compromise on since getting her PI license, come hell or high water. People are messy, complicated. Unreliable. They lie and they cheat and they steal and they kill—which usually works out just fine for Agnes, given the fact that she’d be out of a steady job otherwise. The shitty crimes of shitty people are her bread and butter. 

 

Agnes O’Connor sharpens her teeth digging into the meat and marrow of the world’s degenerate criminal underbelly and, most importantly: she’s really fucking good at it. She enjoys the hunt, savors the chase, revels in the satisfaction of a job well done. It’s addictive, intoxicating—except for the times when it isn’t. 

 

Cases involving kids are the best—worst?—example of this. On principle, Aga—Agnes refuses to touch them. She already deals with enough depravity and tragedy as it is. There’s no reason to add the special hell of handling grieving or guilty parents into the mix. 

 

Luckily, the job that she’s starting today seems like it’ll be pretty straightforward. Or, well, as straightforward as any heretofore unsolved multi-million dollar murder mystery can be. 

 

Agnes pulls up to the entrance of Thornhill Manor in her beat-up Toyota Corolla, honking loudly until a buzzer sounds and the wrought iron gates begin to groan and slowly shudder open. The gravel pathway leading up to the house proper is long and winding, a road she’d hate to walk on foot. Typical rich people stuff. Agnes rolls her eyes. Thank God for the invention of the wheel. 

 

There’s a small figure slouched carelessly along the marble steps in the distance. They’re sitting somewhere in the middle, resting between beginning and end. A woman, Agnes notes with a glimmer of interest as she draws near. Even from afar, Agnes can tell that the stranger is objectively, undeniably beautiful—and, perhaps dangerously, that she knows it. She radiates confidence, effortlessly projecting an aura of power and control. Twenty feet away, ten feet, five… Agnes comes close enough to make out most of her features. Dark hair, long limbs, sun-kissed skin. Dressed in black from head to toe. Some primal, buried part of Agnes warns her that this woman is going to be trouble. That she’d gladly eat Agnes alive and gnaw on her bones afterwards with glee. 

 

The game, it seems, is already afoot.

 

Agnes pulls the car to a sharp stop, choosing to park it mere inches away from the foot of the stairs. Small bits of stone go flying from the abruptness of her maneuver, but the stranger doesn’t bat an eye at the debris. In fact, she looks rather delighted by Agnes’ petty opening salvo. She smiles and waves languidly as Agnes exits the vehicle. There’s something unsettling about the gesture, something painfully, profoundly familiar about the shape of her hand and the curve of her mouth and—

 

“Hello there, officer,” The woman fairly croons, shifting her weight off of her elbows and moving to sit up straight at last. Wide, honey-brown eyes peer up at Agnes, brimming with exaggerated innocence. “Are you here to arrest me?”

 

Agnes snorts. “Not an officer, hon. Have you done something worth being arrested for?“

 

She lifts one shoulder in an impish half-shrug, nearly dislodging the suit jacket that she’s wearing improperly over a fitted vest with no undershirt. The jacket hangs loosely across her shoulders; toned, bare arms beneath left outside of the sleeves. “Nope. Sorry.” She doesn’t sound apologetic in the slightest, but somehow her next words ring true. “That body’s not mine.”

 

Agnes quirks a brow at the obvious insinuation, but doesn’t rise to take the bait.

 

“And you are?”

 

“Rio,” she purrs. Her eyes narrow into slits, hungry, wanting. It reminds Agnes of the nature documentaries she watches when she can’t sleep, the only halfway decent thing left airing on TV alongside shitty soap opera reruns and weird Wolfgang Puck infomercials. The mental picture of a leopard stalking an antelope through the grasslands forms in the back of Agnes’ brain. She feels, all of a sudden, a great swell of pity for the hapless prey animals of the world. “And let me guess… Agatha?”

 

“Not even close.” Agnes frowns at the unexpected throb of pain that shoots through her temples as she speaks. The ache lingers. Echoes. “The name’s Agnes.”

 

Rio hums. “Damn. I really thought I had your number.”

 

Agnes realizes that she should probably get a move on here. The clock is ticking; her case is growing colder by the minute, much like the poor sod lying in his grave. 

 

“Are you a member of the family, Rio?”

 

“I’m a friend,” Rio replies, sounding surprisingly serious now. The lighthearted humor from before has vanished in the blink of an eye.

 

“Well, friend,” says Agnes, itching to get started on the investigation. “Can you show me inside?”

 

“Okay, Agatha.” Rio stands, a strange emphasis on the butchery of Agnes’ name. Agnes scowls, but doesn’t bother wasting any breath to correct the other woman. It’s intentional, that much is clear. Rio spins neatly on her heel, somehow managing to be graceful in stilettos so high they look as painful as they are beautiful. She throws a pointed look over her shoulder before starting to make her way up the stone steps. The heavy velvet jacket artfully draped over her shoulders, empty-sleeved, sways elegantly as she moves. There’s something familiar about the silhouette of it, the way it hangs around her, like a cape, or maybe even a shroud— “Let’s go solve a crime.”

 

 

The manor is stunning, opulent—and empty.

 

Agnes knows this like she knows her own name. She feels, without a doubt, that she and Rio are the only ones inside the sprawling building, walking side by side through a series of interminably long hallways like a pair of rats within a winding, labyrinthian maze. Or, more accurately, Agnes feels like she might be the rat and Rio is, well… Something else entirely. The lure, possibly. Maybe the trap. 

 

“Where are we going?” Agnes asks once her patience is stretched thin, a thread pulled taut to the point of breaking. “Where are you taking me?”

 

Beside her, Rio glances over with an unreadable expression on her face. She doesn’t stop or slow down. Her strides stay long and even, dagger-sharp heels making little to no sound as she saunters on. Agnes is left with no choice but to continue following her through the house. 

 

“The study. You should find everything you need in there.”

 

A mixture of doubt and apprehension crawls its way up from the pit of her stomach to the hollow of her chest, burrowing in like an animal settling into its den. It nips at Agnes, stinging like a newly picked scab. It tells her that something is very, very wrong here. 

 

Still, she keeps moving. 

 

 

In the study, Agnes finds a file on the desk.

 

It’s laughably sparse. Transcripts of the maid’s phone call to emergency services after finding the body, various crime scene photos, a copy of the autopsy report with ‘poisoning’ listed as the official cause of death. A handful of short profiles on the people present in the house around the time of the victim’s demise. Rio’s name is notably absent from the list. Agnes figures that she’ll need to rendezvous with the local PD to begin making some real headway on the case. 

 

“This is nothing,” Agnes says, flicking the file shut with one hand and turning to find Rio, who’s made herself comfortable on a plush leather sofa against the far wall. She’s even taken off her jacket, leaving it carelessly thrown over the arm of the couch. The cut of the vest she’s wearing, sans undershirt, bares far too much skin to be decent. Agnes forces her gaze away, looks up towards the odd painting hanging just above Rio’s head. In it, a mother sits by a forest, holding her child in her lap. She’s angry, though, features twisted in dismay as she angles her body away from the presence of another female figure. The other woman is bent over, leaning down as if to press a kiss to the face of the small, pale child lying still in its mother’s arms. Both stranger and child are robed in pure white. The stranger is also holding a scythe. Aga—Agnes, feeling vaguely ill, swallows hard before continuing, “All of this could’ve been an email.”

 

Rio shrugs, and the motion manages to draw Agnes’ focus away from the painting. Some of the newfound queasiness swirling in the pit of her stomach begins to abate. “Not my department, I’m afraid.” 

 

Agnes shoves the useless file towards the center of the desk and moves towards the couch, hands clenching into fists at her sides. She doesn’t stop stepping forward until she’s much too close for comfort, until Rio’s knees are pressing into her shins. Rio blinks up at her owlishly, apparently unperturbed by the proximity.

 

“Who told you to be here? To take me to this room?”

 

Stop.

 

“I go where I’m needed.”

 

“That’s not an answer.”

 

You have to stop.

 

A strange smile spreads across Rio’s crimson-painted lips, baring too many teeth as she huffs out a sharp, bitter laugh.

 

“It never is, with you.”

 

Agnes’ blood runs cold. Her head starts to pound. The next words she speaks emerge at a volume scarcely louder than a whisper.

 

“You don’t know me.”

 

Stop. Stop. STOP!

 

Again, Rio just shrugs. 

 

“Don’t I?”

 

Then Rio tilts her head, and the way the light catches her eyes takes Agnes’ breath away. Literally. 

 

The blooming ache in her head explodes. Violently. It writhes and twists in the confines of her skull, morphing into a stabbing, crippling agony that reduces her vision to nothing but pure static and makes her muscles spasm. Agnes cries out, feels herself start to fall—then a sea of darkness washes over her, and she's left feeling nothing at all.

 

 

Agnes wakes up to the sound of humming.

 

She realizes several things in quick succession before she even opens her eyes: she’s lying down somewhere soft with her head resting in someone’s lap, there’s a gentle hand running through her hair, and that the voice, while quiet, is unmistakably Rio’s.

 

Also, everything fucking hurts. 

 

When Agnes manages to force her eyes open, she’s greeted by the sight of Rio peering carefully down at her. The humming stops, but the fingers that Rio’s carding softly through her hair don’t even pause for a second. 

 

“What are you doing?” Agnes croaks out, but she doesn’t make a move to stop her. It’s mostly because she’s about seventy-five percent sure that she’ll throw up if she even thinks too hard about trying to get up right now. Agnes is a betting woman in most matters except those involving the possibility of vomiting all over herself. 

 

Rio shrugs.

 

“Well, you kinda took a bit of a dive there, detective. I thought you’d maybe appreciate not having to wake up on the floor.”

 

“Still not a cop,” Agnes replies, before closing her eyes again and trying to breathe the way that some yoga instructor on TikTok had promised would work wonders on symptoms of anxiety. Crippling nausea and a sense of impending doom falls under that umbrella, right? 

 

Agnes feels restless, unmoored. Like something’s been knocked loose inside of her, and left to rattle. It’s making her sick, but she can’t find the energy to move and do something about it—if there’s even anything that she can do about it. 

 

A gentle hand settles onto her forehead. Agnes only allows the contact because Rio’s touch is oddly cool. 

 

“Still Agnes, though?”

 

Some invisible entity tries to shove a screwdriver through her eye socket. Agnes winces. 

 

“What the hell is that supposed to mean?”

 

“… Nothing, I suppose.”

 

God, Agnes is probably lying in the lap of a complete and utter lunatic. She almost doesn’t want to be here anymore. Fuck the money, fuck the mystery, fuck the entire fucking case. This shit is getting weird, and not in any of the many ways that Agnes might actually be comfortable with, which is really saying something. 

 

“What am I doing here?”

 

Agnes feels another shrug work its way through Rio’s body. 

 

“You tell me.”

 

Agnes doesn’t have an answer.

Chapter 2

Chapter Notes

After a fruitless trip to the morgue, Agnes starts the drive back to Thornhill, preemptively gritting her teeth against the headache that she knows awaits her.

 

Rio’s sitting on the steps again, this time dressed in an all-white ensemble that’s entirely inappropriate for the cold snap that the entire east coast is currently experiencing. Linen shorts, a barely buttoned blouse that teeters on the wrong side of sheer, gold-rimmed aviator shades. She’s also not wearing any shoes. What the fuck? Agnes wonders if the woman has anything better to do than lounge around an empty estate that doesn’t belong to her. 

 

“Hello, detective,” Rio calls out as soon as Agnes shoves the car door open. She’d decided to park a bit further from the stairs this time, hoping to give herself a little breathing room before having to step back into the other woman’s space. An exercise in futility, as it turns out. The area of effect for Rio’s strangely unsettling aura appears to be virtually limitless. Agnes feels off-kilter just looking at her. It’s like there’s a door somewhere inside of her. Something’s knocking and she doesn’t want to answer. She doesn’t even know if she can.

 

“Still not a detective,” Agnes replies, gravel crunching underfoot with every step she takes towards the house. “Aren’t you cold?”

 

Rio smiles, shark-like, as she pushes her sunglasses up with one hand to rest on her hair. 

 

“As the grave,” she says, hand dropping to her chest to draw a playful “X” across her heart with her index finger. Agnes definitely does not allow her gaze to linger on Rio’s exposed collarbones as she drags her eyes back up to Rio’s face. “What can I help you with today?” 

 

“Coroner’s office was a bust. What can you tell me about how the old man died?”

 

For a long moment, Rio just peers up at Agnes from her position on the stairs. Somehow, Agnes still feels as though she’s the one being looked down on. Like a bug beneath a magnifying glass, ready to be pinned and primed for display. Would Rio keep her in a place of honor? Or would she hide her away? Agnes’ strange train of thought cuts off as the other woman lurches into motion, swiftly moving to stand. Agnes takes two steps back on instinct before she catches herself and stops. 

 

“Let’s talk inside,” Rio offers, and Agnes is once again left with no choice but to follow her. 

 

 

This time, Rio takes her to the kitchen. 

 

It’s a large room, clearly designed to accommodate a number of people at any time. Shiny appliances and marble countertops and custom-fitted cabinetry as far as the eye can see. A place meant to be filled and used and lived in, now standing empty. Agnes runs the tips of her fingers along the kitchen island as they walk by; they come away clean. Despite the recent cause for disuse, there’s not a single speck of dust to be found. Agnes has yet to see anyone besides Rio in the house.

 

Did the old geezer even have a family big enough to warrant the need for such a well-appointed space? If he did, could they all actually stand one another enough to make use of it? Had there been regular Sunday dinners and cheery get-togethers for all the nonsense capitalistic holidays of the world? Or was this just another monument to the man’s obscene wealth? Given the fact that the man died choking on poison, Agnes is willing hedge her bets on the scales leaning more towards the latter.

 

Rio leads Agnes over to a cozy dining nook in the far corner of the room, perfectly set against the large bay windows overlooking the lawn.

 

“Coffee?” Rio asks, just as Agnes opens her mouth to start pressing for the information she needs. The glimmer of mischief dancing in her dark eyes tells Agnes that she’d known as much. Bitch.

 

“Black,” Agnes replies, flashing a wide and sickeningly saccharine smile. “Please.”

 

Two can play at this game. 

 

Minutes later, Rio slips into the seat across from her with two steaming mugs in hand. The one she slides across the table towards Agnes turns out to filled with coffee doctored just the way she likes it. Light with cream and—Agnes takes a reluctant sip—just a little bit of sugar. Again, what the fuck? Rio, enjoying her own drink, doesn’t bat an eye at Agnes’ sudden scowl. 

 

“This isn’t black,” Agnes grits out, setting her cup back down with an accusatory thunk! If she keeps grinding her teeth like this, her dentist is going to have a field day. Or an aneurysm. She unclenches her jaw. “But thanks.”

 

“I thought you might like it better this way.”

 

“I don’t,” Agnes lies.

 

“Okay,” Rio says, like she can tell. She tilts her head to the side. The sunlight streaming in through the windows turns the brown of her eyes into molten pools of gold. “Weren’t you going to ask me some questions?”

 

Well, if she’s gonna give her an out… Waste not, want not.

 

“Yeah.” Agnes drums her fingers along the table’s sleek surface. Long-held knowledge stirs in the back of her brain. The table is made of oak. An ancient symbol of wisdom. Strength. The endurance of the ages. She doesn’t remember where she’d learned all this. Just that she had, once upon a time. It had been her first experience learning at the hands of someone kind. Aga-Agnes remembers being an eager pupil, remembers wanting nothing more than to impress them with the veracity of her mind. But she doesn’t remember her teacher’s face. Agnes shakes herself back to the present with a frown. “What do you know about how the old man kicked the bucket?”

 

Rio shrugs. The movement nudges the half-buttoned front of her shirt open even wider than before, exposing more of her chest to view. Her sternum is bare. Agnes’ mouth goes dry. The goddamn menace sitting in front of her isn’t even wearing a bra. 

 

“Same as you, I suspect. He was poisoned. The maid found him. End of story.” The corners of Rio’s mouth quirk upwards into an odd smile. “His story, at least. The world spins on.”

 

“You don’t seem all that torn up about this guy’s death. I thought you were friends.”

 

“Death is just another part of life,” Rio says, surprisingly earnest. She leans forward to rest her elbows on the table. “It’s the natural order of all things.”

 

Agnes snorts. She hadn’t pegged Rio to be the woo-woo type. Whatever. There’s a sucker born every minute.

 

“Sure, but there’s nothing natural about the way he died. Someone went and offed him. Specifically. You don’t have any thoughts on who might’ve wanted him dead?”

 

All too calmly, Rio replies, “No, I don’t.”

 

Agnes gulps down another sip of her stupid, perfect coffee before pushing on.

 

“Then what is it you do think, then?”

 

An edge of something flickers across Rio’s face, too quick to puzzle out. There and gone in an instant, folded up and put away before Agnes can even begin to understand. Rio’s long, graceful fingers twitch against the pale ceramic of her mug.

 

“I think that sometimes, people die. It doesn’t always have to make sense. It doesn’t always have to be understood. Sometimes, death just is.”

 

Inexplicably, Agnes feels the muscles of her throat go tight. She forces herself to take another drink, to swallow everything back down.

 

“That’s a philosophical way of looking at things,” Agnes offers Rio a sardonic smile. “Unfortunately, I’m getting paid to find real answers.”

 

Rio shrugs, as if to say: Go ahead. Be my guest. The sheer nonchalance of the motion makes Agnes’ blood boil.

 

“Why are you here, in this house, if you don’t even care?”

 

Before the words even finish leaving her mouth, Agnes knows that she’s said the wrong fucking thing. Rio’s sunlit eyes blaze hotter, burning impossibly bright. Every ounce of her cool insouciance has dissolved into blind, naked fury.

 

“I do care,” she snarls, and then the mug held between her white-knuckled hands shatters. Black coffee spills across the table, puddling as the cup collapses in on itself in large, jagged chunks. Agnes flinches. Rio doesn’t move. Coffee starts soaking into her sleeves. “I care,” Rio insists again, voice thick, lashes wet with unshed tears. Another switch flipped, anger gone in favor of a shockingly desolate grief that Agnes can barely stand to look at. It’s dizzying to witness. “I have always cared.”

 

Aga-Agnes opens her mouth to speak, but nothing comes out.

 

There’s a roll of paper towels somewhere on the counter. She’d seen them on the way in. Agnes gets up and walks over to grab it in silence. When she comes back to the the table, Rio’s still sitting exactly as Agnes had left her, still letting spilled coffee ruin her shirt. Agnes steps forward until the edge of the wood digs into her hip. 

 

“I’ve got—”

 

Rio reaches over to pull the paper towels out of her grasp. 

 

“I’ll clean up,” she says, tiredly but not altogether unkindly. A gnawing, bitter ache starts to build in Agnes’ chest. “Just go sit down.”

 

“I can help.”

 

Rio looks up at her with red-rimmed eyes.

 

“Can you?”

 

“That’s not fair,” Agnes says, only the words are not her own. It’s her voice speaking, her lips moving, her mouth forming the words. But at the same time—it’s not. Everything feels vague and muted and strangely faraway. In the distance, her head is pounding again. Something warm is sliding down from her nose. It trickles over her mouth, seeps through the seam of her lips, floods her tongue with the taste of copper. Rio’s eyes widen in alarm. Agnes is too far away from it all to feel any worry. “You should let me help you. I can—” Her voice catches, falters. The words are still not her own. Rio drops the paper towels. “I can be good.” 

 

Then Agnes’ knees give out, sending her pitching bonelessly forward. Rio rushes to catch her before she face-plants directly into the mess of spilled coffee and broken glass. Agnes ends up sprawled against Rio on the bench in a blur of movement, with the side of her face pressed against Rio’s heaving chest. Awareness slowly trickles through the fog clouding her brain.

 

“—atha? Sweetheart? Are you okay?”

 

Oh God, not that fucking name again. Agnes groans.

 

“Still not my name,” she huffs out, barely managing to lift a shaky hand to swipe at her nose. The back of her hand comes away stained with blood. Agnes would push herself up, get herself the hell away from this woman, this house, this entire case, if she simply had the energy to move. Why can’t she fucking move? “What the fuck are you doing to me? Did you kill the old man this way?” 

 

Rio laughs. The sound is painfully hollow. Agnes feels the echo of it within her own chest, feels it slide into her bloodstream and take root somewhere beneath her ribs.

 

“I didn’t kill anyone. I’m not trying to kill you.” Rio pauses. Shifts. Runs a possessive hand through Agnes’ hair. Lets her nails drag gently along Agnes’ scalp. The sensation is so familiar that it hurts. It’s like a railroad spike is being driven right through her skull, one hard-hammered blow at a time, but Agnes can’t bring herself to tell Rio to knock it off. There’s something soothing about the pain. “Even if I could, I wouldn’t.” 

 

“That doesn’t make any sense,” Agnes whispers back. But somehow, to some shadowy, aching part of her, it does.

 

Nothing but silence exists between them for the span of several desperate heartbeats before Rio finally answers.

 

“Does it really have to?”
