Summary

She marched in stride behind Rio. And like it was nothing, Rio continued “I needed to protect you.”

“Protect me? Up until today I’ve never had shots fired in my direction so it seems like you’ve done the exact opposite of protecting me.” Agatha fired back.

They were in another room now. Rio was crouched down, entering the code to unlock a massive safe. Rio looked back up at her and grabbed Agatha’s hand to pull her down to the ground with her.

Right. They were being shot at.

Or:

They’re jaded ex-wives with one big misunderstanding between them—at least that’s what Rio would say.

Chapter 1

You’re all useless, she thought. 

 

Scratching of pens ceased, shuffling papers stilled, and she only noticed the silence because the sound of her stiletto on the tile rang across the conference room. She turned from the floor to ceiling windows overlooking the city to face her team. 

 

“Oh?” She said, dripping with sarcasm. “Did I say that out loud?” 

 

“Yes, Agatha, you did.” Jen said. Her only useful employee. The only person who seemed to be able to solve problems rather than create them, and for that she was allowed the slightest bit of attitude. 

 

She strutted toward the head of the table, where she had previously been sitting and listening to the drivel from these people before she couldn’t take it anymore. She hoped if she could get their faces out of her line of vision for a moment then she might despise them all a little less, but the skyline only made her wish she was across town in her brownstone with Nicky. But even if she was home now, he wouldn’t be there—it was his week with his other mother. 

 

She woke up her laptop to an urgent email accosting the top of her inbox from her partner. Nothing in the body, only the subject line come to my office.

 

“How lucky for you all,” she said without looking up from her laptop. “There’s only one person to free you from my wrath and she’s summoned me immediately.” 

 

She waved her hand, dismissing them all from the conference room and they all frantically shuffled their belongings together to escape as quickly as possible.

 

“I expect a solution for this by 9 a.m. tomorrow.”

 

She felt them all deflate as they filed out of the room. They’d likely be stuck here all night desperately trying to figure out how to win this case. If they wanted to work at one of the most in demand law firms in New York, then late nights in the office were mandatory and only one of the many sacrifices they’d have to make. They better get used to it now. 

 

Agatha was certain Lilia knew the vague demand of her email would entice Agatha instantly. She could feel the excitement tingling in her fingertips as she marched across the office of Calderu & Harkness considering the possibilities. A new client? A new client stolen from another law firm? A new client stolen from another law firm with millions to spend on legal work? She only hoped. 

 

She entered Lilia’s office without knocking and immediately poured two glasses of bourbon from the decanter Lilia kept next to her shelves of hornbooks.

 

“Celebrating before you even know why I wanted you here?” 

 

She handed the glass to Lilia, cheersing in one motion before she sat in the expensive leather chair from some Italian designer that Lila had custom made. They’re divine, Agatha, it’ll be worth it. 

 

“You wouldn’t have been so vague if it was bad news.” She said, swirling her rocks glass, the rich amber liquid spinning slowly. “You want to see my reaction when you tell me to my face.”

 

Lilia laughed. Sipped her bourbon. And smiled wickedly. 

 

“We’ve got a new client who asked for you by name. Normally, I would be jealous, but I do agree with him that you’re the perfect lawyer to be on retainer for his specific brand of work.” 

 

“Careful, don’t insult me.” Agatha said. She had a special knack for keeping dirty businesses looking squeaky clean if the feds poked around. She knew she had a limit to what she would hide for her clients, but she had yet to reach said limit. 

 

“He’ll tell you everything you need to know over dinner tomorrow night.”

 

Agatha paused. Not the most out of pocket request from a client, but a little alarming that Lilia was continuing to give her nothing about this client. She pursed her lips as she thought.

 

“This better not be a date.” She quipped. 

 

“Honey, I’ve known you long enough that I know you’d set my house on fire if I thought a man was what you were after.” 

 

“Then why are you being so vague?” She asked, moderately irritated now. 

 

“He insisted.” 

 

Men are so infuriating, she thought. 

 

“Fine. Tell him to make a reservation at Marea.”

 

 

The city lights blurred by, flashing through the tinted windows as Agatha caught up on emails in the back of her town car. She didn’t need to keep working at this hour, but if she stopped for a second and let her mind drift, it was guaranteed she would call Nicky. Her weeks away from him were unbearable, but she worked hard to hide the pain from everyone. It was only when she was alone that she had to truly fight her impulse to reach out to him.

 

She was grateful this new client had insisted on dinner so promptly. She’d only have to fill one more night alone before her ex-wife dropped off Nicky on Friday and some sense of balance would return to her world again. 

 

The door to the town car opened and her driver offered her a hand. “Have a good evening, ma’am.” 

 

The hostess at the restaurant recognized her immediately, giving her the attention she demanded despite the crowd of people lingering and waiting to be seated. “Ms. Harkness, right this way.” No doubt they were seething with resentment as she brushed by them all. 

 

She sauntered across the dining room floor, in step behind the hostess. She scanned the faces at the tables while working to dawn a mask of indifference. She never wanted a new client to know how intrigued she was and certainly not one who had worked so hard to conceal the nature of his work.

 

When the hostess finally gestured to the table she was meant to sit at, an unremarkable man in a well fitted suit sat scrolling through his phone. His gaunt, angular face and slicked back hair reminded her of a comic book villain Nicky would be obsessed with. 

 

He stood, slipping his phone into his suit pocket, and offered his hand. “Delighted to meet you, Ms. Harkness.” 

 

She thought nothing remarkable of his handshake.

 

“Likewise, Mr.–” 

 

“Lucifer will do.” 

 

Eyebrow, raised. “You’ve done well to match your aesthetic to your name.”

 

“You’re not the first to say so.” He laughed, almost sinisterly, notably in line with the rest of his appearance. 

 

Yet she wasn’t immediately put off by him. She thought she could certainly come to even like him if he truly had millions to spend with her law firm.

 

The waiter approached the table and Lucifer ordered a bottle of wine, he offered for Agatha to taste it for approval instead of himself. It was chocolatey, rich, and coated the inside of her mouth with a delicious tartness. Lucifer had taste.

 

She ordered the casarecce, Lucifer the fusilli—the signature dish on their menu, served with octopus and bone marrow. Agatha had had it many times and was never once disappointed. She was pleased with his choices so far and had little criticism to make of his company.

 

They maintained the simplest of small talk as the waiter came and went, but by the time the bottle of wine was nearly gone, Agatha was beginning to feel agitated that this man still hadn’t revealed the nature of his work and what he needed from her. She didn’t want to do this dance anymore.

 

“Why did you insist on this dinner, Lucifer?” 

 

He took a sip of wine before he replied. “Because our work rides a very narrow line, and I wanted to see how sharp you are before I hired you.” 

 

The arrogance. Rage flared from deep in her gut, but she washed it away with a sip of wine. She didn’t need to prove herself to this man. There were hundreds of potential clients in this city that would be willing to spend his kind of money. Now she was bored and offended.

 

“So your work is illegal?” 

 

“We provide a discrete service with results. Without error.” 

 

“You didn’t answer my question.” She pushed. 

 

“Ever the lawyer, Agatha.”

 

“I’ve worked with many clients who run—let’s just say—messy businesses, but I couldn’t protect them if they didn’t tell me everything I needed to know.” 

 

“All we need you to do is review our contracts and make sure there aren’t any loopholes for people to escape through.” 

 

“Any average lawyer can do that for you. You’re wasting my time.” She waved her hand, she was getting frustrated again. All the intrigue had faded and she didn’t care to be Lucifer’s personal pet lawyer for simple contract work. She’d offer up Jen and promise to be available for high priority cases. 

 

“We work in covert operations. Not for a country, but for anyone that can afford to hire us.”

 

The confession of this information sent a near orgasmic feeling uncoiling through Agatha, an intoxicating rush that flooded her senses. This wasn’t just any client—this could be the client of her lifetime. This kind of opportunity only came once in a generation. She could already feel the weight of success pressing down on her shoulders, the satisfaction of securing something monumental. Her thoughts raced, envisioning how everything could fall into place, how this case could become the crown jewel of her professional portfolio. In an instant, the mundane tasks of her current caseload seemed insignificant, expendable. She would clear her schedule, put everything else on hold, and dedicate herself entirely to this. A slow, self-assured smirk spread across her face, the first spark of triumph igniting in her chest.

 

“Well, Lucifer, now I’m suddenly very interested in reviewing those contracts.” 

Chapter 2

It wasn’t that she liked helping her clients get away with corporate misdemeanors; it was more that she liked finding ways to bend the law in her favor. And so what if a CEO went bankrupt and lost everything? Most of them had generational wealth to protect from true ruin. 

 

Agatha didn’t have that kind of safety net. Her whole life had been a series of surviving trial after trial, with only one notable period of serenity. But, of course, that eventually came to an end. In the grand tapestry of her life, her time as a family with Rio and Nicky felt like a fleeting moment—almost like a fever dream. Now, the brief time she had spent with Nicky remained the only fragment of peace left in her life.

 

Despite the fact that she could see Rio clearly in his face, she felt nothing but love when he looked up at her. She could not say the same about Rio—merely the thought of her started a fire in her chest. And the sight of her face? Agatha could barely hold back a snarl.

 

She was due with Nicky any minute now, and it was certainly like Rio not to communicate her exact arrival time. Tapping her fingers on the kitchen island, she googled Lucifer’s name, hoping to find more, or really any, information about him. She knew it was a vain attempt, but her curiosity was mounting. She’d have to get one of their tech guys on it next week. They could probably access parts of the internet that Agatha didn't even know existed.

 

She heard the crack of the front door opening, followed by Nicky kicking off his shoes. He ran into the house, up to his room as he cheerily shouted, “Mom I’m home, Má needs to talk to you!” 

 

“Thanks for the greeting, dear.” She murmured walking past the staircase, on her way to the front of the house, picking up Nicky’s shoes and coat to put in the entry closet. Whatever Rio wanted could wait. 

 

Just outside the front door, Rio was leaning on the stone balustrade, inspecting the potted plants on the porch. 

 

“These need water,” she said.

 

“I’ll get right on that.” Agatha replied. She waited for a second, assuming Rio would ask whatever it was on her mind. This sort of interaction would happen every once and awhile between them; Rio asking some oddly specific question about Agatha’s week or day, Agatha playing along to get away from her as soon as possible. But Rio only looked at her and Agatha’s skin crawled under the scrutiny. She didn’t have the right to appraise Agatha so openly anymore. “What do you want, Rio?”

 

“I just wanted to know how you were doing.” 

 

“Please,” she scoffed. “What do you really want?” 

 

Rio smirked. An expression that used to excite Agatha endlessly. Now, it was only irritating.

 

“Were you at Marea last night?” She asked.

 

“What, are you keeping tabs on me?” She couldn’t hide the shock in her voice. 

 

“If I were, I wouldn’t have to ask if you were there."

 

She was too pleased with herself. Agatha wished she could get rid of that smug look with a wave of her hand.

 

“Fine. Yes, I was there.” She admitted. 

 

“Did you get the octopus?” 

 

She rolled her eyes. She still remembered how much Agatha loved that dish. “Get to your point, Rio.” 

 

“I hoped you’d tell me if you were dating someone, you know, since we share custody of our son.” 

 

Agatha laughed, and then simply couldn’t stop herself. God, did she want this woman off her porch, but at least she got a good laugh out of it. “I was out with a new client. Don’t worry, you didn’t turn me straight .” 

 

A strange look passed across Rio’s face—not one of offense, but something that Agatha didn't immediately recognize. Had she not grown to despise this woman so strongly she might have asked what she was thinking in that moment.

 

“Go home, Rio. I’ll see you next week.” 

 

She slipped past Rio to go back inside, but she grabbed Agatha’s wrist before she could get all the way in. She snarled, but her heart betrayed her, beating faster over the gentle contact from someone who once knew her better than she knew herself.

 

“Seriously, water your plants.” Rio smirked. She let her thumb linger on inside of Agatha's wrist as she let go. “Goodbye, Agatha.”  



Chapter 3

Nicky begged to go to the Museum of Natural History to see the new elephant exhibit, and Agatha never denied him anything. They had been here dozens of times, to every museum in New York at least once—Nicky’s curiosity for the world was insatiable.

 

Even when the exhibition itself didn't interest her, Agatha couldn't help but be drawn in by the excitement that lit up his face. His enthusiasm was contagious. Though she hadn't thought about elephants since childhood, as Nicky shared everything he’d learned, they quickly became the most fascinating creatures in the world to her.

 

They wandered around to each exhibit, even the ones Nicky knew well, before they took the train back home. He fell asleep with his head half on her shoulder, snoring lightly into her ear. He was eleven now, Agatha knew these tender moments would be gone soon. She considered taking the train past their stop to savor the moment a little bit longer. 

 

As Nicky slept, she checked her work email out of habit. Still no word from Lucifer. She'd never been this eager about a client in her entire career, and the waiting was starting to drive her mad.

 

 

Agatha made a point to leave work on time when Nicky was staying with her, but her team had continued to blunder the case they were working on and she had no choice but stay late to work on it. But her sweet boy loved coming to her office after school, and that was the only reason she didn’t feel guilty about their abrupt change of plans for the evening. She found him spinning in her desk chair when she came back from a meeting. 

 

“Are you the boss now?” She teased. 

 

“Get me the Kaplan file now!” He held out his hand out, quite like Agatha, while looking down at Agatha’s desk, pretending to deeply interested in the papers on her desk. His imitation of Agatha’s impatience for her employees was uncanny. He smiled back up at her and laughed. “I heard you say that once.” 

 

“You’d make a great lawyer.” She said, rubbing his hair. 

 

“I think I’d rather do what Má does.” He said, clicking a pen repeatedly. 

 

“You want to be a consultant?” She was genuinely perplexed by this. Agatha thought that sounded like the most boring job in the world. And given that Rio’s work caused so many problems for her, she hoped Nicky might hate it as much as she does.

 

“She travels all over the world. I want to do that.” 

 

“Where would you want to go first?” She asked.

 

“South Africa.” He said confidently. “I’d want to see elephants. Like at the museum.” She smiled. Her sweet, pure boy. 

 

“How does pizza sound for dinner?”

 

They ate in the conference room with the rest of the lawyers, the sound of papers shuffling and phones ringing blending with the tense silence. Nicky sat off to the side, absorbed in his Switch, completely oblivious to the storm brewing around him. Agatha, however, was barely holding it together. The mistakes—one after another—were pushing her to the edge. The sloppy work, the careless oversights—it was all too much. She could feel her blood pressure rising with every error she unearthed, every missed detail. She wanted to scream at them, to remind them how high the stakes were and how this firm's reputation was on the line. But instead, she sat there, seething, barely able to mask her frustration.

 

She didn’t want to be here, cleaning up the mess of these incompetent idiots. She wanted to be at home with Nicky. Rio would pick him up tomorrow and she’d be all alone again. She would deny even it if it meant perjury, but she would never admit that perhaps some of her sadness over Nicky leaving was diverting into this frustration with her employees.

 

 

They had gotten home much later than she hoped, but Nicky didn’t complain once—unlike her team of lawyers who were paid top dollar to do mediocre work. She might have to reassess their contracts tomorrow.

 

She was in her bed now, Nicky tucked in his own bed, trying to read the book that had been sitting on her bedside table for an embarrassingly long time. Her eyes couldn’t seem to focus. Maybe reading through documents for over twelve hours had something to do with that. 

 

Out of the corner of her eye, she saw her phone screen light up and she audibly sighed. No doubt another email about the case plaguing her team.

 

She was surprised to see a message from Rio. 

 

Can you talk? 

 

She could, but she didn’t want to. She barely ever had patience for Rio, and after today she didn’t think she could even try to pretend. But she didn’t want to read this book either.

 

Sure. 

 

Her phone rang instantly. “Hello, Rio.” 

 

“Hi. Agatha, I only have a few minutes before I walk into this meeting, but an emergency has come up and I’m not going to be back tomorrow to pick up Nicky.” 

 

“Mhm,” she’d heard this before. It had been awhile, but there was a period of time where Rio regularly rearranged her time with Nicky. And there was an even larger period of time during their marriage when Rio would be delayed and unreachable, unable to clearly communicate why once home. Agatha pushed and pushed, but the vagueness from Rio led her mind down the worst roads to try to comprehend what was happening. Rio passionately denied she was unfaithful to Agatha, but she couldn’t bear it anymore. She was constantly home alone with a toddler while Rio was across the globe doing who knows what with who knows who. 

 

“Look, I know you hate when I do this, but it really is an emergency. Tell Nicky I love him and I’ll see him as soon as I’m back.” She was talking incredibly fast and Agatha could tell she was nearly running now.

 

“Tell him yourself.” 

 

She was unphased by Agatha’s sass.  “I’ll text him, but you tell him too.” 

 

“Just text me when you’re ready to make time for our son, and I’ll have him ready for pick up.” She said, ready to hang up the phone before she started yelling at Rio.

 

“Goodnight, Agatha.” Rio said, oddly sympathetic for someone on the receiving end of Agatha in a terrible mood.

 

“Yeah, you too.” She didn’t even know what country Rio was in or what time it was. She didn’t care, she was ready for bed, ready for this day to be over. She shut off the lights in her room and rolled over in her bed, but her phone betrayed her with another text from Rio.

 

He’s going to a birthday sleepover next weekend. I’ll send you the info. Sleep tight, Agatha.

 

 

In the morning, she told Nicky the change of plans, and he quickly grabbed his phone to text Rio. He seemed more concerned than sad, which she thought was strange, but she had no idea what his relationship with his mother was like now. She had never really thought about the fact that he could be an entirely different person with each of them. Thinking of his own personhood reminded Agatha of his impending teenage years—and likely rebellious phase, if he’s anything like his mothers.

 

“Can I still go to my sleepover?” He asked.

 

“Of course, baby.” 

 

“Yuck, I’m not a baby.”

 

“No, but you’re my baby and you always will be.” She placed his breakfast in front of him and she sat next to him to eat her own. She watched him wait for a reply from his other mother as he shoveled eggs into his mouth. When the reply didn’t come before he had to leave for school, Agatha wanted to squeeze him, but she didn’t want him to know how closely she was observing him.

 

She savored the extra week with Nicky, spoiling him even more than she normally would. Whatever he wanted for dinner. New video games. Staying up past bedtime. She would scold Rio for this behavior, but she didn’t care. She was pissed at Rio, and she refused to have anything but a good time with her son this week. 

 

On Friday afternoon, they took her town car to his sleepover and she hugged him goodbye before he ran into the luxury apartment building on the Upper East Side. She lingered for too long as she thought about Rio picking him up Sunday, how she would get to hear all about the sleepover. For only that moment, she missed when the three of them could share everything together.

 

“Where to ma’am?” Her driver asked.

 

“I need a drink.” She paused. “An overpriced one. In a trendy new bar.”

Chapter 4

Agatha rarely found herself at cocktail bars with people half her age—unless she was out celebrating a big win. Even then she mostly kept to herself, so as not to give her underlings the idea that she was fun or a good time.

 

But here she was, seated at the bar of some place called Superbueno, down to the dregs of her second mole negroni. She texted Jen for a recommendation and she insisted Agatha go here. She could see why Jen liked it here, from the pink ambient lighting alone. 

 

“If you weren’t my ex-wife, I’d offer to buy you a drink.” She twisted in her chair, toward the only person that could possibly utter that sentence. Rio stood, leaning onto the counter of the bar with an impossibly smug look on her face. Annoyance crept up Agatha’s spine as her brain caught up with what she was seeing. She shouldn’t have been here, she should have been literally… anywhere else. Annoyance faded to suspicion as Agatha considered Rio’s behavior the last few times they interacted. What game was she playing? What did she want from her?

 

“Buy me one anyway and explain yourself.” She said, feigning disinterest. 

 

Rio slid into the chair next to Agatha, her body tilted toward her, much like they used to sit. When Agatha wanted Rio’s hand on her at all times. When the rest of the world would fade away when Rio’s attention was only on her. She ordered another round for Agatha, and one for herself before she turned to gaze at Agatha. Her expression gave nothing away.

 

Agatha sat rigidly in her chair, her chin held high as she fixed Rio with an unwavering stare. She was prepared to remain in this silent standoff for as long as it took—an hour, two, it didn’t matter. She knew that with her clients, the pressure of silence always led to confession. The discomfort would eventually force them to speak. But Rio was different. She knew Agatha’s every tactic, every ploy. If neither of them was willing to break the silence, they could sit here until closing time, locked in a wordless stalemate. It wouldn’t be the first time Rio had failed to explain herself.

 

“I got back early, that’s all.” She offered.

 

“And you didn’t think to call me? To call Nicky?” Agatha asked. 

 

“I talked to Nicky,” she said. “And now here I am. Talking to you.” 

 

The waiter placed their drinks in front of them, and Rio, with a slow, deliberate motion, lifted Agatha’s glass and handed it to her. With a teasing glint in her eye, she gestured for Agatha to join her in a toast. Agatha, more out of habit than anything, lifted her glass and clinked it against Rio’s, the sound sharp in the dull chat of the bar patrons. 

 

Their eyes never broke contact as they sipped, a custom in some cultures, yes—but here, now, it felt like an unspoken dare, a silent challenge that pulsed between them. The air between them was thick with something more than just history; it was the lingering heat of passion, now twisted into something darker, something dangerously close to hate. Yet Agatha couldn’t shake the feeling that no matter how far she ran, no matter how much she fought it, she’d always find herself back here—drawn into Rio’s orbit, locked in this tangled, combustible pull. Even if it consumed her.

 

“Yes, here you are.” 

 

The alcohol was starting to melt the defenses she’d carefully constructed around Rio, and with her so close, Agatha couldn’t resist it. It had been ages since she’d allowed herself to truly reflect on their time together—or the wreckage of their split. She had buried it all deep in the recesses of her mind, packaged away and forgotten, pretending it didn’t matter anymore. But with Rio looking at her like this, the past began to stir, and she couldn’t ignore it any longer. 

 

“How’s the new client?” Rio asked, breaking Agatha from her thoughts.

 

“You know I can’t talk about that.” She sipped her drink, then pulled out her phone, hoping for anything from Lucifer. Sure enough, an email waited in her inbox from him with a password protected attachment. A thrill ran through her, excited to dig into that tomorrow.

 

She had what she wanted from Lucifer. She felt less distracted, less anxious, even satisfied. Now. Now she could play around with Rio, taunt her, get the answers she wanted. She leaned in closer, watched the movement of Rio’s throat as she swallowed. “How did you know I would be here?” She whispered, ghosting over Rio’s ear. 

 

Rio leaned in, well aware of what Agatha was up to. “I didn’t,” she said.

 

“Liar.” Agatha teased, her tone harsh. They were still inches from each other now. “I know you still talk to Jen.”

 

A slow, knowing smile tugged at the corner of Rio’s mouth. It had been too easy, and Agatha couldn’t help but wonder why Rio had given in so effortlessly. They stayed locked in a gaze, the tension palpable, until Agatha blinked rapidly, suddenly feeling disoriented. For a fleeting moment, she swore she was seeing two Rios sitting in front of her. Had she really drunk that much already?

 

She placed both hands on the bar, gripping it hard as if it might anchor her. Her head felt strangely heavy, and a cold dread crept up her spine. This wasn’t just the alcohol—it was something worse, something she couldn’t name. Her heart pounded, frantic, as if it were trying to escape her chest. She tried to take deep breaths, but they came in shallow, uneven gasps, each one only making the panic grow.

 

“Rio, I–” she started, but the rest of her sentence slurred out of her mouth, unrecognizable. 

 

“Agatha?” Rio had her hands on her now. One on her thigh, the other on her cheek, holding her face towards Rio. She could see the concern in Rio’s eyes, could tell from her touch that she would make sure she would be okay. But she hated that Rio was seeing her so vulnerable now.

 

“Stop with the…”” she trailed off again. Rio now had both her hands on her face. “...fake concern.” 

 

She tried to push Rio away, but her hands wouldn’t obey, her movements sluggish and uncoordinated. Before she knew it, she was caught, fully wrapped in Rio’s arms. The familiar scent of her flooded Agatha’s senses, and despite every instinct to resist, her body betrayed her, melting into the warmth and comfort of the embrace. Rio’s voice murmured low in her ear, but the words were lost in the blaring noise of the bar, muffled and distant. They stumbled out into the night, and Rio’s soft, almost soothing words reached her through the haze: “I’ll keep you safe,” she promised, as they stood waiting for a taxi, the weight of her voice both comforting and unsettling.



Chapter 5

It was as if a red-hot spike had been hammered straight through her skull, the sharp, searing agony radiating with every heartbeat. Her jaw clenched as a sickening wave of nausea churned in her stomach, the metallic tang of saliva flooding her mouth in warning. Even the thought of dragging herself to the bathroom felt insurmountable, her limbs heavy and trembling under the weight of the pain. She wanted to cry, thinking the hot tears on her cheeks would soothe the pain. What was happening to her? What did she do last night?

 

She tried to get out of bed slowly, but the rising sensation in her stomach forced her to move quicker than her body allowed. She tumbled forward into a room she didn’t recognize and now the nausea abruptly changed to panic. Where the fuck am I? She was still in her clothing from yesterday, albeit quite disheveled. She threw her mess of hair behind her head and gingerly approached the bedroom door, hoping to quietly escape before whoever took her to their home realized she was awake. 

 

She didn’t remember meeting anyone new last night, she just had a few drinks and then… and then? Her mind struggled to fill in the gaps. She gasped, remembering her face only faintly, as the door to the room opened. “Rio?”

 

She stood there looking like a walking advertisement for gym memberships, glistening with sweat and radiating health. Agatha was incredibly aware that in comparison she looked like she had gone too hard at happy hour instead of leg day.

 

“Morning, sunshine.” She smiled.

 

Agatha hated how cheery she looked, it added to the pain coursing through her body. “What happened last night?” 

 

“You had too much to drink, so I brought you back here.” She turned away from the doorframe and Agatha followed her out of the room.
