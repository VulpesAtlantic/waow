DAY 0 6:00 AM COUNSELOR ARRIVAL BUBBLE & TOIL

“No, you don’t.”

Lilia leveled her with a stare, but Rio didn’t look away. They’d been doing this dance since Rio was seventeen. Lilia respected her in the way a military commander might respect a raccoon that kept breaking into the food stores.

They all turned.

“She always comes back,” she added, scowling a little now. “She just likes making sure we wait for her.”

It looked violently out of place against the cracked wooden signs and crooked pine fences. The kind of car that knew the way to Cape Cod and the Vineyard and nothing else.

“Good morning,” she said, voice too chipper.

DAY 49 8:15 AM LAST DAY OF CAMP

The buses were already idling at the bottom of the hill, spitting exhaust into the morning air. Luggage thudded onto the gravel in uneven intervals, counselors hollering names over the low hum of parents climbing out of their cars.

Camp Hexwood looked like a half-taken-apart diorama, everything slightly off kilter. Signs crooked. T-shirts wrinkled. Hearts a little too full.

Agatha sat on the back step of the mess hall, her clipboard finally gone, her hair in a messy ponytail. Nicky was curled up next to her, knees pulled to his chest. His duffel bag sat forgotten by his feet.

Rio dropped down beside them, knees bumping into Agatha’s. “Status?”

“He’s staging an emotional protest,” Agatha said softly.

“I’m not,” Nicky mumbled into his sleeve.

Rio gave a solemn nod. “Right. It’s just your eyes are leaking on strike.”

Nicky wiped at his face fiercely.

“I don’t wanna go,” he said, voice cracking. “What if—what if next summer isn’t the same? What if you guys are gone or you hate each other or—or camp gets shut down because of a frog infestation?”

Rio blinked. “Honestly, the frogs could unionize while we're gone.”

Agatha squeezed Nicky’s shoulder. “Camp’s not going anywhere.”

“But what if it does? My third grade teacher went away at the end of the year and she said she'd be back and she never came back ever again,” he sniffled. “What if you go away?”

Rio looked over at Agatha. Their eyes met for a second too long.

He didn’t mean to. But the words twisted into the quiet spot between them—the one that looked suspiciously like September.

“It’s easy to let things drift away,” Agatha said after a beat, her voice careful. “You think you’ll come back to them. And then you don’t. It happens sometimes.”

Rio glanced sideways, heart thudding.

"But, when something matters,” Agatha continued, “when it really, truly matters—you don’t let it drift. You keep it. You figure it out.”

Nicky blinked up at her. “Even if it’s hard?”

“Especially if it’s hard,” Rio said, quiet now.

“We swear,” Rio said.

“Threat noted,” Agatha added.

Nicky grinned and walked off toward the buses, only glancing back once.

They watched until he disappeared into the crowd.

Silence settled between them again, a little heavier now that the noise had started to fade.

The morning wore on. Goodbyes got louder, messier. Alice cried into Eddie’s shoulder. Jen led an impromptu musical number. Billy tried to fold himself into a suitcase and failed. Camp slowly emptied.

9:30 AM LAST DAY

The last bus pulled out of the gravel lot.

And Camp Hexwood was still.

The silence rang like a bell.

Rio stood in the middle of the field, her duffel at her feet, like she wasn’t sure what to do with herself. Then she turned and walked—fast—up the hill to Hocus Lodge.

Inside, Agatha was packing. She was barefoot, wearing an old Hexwood shirt and striped shorts, her hair in its natural chaos. She was folding her favorite sweater and putting it on top of a neatly zipped bag when the door slammed open.

Rio stood in the doorway, flushed and breathing hard.

Agatha looked up. “What happened? Did you forget—”

But Rio was already crossing the room.

And then she kissed her.

It was all gentle in a way that was so unlike Rio. Fingers curled in fabric, summer salt and cracked lips. Rio kissed her like she was out of time and it might be the last time and she wasn’t ready.

Agatha froze for half a second. Then melted into it like she’d been waiting for the exact same thing.

When Rio finally pulled back, her hands were still in Agatha’s shirt, her forehead pressed to hers.

“I don’t want to drift,” Rio whispered, voice unsteady. “I don’t want this to just be some summer thing. I’m not gonna let that happen. You’re not just a summer thing.”

Agatha’s breath hitched.

Rio blinked fast, trying to stop the stupid burn behind her eyes. “You’re a pain in my ass and you hate my handwriting and you stress clean when you’re mad and you make me feel like my ribs are too small for my heart and I love you… so much.”

Agatha laughed, one small, startled sound that cracked like a breaking wave.

She reached up, touched the side of Rio’s face.

“I love you so much, too.”

Rio made a noise somewhere between a sob and a laugh and kissed her again. Softer this time. Like a promise.

When they pulled apart, Agatha wiped the corner of Rio’s eye with her thumb.

“So,” Rio said, breathless, trying for casual. “When is the soonest I can come visit all the Harvard nerds?”

Agatha smiled. “I already made a table of possible weekend visit dates.”

Rio groaned. “You’re the hottest spreadsheet maker I’ve ever met.”

Agatha rolled her eyes and pulled her in again.

Outside, the trees swayed, quiet and sun soaked.

Camp was over.
