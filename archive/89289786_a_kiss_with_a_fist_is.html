The idea of soulmates feeling each other’s pain is romantic, most people say. It’s sweet and loving and it sucks like a bitch when they die, but isn’t it so nice to share a papercut with the love of your life? Agatha would be relieved except at this point, she’s starting to feel like her soulmate deserves a bit more of a consequence for their recklessness.

Agatha’s dealt with a broken wrist, a shattered rib, a gash above the eyebrow – which was a bitch to cover with makeup – and countless scrapes, cuts, and bruises, none of which have been her fault. It started slow, but has only ramped up in the last couple of years. At first Agatha thought maybe her soulmate was training to be a cop (ew) or a firefighter or something, but that wouldn’t explain The Sickness.

Almost every single morning, Agatha wakes up and feels like she’s dying. Cold skin, clammy hands, dry mouth, heavy nausea that always ends with her hunched over the toilet. All of it. Sometimes, she wakes up and has just caught the tail end of the feeling – sometimes she’s right in the thick of it when she opens her eyes. Sometimes she’s lucky enough to miss it entirely, but those days are few and far between.

She’s gone to every doctor within her regional health plan and they’ve all told her the same thing: not a native illness, talk to your soulmate, blah blah blah. The fact that Agatha’s never met her soulmate – and at this point doesn’t really want to – is the big-ass wrench in the works.

Sure, there’s a little bit of concern there. What exactly her soulmate is going through that leaves them feeling like this. Could be some magical curse, could be cancer – and Agatha would like to say she feels for them, but mostly these days it’s annoyance. Annoyance that she can’t just be a normal person with a normal soulmate who’s not dead-set on breaking their own arm every other week.

When Agatha’s really down in the thick of it – dry heaving into the toilet or putting ointment on another long gash up her fucking thigh – she thinks, unhelpfully, of the prophecies her mother would whisper at her when she couldn’t sleep at night. There is no one out there to love you, Evanora would say in a voice that could maybe, somewhere, be mistaken for soothing. You were not born of love and you will never find it. You are cursed. You are broken. You are damaged goods.

Agatha’s mother and father were an unmated pair. Two people who were not soulmates conceiving a child together was rare, but not unheard of. Being the product of such a pairing carried a hefty stigma. Children of the unmated were said to be cursed with demonic soulmates, or dead soulmates, or death itself. Even in less superstitious areas, they were looked on with pity at best and suspicion at worst. Things had gotten easier over time, but Evanora Harkness still lived in the pre-colonial era mentally, and that dark hatred that buried deep in Agatha’s heart over time until she had no choice but to believe it.

Maybe her stupid soulmate and their stupid injuries is just her consequence for daring to be born to the wrong mother. Or daring to be born at all.

Agatha’s watch beeps angrily, reminding her that the frozen bag of peas has been sitting on her ankle for long enough and also her fifteen is over. Service at Salem Independent Books never stops, which is why Agatha’s suggested more than once to upper management that they implement a commission program, but no dice. And she’s still got magazines to shelve.

Fuck.

Agatha limps out of the break room and down the back aisle of the store, passing Alice in the kid’s section who helpfully calls: “remember, if you ever do meet your soulmate, punching them in the face will only hurt you too!”

Agatha collapses behind the cashwrap and groans facedown onto the counter.

Agatha sighs. “You don’t even know my last name.”

“Harkness.” Rio smirks. “I looked you up on the internet.”

“Goody for you.” Agatha looks from the lilies, to Rio, to the lilies. Rio bends down to scoop Mulch Jr. up into their arms, and Agatha reaches out a hand to scratch his head instinctively. He leans up into her, like even he knows she’s someone safe. His tongue lolls out; Rio rolls their eyes.

“He likes attention.”

“So do I.” Agatha levels them with a look; a test. “I’m a lot to handle.”

“I can handle a lot.” Rio nods.

“I don’t really know how to love people.” Agatha swallows the rock lodged in her throat. “I was never taught. So I might suck at it.”

“I don’t know how to either.” Rio extends one hand for Agatha to shake. “Let’s suck at it together.”

And despite all her better instincts, Agatha accepts Rio’s end.

It’s cold and a little damp, but Agatha’s whole body still feels warm the second they touch. When she looks up at Rio, they’re looking at her like she’s just saved their life.

“May I take you out on a date?” they ask, voice soft. “When are you free? Now, by any chance?”

Agatha glances across the store at Alice, who is watching them both with an unhinged jaw. “Now’s a good time,” she nods. “Buy me a coffee and introduce me properly to this little guy.”

“Happily.” Rio’s grin is like a thousand megawatt sun. The second Agatha steps out from the cashwrap, they link their hands again, and Agatha thinks it might be okay if they never let go.
