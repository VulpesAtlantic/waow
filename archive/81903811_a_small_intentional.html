Summary

Agatha and Rio sit down to plan their wedding. It turns out they already agree on everything that matters.

Notes

This one stays very small. No chaos. No interruptions. No outside opinions. Just Agatha and Rio, figuring out what they want their wedding to look like and, more importantly, why.

There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over the apartment in the evening. Agatha has always liked this time of day. It feels contained. Manageable. The world narrowing itself down to the space immediately around her, to the things she can see and touch and understand without effort. She is sitting at the table when the thought finally surfaces. It arrives the way most of her more important decisions seem to these days, quietly, with a kind of calm inevitability that makes it feel less like a choice and more like a realization she is simply catching up to. She has a pen in her hand. She is not using it. The notebook in front of her is open to a blank page, the edges still crisp, the surface untouched except for the faint indentation where she had rested her hand moments earlier. She looks at it for a second. Then she looks up. Rio is across the room. Not at the table. She is standing near the window, one hand resting lightly against the frame as she adjusts the position of one of her plants. The leaves shift under her fingers, careful, precise, as if she is calibrating them rather than moving them. Agatha watches her for a moment. There is something deeply familiar about this image now. Rio, focused on something small and specific, entirely absorbed in the task in front of her without any visible urgency. The world does not press in on her in the same way it does on other people. She moves through it with a kind of steady certainty, as if everything is already arranged in a way that makes sense. Agatha has learned to recognize the pattern of it. To rely on it. To build around it.

The thought returns. Clearer this time. They should probably talk about the wedding. Agatha exhales slowly, the pen in her hand tapping once, twice, against the edge of the notebook before she stills it. This is not a difficult conclusion. It is, in fact, the most obvious next step. They are engaged. That fact has settled into their lives with surprising ease, integrating itself into their routines without disrupting them in any dramatic way. The rings are still new, still noticeable in small, quiet moments, the way the light catches them unexpectedly, the way her fingers brush against the metal when she reaches for something, but the underlying structure of their life has not changed. Which is, Agatha realizes, exactly the point. Still. There are practicalities. Logistics. Things that need to be decided, even if neither of them feels particularly compelled by the idea of turning any of it into something larger than it needs to be. Agatha taps the pen against the notebook again, more deliberately this time. Then she says it. “We should probably talk about the wedding.” The words settle into the room without resistance. Rio finishes adjusting the plant, her fingers lingering for a second longer than necessary before she lets the leaves fall back into place. She doesn’t turn immediately. Instead, she seems to consider the statement for a brief moment, as if placing it within a broader structure she has already been building. When she does turn, her attention shifts fully. “Yes,” she says, but this time it carries more than simple agreement. “That would be the next logical step.” Agatha’s brow lifts slightly. “You say that like you’ve already mapped it out.”

Rio walks toward the table, her pace unhurried, her expression calm in that particular way Agatha has come to associate with quiet preparation rather than spontaneity. “I have considered several variables,” Rio says as she pulls out the chair and sits down. “Timing, location, scale. Not in detail,” she adds, almost as a correction, “but enough to understand the general parameters.” Agatha leans back slightly in her chair, studying her. “You’ve been thinking about it.” “Yes.” There is no hesitation in the answer. Rio folds her hands loosely on the table, her posture relaxed but attentive. “It seemed inefficient not to,” she continues. “We reached a decision. This is the continuation of that decision.” Agatha lets out a soft, incredulous laugh. “Of course you framed it like that.” Rio tilts her head slightly, not defensive, just curious. “It is accurate.” “It is,” Agatha admits, her mouth curving despite herself. “It’s just—” she gestures vaguely with the pen, “—a very you way of looking at it.” Rio considers that, then nods once, accepting the observation without argument. Agatha sets the pen down, finally abandoning the idea of writing anything. She rests her hands on the table instead, grounding herself in the moment. “How long have you been thinking about it?” she asks. Rio’s gaze shifts briefly, not away from Agatha, but inward, as if she is referencing something already organized. “Since the evening we got engaged,” she says. Then, after a slight pause, she adds, “More specifically, since the following morning. It seemed appropriate to allow some time for… adjustment first.” Agatha stares at her for a second. “You gave it a processing window.” “Yes.” Agatha shakes her head, laughing under her breath.

“That’s incredible.” Rio watches her, her expression softening just slightly at the edges, something warmer beneath the structure. “I did not want to introduce additional variables too quickly,” she says. “You appeared… occupied.” Agatha glances down at her hands briefly, at the ring, then back up. “That’s one way to put it.” There is a small pause. Agatha exhales slowly, then straightens slightly in her chair. “So,” she says, the word carrying more intention now, more direction. “We should probably figure out what that actually looks like.” Rio nods, but this time she does not stop there. “Yes,” she says, her gaze steady, engaged. “But before we define specifics, we should determine constraints.” Agatha blinks. “Constraints.” “Yes.” Rio gestures lightly with one hand, the movement small but precise. “Scale, primarily. The number of people, the type of setting. Those decisions will influence everything else.” Agatha leans back again, crossing one leg over the other. “You already have categories.” “I have a framework.” Agatha studies her, something like amusement and something like admiration flickering together in her expression. “Of course you do.” Rio watches her for a moment, then adds, more quietly, “We do not have to follow it strictly.” That lands differently. Agatha’s expression shifts, softens just a fraction. “You made a framework,” she says, “and you’re already telling me we can ignore it.” Rio tilts her head slightly. “It is a tool. Not a requirement.” Agatha smiles. There’s something in that, something small, something precise, that settles into place for her in a way she doesn’t fully articulate. “Okay,” she says, her voice lighter now, but more certain. She leans forward slightly, resting her forearms on the table. “Let’s talk about it.” The words settle between them with a different kind of weight than the earlier ones had carried. Not tentative. Not exploratory. There is direction in them now, a clear shift from acknowledgment to action, from the abstract idea of a wedding to the more immediate question of how that idea is supposed to take shape. Rio responds to that shift immediately. Not by speaking right away, but by adjusting, subtly, almost imperceptibly, the way she is sitting. Her posture becomes a fraction more aligned, her attention sharpening in a way Agatha has come to recognize as the moment Rio transitions from passive engagement into active structuring. Agatha watches it happen. She has seen it before, in smaller contexts, planning something simple, solving a problem, organizing a task, but here it feels more deliberate. More intentional. Rio rests her hands on the table, fingers loosely interlaced, and considers Agatha for a moment before she begins. “If we are planning an event,” she says, her tone calm but more focused now, “the first step is to define its scale.” Agatha exhales through her nose, something amused already threading through her expression. “You’re really doing this like a system.” Rio tilts her head slightly. “It is a system.” Agatha shakes her head, though the smile lingers. “Of course it is.” Rio does not argue the point.

Instead, she continues, her gaze steady, her voice measured in that precise way that makes it clear she is not just speaking, but organizing as she goes. “Scale determines constraints,” she says. “Number of people, type of space required, logistical complexity. If we define that first, the rest becomes easier to structure.” Agatha leans back slightly in her chair, one arm crossing loosely over her middle as she considers that. “Okay,” she says slowly. “So… guest list first.” Rio nods once. “Yes.” There is a brief pause. Agatha tilts her head, her eyes narrowing just slightly in thought. “We’re not inviting that many people.” The statement is immediate. Definitive. Rio does not contradict her. She does, however, ask: “Define ‘that many.’” Agatha lets out a soft laugh. “I knew you were going to say that.” Rio’s expression remains neutral, but there is a quiet attentiveness in it that suggests she is not dismissing the statement, just asking for clarification. Then, after a brief pause, “but restrictive.” Agatha’s eyes narrow slightly. “Restrictive.” Rio lifts one hand slightly, not dismissing the number, just examining it. “Assuming Jen and Alice are included,” she says, “and we account for your close colleagues and my competition contacts, fifteen may limit flexibility.” Agatha studies her.

“You’ve already started listing people.” “Yes.” Agatha lets out a quiet laugh. “Of course you have.” Rio continues, unbothered by the reaction. “I am not suggesting a large increase,” she adds. “But a slightly higher number may allow for more accurate representation of our social structure.” Agatha leans forward again, resting both forearms on the table now. “Okay,” she says. “What’s your number.” Rio considers. “Twenty.” Agatha stares at her. “Twenty is not a small adjustment.” “It is a twenty-five percent increase,” Rio says evenly. “That is not helping your case.” “It is still within a small-scale event category.” Agatha presses her lips together, trying not to smile. “You’re negotiating.” Rio tilts her head slightly. “I am proposing an alternative.” Agatha shakes her head, but there is no real resistance in it. “This is exactly what I was worried about,” she says. Rio’s expression shifts just slightly. “In what way?” Agatha gestures between them. “You turning this into something structured and me having to argue with a system.” Rio considers that. “I am not the system,” she says.

Agatha raises an eyebrow. “You are absolutely the system.” Rio pauses. Then, quietly: “I am representing the system.” Agatha laughs. “That’s worse.” The sound of it fills the room briefly, warm and unrestrained in a way that softens the edges of the conversation without disrupting its direction. Rio watches her, the faintest hint of something softer appearing in her expression again. Agatha exhales slowly, the laughter fading into something more thoughtful. She taps her fingers lightly against the table, once, twice. “Okay,” she says. “Twenty.” Rio nods once. “Thank you.” Agatha squints at her. “You’re very polite for someone who just increased the guest list.” “I am acknowledging agreement.” “You’re winning.” Rio considers that. “That was not the objective.” Agatha leans back again, shaking her head. “Sure.” There is a brief pause. Not tense. Just transitional. Agatha’s gaze drifts toward the window for a moment, then back to Rio. “Okay,” she says.

“So. Small. Controlled. People we actually like.” Rio nods. “Yes.” Agatha studies her for a second longer. “And no unnecessary complications.” Rio tilts her head slightly. “That will depend on how we define ‘unnecessary.’” Agatha narrows her eyes. “Don’t do that.” Rio’s expression remains calm. “I am clarifying parameters.” “You’re creating loopholes.” “I am identifying potential variables.” Agatha huffs a quiet laugh, shaking her head again. “This is going to be a problem.” Rio does not disagree. “It may require adjustment.” Agatha watches her for a moment. Then she smiles. “Okay,” she says again, softer this time. “We have constraints.” Rio nods once more, her attention still steady, still engaged. “Yes,” she says. “We do.” And for the first time, the shape of it begins to feel real. Agatha can feel that structure. She can also feel, very distinctly, the point where it stops being enough. She leans back slightly in her chair, one hand lifting absently to brush her thumb along the side of her ring.

The movement is unconscious, but it grounds her in a way that pulls her thoughts into sharper focus. “Okay,” she says, her tone changing, not dramatically, but enough that Rio’s attention narrows just a fraction more. “That’s the framework.” Rio nods. “Yes.” Agatha tilts her head slightly, studying her for a second, as if deciding how to approach what comes next. Because this part— This part is not structural. This part does not fit neatly into categories or constraints. It is something else entirely. Agatha exhales slowly, then lets her hand drop back to the table. “I don’t want it to feel like an event,” she says. Rio does not interrupt. She never does when Agatha speaks like this, when her words come a little slower, more deliberate, shaped by something that sits just beneath the surface. Agatha continues, her gaze drifting briefly toward the window before returning. “I know that’s what it is, technically,” she adds, a faint hint of amusement threading through her voice. “But I don’t want it to feel like one.” Rio’s head tilts slightly. “In what way?” Agatha considers the question. Not because she doesn’t understand what she means, but because she is trying to translate something instinctive into something that can be explained. “Not… performative,” she says finally. “Not something where we’re standing in front of a group of people, doing something that feels scripted or… expected.” Rio listens, her expression attentive but neutral, her gaze steady. Agatha shifts slightly in her chair, her fingers tapping once against the table before stilling again. “I don’t want to feel like I’m following a sequence,” she continues. “Or checking off steps. I don’t want it to feel like something we’re supposed to do a certain way just because that’s how it’s usually done.” Rio nods slowly. “That is consistent with your previous preferences.” Agatha glances at her. “My previous—what.” “You tend to avoid externally imposed structures,” Rio says calmly. “Particularly in situations that are personally significant.” Agatha stares at her for a second. Then she huffs out a quiet laugh. “That’s a very clinical way of saying I don’t like being told what to do.” Rio considers that. “It is accurate.” Agatha shakes her head, though the faint smile lingers. “Yeah. It is.” There is a brief pause. Agatha’s gaze drops to her hands again, her fingers loosely interlaced now. She turns her ring slightly. The metal catches the light. “I just want it to feel…” she starts, then trails off. Rio waits. Agatha exhales softly. “Like us,” she says. The words are simple. Uncomplicated. And yet they carry more weight than anything else she has said so far. Rio’s expression shifts, just slightly. Not dramatically.

But enough. “How would you define that,” she asks. Agatha looks up again. There is no challenge in the question. No attempt to reduce what she said into something smaller. Just a request for clarity. Agatha leans forward a little, resting her forearms on the table again. “Small,” she says first. Rio nods. “That aligns with the constraints.” “Not just small,” Agatha continues. “Intentional.” Rio’s gaze sharpens just a fraction. “Explain.” Agatha tilts her head, her thoughts arranging themselves more easily now that she has started. “I don’t want anything there that doesn’t belong,” she says. “Not decorations, not traditions, not… anything that’s just there because it’s expected.” Rio listens carefully. Agatha continues, her voice steadier now, more certain as she follows the shape of the thought rather than trying to define it too precisely. “I want to look at it and know exactly why everything is there,” she says. “Why every person is there. Why it matters.” Rio considers that. “That suggests a high degree of selectivity.” Agatha smiles faintly. “Yeah.” There is a small pause. Then she adds, softer now: “And I don’t want it to be overwhelming.”

Rio’s head tilts slightly. “In what sense?” Agatha exhales. “Too many people. Too much noise. Too much… attention.” Rio processes that immediately. “That is a manageable constraint,” she says. “We can limit external stimuli.” Agatha glances at her. “You’re already optimizing it.” “I am identifying potential issues.” Agatha lets out a quiet laugh. “Of course you are.” Rio watches her for a moment, then says, “You are describing a controlled environment.” Agatha tilts her head. “That sounds very unromantic.” Rio considers that. “It is accurate.” Agatha smiles. “It is.” The conversation settles for a second, the shape of what Agatha is describing hovering there— present, but still undefined. Not a place yet. Just a feeling. Then Rio speaks again. “I think a botanical garden would be optimal.” The sentence lands cleanly. Without hesitation. Agatha stills. It is not recognition in the way it would be if she had already decided.

It is something else. A quiet, immediate alignment. She hadn’t had a word for it before. No image she could point to. Just— Green. Open. Contained without feeling closed. Alive without being overwhelming. She looks at Rio. “Okay,” she says slowly. Rio meets her gaze. Agatha leans back slightly, the realization settling more fully now. “That’s… exactly it.” Rio tilts her head slightly. “It aligns with the parameters you described.” Agatha lets out a quiet breath, almost a laugh. “I didn’t even know that’s what I meant until you said it.” Rio considers that. “You had already defined the structure,” she says. “I identified a location that fits it.” Agatha studies her for a moment. Then she shakes her head, a small smile pulling at her mouth. “Stop being right so efficiently.” Rio does not look particularly apologetic. “It is a good location.” Agatha exhales again, softer this time. “Yeah,” she says. “It is.”

The word settles between them, softer than the conversation that led to it. Rio’s gaze holds hers for a second longer than usual. Then she says, “A botanical garden would also allow for minimal additional decoration.” Agatha exhales a small laugh. “Of course it would.” Rio continues, her tone still calm, still measured, but with something just slightly more engaged beneath it now. “The environment would already be structured,” she says. “We would not need to introduce external elements to create atmosphere.” Agatha tilts her head, considering that. “That’s… actually perfect.” Rio nods once. “Yes.” Agatha studies her for a moment longer. There is something about the way this is unfolding that feels almost too easy. Not in a way that makes her suspicious. In a way that makes her aware. Aware of how aligned they are, even when they approach things differently. Aware of how quickly the shape of this is coming together without either of them needing to force it. She leans forward slightly again, her hands resting on the table, her gaze steady. “Okay,” she says. Rio’s attention remains fixed on her. Agatha’s voice is softer now, but no less certain. “Then that’s the direction.” Rio nods. “Yes.” And for the first time, it stops being an idea. It becomes something real. The shift lingers.

Not in a way that demands attention, but in the quieter sense of something settling into place, like a structure that no longer needs to be held up because it can stand on its own. Agatha leans back slightly in her chair, the conversation easing with it. The sharper edges of decision-making have softened now that the larger pieces are in place. What remains feels less like planning and more like refinement. Her hand moves without much thought, her thumb brushing lightly along the edge of her ring. The motion catches her attention a second later. She pauses, then turns it slightly, watching how the light shifts across the surface. It still feels new in small, specific ways, not unfamiliar, but not yet something she has fully stopped noticing. Across from her, Rio’s focus follows the movement rather than the words. Agatha lets that sit for a moment. There is something almost disarming about being understood that quickly, especially when she has only just found the shape of the thought herself. She leans forward now, more fully, her forearms resting on the table again, her hands loosely folded together. Her gaze doesn’t leave Rio’s. “I just want to marry you,” she says. The sentence arrives without ornament. No buildup, no irony, no attempt to soften it with humor. It lands in the room with a quiet certainty that seems to alter the air around it. Rio doesn’t move. But something in her expression shifts, just slightly, just enough that Agatha feels it with the same immediacy as touch. Her gaze remains fixed on Agatha’s face. Her mouth softens, not into a smile exactly, but into something more open than before. The kind of expression that only appears when Rio has set aside the need to structure a thought before responding to it. When she does speak, her voice is lower. “That is the primary objective.” And there it is. The exact answer only Rio could give, and somehow the most romantic one imaginable. Agatha closes her eyes briefly, not because she is overwhelmed in any dramatic sense, but because the tenderness of it catches her off guard anyway. When she opens them again, she is smiling in a way she cannot quite contain. “You make that sound like a mission statement.” Rio tilts her head slightly, but the softness remains.

“It is the central purpose of the event,” she says. Then, after the smallest pause: “The other decisions only exist in support of that.” Agatha feels that land somewhere deep and quiet inside her. For a moment, she doesn’t answer. She just looks at Rio, at the calm steadiness of her, at the way she can take something enormous and speak it plainly without reducing it in the process. The wedding, Agatha realizes, is not becoming more complicated. It is becoming more visible. That is different. The thing itself—the thing that matters—remains exactly as simple as it always was. Them. She lets out a slow breath, her shoulders easing further, the last residual tension of decision-making draining out of her without her having to force it. “I think,” she says carefully, “that’s the part I needed.” Rio’s gaze holds. “The clarification?” Agatha’s smile returns, softer now. “No,” she says. “The reminder.” Rio considers that, then nods once. There is no further explanation. None is needed. The silence that follows is not the same as the one before. It has more warmth in it now, more ease. Not the uncertainty of transition, but the steadiness of having arrived somewhere true. Agatha reaches across the table without really deciding to do it first. The movement is instinctive, quiet. Her fingers brush against Rio’s hand, then settle there. Rio turns her hand over immediately. Again of course she does. Their fingers slip together with the kind of ease that no longer surprises Agatha and still, somehow, always means something. She looks down at their hands for a second. The rings catch the light side by side, not identical, not meant to be. Just aligned. When she looks up again, Rio is still watching her. Not waiting. Just there.

And the shape of the evening, the shape of the wedding, the shape of everything after it, becomes clear in a way that has nothing to do with logistics at all. It is not the botanical garden. Not the guest list. Not the rings, or the vows, or the exact angle of late afternoon light. It is this. A table. A quiet apartment. The person across from her understanding what matters before she finishes saying it. Agatha’s thumb brushes once across the side of Rio’s hand. Then she smiles, small and certain. “Okay,” she says. Rio’s expression shifts just enough to suggest a question without speaking it. Agatha’s voice is quiet when she continues. “Now it actually feels like something I want to plan.” And that, more than anything else they’ve said all evening, feels like the real beginning.
