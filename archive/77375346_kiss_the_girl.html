“Agatha Harkness! Are you a fucking moron?!”

Agatha sighed, pushed her glasses to the top of her head, and raised her gaze to watch as her manager and oldest friend burst into the room.

“Jen.”

Without saying another word she motioned with her head towards Nicky, now looking up from his book with concern. Jen paused, looked like she was swallowing down some more insults, and pivoted gracefully on to spot to smile through the panic of seeing Nicky watching her from the armchair in the corner of the office.

“Sweetheart! What are you doing here?”

Agatha raised her brows at this obvious attempt to ingratiate herself with both of them and smirked when her son, as smart as he was sweet, frowned up at Jen.

“I’m reading. Mama’s working. Why are you shouting?”

“That’s because Auntie Jen hasn’t had her special medicine tonight.”

“Barolo,” Nicky nodded sagely. Agatha shot him a wink.

Jen was pivoting back now, her smile eradicated as she worked her jaw and placed her fingers shoulder width apart down on the desk. It took everything in Agatha not to sweep the documents she’d been thumbing through from Jen’s sights because if she reached for them, she’d look.

And she really didn’t want her to look.

Drawing herself up to her full height she ignored Jen as she said: “Agatha, may I speak with you?”

Agatha sighed, feeling the familiar bristle of frustration come in at the corners as she leant around to speak to Nicky. “Honey? Go get ready for bed and I’ll be up soon, okay?”

Nicky looked like he might refuse but he slowly shuffled from the large armchair and landed heavily on his feet. His large eyes were full of apprehension and Agatha shot Jen a warning look which made her manager turn and, though she couldn’t see it, she was sure she’d fixed her face into a smile again.

“I’m sorry for shouting, Nicky. Sometimes your mom does silly things at work and it’s my job to-…”

“At work? Did Rio do a silly thing too?”

Agatha’s chest felt hot as she inhaled sharply and raised herself from her chair, “Baby, bed, please.”

“No mama, when is Rio coming to see us?”

When Nicky had been born Agatha had finally understood what people had meant when they’d said having a child was like walking around with your heart outside your body. It was exactly like that. Only she hadn’t expected her walking, talking, mini-me heart to so readily fall for Rio too.

“Baby,” Agatha breathed, moving quickly to get closer to her son. “Soon, okay? You know she’s really sad about her dad.”

Those big brown eyes began to fill with tears and Agatha sank to her knees before him, holding his sides securely as she whispered to him: “She misses you. She just needs some time to be sad, okay?”

Nicky sniffed so loudly and assuredly that it made Agatha smile up at him. “She should come here and we can make sure she’s-…”

“I know, honey. I know you want to make her happy again. But being sad is normal, it’s okay. And it’s okay for you to be sad for her too. Her papa was very kind.”

Nicky nodded and wiped his nose with the back of his hand, “I wouldn’t mind if she was sad with us.”

“I know, baby. But you know what’ll cheer her up? When she finds out how good at Spanish you are! And how hard you’re trying.”

Nicky nodded again and sniffed as Agatha pulled her heart back into her chest for a tight hug. “My sweet, clever boy.”

She felt Nicky’s body relax more the longer she held on and squeezed him a little extra before she pulled back and smiled at the face she’d created in the image of her love. “Go get ready for bed, mm? I’ll be up shortly.”

“Okay, but… you’ll ask Rio about Saturday?”

“I’ll ask, honey.”

Agatha forced the soft smile that was enough reassurance for Nicky to smile back and to go and do as he was told and disappear from the room. On the way out he’d given Jen a quick but polite hug and whispered, “Don’t shout at mama,” then left.

Agatha remained where she was for a few seconds, staring at the chair Nicky had just vacated, thinking about how she would ask Rio to fly cross-country with them when she could barely get out of bed.

“So you wanna hear what Alice told me?”

Pushing herself up again Agatha shook her head and went back to her desk to begin gathering up her papers. Blue eyes briefly darted to her phone, laying in wait for a call from either Alice or Rio.

It remained stubbornly blank.

“No, not really. She hates me so I’ve no interest in her-…”

“She doesn’t hate you. In fact she was a pretty big fan before-…”

Agatha had slapped the gathered papers down, looking more like a disgruntled newsreader than an award-winning actress. She pushed her glasses further up her head as they’d slipped in the shuffle.

“Don’t say it.”

Jen sighed and Agatha felt bile rise in her throat, at the reminder of her fatal flaw. If she were in a Greek tragedy the chorus would be singing of her hamartia and Agatha might soon blind herself off stage.

She wondered if Rio would love her if she were blind. If she’d hold her hand and guide her around. Maybe she’d grow tired of having to help, of Agatha’s constant tears because she couldn’t see her face, couldn’t watch Nicky grow.

She shook her head, making a mental note to mention this thought process at her impending therapy appointment.

Jen was watching her as she put away the legal documents she’d been poring over, watching her with that expression Agatha knew so well now. Her eyes were soft, full of concern, but her lips were pursed, which meant she was about to say something Agatha wouldn’t like.

She hated waiting. Grew tired of the silence. As she straightened up from slamming her drawer shut she said: “Spit it out, Jen.”

“You won’t kiss her?”

Of all the things she’d thought would come out of Jennifer Kale’s mouth, it wasn’t this. She shifted from foot to foot as her cheeks reddened enough to give her away.

“She told Alice.”

“She shouted it at Alice. Why won’t you kiss her?”

Agatha shifted, felt her tongue swell to an unreasonable size in her mouth. This was none of Jennifer’s business. None of Alice’s business. It was only her own. Only Rio’s.

She froze as Jen reached for her shoulder across the desk. “Agatha? Have you changed your mind about her?”

Jerking her shoulder away Agatha glanced at the closed office door before she hissed out: “Are you fucking stupid, Jen?”

“Don’t start with me, okay? Who the fuck doesn’t kiss the person they want to be with when that person is apparently asking to be kissed when they’re grieving.”

Agatha shook her head again, finding some solace in the tightness of her jaw, the way Jen’s eyes flashed with confusion, her posture so tense she’d somehow shrunk to Agatha’s height.

Without meaning to she spat the words out, the truth of them lacing each vicious syllable: “Because I love her. Because it’s the right thing to do. Because I want us to be right. Because I want her to want me when she can think straight, when she’s not out of her mind in grief, when she can feed her body and smile again. I want her when she’s ready, Jennifer. I want her to know I can wait.”

Jen blinked, and reached for her again but Agatha was already moving, already shunning the proffered comfort of her best friend.

She reached the door when she spoke again: “Agatha? She told Alice she was ready. That she was ready for you. For Nicky.”

The shock that ran through Agatha’s body felt so visceral she may as well have thrown herself against an electric fence. It froze her in place, stopped her body from carrying out its duty to keep her upright, in motion, alive.

She only leant against the door, placing her forehead against the heavy grain, staring straight ahead of her as if Rio might appear there in the space her body always made for her.

“She’s too vulnerable,” she eventually choked out. “I have to wait. I have to show her I can. I have to show her I can be good.”

“Agatha,” Jen’s tone now was soft, conciliatory, so gentle it made Agatha hate her for it. “No part of me thinks that if you kiss her she will regret asking you to. Haven’t you both suffered enough?”

“I need to be good, Jen. I need her to know-…”

“Alice has told her to sleep with someone else.”

An electric fence and a cattle prod through her heart. Agatha stammered noiselessly, felt her chest constrict and her vision blur from the hurt of it.

She turned slowly, feeling the chill of fear flash through her veins as she stared across at Jen.

“I don’t think she will. In fact, I’m sure she won’t and I’m only telling you because-…”

“Alice told her to sleep with someone.”

Agatha’s voice was flat, devoid of the overwhelming emotions she felt, as if her body wanted to keep those secret, keep them hidden, trapped within the confines of her frozen skin.

“Yes. But you have to understand that she’s grieving too. She didn’t mean-…”

“Jennifer. Can you leave. Can you get out. Get out of my house get out of my house get out-…”

It happened so fast then, so suddenly that Agatha didn’t even see it happening. Jen had crossed the space between them and heaved Agatha’s petrified body into her long arms. It felt like a rock hitting a hard place.

Jen was trying to be soft. Agatha just wouldn’t let her.

She ripped herself away, and stormed to the other end of the room where she threw her head back, her glasses tumbling forgotten to the floor. Staring at the ceiling she was able to breathe again, to force her heart to keep beating.

In the silence she could hear only Jennifer moving to lean against the door, could feel the way she was watching her, could picture the concern etched into her familiar features. But Agatha didn’t move. She kept still, kept staring, kept hoping her brain would quieten down.

The clawing, grating thoughts were louder than they’d been since Miguel had died. They felt now as loud as the night Agatha had spent crying in the shower over losing one of the two people she couldn’t live without.

Her demons screamed, and the angel with big brown eyes was unable to hush them.

Slowly, as if the action itself pained her, she turned back around to look at Jen. “She wouldn’t. The whole time we were together she didn’t. She won’t now. She won’t. She-…”

“Agatha,” Jen tried in a forcibly calm voice that made Agatha’s body quiver as she listened. “I’m not telling you to hurt you. I’m telling you because you need to go and get your girl, okay? Let her know you want her, that you-…”

Agatha forced in a breath, felt the winged beasts in her brain beat their rage into an inferno inside of her. But Rio was back when she closed her eyes now. She was back and asking, asking her to kiss her, reaching for her for comfort.

The demons stuttered and dissolved.

“I’m not discussing this anymore. I am doing this, Jen. I’m being good. I am good for her.”

“Don’t you think you can be good for her and not be so fucking noble at the same time? Are you punishing yourself, Agatha? Are you denying-…”

“You don’t know anything about it.”

“I know that for years you let me believe that even if you weren’t happy with Ralph that it worked, that you were okay with it because it just wasn’t a priority to you, that you-…”

“This has nothing to do with Ralph,” Agatha snapped, spitting some of that heat in Jen’s direction.

“Actually it has everything to do with Ralph! You finally could be happy and-…!”

“I could never be happy when she is heartbroken! Can’t you see that?! Can’t you see that I’m trying my best for her because she needs be to be my best to be there for her to let her know that I don’t just want her now I want her forever Jen?!”

Agatha’s breathing was ragged, her lungs screaming for a saving breath, a balm in the form of respite from the tirade.

She breathed out heavily, briefly placing her head in her hands before she looked back up. “Rio won’t sleep with anyone else. Tell your girlfriend to keep her mouth shut. Tell her that I love Rio, that I’m waiting for her, that I can’t sleep because I’m not with her and then ask her, ask her if she can take her head out of her ass for one-…”

“Hey! She’s still my girlfriend. Watch your mouth.”

Agatha scoffed a laugh and turned away, moving to perch on the edge of the desk. She didn’t say anything for a little while until: “I’ll kiss her soon. I want to kiss her. I just… I want to give her some time. It’s been not even been two weeks. I can do this for her. Be there for her.”

“If it helps,” Jen said quietly, not moving from her spot by the door, “Alice does feel guilty for what she said. Not because of you, of course, but because it hurt Rio.”

Agatha nodded and swallowed, violently aware of the tears that threatened her glassy blue eyes.
“What did she say?”

“She shouted at her. Told her she only wanted you. That’s when she told her you wouldn’t kiss her.”

Agatha pulled her unkissed lower lip between her teeth, gnawing on it as she tried to recall the last time they’d kissed. How long it had lasted, who had initiated, who had pulled away first.

Her heart constricted again because she couldn’t remember.

“I love her. I’m going to make this right.”
