Summary

Five years ago, Rio Vidal was on the verge of something great. Music had always been her side hustle—something she did between biology lectures and late-night study sessions. But then, suddenly, it was everything. One viral song, one breakout moment, and the life she thought she was building turned into something else entirely.

But success came with a cost.

Now, struggling with writer’s block and a career that feels stalled, Rio is forced to do the one thing she swore she’d never do—work with Agatha Harkness again. The woman who was once her best friend, her producer, her everything. The woman she walked away from. The woman who, to this day, looks at her like she’s the worst mistake she ever made.

As late-night studio sessions turn into something heavier, as old wounds reopen and new truths come to light, Rio starts to realize that some things—some people—never really leave you.

The real question is: can they build something new, or were they always destined to fall apart?

Chapter 1: Prologue

The neon signs of New York flashed above her head, though she’d always prefer the colour of Cali’s billboards. A young woman stood in the shadows, shifting on her feet. Rio Vidal had spent years chasing something she couldn’t quite name. Nights on the road blurred together, hotel rooms that never felt like home, stages where the applause faded too quickly. She should have been used to it by now—the quiet, the loneliness that crept in between the noise—but some nights, like this one, it pressed down harder than she could bear.

The studio doors loomed ahead, sleek and impersonal. She wasn’t sure if it was fear or pride that kept her standing on the curb instead of stepping inside. But she had no choice. This was the next step. Even if it led her right back to where she swore she’d never return.

Her grip on the strap of her guitar case tightened as she walked through the doors. The air inside was cooler, sterile in a way that made her skin prickle. The receptionist barely looked up before motioning her toward the elevator.

“Miss Harkness is expecting you. Studio B.”

Studio B. Of course.

Rio swallowed hard, stepping inside the elevator. The ride up was agonizingly slow, every second stretching out, leaving her alone with her thoughts. She was happy for Agatha. Really, she was. This was everything she had ever wanted. She’d worked for it, sacrificed for it. Rio had no right to feel anything but pride.

And yet.

When the doors slid open, she saw her standing there—Agatha Harkness, polished, composed, exactly as she’d imagined her to be. The sharp lines of her suit, the effortless confidence in the way she leaned against the soundboard. She looked like she belonged here. Like she had never once questioned where she was meant to be.

Rio hesitated just a second too long. Agatha’s gaze met hers, and for a moment, the studio felt impossibly small.

Then, with that familiar smirk, Agatha tilted her head.

“Well,” she drawled, voice smooth as ever, “look what the cat dragged in.”

A little.

Only a little.

Did she go?

Did she keep pushing forward — pretending like she wasn’t in pieces?

Let herself rest. Let herself heal. Let herself grieve the girl she used to be — and the one she never got to become.

Took a breath.

And chose.

Dr. Patel – Psych

She wasn’t okay.

But she’d taken the first step.

And maybe—just maybe—that counted for something.

Rooms echoing with absence. Like someone had stripped the place of meaning and left only the outline of where things used to be.

She hadn’t been here long. Two days, maybe three. Time felt slippery now—unreliable. Long enough for her body to stop shaking constantly. Long enough for the adrenaline to wear off and leave something heavier behind.

Grief, probably.

Shame, more likely.

Those two had always liked each other.

She hated that.

She deleted all of them.

Agatha,
