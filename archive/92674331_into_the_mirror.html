Summary

At almost fifty years old, Rio has a life she never could have dreamed of as a child. As the Editor In Chief at Spotlight fashion magazine, she's got the job of her dreams and a life of luxury as a result. At the same time, the public presence side of it has never really been her thing. In fact, most would call Rio a recluse, but that's always been by design.

When she comes home a day early from a business trip, the last thing she expects is to find her partner of thirty years in bed with another woman. While it should destroy her, Rio has never been and will never be the type to let anything sink her.

So what does she do?

She puts on a dress worth more than some people's salaries and plants her ass in some shitty dive bar in the middle of New York's East Village, ready to drink her night away and forget that in the matter of ten minutes, her life was turned upside down irrevocably.

It just becomes a little harder when none other than world renowned rockstar Agatha Harkness decides to take the spot next to her, managing to change both of their lives forever without even realizing it.

Chapter 1: The Stars Don't Mean Anything

In forty-nine years, Rio Vidal had suffered through many days in her life that she had properly titled ‘The Worst Day of Her Life.’ There were funerals, break ups, buy outs where she lost prior jobs, and days where everything just went wrong and nothing went right.

On an unsuspecting Tuesday after returning home from a very quiet, very hush-hush trip to Los Angeles, Rio never could have known it would be the worst day of her life, and rightly so.

Being the Editor In Chief of one of the largest fashion magazines in the world was not for the faint of heart; Spotlight often felt like the world’s best dressed circus most days, but it was Rio’s pride and joy. It was her baby, the one thing she cared most about and no matter the ups and downs that came her way, it was always the safest place for her to hide away.

Despite being someone renowned for her boardroom prowess and her concise editorial hand, that was… pretty much all to know about Rio Vidal, at least in the eyes of the public, anyway.

While she had friends, there weren’t many, but she did have one thing that she kept to herself entirely.

A relationship. A very longstanding relationship with one Val Bruun.

She was a beautiful woman, tall and muscular with soft eyes and what Rio always considered to be a smile that could charm anyone who laid eyes on it.

Meeting Val in college was never supposed to end in a three decades long relationship, but it had, and Rio was better for it.

Until that unsuspecting Tuesday afternoon.

Being with someone for nearly thirty years was never without its struggles, but Rio always thought it was worth it to have someone she depended on when all else felt bleak or isolating, until it wasn’t.

She should have known when she walked in quietly after unlocking the door to their brownstone in Brooklyn and the curtains were pulled. She should have known when the dog was curled up at the foot of the steps asleep, a weird place for her to lay. She should have known when she saw a shirt on the stairs she didn’t recognize. She should have known when she heard a moan that didn’t sound like her girlfriend’s. (Which, yes, almost thirty years later and still hadn’t tied the knot, which was its own thing.)

The moment she stepped into the doorway to see Val’s (perfectly sculpted) bare back as she rode the face of another woman in their fucking bed, she finally knew.

“You fucking bitch!” Rio hissed, the tears welling in her eyes.

It was such a shock, a true jolt to the system that she didn’t know what choice to make in the moment. She could have marched in and beat the shit out of both of them with how angry she was, but it felt pointless. It would likely accomplish nothing other than a raging headline that read: “RECLUSIVE MAGAZINE EDITOR AND MILLIONAIRE ARRESTED FOR DOMESTIC VIOLENCE” and that was something she did not need on her plate after finding her long time partner fucking cheating on her.

“Fuck you. Fuck you, fuck you, fuck you,” Rio repeated, marching into the bedroom to grab her things from the safe in the closet as Val scrambled out of bed, trying to apologize.

There was no apology, not one that could stitch up the sudden gash Rio found in her chest.

“You could have just left!” Rio cried, shoving jewels and cash into her pockets. As she turned to make her exit, she came face to face with the very source of her hurt. “Get out of my way,” she ordered, her tone low and fierce.

“Rio, Rio please…” Val begged, but it didn’t matter. Rio needed out. She needed open air and to not be face to face with someone who was actively in the process of turning her world upside down.

With both hands, Rio shoved her, causing her to hit the center island in the closet, eyes wide and wild.

See, Rio wasn’t typically a violent person. Sure, she boxed to work out and learned Muay Thai because she was bored, but never in their twenty odd years had Rio ever put her hands on someone. A rule had been broken and in the heat of the moment, Rio felt like breaking them all, breaking everything they’d built for most of their lives.

“Don’t fucking call me, don’t text me. By the time I get back, I want you out.”

Her tone was cold, but she flipped her dark hair over her shoulder and marched straight back down the stairs, knelt to give Karma a gentle rub between the ears with tears in her eyes, and grabbed her suitcase by the door before slamming it behind her.

She made it to the car before she really broke down.

After twenty minutes of tears and numbness, she managed to pull it together enough to book herself a room at the NoMad (ironically, while she was very, very mad) and decided on a cab to get there. No matter how stubborn Rio could be, she knew full well she was in no shape to drive.

Checking in, Rio had been of the mind that she could simply throw herself in the bed in her room and spend the rest of the night crying. Somewhere along the elevator ride up, something changed. A thought struck her.

She could lament and cry and wallow in her own misery. Or…

What if she simply didn’t?

What if she put on the red dress in her suitcase and went to some shitty bar in the city where no one would ever suspect her or question her? What if she hammered back bourbon all night and chain smoked a pack of menthols just because she could – and because she quit at Val’s behest twenty years ago?

What then?

It took her ten minutes of pacing around the hotel room to make up her mind.

Red dress it was.

Before changing, she downed two bottles of whiskey from the mini bar and showered, though it didn’t help at all in ridding her of the grimy feeling that she’d been left with after what she walked in on earlier in the afternoon. After drying her hair, she curled it loosely and applied a moderate layer of makeup; foundation, red lipstick to match the dress, blush, and a smoky eye to top it all off. When she was finished, she gave herself a good look in the mirror and while the areas beneath her eyes were still a bit puffy, she looked damn good.

She pulled up her phone and did a bit of research, finding her destination in East Village.

With another cab ride, Rio climbed out in her red heels and stood tall on the sidewalk, lifting her head to see the title of the bar scrawled above the door.

The Scratcher

Seemed fitting; after all, getting dressed up on the evening of the worst day of her life, she had several itches that needed scratching, even if she couldn’t begin to name them all.

For just a moment, she had her doubts. She should have climbed back into the cab, gone to the hotel, and spent her first night truly alone in years. She should have licked her wounds, mourned her losses, and dealt with her feelings properly. With two shots in her system and a magnetic force tugging her toward the bar, going back was out of the question.

She was no weak willed little bitch.

She was single! She was free! She was welcome to do whatever the fuck she wanted! If that meant going to a tiny shithole dive bar dressed like a million dollars (in a dress that cost almost thirty grand and heels to match), then that was what she was going to do. Maybe it wasn’t the right way to grieve, but who was there to tell her what to do?

Shit, she thought as she glanced to her left down the sidewalk, littered with the evening crowd as they came and went from after work or dinner dates, almost like she would see the face of the person who had just crossed her mind.

Her PR manager, Jennifer Kale.

She probably should have texted her, told her not to freak out if there were whispers of her in the city, but that was an entire conversation she was absolutely not ready to have.

Jen would live. After all, the last ten years she’d been working for Rio, Rio had made her job rather easy. That was simple enough to do when the flashy life of lavishness wasn’t exactly Rio’s cup of tea. Events, galas, appearances were all expected of her and she fulfilled such obligations, but an evening out or an unscheduled public appearance at some swanky club or fancy diner were never really her style.

It was why Jen would flip her shit if she found out, but wasn’t that what Rio paid her for? Maybe she’d actually have to do something finally.

With pursed lips, Rio gave the bar front one more look over before finally deciding to head in. The second her toe crossed the threshold, something she already knew became very apparent.

She was entirely out of place and very, very over dressed.

Nonetheless, she lifted her chin and marched right over to the bar, placing herself with finesse onto a stool as an unruly haired young man – presumably the bartender – made his way toward her. The eyeliner he was wearing was a bit much, but it matched his overall vibe, aside from the bright smile plastered to his face.

“What can I get ya?” he asked in a bright tone, far too bright for Rio’s liking.

“Are you even old enough to work here?” she questioned, laying her satin red clutch on the bar.

The young man chuckled and shrugged. “You may never know. But I can make a mean drink all the same.”

Rio offered him a skeptical smile, a brow raising before she posed her question. “Can you make a decent Negroni?”

“Sbagliato?” the man fired back with an even wider grin.

“You know your shit,” she replied, an impressed look on her face. “But yes, Sbagliato, please. Though I wouldn’t have pegged this place for keeping a bottle of Prosecco around.”

The man shrugged and set off to work, chuckling to himself. “You’ll find we like being full of surprises here,” he called from the ice bin. He doubled back and prepared the drink before her, leaving her rather amazed at his precision and skill.

“Thank you,” Rio offered as he slid the finished drink across the bar.

“Starting a tab?” he asked, watching her pull out her card.

She considered for a moment, her eyes studying him for answers he sure as fuck wouldn’t have. She knew the best answer was a simple ‘no,’ but if she was going to commit to the bit, what could it hurt?

“Yeah, I think so.”

“You got it.” He offered her a friendly wink and turned to his computer to do whatever it was he needed to do, leaving Rio alone with her drink and alone with her thoughts. Both of those were probably not the safest places to be.

Over that drink and one and a half more, Rio silently lamented over the state of her life. She was confused, hurt, sad, angry, frustrated and downright distraught all at the same time. She didn’t understand, nor did she think she ever would, but at least the alcohol tasted nice.

That was a thought.

At least on the worst day of her life, she could have a decent drink.

With half glazed eyes and a heavy heart, Rio stared at the television hanging from the wall for several long minutes. When a flash of movement in the mirror beneath it drew her attention, she started to look but jumped when the lightest touch grazed her shoulder.

“Is this seat taken?”

It was almost like a purr, soft but curious.

Rio’s head turned to find the source of such an obnoxious question, but what she found looking back at her wasn’t obnoxious at all. It was sharp but inquisitive blue eyes, a charming smile and the littlest head tilt, all amplifying the question.

The woman beside her, clad in a deep burgundy suit gestured toward the seat as if to ask again.

Facing a slew of “should haves” again, Rio’s buzzed brain made the decision before rationale could seep in.

“No,” she informed, flashing a drunken smile at the beautiful woman. “Not at all.”

With a grin all her own, the woman took the seat.

Maybe the worst day of Rio’s life wouldn’t end with the worst night of her life, too.

There was only one way to find out.

“Which is very unlike you,” she pointed out, causing Rio to shrink in place. Yeah, a lot of what happened in the last 48 hours was quite unlike her, but it was her reality and it was one of her own making.

“I haven’t been feeling very me in a while,” she admitted, and that part was a little more truth than lie. She had felt stuck for some time, but she used work as an escape and an excuse to get away from the feeling. It was only going to work for so long.

“Well, next time you have a mid-life crisis, please reach out to someone who gives an insurmountable shit about you past our working relationship. I can’t promise I’ll always give the best advice, but I can always recommend a closed door wedding ceremony not in the French courts. How does that even work, anyway?”

“Passports are wonderful things to keep on your phone, I guess,” Rio surmised, truthfully having no actual clue how they wound up eligible for a marriage certificate in France of all places. She didn’t remember and she had a sneaking suspicion that if she asked Agatha, she wouldn’t remember either.

“You piss me off,” Jen stated defiantly. A moment later, Rio could practically hear her fold. “Are you happy?”

That was a very subjective question, and an incredibly complicated one. Rio didn’t know how to answer at first, but it came to her a moment later when she thought about Agatha sitting out in the courtyard talking to her own manager.

“For now, I am. And I think that’s what’s most important, don’t you?”

Jen was quiet for a long moment, possibly contemplating between being snarky or recognizing that if Rio had kept something from her, it was probably for a good reason.

“I guess so. But… Please for the love of God, keep me in the loop while you enjoy your honeymoon. I need to know if there are any photos I should be buying up to cover your ass. God, I was hoping this would never be part of my job.”

“Surprise?” Rio tried to joke, her voice squeaking slightly.

“Still pissed. You owe me big time.”

“You’re right. So remember this moment when you get an extra ten grand for your Christmas bonus,” Rio informed her, probably making the whole ordeal a little more worth the headache.

“Make it fifteen and I’ll stop harassing you about it,” Jen countered with a laugh. “I’m kidding. I’m going to harass you about this for as long as you employ me or as long as your marriage lasts. Whichever comes first.”

“Hey, wow, rude,” Rio pouted. “You’re going to love her, I promise.”

Yeah, right. Realistically speaking, Jen was going to hold a conversation with Agatha for all of two minutes and make the executive decision that she hated her. Rio could see it coming from a mile out. For her, it made things that much more humorous.

“Yeah, I better for all the strife you two are causing me,” Jen griped in return.

“Go to sleep, Jennifer.”

A sigh and then a groan. “I’m taking a fucking Ambien.”

Rio laughed and shook her head, a smile on her face. “Sleep tight princess,” she teased. “Bye, Jen.”

“Bye, Rio.”

The line went dead and for just a moment, Rio felt the faintest ripple of relief. Jen seemed to accept the lie she was being spoon-fed and for the time being, it was Rio’s saving grace. They were going to lie their way through it, and depending on how things with Agatha were going, they very well could get away with it.

Sure, they were going to need to make living arrangements and decisions of that nature before they went back, but for the time being, Rio was going to do what Jen suggested: she was going to enjoy her damn honeymoon, real or not.

 

Chapter 9: The Payoff Sells Itself

Ten minutes after hanging up the phone with Jen and wishing she’d sleep for three days straight, there was a soft tap on the door to their room. No voice, no call, just a tap but Rio already knew who it was.

“You can come in, my wife,” Rio called in jest from her place on the bed, still sitting with her legs crossed and the tv off.

The lock to the door clicked and it swung open, a very relaxed Agatha walking through with a smile on her face.

“Soooo…” she started, climbing onto the bed next to Rio. “How did that go?” She crossed her legs too, but only after she turned so they could face each other a little better.

“She’s pissed?” Rio posed, shrugging her shoulders. “But I also think she bought it.” She, of course, was not going to recant how rude Jen had been about Agatha as a person, but it was still on her mind.

“Alice definitely bought it, so we can take that off our list of things to sweat about.” It was a relief to know that at least one person in the world wasn’t having a complete meltdown about their little stunt. It would have been fair of her, just as Jen’s reaction was, but it was nice that Agatha seemed nonplussed about it. She was right: one less thing to worry over.

“I mean… I know Jen has a right to be upset,” Rio stated, her head dropping as she picked at the duvet beneath her. “But I think she’s more worried than anything. She’s known me for a long time and this… Well, safe to say this isn’t my typical outing.”

“Nor your typical partner,” Agatha suggested with a small smile.

“Definitely that,” Rio agreed. She lifted her head and found herself looking into curious blue eyes, ones that had a question in them.

“Are you okay?” She asked it so softly, but it was clear she wanted to know the real answer.

Rio wasn’t even sure if she knew the real answer. Yes, she was physically fine but… When she looked at their current situation, she mostly felt sad. She was sad that someone she thought she would love forever cheated on her. She was sad she drank so much and got whisked away on a whim. She was sad that she chose to marry a stranger while actively staving off such a commitment to someone else. Granted, that feeling proved worthy and she saved herself a messy divorce in the process, but who was to say when she and Agatha finally decided to call it quits, it wouldn’t be messy, too?

She didn’t know the woman sitting next to her and yet, she felt safe. She felt like Agatha wasn’t going to judge her for the terrible choices she’d made (especially given Agatha herself made the same ones) or the life she’d been living. Agatha didn’t seem to want to judge her at all. In fact, all she seemed to want to do was make sure that Rio was alright.

“I just… Know what kind of storm I’ll be diving into when we go back… And it makes me never want to leave,” Rio answered honestly, never once breaking eye contact. “And I just wonder if we’re making the right decision to lie. This… It really gets messy when we go home.”

Agatha smiled at her, soft and kind. “But… If we play our cards right, it’s also a break for both of us. And I meant what I said about you coming to live with me. Billy was right at the bar the other night, I remember that much. My house is big, bigger than it needs to be, and you can have the whole north wing to yourself. Bedroom, bathroom, den. We’d just have to share a kitchen.”

It was sweet, really, that Agatha was offering to share her home despite not knowing the most basic things about Rio. It showed that she was as culpable for their situation as Rio was and she was offering a way out.

“So we just… live together… and any time we go out, we put on a show?” she questioned, raising a brow.

“I mean… You don’t have to go out with me, considering your… reclusive habits. But… If you wanted to, you’d have to do so knowing that the public now expects something of us.”

She had a point. If Rio was going to open up her image and let the world see more of her, there were demands she needed to meet in that regard. It was a lot to consider; just because her world had been upended by Val’s betrayal, it didn’t mean that she needed to change who she was completely. Maybe they could do a night out here and there, but over all, Rio still liked her privacy.

That was sort of blown out of the water, but she knew she would recover.

“And you’re just… okay with all of this?” Rio asked her, still fidgeting with the cover of the duvet beneath her.

“I look at it this way,” Agatha began, reaching out for her hand. She took hold of it and gave it a light squeeze, looking Rio in the eye again. “We’re adults and we made some… extreme decisions. But… We can still have fun. We can look at it like an experiment, like a trial run that could create a new friendship.”

Friendship? That was what she was going for?

Rio’s head dropped forward but her eyes stayed locked on Agatha, peering at her from beneath her brow. “That’s what you want? Friendship?” she asked, lifting her head again to look at her a bit more seriously.

“I mean… no,” Agatha answered honestly. “But you’re freshly out of a relationship that has taken up almost your whole life, right? A) I’m not trying to be a rebound to all of that,” she teased. “And b) maybe you just need the space to be alone while not being alone. If you ever need me, I’d be on the other side of the house.”

When she pitched it like that, it actually sounded… kind of nice. At the same time, there she sat, a stranger still, offering to be a support system because she likely realized Rio didn’t have anyone else. Jen was there, sure, and Rio would consider her a friend, but it was also a friendship borne from a working relationship. And more to the point, Jen didn’t really get it anyway. If Rio told her the truth, the real, honest truth, it was likely to only to get worse.

“You’ve really thought a lot about this, haven’t you?” Rio questioned, playing lightly with Agatha’s fingers where their hands rested on the bed in the space between them.

“It’s all I can think about,” she admitted with a shrug. “I’m part of the reason you’re here and in this mess, so I just… want to help, I guess. And if it means pulling a long con to shove wool over the public’s eyes, I say why not?”

She was so cavalier, so brazen as she explained it, but it didn’t put Rio off. She was right; it could be a fun little trial, mostly to see what they might build between them. It was also nice of Agatha to point out that while Rio needed the time and the space to lick her wounds, she didn’t have to go it completely alone.

“You’re really sure you’re okay with it all?” Rio asked her softly, spinning one of her rings around her pointer finger.

“I am. And if I think you’re a terrible house guest and we can’t get along, I’ll just charge you rent.”

A soft laugh left Rio and she shook her head. “I promise, quiet as a mouse and I clean up after myself.”

“Sounds like we’ve got ourselves a deal then, doesn’t it?”

Rio shifted her hand in Agatha’s, pressing their palms together as she gave a teasing shake of their hands.

“Sounds like we’ve got a deal.”

The smile on Agatha’s face as they shook hands did something to Rio’s stomach, causing it to flutter the most minute amount. She almost missed it, but it turned her hand suddenly clammy and she pulled it away, glancing around the room as if Agatha would not notice.

“So… What do you say we get in a nap and then go explore? You promised to be my tour guide, after all,” Agatha prodded, lightly digging a knuckle in her side, causing her to shy away with a soft giggle.

For being strangers, Agatha sure was a flirt.

“I think that sounds nice. My head still kind of hurts,” Rio admitted, raising her arms over her head to stretch.

“Yeah, that eighteen hour bender made for a nasty hangover. But I will say, I’m proud that neither of us threw up this morning.” Agatha laughed at her own observation, her amusement pulling Rio in as well, their laughter sweet in the noontime air.

“I am too.” Rio glanced at her pillow and the back at Agatha, a tired smile curling her lips. “Let’s nap.”

“Yes, let’s.”

The pair of them laid down, rolling to their sides opposite one another to face their respective sides of the room. It was weird knowing they were out in public mere hours before, kissing and holding hands and yet as they laid there, it felt like there was an ocean between them. Rio might not have known Agatha, but it didn’t stop her from feeling magnetized, pulled in by Agatha’s charm and good looks.

After all, who wouldn’t want to be seen on her arm? She was beautiful in every way Rio could articulate, from her nose to that dashing smile she often wore, especially when she was being positively rotten. Maybe it had only been a couple of days, but whatever Agatha was selling, Rio was buying it right up without any hesitation.

Okay, maybe some hesitation, or they would have been tangled in one another in the middle of the bed instead of their own islands on opposite sides of the sheets.

The silence was deafening as Rio laid on her side for ten minutes, then twenty. She could faintly hear the bustle of the city outside, but over all, there was just nothing aside from the sound of Agatha’s breathing behind her, steady and soft. Another five minutes passed and Rio simply could not take it anymore.

“Agatha?” she whispered.

For a second, there was nothing, and then she felt the bed shift behind her. Agatha rolled over and placed a hand on Rio’s side, so casual and yet, it caused Rio’s heart to leap right into her throat.

“Yeah?” she asked, her voice a little sleepy.

Rio was quiet for a long moment before she shifted, placing her hand on Agatha’s to pull it over her middle. “Can we nap like this?” she asked softly.

Maybe Agatha realized what she was really saying: Please don’t let me fall asleep as an island.

Almost immediately, Agatha settled in behind her and tightened her grip, pulling Rio a little closer. It was exactly what she’d been hoping for, what she knew she needed but couldn’t outright demand. The truth was, Agatha owed her nothing and yet, she was giving her everything. She didn’t understand why, but in the moment, she was thankful.

It was an anchor, a way to hold her down and keep her grounded in a moment that could have hurt beyond comprehension – falling asleep while fully understanding what was happening to her – and she knew she could only ask for one more thing.

“Don’t let go,” Rio whispered, her arm laying atop Agatha’s so that she could slide her fingers into the spaces between the other woman’s, squeezing her hand gently.

“I’m not going anywhere,” Agatha told her quietly.

It could have been said that nothing made sense as Rio closed her eyes and settled into the feeling of being held by Agatha from behind, but somehow as she drifted off to sleep, she could not remember feeling more at peace.

 

Chapter 10: If You Come With Me, We Won't Leave

Waking up some time later, Rio felt like she’d slept so hard she time traveled. Upon rousing, she found herself confused by her surroundings and rolled over to the feeling of an empty bed. As she blinked at the stark white ceiling, lined with decorative moulding, everything started to come back.

She was in Paris with famous rockstar Agatha Harkness. They’d been drunk. They’d been stupid. They’d gotten fucking married. Shit. That was right, even if it seemed so wrong.

As she tuned into her surroundings, she heard the sound of running water in the room adjacent to the one she was in and the faintest sound of humming. It waxed and waned, growing louder one moment before fading the next. Just as it cut off completely, so too did the sound of the water as Agatha undoubtedly cut the shower.

Rio thought about calling out to her, but it felt foolish. Clearly she hadn’t left and if anything, she was probably getting ready to follow through on Rio’s promise to show her around.

She felt around for her phone and checked the time, realizing she’d slept the better part of the afternoon. It wasn’t intentional, but as she blinked herself more into consciousness, she realized it had helped ease away her headache. Finally feeling like she was actually waking up, she pushed herself into an upright position and stretched through a long yawn. Just as she pulled her arm down, Agatha came around the corner.

What a vision she was.

Her hair was wrapped in a towel that wound around her head, but she was dressed in a bright white blouse with gold buttons, tucked into a pair of dark navy slacks with an accentuating black and gold belt.

Okay, maybe the fashion comment from earlier was totally unnecessary.

Still, Rio couldn’t pass up the opportunity to poke at her a bit. “You get dressed before you dry your hair? You psychopath,” she teased, a bright grin covering her face.

Agatha stopped, a playful scowl crossing her features. “I’ll have you know I thought about coming out in a towel, but aside from the ‘waking up naked in a bathtub together’ thing, I don’t think we’re there… yet.” She tossed a wink in Rio’s direction and made her way over to her little pile of bags, pulling a tiny box from a smaller one.

“What’s that?” Rio asked as she leaned back on the heels of her hands, still not entirely ready to get out of bed.

“Peony and blush suede,” Agatha answered with a grin, taking the tiny bottle out to spritz some of it across her chest.

“Is that what you smelled like last – the other night?”

“It’s what I smell like all the time,” Agatha informed as she set the bottle down and bent over to finish towel drying her hair.

At that moment, Rio realized something special.

She had just learned one of Agatha’s favorite things. It was casual, just happening to come up in conversation, and it gave her a little more to work with. It also gave her an idea.

“Hey,” she voiced as Agatha snapped upright again, her head cocking to the side to meet Rio’s curious gaze, blue eyes curious all their own. “Wanna go to dinner?”

Agatha eyed her suspiciously, sensing she was up to something. She tipped her head slightly, silently urging Rio to continue.

“I just mean… Why don’t we treat it kind of like a date?”

Agatha’s face puckered but she shuffled over and sat on bed anyway, a curious gleam in her eyes. “Okay, and what exactly does that mean?” She twisted the towel in her hand, perhaps a nervous tick, but she never took her eyes off Rio.

“It means… When we go back, people are going to ask us questions. And we should probably be prepared so we can answer them correctly when they do.” As she explained, the look on Agatha’s face began to shift, a sly smile tugging on her lips.

“So you want to date like normal people do so we can get to know each other,” she deduced, smirking. “That’s actually so brilliantly backwards. I fucking love it.”

“Right, but not actually dating,” Rio clarified based on their earlier conversation. “Just… the concept while already being visibly married. If that makes any sense at all.”

Agatha laughed, shaking her head as she got to her feet again. “It doesn’t but what about this does?” She strode off toward the bathroom again, returning without the towel, but still wearing a grin. “Alright then, let’s go on a date.”

It was truly strange to hear a stranger state as much, but given their circumstances, it seemed as though it was the only feasible way forward. If they really were going to try to sell their fake relationship, they needed to put in the work to do so.

After a quick shower and the longer process of getting ready in the bathroom while Agatha finished in the main room, Rio stepped out in her own blouse and deep olive slacks with her own gold belt. In a way, they matched while still looking strikingly different. The moment she caught Agatha’s eye, her head whipped up and a dashing grin covered her face.

“Well, look at you,” she beamed from her seat at the desk in their room. “You can pull that off just as well as that red dress.”

Despite what Agatha said about not wanting to be a rebound, she sure was one hell of a flirt. Rio knew it was a complicated situation and while the other woman was beautiful – and had one hell of a body – Rio thought it to be uncouth to jump straight into a relationship while digging herself out of another. It didn’t seem to stop the way her stomach jumped slightly as Agatha got to her feet and looked her over from head to toe.

“You know,” she mused, her eyes landing in Rio’s. “Of all the people that could have shown up in that bar, I’m glad it was you looking as good as you do. Anyone less good looking and Alice definitely would not have bought the lie.”

“Thanks, I think?” Rio laughed, stepping a little closer to where Agatha stood, though she left a considerable amount of space between them as to not cross any lines. “You know, you really don’t have to be such a flirt behind closed doors,” she informed, tilting her head forward seriously.

“I can’t help it,” Agatha shrugged. Her look of feigned nonchalance quickly melted away, a grin forming on her face. “You’re hot. And now, we get to have fun while getting to know each other. If we’re going to be spending time together, we should at least have a good time with it.”

She seemed so airy and carefree about it in a way Rio didn’t know how to be; this was all fun and games, sure, but Rio was the one dealing with the heartbreak and betrayal. Agatha was a nice distraction, sure, but what about when they went home? What about when the real world came crashing down on them? Would Agatha still want to flirt and banter when the high wore off and all Rio could do was cry? They were questions she did not have answers for and to get them, it seemed as though the only way out was through.

For a moment, Rio studied her, stunned when she took a step closer and placed her hands on Rio’s sides, holding her still and stopping her from shifting her weight from one side to the other.

“This is a lot, I get it. Which is part of the reason I’m flirting. I’m trying to put you at ease because we can’t be out in public with you having a complete breakdown,” she teased, looking Rio straight in the eyes. “You’re only good for my image if you don’t look like a crazy person.”

Rio huffed a soft laugh and rolled her eyes, her hands coming to land on both sides of Agatha’s chest, her thumbs running over the ripples of her blouse. “And that’s why you’re doing this with me? Because of your image?” she asked dubiously, clearly not buying it.

“Yes. And because I’ve been bored lately. It’s been a lot of the same over and over again. Write, record, tour, repeat. It’s just a cycle, but you…” she offered, that brilliant grin returning to her face. She almost looked wild, her eyes alight, but she was simply excited. “You’re a fun new mystery. So let me unravel you and figure you out.”

Standing so close, touching each other the way they were, Rio almost felt like she’d known Agatha far longer than just a couple of days. It was almost terrifying how easy it was with her, to be close, to look into her eyes, to have that conversation. Rio felt like she was living in a distant reality where nothing was as she knew it, but somehow having Agatha there grounded her, rooted her in place, and made her feel as though her life was still liveable.

Maybe she was hurt, and maybe that hurt would never go away, but Agatha was proving she could be an incredible distraction.

“Let’s go to dinner, then.”

The expectation upon leaving the hotel, much like earlier in the day, was that there were going to be eyes on them. It wasn’t prominent or overt, but as they walked hand and hand down the street to a nearby bistro – in far more sensible shoes than the day before – Rio caught a couple of people with their phones up, or pointing.

It felt strange; it was the type of attention she spent decades hiding from, telling herself her privacy was paramount. At the same time, she didn’t completely hate it. In reality, it wasn’t her they were more excited about anyway; Agatha was the true marvel, the one people were drawn to.

For good reason.

Rio was just as guilty of feeding into that attraction, even if it was for different reasons. Hers were much more complex than just idolizing someone for their ability. No, for Rio, it was the way Agatha smiled at her at the bar, the way she was a flirt while listening at the same time. It was clear she was a woman who had lived many lives, but Rio was happy to be living that one with her, no matter how new it was.

They made it to the bistro and chose to sit on the patio with Agatha noting that it was too nice of a night not to. At the same time, Rio knew she did it on purpose even without her saying as much. People would look, would gawk, and they would be international news again by the time morning came in New York City.

Rio should have hated it, but as they sat down and the sommelier arrived with a bottle of wine, she could do little more than gaze out at the street with a smile on her face.

“You do this often?” Agatha asked, picking up her wine glass to swirl it around, the liquid sloshing in a circular pattern.

“Not as often as I’d like,” Rio answered honestly, picking up her own glass to do the same. “I do like experiencing the local culture, but a lot of the time I’m here, I’m working. So… It’s nice to just sightsee.”

“And here I thought you were going to make a whole itinerary for the rest of our trip,” Agatha teased, holding the glass up to truly smell the different aromas emanating from the wine. She took a sip, held it in her mouth for a moment and then swallowed, contrary to wine tasting etiquette.

“Look at you,” Rio indicated with a tip of her chin. “You’ve got some culture about you.”

Agatha rolled her eyes and took a longer sip of the wine, shifting in her seat to get more comfortable as she crossed her legs at the knee and leaned back, her arm along the backing of the chair. “I’ll have you know you don’t get this far in life without learning a few things.”

God, Rio thought, she looked so fucking good.

“You make it sound like you’re more cultured than me,” Rio teased, leaning back as well although her eyes never left Agatha.

“Yeah, well, I think the only thing you’ve been married to in your life aside from me is your job,” Agatha punched back, a small smirk on her lips. She wasn’t trying to press buttons, necessarily, but rather she was pointing out the obvious.

“You know what, fine. I’ll give you that,” Rio offered in return, holding her glass up in surrender before taking a long sip.

“So…” Agatha glanced around, gauging their surroundings. Her focus shifted back to Rio, her eyes sparkling. “Is this the part where I get to ask you about the fun stuff?”

“Fun stuff?” Rio repeated, a curious brow raising in question.

“Yeah, you know. Childhood traumas, adulthood traumas, favorite color, favorite flower. That whole thing,” she explained, waving her free hand over the table to indicate any and all of the space between them.

Rio laughed, shaking her head. “So this is the part where the date begins?”

Agatha nodded, still proud and still smiling.

“I guess it is.”
