Summary

At almost fifty years old, Rio has a life she never could have dreamed of as a child. As the Editor In Chief at Spotlight fashion magazine, she's got the job of her dreams and a life of luxury as a result. At the same time, the public presence side of it has never really been her thing. In fact, most would call Rio a recluse, but that's always been by design.

When she comes home a day early from a business trip, the last thing she expects is to find her partner of thirty years in bed with another woman. While it should destroy her, Rio has never been and will never be the type to let anything sink her.

So what does she do?

She puts on a dress worth more than some people's salaries and plants her ass in some shitty dive bar in the middle of New York's East Village, ready to drink her night away and forget that in the matter of ten minutes, her life was turned upside down irrevocably.

It just becomes a little harder when none other than world renowned rockstar Agatha Harkness decides to take the spot next to her, managing to change both of their lives forever without even realizing it.

Chapter 1: The Stars Don't Mean Anything

In forty-nine years, Rio Vidal had suffered through many days in her life that she had properly titled ‘The Worst Day of Her Life.’ There were funerals, break ups, buy outs where she lost prior jobs, and days where everything just went wrong and nothing went right.

On an unsuspecting Tuesday after returning home from a very quiet, very hush-hush trip to Los Angeles, Rio never could have known it would be the worst day of her life, and rightly so.

Being the Editor In Chief of one of the largest fashion magazines in the world was not for the faint of heart; Spotlight often felt like the world’s best dressed circus most days, but it was Rio’s pride and joy. It was her baby, the one thing she cared most about and no matter the ups and downs that came her way, it was always the safest place for her to hide away.

Despite being someone renowned for her boardroom prowess and her concise editorial hand, that was… pretty much all to know about Rio Vidal, at least in the eyes of the public, anyway.

While she had friends, there weren’t many, but she did have one thing that she kept to herself entirely.

A relationship. A very longstanding relationship with one Val Bruun.

She was a beautiful woman, tall and muscular with soft eyes and what Rio always considered to be a smile that could charm anyone who laid eyes on it.

Meeting Val in college was never supposed to end in a three decades long relationship, but it had, and Rio was better for it.

Until that unsuspecting Tuesday afternoon.

Being with someone for nearly thirty years was never without its struggles, but Rio always thought it was worth it to have someone she depended on when all else felt bleak or isolating, until it wasn’t.

She should have known when she walked in quietly after unlocking the door to their brownstone in Brooklyn and the curtains were pulled. She should have known when the dog was curled up at the foot of the steps asleep, a weird place for her to lay. She should have known when she saw a shirt on the stairs she didn’t recognize. She should have known when she heard a moan that didn’t sound like her girlfriend’s. (Which, yes, almost thirty years later and still hadn’t tied the knot, which was its own thing.)

The moment she stepped into the doorway to see Val’s (perfectly sculpted) bare back as she rode the face of another woman in their fucking bed, she finally knew.

“You fucking bitch!” Rio hissed, the tears welling in her eyes.

It was such a shock, a true jolt to the system that she didn’t know what choice to make in the moment. She could have marched in and beat the shit out of both of them with how angry she was, but it felt pointless. It would likely accomplish nothing other than a raging headline that read: “RECLUSIVE MAGAZINE EDITOR AND MILLIONAIRE ARRESTED FOR DOMESTIC VIOLENCE” and that was something she did not need on her plate after finding her long time partner fucking cheating on her.

“Fuck you. Fuck you, fuck you, fuck you,” Rio repeated, marching into the bedroom to grab her things from the safe in the closet as Val scrambled out of bed, trying to apologize.

There was no apology, not one that could stitch up the sudden gash Rio found in her chest.

“You could have just left!” Rio cried, shoving jewels and cash into her pockets. As she turned to make her exit, she came face to face with the very source of her hurt. “Get out of my way,” she ordered, her tone low and fierce.

“Rio, Rio please…” Val begged, but it didn’t matter. Rio needed out. She needed open air and to not be face to face with someone who was actively in the process of turning her world upside down.

With both hands, Rio shoved her, causing her to hit the center island in the closet, eyes wide and wild.

See, Rio wasn’t typically a violent person. Sure, she boxed to work out and learned Muay Thai because she was bored, but never in their twenty odd years had Rio ever put her hands on someone. A rule had been broken and in the heat of the moment, Rio felt like breaking them all, breaking everything they’d built for most of their lives.

“Don’t fucking call me, don’t text me. By the time I get back, I want you out.”

Her tone was cold, but she flipped her dark hair over her shoulder and marched straight back down the stairs, knelt to give Karma a gentle rub between the ears with tears in her eyes, and grabbed her suitcase by the door before slamming it behind her.

She made it to the car before she really broke down.

After twenty minutes of tears and numbness, she managed to pull it together enough to book herself a room at the NoMad (ironically, while she was very, very mad) and decided on a cab to get there. No matter how stubborn Rio could be, she knew full well she was in no shape to drive.

Checking in, Rio had been of the mind that she could simply throw herself in the bed in her room and spend the rest of the night crying. Somewhere along the elevator ride up, something changed. A thought struck her.

She could lament and cry and wallow in her own misery. Or…

What if she simply didn’t?

What if she put on the red dress in her suitcase and went to some shitty bar in the city where no one would ever suspect her or question her? What if she hammered back bourbon all night and chain smoked a pack of menthols just because she could – and because she quit at Val’s behest twenty years ago?

What then?

It took her ten minutes of pacing around the hotel room to make up her mind.

Red dress it was.

Before changing, she downed two bottles of whiskey from the mini bar and showered, though it didn’t help at all in ridding her of the grimy feeling that she’d been left with after what she walked in on earlier in the afternoon. After drying her hair, she curled it loosely and applied a moderate layer of makeup; foundation, red lipstick to match the dress, blush, and a smoky eye to top it all off. When she was finished, she gave herself a good look in the mirror and while the areas beneath her eyes were still a bit puffy, she looked damn good.

She pulled up her phone and did a bit of research, finding her destination in East Village.

With another cab ride, Rio climbed out in her red heels and stood tall on the sidewalk, lifting her head to see the title of the bar scrawled above the door.

The Scratcher

Seemed fitting; after all, getting dressed up on the evening of the worst day of her life, she had several itches that needed scratching, even if she couldn’t begin to name them all.

For just a moment, she had her doubts. She should have climbed back into the cab, gone to the hotel, and spent her first night truly alone in years. She should have licked her wounds, mourned her losses, and dealt with her feelings properly. With two shots in her system and a magnetic force tugging her toward the bar, going back was out of the question.

She was no weak willed little bitch.

She was single! She was free! She was welcome to do whatever the fuck she wanted! If that meant going to a tiny shithole dive bar dressed like a million dollars (in a dress that cost almost thirty grand and heels to match), then that was what she was going to do. Maybe it wasn’t the right way to grieve, but who was there to tell her what to do?

Shit, she thought as she glanced to her left down the sidewalk, littered with the evening crowd as they came and went from after work or dinner dates, almost like she would see the face of the person who had just crossed her mind.

Her PR manager, Jennifer Kale.

She probably should have texted her, told her not to freak out if there were whispers of her in the city, but that was an entire conversation she was absolutely not ready to have.

Jen would live. After all, the last ten years she’d been working for Rio, Rio had made her job rather easy. That was simple enough to do when the flashy life of lavishness wasn’t exactly Rio’s cup of tea. Events, galas, appearances were all expected of her and she fulfilled such obligations, but an evening out or an unscheduled public appearance at some swanky club or fancy diner were never really her style.

It was why Jen would flip her shit if she found out, but wasn’t that what Rio paid her for? Maybe she’d actually have to do something finally.

With pursed lips, Rio gave the bar front one more look over before finally deciding to head in. The second her toe crossed the threshold, something she already knew became very apparent.

She was entirely out of place and very, very over dressed.

Nonetheless, she lifted her chin and marched right over to the bar, placing herself with finesse onto a stool as an unruly haired young man – presumably the bartender – made his way toward her. The eyeliner he was wearing was a bit much, but it matched his overall vibe, aside from the bright smile plastered to his face.

“What can I get ya?” he asked in a bright tone, far too bright for Rio’s liking.

“Are you even old enough to work here?” she questioned, laying her satin red clutch on the bar.

The young man chuckled and shrugged. “You may never know. But I can make a mean drink all the same.”

Rio offered him a skeptical smile, a brow raising before she posed her question. “Can you make a decent Negroni?”

“Sbagliato?” the man fired back with an even wider grin.

“You know your shit,” she replied, an impressed look on her face. “But yes, Sbagliato, please. Though I wouldn’t have pegged this place for keeping a bottle of Prosecco around.”

The man shrugged and set off to work, chuckling to himself. “You’ll find we like being full of surprises here,” he called from the ice bin. He doubled back and prepared the drink before her, leaving her rather amazed at his precision and skill.

“Thank you,” Rio offered as he slid the finished drink across the bar.

“Starting a tab?” he asked, watching her pull out her card.

She considered for a moment, her eyes studying him for answers he sure as fuck wouldn’t have. She knew the best answer was a simple ‘no,’ but if she was going to commit to the bit, what could it hurt?

“Yeah, I think so.”

“You got it.” He offered her a friendly wink and turned to his computer to do whatever it was he needed to do, leaving Rio alone with her drink and alone with her thoughts. Both of those were probably not the safest places to be.

Over that drink and one and a half more, Rio silently lamented over the state of her life. She was confused, hurt, sad, angry, frustrated and downright distraught all at the same time. She didn’t understand, nor did she think she ever would, but at least the alcohol tasted nice.

That was a thought.

At least on the worst day of her life, she could have a decent drink.

With half glazed eyes and a heavy heart, Rio stared at the television hanging from the wall for several long minutes. When a flash of movement in the mirror beneath it drew her attention, she started to look but jumped when the lightest touch grazed her shoulder.

“Is this seat taken?”

It was almost like a purr, soft but curious.

Rio’s head turned to find the source of such an obnoxious question, but what she found looking back at her wasn’t obnoxious at all. It was sharp but inquisitive blue eyes, a charming smile and the littlest head tilt, all amplifying the question.

The woman beside her, clad in a deep burgundy suit gestured toward the seat as if to ask again.

Facing a slew of “should haves” again, Rio’s buzzed brain made the decision before rationale could seep in.

“No,” she informed, flashing a drunken smile at the beautiful woman. “Not at all.”

With a grin all her own, the woman took the seat.

Maybe the worst day of Rio’s life wouldn’t end with the worst night of her life, too.

There was only one way to find out.

Chapter 9: The Payoff Sells Itself

She felt like Agatha wasn’t going to judge her for the terrible choices she’d made (especially given Agatha herself made the same ones) or the life she’d been living. Friendship? That was what she was going for?

She was so cavalier, so brazen as she explained it, but it didn’t put Rio off. She was right; it could be a fun little trial, mostly to see what they might build between them. “I am. And if I think you’re a terrible house guest and we can’t get along, I’ll just charge you rent.”

Rio shifted her hand in Agatha’s, pressing their palms together as she gave a teasing shake of their hands.

“Sounds like we’ve got a deal.”

The smile on Agatha’s face as they shook hands did something to Rio’s stomach, causing it to flutter the most minute amount. She almost missed it, but it turned her hand suddenly clammy and she pulled it away, glancing around the room as if Agatha would not notice.

“So… What do you say we get in a nap and then go explore? You promised to be my tour guide, after all,” Agatha prodded, lightly digging a knuckle in her side, causing her to shy away with a soft giggle.

For being strangers, Agatha sure was a flirt.

“Yeah, that eighteen hour bender made for a nasty hangover. But I will say, I’m proud that neither of us threw up this morning.” Agatha laughed at her own observation, her amusement pulling Rio in as well, their laughter sweet in the noontime air.

It was weird knowing they were out in public mere hours before, kissing and holding hands and yet as they laid there, it felt like there was an ocean between them. Rio might not have known Agatha, but it didn’t stop her from feeling magnetized, pulled in by Agatha’s charm and good looks.

Maybe it had only been a couple of days, but whatever Agatha was selling, Rio was buying it right up without any hesitation.

Okay, maybe some hesitation, or they would have been tangled in one another in the middle of the bed instead of their own islands on opposite sides of the sheets.

She could faintly hear the bustle of the city outside, but over all, there was just nothing aside from the sound of Agatha’s breathing behind her, steady and soft. Another five minutes passed and Rio simply could not take it anymore.

“Agatha?” she whispered.

For a second, there was nothing, and then she felt the bed shift behind her. Agatha rolled over and placed a hand on Rio’s side, so casual and yet, it caused Rio’s heart to leap right into her throat.

“Yeah?” she asked, her voice a little sleepy.

Rio was quiet for a long moment before she shifted, placing her hand on Agatha’s to pull it over her middle. “Can we nap like this?” she asked softly.

Maybe Agatha realized what she was really saying: Please don’t let me fall asleep as an island.

Almost immediately, Agatha settled in behind her and tightened her grip, pulling Rio a little closer. It was exactly what she’d been hoping for, what she knew she needed but couldn’t outright demand. The truth was, Agatha owed her nothing and yet, she was giving her everything. She didn’t understand why, but in the moment, she was thankful.

It was an anchor, a way to hold her down and keep her grounded in a moment that could have hurt beyond comprehension – falling asleep while fully understanding what was happening to her – and she knew she could only ask for one more thing.

“Don’t let go,” Rio whispered, her arm laying atop Agatha’s so that she could slide her fingers into the spaces between the other woman’s, squeezing her hand gently.

“I’m not going anywhere,” Agatha told her quietly.

It could have been said that nothing made sense as Rio closed her eyes and settled into the feeling of being held by Agatha from behind, but somehow as she drifted off to sleep, she could not remember feeling more at peace.

 

Chapter 10: If You Come With Me, We Won't Leave

Waking up some time later, Rio felt like she’d slept so hard she time traveled. Upon rousing, she found herself confused by her surroundings and rolled over to the feeling of an empty bed. As she blinked at the stark white ceiling, lined with decorative moulding, everything started to come back.

She was in Paris with famous rockstar Agatha Harkness. They’d been drunk. They’d been stupid. They’d gotten fucking married. Shit. That was right, even if it seemed so wrong.

As she tuned into her surroundings, she heard the sound of running water in the room adjacent to the one she was in and the faintest sound of humming. It wasn’t intentional, but as she blinked herself more into consciousness, she realized it had helped ease away her headache. Finally feeling like she was actually waking up, she pushed herself into an upright position and stretched through a long yawn. Just as she pulled her arm down, Agatha came around the corner.

What a vision she was.

Her hair was wrapped in a towel that wound around her head, but she was dressed in a bright white blouse with gold buttons, tucked into a pair of dark navy slacks with an accentuating black and gold belt.

Okay, maybe the fashion comment from earlier was totally unnecessary.

Agatha stopped, a playful scowl crossing her features. “I’ll have you know I thought about coming out in a towel, but aside from the ‘waking up naked in a bathtub together’ thing, I don’t think we’re there… yet.” She tossed a wink in Rio’s direction and made her way over to her little pile of bags, pulling a tiny box from a smaller one.

“What’s that?” Rio asked as she leaned back on the heels of her hands, still not entirely ready to get out of bed.

“Peony and blush suede,” Agatha answered with a grin, taking the tiny bottle out to spritz some of it across her chest.

“Is that what you smelled like last – the other night?”

“It’s what I smell like all the time,” Agatha informed as she set the bottle down and bent over to finish towel drying her hair.

At that moment, Rio realized something special.

She had just learned one of Agatha’s favorite things. It was casual, just happening to come up in conversation, and it gave her a little more to work with. It also gave her an idea.

“Hey,” she voiced as Agatha snapped upright again, her head cocking to the side to meet Rio’s curious gaze, blue eyes curious all their own. “Wanna go to dinner?”

Agatha laughed, shaking her head as she got to her feet again. “It doesn’t but what about this does?” She strode off toward the bathroom again, returning without the towel, but still wearing a grin. “Alright then, let’s go on a date.”

Agatha was a nice distraction, sure, but what about when they went home? What about when the real world came crashing down on them? Would Agatha still want to flirt and banter when the high wore off and all Rio could do was cry? Rio felt like she was living in a distant reality where nothing was as she knew it, but somehow having Agatha there grounded her, rooted her in place, and made her feel as though her life was still liveable.

“Let’s go to dinner, then.”

For good reason.

People would look, would gawk, and they would be international news again by the time morning came in New York City.

Agatha rolled her eyes and took a longer sip of the wine, shifting in her seat to get more comfortable as she crossed her legs at the knee and leaned back, her arm along the backing of the chair. “I’ll have you know you don’t get this far in life without learning a few things.”

God, Rio thought, she looked so fucking good.

“Yeah, well, I think the only thing you’ve been married to in your life aside from me is your job,” Agatha punched back, a small smirk on her lips. She wasn’t trying to press buttons, necessarily, but rather she was pointing out the obvious.

“Yeah, you know. Childhood traumas, adulthood traumas, favorite color, favorite flower. That whole thing,” she explained, waving her free hand over the table to indicate any and all of the space between them.

“I guess it is.”

 

Chapter 11: Don't Look At the Clock

“That’s… That’s quite the picture,” Rio told her, rolling carefully in her arms until they were chest to chest, their faces only inches away from one another across the pillows. She was quiet for a long few moments as she studied Agatha’s face at close range, mapped out each crease and wrinkle to log into her mind. Rio wasn’t sure what would become of them, or when the intensity would wear off, but in that moment, she knew she was where she needed to be.

“You can just not go,” Agatha offered, the corner of her mouth hitching up slightly in a half smile.

“She’d find your address in two hours. Maybe one if she’s really pissed,” she retorted, chuckling softly. “And while she probably will anyway for the sake of being who she is, it’s probably best if we don’t give her a reason to fast track it.”

Agatha reached up and tucked a strand of hair behind her ear, watching Rio for just a moment.

“You’re really concerned with my privacy when yours got blasted all over international media,” she pointed out, causing Rio to roll her eyes.

“Don’t flatter yourself. I live here now, too. The last thing I want is Jen showing up unannounced.” She tried to sound indifferent, but it was just a show. She’d already buckled under Jen’s assault on Agatha’s character the first morning but Agatha didn’t need to know that. In fact, it was probably better if she didn’t; that would have been one hell of a thing to try and explain.

Agatha was quiet for a moment, the thoughts whizzing by in those beautiful blue eyes. Rio should have known she was going to say something out of pocket, but she was still a little surprised when Agatha spoke.

“I can go with you.”

She didn’t have to say it, and she definitely did not even have to volunteer, but it was nice that she did. In accordance with the nature of who she was as a human, Rio naturally wanted to retract and keep the kindness at bay.

She shouldn't have trusted Agatha so easily, but she did. Explaining why was off the table, but it was still true.

“You don’t need to do that,” Rio told her, lightly brushing the skin of her chest with her thumb.

“I don’t, but if it would help get her off your back so we have a bit more time to figure things out, what could it hurt?” Agatha shrugged the shoulder she wasn’t laying on, a gesture that Rio would not admit melted her the tiniest bit.

“I think right now, she’d want to give you the third degree to make sure our stories match up. It would be like the witch trials, being burnt at the stake and all if we’re indicted in her court,” Rio half joked, though only half. There was probably a sliver of truth in there, knowing Jen.

“Well, in my defense, I’ve faced some tough interrogations over the years. I know how to stand my ground,” she teased, causing Rio to chuckle softly.

“Right. Tough guy. Forgot.”

She knew she needed to get out of bed and go about her day, but laying tangled in Agatha felt too much like heaven for her to want to pull away. She managed, but only after leaning forward to give Agatha a soft kiss.

“Thank you, again,” she murmured before slipping out from her hold to retreat back to her new room.

“You’ll quit doing that one day!” Agatha called after her, laughing and probably shaking her head.

After a shower and dressing herself in one of the more fashionable outfits she’d purchased in Paris, Agatha called her a car and she set off, heading uptown to Jen’s office. Passing by the Spotlight offices on the way gave her pause, but only in the way that left her wondering how the hell she was going to show her face there without being bombarded with questions she wasn’t entirely sure how to answer. It would be fine, and most of those who worked under her knew better than to push, but they were human. Curiosity was in their nature.

Taking the elevator to the twenty fifth floor of Jen’s building at 10:03, her phone rang. It rang and it rang, all the way to the right floor. Rio didn’t need to pull it out of her purse to know who it was.

When the doors pulled open, there stood Jen, phone in hand, pressed to her ear.

“There you are! What did you not understand about ten am sharp?!”

Rio rolled her eyes and strode out of the elevator, brushing by Jen to head for her office. Finally, her phone stopped ringing on the way.

“You’re making me want to fire you as a client,” she growled as she stalked behind Rio, sharply turning on her heel once they made it inside to close the door.

“You make too much money to fire me as a client,” Rio countered, heading for the tea kettle that was still steaming to fix herself a cup. She poured the water, added a spoon full of sugar, gave a stir and dropped a tea bag in to let it steep on the corner of Jen’s desk while she took a seat.

The entire time, Jen was screeching behind her.

“Nice of you to finally come back!” she began, pacing a hole in the floor behind Rio. “After you created a whole mess for me to clean up! Do you know how much money I’ve spent buying picture after picture to keep what I can offline?!”

Rio turned in her cushy little white chair, to match the rest of the white and gold decor of the office, and looked Jen in the eye as she came to a halt.

“So don’t,” she said simply.

“Excuse me?” Jen snapped, her arms folding across her middle defiantly.

“Stop buying them. Let them run. People know now, so what’s the harm?”

“Rio! Are you even listening to yourself?” she practically screeched. “You have curated a very specific public image – that you pay me very well to protect – and now you’re just throwing it out the window after decades?! What is going on with you?”

Very calmly, Rio turned to her tea and gave the beg a gentle pull and let it settle in the water again, needing just a bit longer.

Maybe that was what Rio had needed, just a little longer. She was quiet as she turned to face Jen again, leveling her with direct eye contact.

“Maybe,” she began. She chewed on her lip and then cleared her throat, finally settling on what she wanted to say. “Maybe some things are worth changing for.”

There in her sleek pink suit, Jen looked like Rio had genuinely slapped her across the face. She looked so struck, so taken aback to hear something so profound come from Rio’s lips. She also didn’t look very happy about it.

“Did you hit your head or something? You hide this relationship, and your breakup, from everyone including me, only to then go and get very publicly eloped to some random rockstar?”

She was trying to give Rio the third degree, but it wasn’t working. Rio had already braced for it, knew way ahead of time that it was coming. In fact, she spent her entire shower that morning rehearsing in her head just how nonchalant she was going to be. It was working, mostly.

“You know,” she said, pulling the tea bag from the cup after using the spoon to squeeze it against the cup to throw it away in the small can next to Jen’s desk. “It doesn’t matter how hard you try to act like she isn’t a household name, because she is.”

“I know that, Rio!” Jen countered, stamping her booted heel. “That’s the problem! At least Val was a nobody –” She cut herself off, a slightly surprised look crossing her face that she’d said something so harsh out loud.

Rio simply shrugged.

She was right.

“Val is a nobody,” she corrected. It caused Jen to soften and pace to her desk, taking a seat behind it while Rio casually sipped her tea.

“What happened?” Jen asked, but was quick to continue. “And don’t give me that ‘we amicably split because we got bored’ bullshit you tried to sell me from Paris. Look me in the eye and tell me what happened.” Her voice was strong, but it was clear she just wanted to know what had happened.

Rio inhaled slowly through her nose and set her tea cup down, leaning back in her chair.

“She cheated on me.”

Well, that part wasn’t a lie. That was just a fact.

Jen’s eyes went so wide, Rio thought for a second they might pop out of her head.

“She what?”

Rio shrugged and held herself, arms across her middle like Jen but in a far less defensive manner.

“I walked in on her with someone else. A while back, before I met Agatha,” she explained, that time weaving a bit of their lie in with the truth. “And then we met and just…” She sighed softly and gave a light shrug of her shoulders. “It’s just easy.”

Jen leaned forward on her elbows, her expression notably softer. “I’m sorry. And… I get why you didn’t tell me, but you could have.”

“I know,” Rio agreed. She could have told Jen, if a large part of what she was saying had been true. “I just tried to deal with it on my own. I thought it would be quiet, thought I could keep going the way I was, but…”

“You let her whisk you to Paris like you’d lost your damn mind,” Jen cut in, eyebrows raising pointedly.

“I did. And when she proposed, I didn’t want to say no. And yeah, maybe we were a little drunk, but… I don’t regret it.” That statement was only partially true. They had been more than just a little drunk. They’d been hammered for twelve hours and counting at that point.

Jen studied her long and hard, her expression like stone. After a few moments, she finally sat back in her chair.

“You’re happy, right?” she asked gently, watching Rio for any insincerity.

“I am. Agatha cares. I don’t know why, because as we know, I can be impossible. But she does, and I… I’m thankful.”

“Do you love her?” Jen asked and for the first time, Rio actually paused. It wasn’t a hesitation, but a reflection.

As she gave her answer, she wasn’t entirely sure she was telling a lie.

“I do.”

Jen cracked a smile, warm and wide.

“Then I am happy for you. Really, Rio.”

“Thanks, Jen. I’m happy for me, too.”

 

Chapter 17: Time's Getting Thin

Arriving home that evening after stopping at the office – to many stares and soft ‘congratulations’ from her staff, though most of them looked too terrified to speak to her out of fear of getting canned – to do a bit of work, or at least try, Rio gave Agatha a bit of a rundown in regards to her conversation with Jen. It wasn’t too detailed, but there was a thing or two Rio chose to admit.

Telling Agatha about Jen’s question, and her respective answer, felt foolish and out of line. It was part of the lie, part of the bit they were trying to sell. It had only been five days; there was no way in hell she could claim to actually be in love with someone and mean it. She could claim it, of course, but it was stupid to actually think it was true.

Rio couldn’t love her because she didn’t know her. It was the same thing she kept telling herself on repeat, almost like she was trying to be convincing to an audience of one and only one.

She kept telling herself for one week and then two. After a third, she was still trying to convince herself a month in. Four weeks and most of it was spent with Rio inwardly claiming that what they were doing was normal and whatever “feelings” she had were only a direct result of their time together.

Then again, their time together had changed quite a bit in those weeks.

A moving company grabbed Rio’s things from the old brownstone and moved a great deal of them into storage, but some came to Agatha’s. She got to decorate a little, mostly on the north wing of the house where she spent her time when Agatha was away, either meeting with execs or spending time in the studio. She placed trinkets and keepsakes in the bedroom, but it felt a little silly given the fact that she spent very little time there.

Mornings, she woke up in Agatha’s bed, skin to skin and breath hot. Days, they spent tending to their careers and doing what they needed to keep up appearances outside of their relationship. Evenings, they met in the living area where one of them got home before the other and took up residence on the sectional. They would make or order dinner, sharing in details about the day’s they’d had. Nights, they showered – sometimes together, sometimes separately – and fell into bed with one another, usually until the early hours of the morning when they fell asleep, skin to skin and breath hot to wake up the same way the next day.

It was a routine they built, a way for them to keep their act strong.

Rio had to wonder, though, if it had stopped being an act some time ago. She could only speak for herself, but she personally was thankful to come home to someone who asked about her day and actually listened even if she didn’t care, per say. She was grateful for the meals they made together, the way Agatha helped teach her new things to cook. What Rio most appreciated, though, was simply feeling like someone was on her side.

Even when the rest of the world felt heavy and challenging, Agatha had some “sage wisdom” (really facetious advice) to impart on her for almost every situation.

Bad day? “It could be worse. You could wake up in a bathtub married to a stranger.”

Issues at work? “You could just fire your whole staff and start fresh.”

Thinking about Val? “Come get in bed with me. I’ll make you forget.”

Overwhelmed? “Let me play you the worst song I ever wrote.”

Whatever the instance, Agatha always found a way to make her laugh and bring a smile to her face. It was almost effortless for her, really, and if Rio was honest with herself, it was making her fall. It was pulling her down, drowning her in every bit of Agatha’s humor and wit and charm.

A month in and Rio knew, without a doubt, that she was losing the battle of wills when it came to guarding her heart. Maybe five days wasn’t an adequate amount of time to decide, despite her lie, but a month was, no matter how terrifying it could be.

One late morning, Rio was posted at her desk in her office, poring over the most minute details on a spread that was set to be published for the next month’s issue. Everything looked to her tastes, but as she ran the loupe over the page, she flagged an editing issue where the model appeared to have two heels on the same foot. With a red pen, she circled the defect and flipped to the next page. Halfway down, there was a tap on her door that drew her attention.

Lifting her head, Rio was entirely surprised to see Jen in the doorway, a small smile on her lips.

“Hey,” Rio greeted, confused. “Did we have a meeting today?”

When Jen shook her head, something in Rio’s stomach sank.

Oh god, she thought. What did Agatha do?

“Is something wrong?” she asked, but Jen did not wait for permission to enter, closing the door behind her. She looked the picture perfect image of class and elegance in her pastel yellow suit, but there was something on her face that made Rio’s stomach twist and churn. If she was closing them off to the rest of the office, it was likely for a reason.

“I guess that depends on your definition of ‘wrong,’” Jen offered as she took a seat in one of the chairs across from Rio’s desk.

Rio didn’t like that answer. She didn’t like it at all.

“Are you going to cut the bullshit or are you going to tell me what you’re doing here?” Rio questioned, bristling. She was trying to not get her defenses up so quickly, but it was hard with the way Jen was being so cagey. It wasn’t like her; Jen was a no nonsense kind of woman and did not take time or energy to beat around the bush. The fact that she was being so elusive while knowing she had new was more than just unnerving, but Rio knew all she could do was wait until Jen was ready.

“I didn’t want to call,” she admitted, setting her bag down on the floor to pull something out of it – a manilla envelope. “I think you deserve this news in person.”

Rio’s brow furrowed. “News? What news?” She didn’t like the sound of that, not in the least.

Jen slowly opened the envelope and pulled out a small stack of papers, laying it on the desk to the side of Rio’s mockup book. Slowly, Rio’s eyes dropped to the pages, not sure what it was she was being given.

“Jen, what is this?” she asked, picking them up to look them over. She saw words like invalid and not legally binding and null-in-void but didn’t understand them in any form of context. Her brow furrowed further, causing her to read deeper, though she still didn’t quite get it.

“I’ve been… looking into things,” Jen said as Rio kept reading, trying to make sense of it.

“Things?” Rio clipped, flipping to the second page.

“Yes. Things. Like the validity of the certificate you signed in a completely different country.”

Oh. Ouch.

“And why did you do that?” Rio asked, her voice growing a little hard. It felt like a very clear overstep on Jen’s part, but she knew that lashing out without hearing her side of things first was likely unfair. After all, it was part of her job to make sure that Rio’s career and image were well protected.

“Because I didn’t want anyone else to do it first,” she answered honestly, leaning back in her chair. Her eyes were clear and sharp, but they were full of… What was that? Was it guilt? Was it regret?

“And what did you find?” Rio asked, her head dropping to the papers again.

“That.”

“And what is this, exactly?” She still wasn’t getting it, wasn’t slowing herself down enough to understand what it was she was reading. She was reading it, she was seeing the words, but nothing was sticking. Nothing wanted to stick, to make sense, to settle.

“Without a supporting license from here, it’s… Just a piece of paper.”

Just a piece of paper.

It wasn’t real.

Fuck.

“You…” Rio tried, her mouth suddenly feeling like it was filled with sand. “You went behind my back just to prove that my marriage wasn’t real?” Her voice was sharp, an edge to it. Her eyes lifted and pinned Jen from across the desk, leaving her feeling like she could scream.

“Rio, it isn’t like that and you know it,” Jen argued back, stiffening. She was getting defensive and honestly, Rio knew why even before she said it. “I’m just doing my job.”

Rio’s jaw set, tight and knotted as she tried to calm herself down. She was realizing in real time that their perfectly constructed lie was going to come crumbling down. It was fake. It wasn’t real. If none of it was real, it could be said that they weren’t, either. If it was true and their license was just a piece of paper with no real weight, then maybe the same would be said for their relationship.

How the fuck was she going to tell Agatha?

More than that, what the fuck was going to happen between them?

Rio didn’t know, and that was terrifying.

“You should have told me, Jennifer,” came Rio’s cutting quip. “You owe it to me as your client to let me know when you’re digging into my life.”

“Rio, come on. Get real right now,” Jen snapped, growing defensive. “When have I ever asked for your permission when trying to clean up a mess?”

“Never!”

“Because I’ve never needed to!” Jen clearly felt justified in her decision, and outside of her own personal feelings, Rio knew she was. Rio had made things rather easy on her for the duration of their working relationship, but those days were very obviously over.

She sighed and slumped back into her chair, raising a hand to rub at her face, careful not to smear her makeup. “I know, and I’m sorry. I just… Ow, Jen,” she said softly, flipping the papers over dramatically, not wanting to look at them.

“I know, Rio. And I’m sorry, but that’s why I came over instead of calling. Because you deserved not to be alone when you found out. And I know it probably doesn’t change much and you’ll just go get a license here, but… I know it’s still hard, especially if I’m bad at my job and this leaks.” Jen was a good manager, really, and Rio knew she was lucky to have her, even if she was making her want to rip her own head clean off of her shoulders and punt it out the window from the thirtieth floor.

“Then it’s a damn good thing you’re good at your job and that’s why I pay you, huh?” Rio retorted with a sigh and a sad smile.

“It is.”

The office fell silent for a few moments as Rio stared at the overturned papers, mourning whatever she and Agatha had built for a month, and Jen stared at Rio. She was the first one to break the silence.

“Are you okay?”

Rio’s eyes lifted and her lips weakly turned up at the corners, nodding her head the tiniest bit.

“Yeah. I just… Don’t know how to tell her, you know? I don’t… know if it changes anything.”

Jen’s face fell, her expression turning slightly confused at the revelation.

“Why would it?” she pressed, but Rio shook her off.

“I don’t know. I don’t know,” she tried to recover, picking the papers up off the desk to tuck them into her purse. She would have to take them home if she hoped to be believed. “But… Thank you, Jen. For finding out and for letting me know. I… I’ll take it from here.”

That was Jen’s cue to leave, and she seemed to pick up on it as she stood from her chair and slid the straps of her purse over her forearm, staring down at her friend with a shine of pity in her eyes. “It’s going to be okay, Rio. Try to have a good rest of your day.”

“Yeah, thanks. You too, Jen.”

With one last tight smile from both of them, Jen gave a nod of her head and let herself out, thankfully pulling the door shut behind her. The moment it clicked, Rio’s head dropped and landed on her forearms where they rested on her desk.

Fuck. Fuck fuck fuck. Fuck.

What the fuck was she going to do?
