Summary

Agatha Harkness had built her life on control, thirty, successful, living high above the city in a glass-and-marble apartment that gleamed with proof of her accomplishments. Yet despite her curated world, every date felt the same: predictable, uninspired, fleeting. When Agatha’s date arrived at the door of her apartment, she always looked toward her doorwoman for assessment. If there was anyone with sharper eyes for people, it was Rio Vidal, unflappable, observant, and disarmingly perceptive.

It wasn’t until she started paying attention to the woman holding the door that Agatha began to wonder whether she’d been looking in the wrong direction all along.

Chapter 1

Being a successful lawyer at thirty meant Agatha lived in an apartment that practically gleamed with proof of it. Every line was clean, every surface immaculate, the kind of place where clutter simply didn’t exist. The air carried a faint trace of bergamot and fresh linen, expensive, understated, intentional. After a long day of arguing, deflecting, and listening to the most creative bullshit imaginable just to keep her clients out of jail, she was reminded why she charged as much as she did.

Still, the best part of her job came at the end of the day, when she got to come home, to her sanctuary high above the city. Floor-to-ceiling windows opened to the skyline, light pooling over marble counters and cool wood floors. Her heels clicked sharply across the quiet, a sound she found oddly soothing. Her bedroom. Her bathroom. Her silence. No one to debate her, no one to talk over her, and definitely no one to make another stupid argument to her face. 

By the time she made it to the lobby, the scent of polished stone and perfume filled the air. The marble floors gleamed like a mirror, reflecting the soft amber light above. Smooth jazz drifted through hidden speakers, wrapping the space in quiet sophistication. A tall vase of fresh lilies stood by the reception desk, replaced daily, because management insisted on appearances. Floor-to-ceiling windows framed the skyline like a painting, turning the city’s chaos into something curated and costly.

Her mail was sorted before she ever touched it, packages always arrived on time, and someone always remembered her name. It was a place that ran on efficiency, on quiet, expensive precision. And at the front of it all, the person who held the door when she walked through, her doorman.

Well, a doorwoman, since she was a woman.

Agatha had imagined someone in charge of the front gate would be a buff, grumpy-faced guy, six feet tall and intimidating enough to ward off unwanted guests. But no. Instead, there was a slender woman with brown hair pinned neatly in a bun, a few loose strands softening her sharp profile. Her copper eyes glistened in the afternoon light, and her suit fit so well it felt almost criminal. Yeah, Agatha was not complaining about the doorwoman part. 

“Good evening, Ms. Harkness!” The woman greeted her, holding the door open. Her voice is soft, warm like a well tuned saxophone. A smile curved on her lips, revealing a small gap between her front teeth. Agatha found herself wondering if she smiled like that for everyone… or just for her.

The chill of the evening air brushed against Agatha’s bare shoulders, raising goosebumps along her skin and carrying the faint scent of rain on asphalt. Somewhere down the block, a cab honked, sharp, impatient, the sound bouncing between glass towers. The night sky glowed a deep navy, clouds drifting lazily to hide a handful of shy stars. Voices from passersby hummed around her, blending with the distant clatter of dishes from the café across the street. A thin breeze tugged at the hem of her dress, cool against her legs. She drew in a breath that tasted faintly of exhaust and autumn, then exhaled, slow. She scanned the street, no sign of her date.

Punctuality, she thought, was something she should start enforcing. Fortunately, Agatha could afford a place with a charming doorwoman, someone to keep her company while she waited for tardiness wrapped in a suit and cologne. 

Agatha offered a faint smile. “I told you, it’s Agatha. Ms. Harkness is my mother, and I’d rather not be associated with her, thank you very much.”

“Of course, Ms. Harkness,” she replied smoothly.

“Ay!” Agatha protested, half-laughing, half-serious.

Ms. Vidal’s lips curved into an apologetic smile as she stepped aside to open the door for another guest, effortless, professional, unflappable. “My apologies,” she said. “Give me some time to adjust. It just feels... impolite. After all, I’m only the door guard.”

“Nonsense,” Agatha waved off the comment. “I see you every day, more than I see my own mother, honestly. We’re friends, aren’t we?”

“Yes, but technically, you’re my employer.”

“Then you’ll do as I say,” Agatha said, arching a brow. “Are we friends?”

Ms. Vidal’s expression didn’t falter, though there was a glint of amusement in her copper eyes. “Yes.”

“Good. Then what will you call me?”

A beat of silence. Then, perfectly deadpan: “Ms. Harkness.”

Ms. Vidal always carried that same calm, unbothered expression, whether it was snowing, raining, or the kind of summer heat that made Agatha want to sue the weather itself. Her posture never faltered: back straight, shoulders steady, chest lifted with quiet pride. Even her hands seemed deliberate, every movement precise and measured. The kind of stillness that came not from indifference, but control.

Agatha couldn’t help wondering how she did it, how someone could stand there for hours, greeting strangers with the same poise and warmth as if she actually meant it. Hundreds of people passed her every day, yet she looked neither drained nor detached, her caring smile never faded. Agatha, who dealt with maybe a dozen people before lunch, often went home ready to throw her briefcase off the balcony. She really ought to ask the woman her secret one of these days.

“Maybe next time.” Agatha held up her hands in surrender.

“Heading somewhere? You should wear a coat, it’s getting chilly at night,” Ms. Vidal replied, still polite, still perfectly composed. “Do you want me to call you a cab?”

“Thanks, but my date should be here soon.”

“Should be means late?”

Agatha narrowed her eyes, intrigued. “Excuse me?”

Ms. Vidal glanced at her watch. “Most people plan things on the hour, or maybe quarter past. It’s 7:37 p.m. now, so I’d guess he’s either seven minutes, twenty-two minutes or thirty-seven minutes late. Hopefully the first.”

What a sharp mind this woman had. Agatha wished her date had even a fraction of that awareness, or at least knew how to read a clock.

Agatha let out a soft laugh. “Impressive. It’s the last.”

“A man with no respect for time isn’t much of a man,” she said lightly.

“Hi, sweetheart.”

The familiar voice crashed with an embrace that made Agatha stiffen. David. She really needed to talk to him about boundaries; third date or not, she wasn’t ready for that kind of closeness. His vest was crisp, almost flamboyant, and his hair was slicked back so precisely he could’ve passed for a discount Elvis. The air around him was heavy with cologne, sweet, sharp, and far too eager to make an impression. Despite being the one dressed for a date, he somehow looked less composed than her doorwoman.

“Flowers for you,” he said, holding out a bouquet of red roses.

“Traffic.”

It was either traffic, or he’d been too busy deciding which pair of panties to put on. Why had Agatha even agreed to a third date with him? Admittedly, his LinkedIn profile hadn’t been the worst she’d seen, Columbia alumnus, manager at some consulting firm, nice Mercedes. Yes, Agatha Harkness vetted her dates through LinkedIn, even if they’d met, shamefully, on Bumble.

He’d seemed decent enough on their first date, at least he hadn’t bored her to death, so she’d agreed to a second. That was when she learned his favorite topic of conversation was his cologne collection. By the fifth glass of wine, somewhere between the story about his “big promotion” and his assistant’s gluten allergy, she’d made the mistake of agreeing to a third. 

“You could’ve called. I was worried.”

“Yeah, yeah. I’m here now, aren’t I?”

Out of the corner of her eye, Agatha caught Ms. Vidal shaking her head, a soft chuckle escaping her lips. 

“Let’s just go.”

Agatha walked toward his car, expecting him to open the door. He didn’t. Ms. Vidal, however, moved without hesitation, circling around and opening it for her with practiced grace. 

Their eyes met briefly. Her expression was calm, but there was a flicker of something disapproving there, like she wanted to pull David aside and lecture him, spanking him like a naughty school boy.

“Have a nice evening, Agatha.” she said softly. Agatha caught the faint scent of coffee and sandalwood when she passed her, and for some reason, it lingered longer than it should have.

“Thank you,” Agatha replied, slipping into the car.

The door closed with a gentle clasp. David climbed in, checking his reflection in the mirror. He probably spent longer on his hair than she had getting ready.

“Who was that?” he asked, annoyance creeping into his tone.

“My doorwoman,” Agatha said, her eyes flicking to the rearview mirror where Ms. Vidal still stood, greeting guests with that same easy composure, continuing her evening as usual. 

“Now drive before the restaurant gives away our table.” Agatha dryly said, she didn’t want any more disappointment for the night.

David muttered something under his breath, but Agatha wasn’t listening. Her gaze lingered on the woman in the sharp suit, framed by the building’s warm light. Something about her presence felt… steady. Grounded. 

And as the car pulled away, Agatha realized she was already looking forward to coming home.

Agatha Harkness sat rigidly in the waiting area of Jen’s clinic, shielding her face behind a glossy architecture magazine and praying to every deity she didn't believe in that nobody would try to make small talk.

Why are you still billing eighty hours a week? Because the MTA refuses to run the Manhattan subways without her personal sign-off.

Is it a boy or a girl? She has no idea; her ancient religious tradition strictly forbids gendering a child prior to their eighteenth birthday.

And her personal favorite—Where is the father? Oh, he’s dead. Sometimes in a tragic multi-car pileup on the I-95. Sometimes in a classified overseas skirmish. Sometimes a sudden, aggressive bout of bone marrow cancer. Regardless of the fictional pathology, it reliably shut people up within three seconds flat.

Luckily, today nobody seemed particularly interested in Agatha’s solitude—except for Agatha herself.

She eyed the couple seated diagonally across from her. The woman was visibly further along, her hand resting naturally over a high, pronounced bump. The husband was flipping eagerly through a dog-eared paperback titled The Expectant Father's Handbook, while his wife skimmed a fashion spread. Every few seconds, he would point to a diagram, earning a soft, bubbling giggle from her. They both smiled down at her rounded stomach with identical looks of giddy, starry-eyed awe.

Ugh. Disgustingly happy families.

Even the clinic’s pastel walls were plastered with framed stock photography of radiant couples staring down lovingly at newborn infants. Apparently, stock images of a fiercely independent, single corporate litigator holding a briefcase in one hand and prenatal vitamins in the other were in short supply.

What truly gnawed at Agatha’s ribs, however, was that she wasn't actually supposed to be sitting here alone today. Rio was supposed to be with her.

Except Rio wasn't here.

Agatha glanced at her watch. To be fair, Agatha had arrived a full thirty minutes ahead of schedule out of sheer nervous habit, meaning there were still fifteen minutes left before their three o'clock slot. She had zero right to be bitter. Rio was off the clock. Rio had zero legal or moral obligation to babysit an anxious tenant, yet she had willingly offered to burn a free Friday afternoon playing "emotional support doorwoman" for an ultrasound scan.

With a heavy, self-deprecating sigh, Agatha snapped the magazine page over with a sharp flick.

“Are you here all by yourself, dear?” an older, gravelly voice piped up from the adjacent vinyl chair.

Agatha squeezed her eyes shut for half a second, willing the universe to swallow her whole, before deliberately turning another page without looking up.

“My daughter’s husband got held up with some last-minute commercial work out of state, so I came along to keep her company,” the woman continued cheerfully, oblivious to Agatha’s icy wall of silence.

“Uh-huh,” Agatha offered with maximum monosyllabic hostility.

“He’s been such a saint, though. Taken her to every single OB appointment since week six.”

“Fascinating.”

“And to think, they were so on the fence when they first found out!” The woman let out a nostalgic chuckle. “It’s truly remarkable how far they’ve come.”

What is this? An unprompted free trial of someone else's domestic melodrama? Agatha thought bitterly. I’d rather drink generic baby formula through a straw.

“He actually panicked and disappeared for an entire week right after the positive test, you know,” the woman gossiped, shaking her head. “My poor girl cried her eyes out day and night.”

Agatha’s manicured fingers tightened their grip on the magazine until the glossy paper wrinkled under the pressure.

“But then he came to his senses, came home with a bouquet of lilies, and they tied the knot at city hall. And look at them now—happier than ever.”

“Yes. A true fairy tale.”

“Is your husband just tied up at the office today?” the woman pressed, leaning in closer. “How far along are you? Around twenty weeks, I’d guess? Today’s a massive milestone! You finally find out if you're buying pink or blue.”

Agatha’s jaw clenched so hard her molars ached.

Snap.

The woman blinked, taken aback. “Excuse me? I was just trying to be—”

“Agatha? Hey, what’s going on?”

Her hazel eyes immediately locked onto Agatha, assessing her flushed face and trembling hands before sweeping over to the stunned older woman with an unmistakable edge of protective steel.

“Is everything okay?” Rio asked, her eyes flicking sharply between Agatha and the older woman.

“You’re with…” the woman stuttered, her hand fluttering nervously over her purse.

“I’m with her,” Rio said, stepping closer until her presence filled the space. “Can you please stop bothering my partner?”

My partner.

The phrase landed squarely in Agatha’s chest, stunning her into silence. It was unexpected, bold, and entirely unearned in the eyes of the law firm—yet hearing the words roll off Rio’s tongue sent a strange, pleasant hum through Agatha's veins.

“I’m sorry. I just saw her sitting alone, and I thought a little chat wouldn’t hurt,” the woman stammered, raising her hands defensively. “Just to be clear, I’m completely open-minded. I have absolutely no problem with whatever you two are doing.”

“Whatever we’re doing?” Rio raised an eyebrow, her tone razor-sharp. “Lady, if you have to announce that you’re open-minded, it usually means you aren't.”

Rio held the woman's gaze for one long, chilling second before dropping effortlessly into the empty vinyl chair beside Agatha.

“Am I late?” Rio asked, turning toward her. Her denim jacket brushed against the sleeve of Agatha’s wool coat.

Agatha felt her thoughts reel. Her brain seemed trapped on a single track, replaying my partner over and over. She suddenly realized she had been staring at the slope of Rio's jaw without speaking.

“Agatha?” Rio murmured, hazel eyes searching hers with quiet concern.

“No,” Agatha finally managed, shaking the fog away. “No, you're fine. I was just ridiculously early.”

“Sorry I made you wait by yourself.”

“It’s fine. Didn't you have a lecture?”

“Yeah,” Rio nodded, leaning back. “Just one seminar this afternoon. Then the rest of the day belongs to you. And this one.” Rio’s gaze dropped to Agatha’s stomach, her expression softening into something unmistakably tender.

“How did you get here so fast?”

“Took the express train to 14th, then jogged the last five blocks.”

Agatha studied her. “You haven't been on the lobby shifts as much lately. Is everything alright?”

“Yeah. Just trying to dial in on school before finals hit. Gotta make the tuition count,” Rio chuckled. She looked around the waiting room, taking in the pastel pamphlets and infant posters. “So, you do this routine every single month?”

“Pretty much,” Agatha said. “Usually I just come on my own. The head OB is a close friend, so it takes the edge off.”

“Right—Dr. Kale?”

“You remembered. The absolute worst kind of leafy green.”

“Hey, kale is good for you,” Rio teased.

“So is Jen. Tough, but good. We’ve known each other since undergrad—we were roommates back when she was pre-med and I was drowning in pre-law prep.”

“We were practically vampires,” Agatha laughed softly. “We bought espresso beans by the pallet.”

“I don’t think I’ve held onto anyone that long, outside of my own family.”

Agatha raised an eyebrow. “Really? With how effortlessly charming you are, I assumed you had an army of friends.”

“Not really,” Rio shrugged, stretching her long legs out. “I can make small talk with a brick wall, but genuine friends? That’s rare. There’s Bucky, who I met recently through mutuals. And then Alice, who I served with back during my Marine days. She’s a full-time beat cop and a weekend lounge singer.”

Rio turned her head, a mock scowl playing on her lips. “You can’t ask for another woman’s number while sitting in an OB-GYN clinic with me.”

“And why is that?”

“Because I don’t want you calling her to come sit in these chairs instead of me.”

“Why would I ever do that?” Agatha huffed, crossing her arms over her bump.

“Because Alice has game,” Rio said simply.

“And what makes you so certain I’ll invite you back for the next one?”

“Because I know you actually enjoy having me around.” Rio leaned in close, closing the distance until her voice dropped to a low, husky purr right beside Agatha’s ear. “Don’t you?”

She was entirely too close.

Agatha could see the faint constellation of gold flecks ringing Rio’s pupils and the sweep of dark lashes against her olive skin. The crisp scent of pine, cedar, and citrus flooded her senses, thoroughly wrecking her composure. Her heart hammered against her ribs, entirely uncooperative.

Agatha jerked her chin toward the opposite wall, praying the flush on her cheeks looked like bad clinic lighting.

“No,” Agatha lied through her teeth.

Rio let out a low, victorious chuckle from behind her shoulder.

“Ms. Harkness?”

A nurse in seafoam scrubs appeared in the doorway with a clipboard, saving Agatha from instantaneous cardiovascular collapse.

Agatha stood up so fast she nearly bumped Rio’s knee. “Here.”

“Right this way, room three.”

Agatha paused at the threshold of the hallway, glancing back. “Are you coming or not?”

“Right behind you,” Rio said, standing up and smoothing down the front of her Nirvana shirt with a crooked grin. “Gotta make sure I leave a sterling first impression. Lead the way, Counselor.”

The nurse led them into the softly dimmed ultrasound room that Agatha had grown intimately familiar with. The reclined examination table was lined with crisp white paper that crinkled loudly under Agatha's weight as she climbed up and tried to find a comfortable angle. A cylindrical bottle rested snugly inside its designated warmer—the acoustic gel Agatha was definitely not looking forward to feeling against her skin. “Here you go,” the nurse offered, wheeling a vinyl armchair close to the head of the bed. “Dr. Kale will be with you in just a moment.”

Rio let out a soft, low chuckle.
