Plato, The Symposium.

Before time began, or, before it was measured in any discernible way, some lucky creatures of this earth were born as pairs.

Born first, somewhat unluckily, separately, they would then find one another and all but fuse together.

This union of mind, body, and soul, was envied by those who were around to witness it. And news of their joy would be spread the world over until the universe herself heard tell of it and started to grow curious.

And so it watched this strange anomaly that had occurred in the world, and observed all the creatures who were lucky enough to be part of a set.

The universe, apathetic to the goings on of mammal or man, soon began to take pleasure in the show of love and devotion these pairs performed.

For there were no two alike. And their love was shown in many varied, delicious ways. Though all were alike in their veneration of the other, and their being only whole when together.

But as the universe witnessed, so did the Gods. Jealous and proud that they were, they soon turned their mighty eyes to this rarefied union of souls.

Though few, their happiness, and therefore their power, was enough to enrage the envy of the Gods. And soon they rained down their wrath and tore these lucky pairs asunder.

Now apart, they suffered and pined for their lost half and wandered the earth in search of them. Few found their other before they simply died of broken hearts and destroyed hope.

And soon, these σύνψυχος soulmates ceased to ever appear again upon Earth’s green lands.

For millennia, then, the Gods triumphed in their defeat of those souls who had the hubris to worship but the other and not Them.

But the universe, a wily beast, unsympathetic to most but tricksy when riled, held steady for long enough that the Gods soon died, having gorged themselves to death on what little praise was left to Them.

And she, now alone in power, alongside her great friend, The Mother Earth, waited and planned and soon created one special soul, cleaved in two.

Though, unbeknownst to the universe, the Gods, greedy and jealous as They were, had left behind old magick. For they had been cunning enough to foresee their own demise in the burgeoning disinterest of their once venerating public.

The magick, so ancient that even the universe herself could not begin to unravel it, meant that though these soul mates could be born they would spend lifetimes apart.

And so the show would go on, built of tragedy rather than romance. One human born with no knowledge of the other but perpetual longing built into her bones that would inevitably etch into them vicious rage and violent fear that would continue throughout each of her lives.

The other, receiving perhaps a worse fate, was born to remember their other half whenever they first encountered her in any lifetime.

This second partner would feel similar longings to her other half, though the pain of it would be tempered by the recollection of their soulmate, of the lives they had lived together, whenever their eyes found her across fields, streets, ballrooms, hospitals.

She would strive to make the other fall for them as they always should have; sometimes she would be successful, other times not. But success would not make her soul mate recognise her. Such was the power of the Gods’ curse.

But still they would try, they would risk everything for their other half. If only for a glimpse of them, for a touch of their hand, for a kiss so yearned for after centuries of searching.

But the universe, tired of seeing these humans suffer when she had created them for great joy, had a trick up her sleeve.

She could not unravel the magick but she could see a way through it.

For suffering had been as the Gods had intended, but this soul that had been conjured by the universe and her friend, had enough power in it to be only once joined.

The trick, the loophole in this ancient magick, was that it needed to be earned. For Gods of such power and majesty, had no knowledge of earning so they had not considered it when creating this curse.

They underestimated the devotion that humans, or creatures of any kind, could have for one another. Thinking they alone would and should be worshipped above all else.

The soul then, split between two yearning bodies, had to earn its right to be whole again; proving through devotion that they deserved to be together again.

And the two who shared this new soul had such great capacity for devotion such as this earth had never seen.

And once they had earned their togetherness, both partners would remember every lifetime together and live, as the universe had intended, with such joy that the world would alight with it.

And so the universe, and her dear friend Mother Earth, had to but wait, and watch, and hope.

It is the burden of a writer to contain within just a few words the sorrows and joys of a lifetime.

Each choice of word must carry with it the memory of childhood or the hopes for a future. And everything, you understand, in between.

And so, each word is chosen with as much care as the universe bestowed upon one soul made for two.

Though an author should be forgiven if their words do not adequately arouse the sheer magnitude of this gift given to two worthy individuals.

Rio Vidal was born of a Spanish fisherman and his wife. They had come to the Americas to seek a new life for themselves in the newly found settlement of St. Augustine on the southeast coast.

Soon after they set foot upon the New World, Rio was made sister to three brothers. She was the calmest of her family, except on the rare occasion when her feelings became too much to bear and they came bursting out of her like fire upon bracken.

So, fiery only when necessary, Rio Vidal was also beautiful and full of sorrow.

As such, she was flighty too, and would often disappear off to the woods in search of she knew not what. But something. Something she could never find.

The Spanish colony of St. Augustine, where we now find ourselves, was freshly formed as Rio was freshly coming of age.

Her father, though loving, had warned her that he could no longer protect her from the inevitable chains of matrimony. He’d refused every suitor that came her way for the better part of seven years. But, now approaching twenty one, he’d warned her that he would accept the hand of the next eligible man who offered.

Rio, knowing not why this thought repulsed her more than it frightened her, could do nothing but accept her fate.

So now, deep in the thick, unwelcoming forest, she hid herself amongst the pinewood trees and the lupine flowers that guided her way through. These lonely forest walks were her only freedom.

And since they’d arrived on these foreign shores the trees had called to her as if they knew something she didn’t.

The day was warm, much warmer than any day she had experienced in her home country. And Rio felt a trickle of sweat creep down her back as she moved further from the sounds of the shore and the people who worked across it.

St. Augustine was a burgeoning colony, almost now a full township. Almost a thousand strong. It was full of soldiers, fishermen, priests with ideas to evangelise.

And then wives and daughters they brought with them.

They were theslrgest settlement for miles around, at least, largest new settlement. For this land was not theirs, but instead stolen from the indigenous population.

Rio had seen little of them but was wise enough to know that her own people were the savages and that they should not be here.

Despite this, she also knew her mission was similar to that of the religious men here. She was to help the settlement to grow by giving them, God willing, new men to strengthen and guard it.

And so her father would soon allow her to be married and she would be one of many new brides swollen with child before the year was out.

But for now, unattached and so heavily burdened, she moved through the forest seeking peace and that something she could not name.

The quiet of the forest soon seeped into her loud mind and Rio, for a little while at least, was able to hear the soft sounds of her heart calling that familiar, indecipherable song.

All around her the very trees began to respond. They seemed to lean in as if to listen more intently, or else to shelter her more closely for the time they could be her guardians.

But peace, or quiet of any kind, did never last long in the surrounds of St. Augustine.

“Who goes there?”

Rio turned to the unfamiliar voice, so unheard of in these woods she traveled so often alone in. She realised she did not recognise either the voice or the words.

“Who are you? Declare yourself!”

A female voice, a voice that made Rio’s heart purr with excitement and the trees whisper their own.

She turned to where the voice had come from and laid eyes, for the first time, upon her soul’s counterpart.

And Rio Vidal, in the middle of a forest in a still-unknown country, realised all this time what she’d been missing.

It wasn’t something it was someone.

A queen, Rio thought as she blindly stumbled towards her, feeling a sudden, urgent need to be close.

The woman staggered back and Rio lurched towards her as if to catch her but found her fall to be already broken by a tall pine.

She could not understand her words but she could read their meaning well enough.

So she stopped not two feet from the woman but found she could not lower her arms for want of still being able to touch her.

Rio knew little about the birthing process but she knew a lot about the months in between. Months of discomfort for the expectant mother, soothed more often by her friends and family and rarely by the man who had caused it.

Rio could not understand what the woman was saying. Her schooling had focused mostly on religious instruction, and the best way to launder linen. She could read and write but took no great pleasure in either.

Besides, it was all in Spanish.

Are you comfortable?

Because she felt it too.

“No.” Rio knew this word so adhered to it. “Stay. Do not leave me.”

The request did not need to be translated.

“Estoy aquí. Estás bien.”

Though she did not.

She did something both reckless and unimaginable.

She climbed onto the bed with Agatha and she sat behind her, cradling her as best her body could. Agatha’s mother protested with all her might until the doctor intervened and Rio was no longer the victim of untranslated furies.

“Estoy aquí. Estás bien.”

Her hands were taken by Agatha, those same pale fingers that had not been far from her own since the meeting in the forest, slipped between the gaps.

Agatha screamed through the delivery of a too-small baby. And Rio knew from the sight of the blood that came with it, that she had already lost Agatha.

“Estoy aquí. Estás bien.”

Agatha’s screams had faded now, sucked up into the heat of the room as the doctor and her mother took away her baby and left Rio holding her other half.

Above her heart, a golden stone, sewn in place there for the woman who had gifted her it.

Rio Vidal returned to the water that had so gifted her Agatha Harkness.

The universe's friend, the great mother, made sure to take her quickly, so as to put an end to the young woman’s immense suffering.

Rio shook her head, her eyes closed now as Lilia swept away her tears.

“No. She loved me, but she did not remember having done so.”

“That will be a heavy burden for her soul to bear,” Lilia breathed, her voice now more a vibration upon the still air than a sound the ear could attune to. “You must be cognisant of the price she must pay for her nescience.”

Rio shook her head, “What of mine?”

“Your price is paid, my sweet lady. For knowledge is as painful as any state of being. But Agatha’s price, it is being paid unknowingly. Her soul is suffering though her mind knows not. And that, that can be a heavier burden than any.”

Rio remained silent for a while, until her tears had dried and Lilia’s hand was her own again.

“You knew my melancholy was for the longing of another?”

“I knew it to be related to matters of the heart, yes. It was happenstance I came across a girl seeking employ, whom, when I touched her skin, told of a soul torn in two.”

Rio straightened, gaining inches on Lilia who remained standing before her, unabashed by the difference in posture.

“My soul,” Rio breathed, half agony, half hope. “It belongs to her.”

“No, dear,” Lilia whispered back, her hand once again finding the curve of Rio’s cheek, “your souls are one and the same. Yours does not exist without hers. I have seen it. Torn in two, destined to be joined, in this lifetime or the next.”

“Please,” Rio gasped, her tears tumbling to waiting thumbs, “allow it to be this, Lilia. If you know of any magic, of any godly or ungodly way to make it so, then please allow us this lifetime of happiness. I have lost her twice. I cannot lose her again and I do not yet even have her.”

Lilia shook her head and Rio knew then that it was hopeless. Her agony grew and her hope died. She allowed Lilia to dry her tears but no soft reassurance could soothe a heart that had been drowned and burnt in the name of love.

“I have not the power,” Lilia said after a while of gentle nothings that did nothing to mend such brokenness. “I can read, but I cannot heal. But I know this, sweet Rio, that the universe is not random. There is a pattern and a purpose. There will be an end to your suffering, I am sure of it.”

“I have burned for her,” Rio gasped, turning from the softness she was unused to. “I have swallowed sea water as though it were air for her. I have laid with her as if we were man and wife, as if god had brought us together and as if no man could tear us asunder.”

“Perhaps it was a mistake in bringing her to you.”

“How could it be?!” Rio gasped out, her hands buried in her hair and her eyes up to a god she had longsince lost faith in. “In every life I have found her. In every life I have suffered to love her. Perchance time is what we have been denied. Maybe-,” and here she turned to Lilia, her eyes as wide as her love was for the new housemaid, “maybe time, more of it, is what we need. We first had but a day. In the forest, in her bed. And then… barely a fortnight. Perhaps this is our time.”

Lilia, willing to reassure earlier, looked less convinced of it now. And Rio, clinging to nothing but the hope that kept her from hanging, shook her head.

“Is she in pain? Does she mourn me?”

“I cannot-...”

“How can you have seen her soul is half my own and yet-...?!”

“There is a hardening there,” Lilia whispered, her voice that same vibration on the still air. “She is not the girl you once knew.”

“Her soul is the same?” Rio asked, with all the urgency of young love despite the centuries that had passed.

“Yes.”

“Then she is the same. My soul will be hardened too-...”

“Rio, dear, listen to me, though I am but a humble servant. Your soul is granted a reprieve. For your brain and body suffer for and with it when you come to know of Agatha. You know of your separation. You know of the past, of your previous suffering and pain. And yet Agatha, she knows not. She does not know she had once loved you and lost you. Her soul suffers alone, where yours suffers as part of a whole. And so hers is darkened where yours can remain light.”

Rio had stepped backwards, stumbling as she did into the window panes behind her.

“This Agatha, now in your employ, is a petty thief. She is a criminal and a charlatan. She has lived a dishonest life and has-...”

“And you wish to judge her, Lilia?”

“No,” Lilia said firmly, “I wish for her to be reunited with you. The only saving of her soul I wish to do is to have it formed as one with your own. I have seen the darkness in her but there is too a lot of light.”

Rio turned away, seeking solace in the grounds she was mistress of and finding none. She knew now why her position had meant nothing to her the moment she had secured it. Because everything she had ever truly sought belonged in the eyes of the woman who shared her soul.

If, she knew, Lilia were to be believed.

“I must make leave of you,” she said, not a second after she had turned away. She offered Lilia the courtesy of a short bow of her head, and then exited without another word.

For days after, Rio say nobody in her rooms. She accepted light breakfasts and dinners and allowed buckets of hot water to be carried through for bathing, but otherwise she did not welcome another into her chambers.

The time was instead spent reviewing every memory she had of Agatha. In the nights she relived every agony; she choked on salt water and her skin burned with the flames of hatred.

And still, when she woke, she remained unsure and unable to leave the sanctuary of her chambers.

Her husband, so often in London on business, was not a concern. Lilia, too, though more interested in her than her husband ever was, knew not to trouble her when she was in such a state.

It was on the seventh night, when the moon was at its brightest, and the night terrors had lessened to dreams of pale skin and lupine eyes, that somebody trespassed upon her space.

“Who goes there?” Rio had asked, with some violence, sitting bolt-upright in her bed. Her room, large as it was, was lit only by a small candle she had forgotten to extinguish before she slept.

So shadows loomed large, creating monsters of even the soft armchair she so often could be found reading in by the window.

There was no answer, though Rio knew she was not alone. She sat back against the headboard and reached for the heaviest object she could find; an unread, unopened book of poetry.

“Not sure a verse will scare me off.”

Agatha. Rio would recognise her voice even if she were across a crowded room.

“A whole poem, then?”

“I do not care much for poetry. It is for fickle, fleeting minds.”

“Then might I suggest prose, for a mind so stout as your own.”

“Stout?” Rio smiled at the surprise in Agatha’s tone and wished but she could see her through the darkness, though she knew she must be stood nearby her vanity.

“Quick, then,” she recovered, raising her brows into the darkness.

“You know who I am,” Agatha deduced, the quickness of her mind evident.

“Agatha,” Rio breathed before she could stop herself. “I would recognise you even if I were blind.”

A breath, a candle blown out. Rio saw nothing but blackness, not even the shapes she had been vaguely able to discern earlier.

“And now?”

Agatha was closer, that much was clear. Her voice, still soft as a psalm, was nearby. Rio turned her head, not fearing that which she adored.

“Agatha,” she said again, this time with a smile evident in her voice.

A hand, born of the darkness, found its way to her throat and Rio did not have it in her to be frightened. Because Agatha held her again. Agatha with her warm touch and her pale skin, her blue eyes now bright but black in the darkness as she joined her on the bed.

She felt the squeeze of her hand but felt no fear still.

“You wish to kill me?”

“Why are you not afraid?”

“Why are you in my chambers?” Rio coughed once, Agatha’s hold too tight to ignore entirely.

“You are a Lady, you have many jewels you would not miss should they slip into a maid’s reticule.”

Rio’s brows shot up the very moment she thought she saw Agatha’s gaze drop down. A trick of the light, perhaps. She could not be so lucky that in her misery, Agatha would be interested in anything other than her jewellery.

“Take what you desire,” Rio breathed out, feeling the intermittent squeeze at her throat that seemed to pulse like her own heart.

“You are not frightened?”

Rio smiled, hearing hesitance where she felt nothing but pressure against her windpipe.

“It has taken you a week to build up the courage to rob me, I should believe it would take a lot longer for you to build the courage to murder me also.”

Agatha’s fingers constricted about her throat but Rio said not a word, and breathed not a breath until Agatha allowed her to.

“I have been told that your marriage is not a happy one.”

Her fingers relented and Rio took a deep inhale of beloved breath, exhaling faster than she meant to before sucking in again.

“You have heard correctly indeed.”

“Would he have me hanged for being in his wife’s bed?”

Rio’s brows, which had sunk down again in the interim, raised once more and Rio was momentarily breathless for reasons other than Agatha’s hand.

“You are only on his wife’s bed, not in it.”

Rio found she couldn’t breathe again, entirely Agatha’s doing now. She was allowed breath once again when Agatha had decided upon a response.

“Your finest jewels, where are they kept?”

“The wooden case, to the right of the mirror.” Rio directed, pointing into the darkness and staring up at Agatha as she held her throat still.

“Your favourite,” she said quickly, not even taking the time to choke Rio before speaking once more. “Tell me which is your favourite.”

Rio coughed a little because Agatha’s hand had tightened again, almost as if she were not interested in an answer. But she released and Rio responded: “The garnet.”

It was not true. Rio had no real eye for jewels or fashion of any kind. It was simply the first that sprang to mind, the item that she knew Agatha could fetch a pretty sum for if she were to take it and sell it.

Darkness consumed her the moment Agatha was gone. She heard her seek her wooden jewellery box, heard her extract the contents, close it up. Then the sound of her door, a brief flash of light, the silhouette of the other half of herself, and then she was alone again.

Rio slept not a wink that night. She caressed her own throat where Agatha had held her so fiercely, and Rio had been so willing to leave her life in her hands.

When the sun had returned and the darkness ran from it, Rio surfaced to see what was missing. From her little wooden box everything had been taken. Everything apart from her garnet necklace.

This, unusually, sat in the very centre of the little space, sparkling up at her as if winking. Rio touched it, and felt hope spark in her chest.

This petty thief was still her Agatha. There was softness there, hidden beneath a violent desire for jewels or murder. But she was Agatha all the same.

Rio broke her fast as she usually would. But, unusually, she wore her garnet necklace as she did.

She did not see Agatha again until the late afternoon when tea was served in the parlour and, unusually, Agatha was the one to bring it.

In her pale face Rio saw that she had not slept for she had bags under her eyes and her lupine blue had withered somewhat, and in the evening light they looked almost green.

“My Lady,” Agatha breathed as she curtseyed and placed the small tray of tea and cakes down. Her eyes raised briefly, lingering on Rio’s necklace for but a moment, and then she turned from her.

“Agatha,” Rio called out, standing the moment she did.

The maid paused in her stride and Rio swallowed, feeling the dull ache of a throat not long strangled.

“Life has not been kind… to either of us.”

No life, she thought, had been kind to them. For kindness would be to let them run away together, to be free, to live together with just one another.

“I do believe you are the Lady of this house, ma’am. I believe life has been most kind to you.”

Rio’s jaw flexed, something in her body twitching until she took a step closer and Agatha must have heard the rustle of her skirts because she turned suddenly and far from tired, she instead looked furious.

“Is there something else you need from me, my Lady?” She almost spat the words at Rio’s feet, no longer looking at her with her changeable gaze.

And Rio, unable to ignore the pull or fight the urge to be close any longer, took long, quick strides until she was close again and could raise her fingers up to hold Agatha’s pale face.

Agatha allowed this, looked up as if it were her duty to obey. And Rio realised with a sickening turn of her stomach that it was.

Agatha did not look at her because she wished to, or because Rio’s touch was soft, she instead looked because of her station, because Rio knew what she had done the night before; because one word from Rio and she could be hanged.

Rio was breathless again, as if Agatha’s hand were once more curled around her throat. Her thumb, though she hadn’t meant it to, reached barely the inch it needed to travel to brush the corner of Agatha’s lips and from them she heard a small intake of breath.

“You are not frightened,” Agatha whispered, repeating what she had said to Rio in the darkness of the night.

Rio’s thumb grew bolder and traced an inch along the line of Agatha’s lower lip, so transfixed as she was the by the shape of them. She knew their taste, knew how they could curl around her fingers or part to welcome her tongue. She knew that Agatha could be smart with them, she could wound with but a word or console with a kiss.

And a kiss is what Agatha gave her. Her Agatha. The Agatha she knew to be in the body of this unfortunate maid. Only it was a ghost of a kiss, as Agatha was a ghost of the woman Rio had once met.

Barely there, and hardly acknowledged.

As Rio registered it had happened, it was over. Agatha had turned from her and marched from her presence to leave the parlor altogether, leaving Rio, naturally all alone.

That night, in the darkness of her room, Rio prayed that Agatha would come to her once more. She prayed that she could feel her lips against her own or her hand curled around her throat.

She prayed she might see her eyes, blue or green as they were.

She prayed to know her the way she once had; biblically, ravenously.

And yet Agatha did not come to her.

Nor was she present the next day. And so Rio, in the following hours of darkness, took herself a candle and slipped into the servants quarters where she hoped to find her, to exorcise the hardness of their shared soul.

She wished to feel Agatha against her, soft and willing. And yet she found but an empty bed and a note of scribbled penmanship: The garnet looks well on you.

Agatha had gone from the house, taking her jewels with her.

Rio sought out Lilia, waking her from her bed in such a flurry of shouts and hurried bangs that half the corridor woke too. The servants, unafraid of their kindly mistress, became inquisitive, and soon discovered who was missing.

Agatha was found not two miles from the house, sheltering in an abandoned shack along the dirt road that led to the nearest town. Rio’s jewels were found on her person, and she was hanged in the early morning.

No trial was given to the poor maid and the only mercy shown to her was by the Lady Thanos who visited her within her jail cell.

“I will find you again,” Rio had promised her, desperately reaching for Agatha’s curled up form in the far corner of the stone jail room.

Agatha did not spare her a glance, she did not respond to her words. She only cried silently into the grubby brown dress that the guards had forced upon her. Her hair had been shorn as part of her punishment and humiliation and so her face was hidden by her off-white mobcap as Rio had to be dragged away, wailing with inconsolable grief that Agatha did not respond to and did not understand.

Only Lilia knew. Only Lilia was able to hold her mistress up as Agatha was walked to the gallows. Only Lilia could hold her as blue green lupine eyes moved, mercifully, to Rio’s tigerstone brown as the noose was fitted and the verdict called.

Rio cried out as the floor below Agatha fell and her body dropped and jerked with the rope that held her.

And in the darkness of her room, Rio’s body fell in the same way, hanged as it was from her bedpost.

The witch Lilia Calderu performed a small miracle in the days that followed. She buried her dear friend Rio in an unmarked grave so she would not be disturbed, beneath the grass of a meadow that bloomed flowers of deep purple and there she lay in a deep grave in the arms of the criminal Agatha Harkness.

The universe was grateful for this act of kindness shown to the split soul of her dearest Rio and Agatha and granted Lilia a long life in exchange for it.

But otherwise she mourned for the two who could not yet find their way to one another as she so wished.

Again, she thought solemnly as Mother Nature intervened and the purple flowers bloomed unseasonably, we shall try again.

Darkness, as day turns to night, creeps in so slowly that it is often not noticed until it is too late.

But upon a soul it can happen so fast that the light becomes not a distant memory but a thing entirely forgotten.

The part of a soul that resided in the form of Agatha was now so dark that the universe held to it for longer than she usually might. For decades she willed it to become what it once had been.

Rio looked up at the house that was to be her home for the next year and sighed heavily. It wasn’t that it wasn’t beautiful - a large colonial style manse with a manicured lawn our front - it was just that it was to be shared with one Ms Kale.

“Hah!” Carol barked, making Rio’s smile become a grin instead. “Jen is a pussy cat compared to these girls. But don’t let them get to you. You’ll do just fine.”

Before she’d even fully turned around one of the girls from the front row had hurried to collect them and take them back with her to the large projector in the centre of the raised seats.

“Oh,” Rio uttered in surprise, “thank you.”

Agatha.

Rio found she could not breathe, nor could she look away. The pearls around Agatha’s neck were suddenly thick rope, intended to strangle rather than ornament. Rio’s own neck grew tight with the memory of a tight hold from a pale hand. Her clothes felt wet from sea water and her skin burned from flames of hatred and bigotry.

And yet she still stood. She was still here. And there, too, was Agatha.

“Good morning, ladies,” she managed to say, and though Agatha’s voice sounded the same, she felt her own did not.

There was a smattering of a response and Rio cleared her throat as she stared down at her notes. The words seemed to swirl on the page, joined now by her memories of the woman she had never been able to save.

“Welcome to Botany 101. I assume that-...”

She looked up as she was interrupted: “You assume that we have all read the assigned reading? You would be correct. I would hazard a guess there is nothing you can teach us, Ms Vidal that we do not already know. It is Ms? You are not married?”

More laughter followed Agatha’s words as Rio looked back up at her again. She was sneering now, an expression Rio had never seen her make. And she realised, with a stab of her chest, that what her old friend Lilia had once told her was true; Agatha was suffering even though she was not aware of it.

Because Rio had not been able to save her.

Rio swallowed, “Correct, I am not married. If there is anyone in this class who wishes to leave because they believe they know more than I do, then please do so. Of course I shall have to mark you down as absent and you shall fail the class anyway.”

The laughter had died then, along with Agatha’s sneer. She instead looked irritated, and her gaze dropped from Rio’s for long enough that Rio could catch her breath again.

“Your name, please,” she called out, looking again at the only person whose name she knew.

Agatha shifted a little and raised her chin. “Agatha. Agatha Harkness.”

“Miss Harkness,” Rio said, enjoying the feel of the name rolling around in her mouth and slipping out on her tongue. “Do you wish to teach this class?”

Agatha’s jaw flexed and Rio imagined caressing it until it eased again.

The silence was enough answer for Rio to look back to her notes. Her mouth moved and she called for slides to be moved on, for facts to be shared, for outlines and next steps to be given, but her mind was on the woman who was glaring at her from her seat.

For an hour Rio taught, but wasn’t aware of it. All she could think about was the time that it had taken for her to get to Agatha again. It had taken over two hundred years for their souls to manifest in the same space once again. Would it take longer next time?

No, she thought as she reeled off the attributes of the chrysanthemum pollen, she would make this their last. She would make Agatha love her again and they would live together as they were always meant to.

Death would not take her heart so easily.

When class was ended and the girls in the seats hurried from the hall, some shooting Rio quick smiles and others no looking at her at all, Rio’s gaze followed Agatha down the steps until she was walking straight towards the lectern.

“You won’t last long,” she said as she got close. And Rio’s stomach flipped because it felt like an omen, rather than a mild threat from an angry student.

“Don’t bet on it,” she responded, watching as Agatha’s pursed her lips and turned from her. But at the door she looked back at her and for a moment Rio thought she saw a softening, a curiosity, but then it, and she, were gone.

The hall empty now, Rio collapsed back into the desk behind her and clutched at her heart.

What punishment was this? To have her soul torn in two and be made to suffer without her other half for four lifetimes now? What had she done to deserve a grief so unfathomable?

She remained there, unmoving, for close to an hour. It was only as another class filtered in that she moved from where she had fallen and hastily gathered her things to escape the suffocating halls of Xavier College.

Outside in the cloisters, where she could find a second to be alone, she stared at the sky and thought of the times she had spent with Agatha; allowing herself to feel every joy and agony as each memory came.

By the time she left the grounds that day, she was exhausted from the remembrance of it, and did not leave her bedroom to have dinner with her new housemates, even if Carol did knock on her door to check she didn’t need anything.

She’d had days like this in Eternal Park, days where as mistress of the house she could be of no use to anyone. At the time she had not known the source of her melancholia, but now she knew it was sourced from pools of deep blue set in a pale face.

An evening of suffering alone was all she was allowed this time. For the next day she had her first practical lesson with the class, which meant another chance to see Agatha as she now was.

She chose her outfit more carefully this time. Woolen brown pants in an expensive cut that had cost more than her old rent check, coupled with a deeper brown shirt, open at the neck to show the loose gold chains she had collected from various outdoor markets in San Francisco. She tied her hair in a high pony that was looser about the edges than the fashion of the women’s college but Rio hadn’t been hired for her assimilation to trends; she had been hired for her teaching ability and for her knowledge of her subject.

The botanical laboratory was indicated for her on the map Carol had drawn but again it took Rio a while to find it for her own lack of internal compass.

She arrived a little later than she meant to and stepped into the space to see the women all dressed for a class where they would not be getting dirt under their nails.

“Nothing like dressing for the occasion,” Rio announced, throwing her heavy coat on the side and standing at the top of the long table as she rolled up her sleeves to her elbows.

“We usually observe, rather than… work,” Agatha’s voice sounded again, from five girls along, sneering at Rio once more.

But Rio, having languished all night for that face, simply smiled at her.

“Things will be a little different now I’m here. Come on, ladies. Grab a pot each and some compost and we’re going to learn a little about root structure.”

Some moved sooner than others and Agatha moved last of all. And for a fleeting moment, as Agatha carried her heavy pot far away from her pretty dress, Rio felt a spark of enjoyment in her chest.

They were simply two humans existing together in that moment. The weight of a torn soul and its unreachable counterpart was lifted. And Rio, for the duration of a class spent guiding the hands of squeamish students into the soil-filled pots, was able to relax.

Only, as the bell rang to signal the end of class, the girls hurried away faster than they had the first session, and Rio would have been concerned only she definitely received more “thank yous” and “goodbyes” than she had the previous day.

The other thing that easied her worried mind was that Agatha was not rushing. She had shooed her gaggle of hangers on away and was lifting her pot back to its original place on the side, and Rio, watched her as she lifted a dirt-covered hand to a pink flower nearby.

“The bleeding heart,” she said softly, from her place by the end of the table. Agatha jumped slightly at the sound of her voice and Rio suddenly wondered if she’d thought she was alone.

She knew Agatha would not be as aware of her presence as she was of Agatha’s.

“Lamprocapnos 'Spectabilis”, she continued, briefly biting down on the corner of her lip. “Incredibly poisonous as well as-...”

“Incredibly beautiful,” Agatha whispered, her forefinger caressing the curve of the petal.

Rio didn’t know what to say and so said nothing as she watched Agatha until Agatha obviously grew conscious of her staring and glanced over her shoulder at her.

“Why are you not married?”

Rio raised her brows, the spark of joy in her chest transforming into hope.

“I never met anyone I wanted to be tied to for life.”

Agatha exhaled stiffly through her nose and removed her other hand from the pot where Rio saw the diamond ring she wore covered in dirt.

Unable to stop herself, Rio moved around to stand by her side and reached as cautiously for the ring as Agatha had done for the flower. “You are engaged to be married?”

“Some of us have no choice who we are tied to for life.”

Agatha’s words were bitter and she seemed to thing better of them the moment they’d been spoken. She cleared her throat and pulled her hand away.

“Here,” Rio said quickly, keen for Agatha to stay just a little longer. “Come let me help you clean up. You’ll be late for your next class otherwise.”

She turned to the large basin at the end of the room and heaved the heavy faucet in an anticlockwise turn to get the water to spill from the curved pipe. She fingered through the stream until it was warm enough that she turned and offered her other hand to an Agatha who had not moved from her place by her pot.

“You are from California?”

Rio raised her brows, her hand still raised towards Agatha. “How did you know that?”

“My mother is on the governing board. I know everything about you.” There was a pause and then, “Rio Vidal. Is it Spanish?”

Rio nodded once, thinking back to a time where Spanish was all she spoke.

“¿Lo hablas?” Do you speak it?

Agatha said nothing but shook her head. So she knew a little, then.

“You are a liberal,” Agatha stated, no longer asking. “A communist.”

Rio tilted her head, her hand still raised towards the woman who was yet to move.

“Then you must be a conservative. A fascist?” Her lips twitched at her own joke and Agatha shifted on the spot, her eyes, briefly, dropping to Rio’s hand.

“One word from me and I could have you fired.”

“Then be quiet and let me help you wash your hands. We would not want that diamond ring to lose its sparkle.”

Rio hoped she didn’t sound as desperate as she felt. But all she wanted to do was to hold Agatha’s hand again, in the vague hope that proximity would spark a memory, or even the ghost of one.

To her great surprise and relief, Agatha did move closer, but she ignored Rio’s hand and instead stood beside her, washing her hands beneath the warm water herself as Rio’s hand fell and she instead stood silently and studied her profile.

Agatha was tense, her whole face taught and unfriendly. But she was close, closer than she needed to be to access the stream and Rio wondered if there was at least some attraction there, if not a memory.

“Soap,” Agatha demanded, without looking up, making Rio turn from her briefly to pass over the little round bar that was already sudsy from use. Their fingers met as she handed it over and their eyes above that.

Agatha had softened a little in the meeting of their hands and gazes and Rio’s lips parted as if to say something, though she knew not what.

This Agatha was younger than her, which was a first for them. She was at least eight years younger, with Rio at thirty now and Agatha in her third year of college. But still, young or old or anything in between, she was her Agatha.

“Wash my hands,” Agatha said, her words another demand but her tone softer again. And Rio, still holding the soap as Agatha held it, nodded.

She turned her gaze to their joined fingers and as she did felt a breath against her cheek and turned into it again. Agatha had leant in so close that their noses now nudged and Rio lost all sense of space and time as she felt her breath tickle her parted lips.

Her Agatha was in there, desperate to climb to the surface. And Rio, Rio offered a ladder out as she leant in to reach her where she waited. Their lips brushed, but barely, and then Agatha had pulled away so suddenly that the soap slipped from Rio’s hand and clattered about the basin.

As Agatha hurried from her with her wet hands and her flushed face, Rio couldn’t help but feel sick with fear that she’d already ruined their chances of a happy lifetime.

But Agatha wanted her. She didn’t know her, didn’t know of their love, but she still wanted her. Perhaps it was a fleeting attraction she felt, perhaps it was something inside her telling her that Rio was more than she appeared. It didn’t matter. It only mattered that Agatha had tried to kiss her, that she’d wanted Rio to kiss her back, until she suddenly hadn’t.

Rio didn’t see Agatha for the next two days. But she saw more of Carol, who was home both nights, casually smoking out on the terrace with her, and sharing her wine.

Out of earshot of Jennifer, Rio shared with her her secret, changing a few details like living through many centuries and having a soulmate in her Botany class. Carol was a good listener, and empathetic to her struggle, or what little she knew of it.

And Rio was eased by her friendship. It felt again like there might be some hope for her and Agatha. Afterall, this was the fifties, things were a lot different than they were in the nineteenth century.

On the third day, the day she would finally have Agatha in her class again, Rio came down the stairs to a different atmosphere than she’d left the previous night. Carol was sobbing at the dining table and Jen was smoking out the window.

“I’ve been fired,” Carol explained as Rio took a seat beside her and wrapped her in her arms. “That… bitch of a governess’s daughter published a piece about me in the school paper. They called this morning to say I’d been let go, that they were “deeply sorry and grateful for my years of service”.”

Rio frowned as she was offered the paper sitting on the table before them, and, one arm still around Carol, she read all she needed to read.

SCHOOL NURSE ACCUSED OF INVERSION

By Agatha Harkness

Longtime Xavier College employee, Carol Danvers, has been accused by multiple pupils of sharing her unchristian relationship with her ‘companion’ of many years, ex-Biology professor, Maria Rambeau.

Ms Danvers, who chose to work at an all-female college rather than-...

Rio couldn’t read any more.

She felt sick. She held Carol until her tears dried and only when Jennifer reminded her she had class did she leave her.

It was with a heavy heart that she entered the lecture hall that morning and her first glance up at the risers told her that Agatha knew exactly the damage she’d reaped. Her pale face was downturned, there were bags beneath her blue eyes, and her hands were fists on the small desk before her.

“Good morning,” Rio called, her voice more stern than usual. She didn’t have time for niceties.

The usual student ran to get her slides but she held up a hand and said, “I’ll be running the projector today.”

She marched to the back of the risers and stopped at the large projector there with her box of slides. It was bigger than anything she’d used before but most mechanisms were the same; a switch to turn the light on and a surface for the light to project.

First slide chosen, the shutter echoed around the silent room as the first image came on the screen at the front.

“Can anybody tell me the name of this?”

A hand shot up and Rio pointed at the student who offered up: “Polygonum cuspidatum.”

“Correct,” Rio said, turning back to the image and sparing a quick glance at the back of Agatha’s head where she sat two rows down from where Rio stood.

“Otherwise known as Japanese Knotwood. It’s an invasive species. Can anyone tell me what that means?”

“If introduced to a new environment,” a student to Rio’s right offered, “it can cause harm to the biosystem, the habitat of animals, the-...”

“Thank you,” Rio said, cutting her off. “In short, it is unwelcome. It is insidious. It invades, plants its roots, and is almost impossible to get rid of. Even a tiny spore can cause untold havoc. It finds a crack, a weakness, and it spreads beneath the surface until it's too late. Like gossip.”

A few of the students looked at each other, some looked at Rio. The only one who looked at no one, was Agatha.

Rio’s jaw flexed as she continued on. “One person hears something, they pass it to another. The second person changes a detail, expands on the gossip, until it's transmogrified and made into something evil, when before it had simply been a spore. That spore, innocent enough, has now changed into something that destroys lives.”

All eyes were on her now but Rio only had eyes for the back of Agatha’s head.

She remained silent for a while, willing Agatha to look at her. But she wouldn’t. Rio knew she wouldn’t.

She turned back to the screen and clicked to turn the projector off. The hall felt darker than it had before the projector had been used and Rio took her time in sourcing her next slide.

“Can anybody tell me the name of this flower?”

She did not wait to choose this time and instead said, “Miss Harkness? Agatha? Can you-...?”

“Lupinus perennis,” Agatha said quietly, but loud enough for Rio to hear. “Wild lupine.”

“Yes,” Rio said, the anger and vitriol gone from her voice now. “My favourite. It’s rare to find now but we are in the right area for it. It blooms in pine barrens, and in sandy spots, so can be found close to the water, or the ocean.”

She swallowed as she remembered her home in St. Augustine and the lupines that bloomed there.

“This flower is growing extinct, mostly due to the destruction of its natural habitat. But I think you can all agree with me that it is a beauty, that it should be protected at all costs. That something so precious should not be destroyed.”

She trailed off, unable to continue now her fury had faded and instead she felt hopeless again, unable to do what she knew she needed to; she could not protect Agatha.

Her soul was darker now than even in the lifetime before when she had refused to look at her as Rio had screamed that she would find her again as she sat curled in the small stone jail cell.

They were far from that now, instead in the warm confines of a grand lecture hall, and yet Rio could no more scream her promise to Agatha than she could whisper it. Because she could not force Agatha to remember nor could she beg her to love her when the darkness had made her cruel and the time had made her afraid.

“Class dismissed,” Rio said softly, switching the projector off before anyone could ask anything else.

There were murmurs all around her but she kept her gaze down on the slide atop the light table and remained that way until the hall had emptied of the students it had once contained.

“She deserved it,” came Agatha’s voice, two rows down from where Rio stood.

“I know you don’t believe that.”

“You know nothing about me.”

Rio smiled sadly, lifting her head to turn and look at the woman who had turned to look at her.

“Why did you run from me the other day?”

Agatha turned back around and Rio sat down on the step as she waited for her to speak again.

“You misunderstood,” Agatha stated plainly, still no longer looking at Rio.

“I don’t believe I did.”

“Then you are as inverted as your friend.”

Rio nodded slowly, chewing on the inside of her cheek for a while. “Is that it? Are you afraid of what you are?”

Agatha was on her feet in a second, rounding on Rio with a fury she had not yet seen from her.

“How dare you accuse me of that! I am engaged to be married! I am soon to be a wife and then a mother and I am-...!” She trailed off because Rio hadn’t even flinched.

“I could fire you in an instant.”

“Then do.”

Agatha’s jaw flexed and Rio looked to it, wishing again that she could soothe it with her touch.

“You are not afraid of me?” Agatha asked and Rio smiled because she’d asked her similar before.

“No, mi alma.”

“Soul,” Agatha said slowly, “you call me your soul?”

Rio nodded just once, her forearms resting across her thighs as she looked up at Agatha.

“What do you feel when you look at me?”

Her jaw flexed again, and Rio looked at it as Agatha spoke. “Nothing.”

She raised her gaze once more and found suddenly that Agatha’s lupine eyes were watering, that her lower lip was trembling and Rio could do nothing but stand and reach for her face, holding it in both her hands as she stood on the step above her.

“Do not,” Agatha breathed, her head shaking just once in Rio’s gentle hold, “I cannot. This isn’t… I cannot have this.”

Agatha pulled away from her and Rio fell down a step as she’d reached for her, but there was no stopping her as she went.

“Come to me. Tonight. Here,” Rio said, her last change before Agatha disappeared. “I will wait for you. We can talk. We can… we can run away together.”

It was pointless to say it, but Rio could no longer keep silent about wanting Agatha. And as she whimpered for Agatha’s refusal to look back at her, she felt her heart cease to beat in her chest. Beating went hand in hand with living and a life without Agatha in it was no life at all.

But Agatha had ignored her plea and had left her all alone in the darkness of the hall. Her final act as she’d walked out the door had been to shut all the lights off to plunge Rio into her eternal night.

Rio wandered around campus for the rest of the day. She didn’t eat or drink but she smoked as many cigarettes as she could stomach and watched all the students as they marched between classes and gossiped on the commons.

Agatha would come to her, she was sure of it. She would find her in the hall and sit with her until Rio could bring her back into the light. Surely she would. Surely this would be their last change and their ultimate success.

Because Rio knew the past but she could not fathom a future. The idea that, two hundred years from now, Agatha would still not be hers, made her feel icy cold as she sat huddled in her thick woolen coat with only the flame of her cigarette to warm her frozen hands.

As evening drew over her like a mourning shroud, Rio made her way to the lecture hall and found it to be empty and dark. She sat on the first row of seats for almost three hours before she gave in.

Jennifer would be angry. Carol needed consoling. And Rio’s heart could not handle anymore torment.

It was as she was leaving the grounds of the campus that she saw a light in the middle distance that did not emanate from the heavens above. This light was instead manmade, an electric bulb, casting strange shadows upon the greenhouse where her practical lessons were held.

Rio moved to it, like a moth to a flame. And when she entered the greenhouse she saw, not the usual dirty upon the floor, but instead, sand.

The trail led up to the end of the table where her heart lay sprawled upon the floor, her lupine eyes wide and unseeing as they stared up at Rio’s broken face.

Agatha had chosen permanent darkness rather than to face her fear of living in the light.

The petals of the bleeding heart flower were clutched in her left hand alongside the reason for the sand. She was holding a blue lupine, stolen from the nearby wild shores of the Atlantic coastline.

Beside her, too, was a half empty bottle of Scotch, used to wash down the poisonous seeds of the deadly flower.

Rio sank to her knees beside her and caressed her jaw with a shaking hand. There was no need to soothe it now, Agatha was no longer tense, she no longer felt the fury she once had.

She closed her mouth gently and sealed it with a kiss before she closed the eyes that no longer saw. And because Rio was no good without Agatha, because she would rather face another two hundred years before she could try again, she took great swigs of Scotch and swallowed down enough seeds to be a lethal dose.

She curled around the other half of her and placed the lupine in the centre of Agatha’s chest as she held her. Her sobs filled the glass shed until they died on her tongue as Rio died in Agatha’s unknowing arms.

The universe bowed her head as Rio took her last breath. But, for once, hope was not lost to her. Rio, and the unfathomable ingenuity of her human mind, had sparked an idea. One last attempt, she thought, one more try.

And this time, she would introduce a spore.
