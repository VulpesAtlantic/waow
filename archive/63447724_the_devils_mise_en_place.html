Finally, Rio spoke.

“Come over.”

Agatha blinked, glancing at her. “What?”

“Tonight.” Rio’s voice was steady. Direct. “You should come over.”

Agatha arched a brow, smirking despite herself. She picked up a whole carrot from her station, pointing it at Rio like a knife.

Chapter 1: Order Up!

The kitchen was alive, a breathing, sweating beast of fire and steel. The Friday night rush was in full swing, and Agatha Harkness was at its heart, wielding her knives like a conductor leading a chaotic symphony. Flames flared from the stovetop as she seared a filet, her other hand expertly plating a delicate nest of saffron-infused risotto.

“Order up! Lobster bisque, two filets, one medium-rare, one rare-plus. Three scallops, extra beurre blanc!” Jen’s voice cut through the air as she slapped another ticket onto the rail.

“Chef!” Agatha barked, not missing a beat.

Her brigade moved in sync; each step choreographed to perfection. Pans hissed, knives chopped, steam curled from pots like spirits escaping to the night. It was chaos. Beautiful, perfect chaos.

Then Rio Vidal stormed in.

“We have a problem,” she announced, holding a plate aloft.

Agatha didn’t look up, focused on flipping a duck breast with the precision of a surgeon. “Not now, Vidal.”

“Now.” Rio shoved the plate onto the pass, narrowly missing her elbow. “Table seven says their branzino is overcooked.”

A beat of silence.

Then Agatha turned, eyes like the blade she wielded. “Excuse me?”

Rio crosses her arms. “You heard me. Customer’s complaining. Wants a new one.”

A slow, dangerous smile curled on Agatha’s lips. “You tell table seven that this branzino is cooked to absolute perfection and that if they wanted a boiled filet with the texture of wet cardboard, they should’ve gone to a chain restaurant.”

Rio smirked. “I’ll be sure to phrase it exactly like that.”

“Do it, and I’ll flambe your ass before dessert.”

She held up her hands in mock surrender but didn’t move.

Agatha narrowed her eyes. “What?”

Rio leaned in just slightly, a glint of amusement in her gaze. “You thrive on this madness, don’t you?”

Agatha huffed, turning back to the stove. “Get out of my kitchen, Vidal.”

“Gladly.” Rio said, but Agatha caught the way her lips twitched as she walked off, plate still in hand.

She rolled her eyes, shaking her head as she reached for another ticket. The night was far from over.

--------------------------

Agatha exhales sharply through her nose, shoving the interaction with Rio to the back of her mind. She had no time for distractions—especially not the kind wrapped in a sharp tongue and an infuriating smirk.

“Fire three halibut, one pork chop, and I need that duck resting in the pass, now!” she called out, wiping her brow with the sleeve of her chef’s coat.

“Yes, Chef!” came the chorus of voices around her.

The air was thick with heat, oil, and the unmistakable scent of perfection teetering on the edge of disaster. Agatha moved like a force of nature, checking plates as they hit the pass, adjusting seasoning with a flick of her wrist, plating with precision only years in the industry could forge.

Then, from the corner of her eye, she saw movement. Not a cook, not a runner.

Rio.

Back again.

Agatha’s grip on the plating spoon tightened. “I thought I told you to get out of my kitchen.”

Rio leaned casually against the counter, clearly unfazed. “Yeah, well, Table Seven was very interested in your expert opinion on the branzino. Said they’d love to hear it straight from you.” She grinned. “I think they expect an apology.”

Agatha let out a sharp laugh. “Oh, do they?”

“Mm-hmm.” Rio popped a hip, watching her like a cat toying with a mouse.

Agatha stepped forward, closing the space between them just enough for the heat of the burners to be matched by something else. “Let me make one thing very clear, Vidal—”

“Chef, the duck!”

The urgent voice from the line yanked Agatha’s attention back to the stove. With a practiced sweep, she grabbed the pan and expertly plated the duck breast, slicing it with a precision that made even that simplest movement a display of skill.

She spun back to Rio, wiping the blade of her knife against her towel. “You can tell them that I don’t apologize for knowing what the hell I’m doing.”

Rio hummed, tilting her head. “And If I don’t?”

Agatha set the knife down with deliberate slowness. “Then I hope you like sleeping in the alley with the empty wine crates.”

A slow, infuriating smile stretched across Rio’s lips. “Noted.” She turned on her heel, sauntering toward the exit like she hadn’t just come into the lion’s den for the second time that night.

Agatha exhaled through her nose, shaking her head as she called,” Pick up on Table Eleven!”

The kitchen roared back to life around her, but the irritation still lingered at the back of her mind—tinged with something dangerously close to amusement.

--------------------------

The night wore on, but the pace never slowed. If anything, the kitchen only grew hotter, the orders coming in relentless waves. Agatha was in her element, orchestrating the madness with an iron will and a razor-sharp tongue.

“Behind!” a line cook barked as he maneuvered past with a tray of plated entrees.

“Fire two New York strips, medium and medium-rare! And where the hell is my risotto?”

“Thirty seconds, Chef!”

Agatha nodded, her fingers already moving to adjust the garnish on the halibut dish, but the moment she turned back to the pass—

Rio.

Again.

Agatha inhaled sharply through her nose, fingers flexing as she placed her hands flat against the counter. “You have got to be joking.”

Rio smirked, holding up two plates. “Before you start threatening me with your knives again, let me just say—this one’s not my fault.”

Agatha’s eyes flicked to the food. Two half-eaten plates. Her lips pressed into a thin line. “What now?”

“Table Five,” Rio drawled, setting the plates down. “Said their steaks aren’t cooked to temp. One’s too rare, the other’s too well-done.”

Agatha clenched her jaw so tight she could feel the tension crackle down her spine. “That’s impossible.”

“That’s what I said.”

“And yet, here you are.”

Rio tapped a finger against the plate, looking far too entertained by the whole thing. “I figured you’d want to handle this personally since, you know, you don’t make mistakes.”

Agatha stared at her for a long, silent moment before picking up a fork. With a precise cut she sliced into the steak. Warm, red center, perfect sear. The second one? Textbook medium-rare.

Agatha’s nostrils flared. “They’re perfect.”

“Yup.”

Her gaze snapped to Rio. “Then why the hell are they back here?”

“Because,” Rio said, leaning against the counter with a conspiratorial tilt of her head. “Table Five is full of insufferable snobs who think sending food back makes them look sophisticated.”

Agatha exhaled sharply. “I should march out there and—”

“No need.” Rio straightened, grabbing the plates again. “I’ll handle it.”

Agatha narrowed her eyes. “And how exactly do you plan to do that?”

A mischievous glint flickered in Rio’s gaze. “Let’s just say, by the time I’m done, they’ll be singing your praises and ordering dessert.”

Agatha studied her, searching for any sign of a trick, but Rio only winked before turning back toward the dining room, plates in hand.

For the first time that night, Agatha was left standing still. She didn’t like it.

With a frustrating shake of her head, she turned back to the line. “I need that risotto plated now.”

The kitchen roared back into motion, but a small, unwelcome thought lingered in the back of her mind.

She was getting used to Rio’s interruptions.

And worse?

She didn’t entirely hate them.

The dining room was its own kind of battlefield.

Unlike the high-pressure chaos of the kitchen, the front of house demanded a different type of control—measured smiles, smooth steps, the art of handling impossible people with just enough charm to keep them from losing their tempers.

And Table Five?

They were the kind of people who thought sending back a perfectly cooked steak made them connoisseurs.

Rio approached the table with the same easy confidence she carried everywhere, setting down the plates with practiced ease. “I had Chef personally re-evaluate both dishes,” she said, voice smooth as a vintage Bordeaux. “She was extremely invested in making sure everything was absolutely perfect.”

The older man at the table, a pompous-looking guy in a too-tight suit, sniffed as he picked up his knife. “Well, I certainly hope it’s better this time.”

Rio watched as he cut into the steak—same perfect temperature, same flawless sear. She schooled her expression into one of mild concern as he chewed, waiting for his verdict.

“Well?” one of the women at the table asked, watching him expectantly.

The man hesitated, clearly searching for something to complain about.

Rio struck first.

“I completely understand,” she said with an exaggerated nod, lowering her voice like she was sharing a secret. “It’s a very refined palate that can detect the difference between a standard preparation and something truly exceptional.”

The man straightened slightly. “Exactly.”

“Which is why Chef took a very particular approach with this re-fire.” She tilted her head, as if letting them in on something exclusive. “She used a specialized French technique, adjusting the resting time and temperature control to create a more balanced flavor profile. It’s quite subtle, but I wouldn’t be surprised if you picked up on it.”

The man’s ego practically puffed up before her eyes.

“Yes, yes, I thought I noticed a difference.” He took another bite, nodding appreciatively as his dinner companions followed suit, murmuring their approval.

Rio smiled. “Chef will be thrilled to hear it. And to thank you for your patience, may I offer a recommendation for dessert? Our souffle is impeccable tonight.”

By the time she walked away, Table Five was happily sipping their wine and debating whether to order two desserts instead of one.

She allowed herself a satisfied smirk as she headed back toward the kitchen. Maybe she’d gloat a little.

Just a little.

--------------------------

Agatha barely had a moment to breathe before another ticket was slapped onto the rail.

“Fire one lamb rack, two scallops, and I need the duck mid-rare this time!” she called, her hands moving as fast as her mind. The heat, the noise, the sheer momentum of the kitchen— it was everything she lived for.

And yet, as she checked the sear on the lamb, she caught herself glancing at the dining room doors.

Damn it.

She wasn’t wondering how Rio was handling Table Five. She wasn’t.

She turned back to the stove, adding another pat of butter to the pan, shaking off the distraction.

Then, as if summoned by sheer annoyance, Rio swept back into the kitchen.

Agatha didn’t even look up. “If you’re about to tell me they sent the steaks back again, I’m walking out of this kitchen and setting that entire table on fire.”

Rio set the plates down on the counter. Empty.

“No need,” she said, voice laced with amusement. “Table Five is now convinced they have the most refined palates in the city. They’re also ordering souffles, so you’re welcome.”

Agatha finally turned, eyes narrowing. “What did you do?”

“Nothing dishonest.” Rio held up her hands in mock innocence, though her smirk betrayed her. “Just helped them feel important.”

Agatha crossed her arms. “How?”

“I told them you adjusted the technique specifically for them, played it up like they had the kind of taste buds that could detect an ‘elevated approach.’” Rio shrugged. “They ate it up. Literally.”

Agatha stared at her. Then exhaled, pinching the bridge of her nose. “You stroked their egos.”

“Like a fine, overpriced leather glove.”

Agatha shook her head, but there was something begrudgingly—painfully—impressive about the way Rio handled people. It was infuriating. And effective.

Still, she couldn’t let her get too cocky.

“You’re a menace,” Agatha muttered, turning back to the stove.

“And yet,” Rio said, resting a hip against the counter. “Your kitchen runs a lot smoother when I’m around.”

Agatha grabbed a towel, snapping it in Rio’s direction without looking.

Rio dodged, laughing as she back toward the door. “I’ll be back for those souffles.”

“Get out of my kitchen, Vidal.”

“Miss me already, Chef?”

“Go.”

Rio winked before disappearing through the doors, leaving Agatha gripping the edge of the counter, jaw tight.

Damn her.

Damn that insufferable smirk.

And damn the part of Agatah that didn’t completely hate it.

--------------------------

The kitchen, once a roaring battlefield of fire and motion, had quieted to a low hum. Pots clattered in the dish pit, the last of the cutting boards were being scrubbed own, and the sharp scent of citrus cleaner replaced the heavy aroma of seared meat and butter.

Agatha rolled out her shoulders, exhaustion settling in now that the adrenaline had faded. She surveyed the kitchen like a general after a hard-won war—satisfied, but still on edge. “Good work tonight, “she called to the remaining line cooks as they wrapped up their stations. A few murmured their thanks before heading out, leaving her alone with the fading embers of the night.

Well. Almost alone.

She heard the door swing open behind her.

“Didn’t think you were the sentimental type, Chef,” Rio’s voice teased. “Taking a quiet moment to soak it all in?”

Agatha sighed, already regretting not leaving earlier. “Do you have an actual reason for being here, or are you just here to be a pain in my ass?”

Rio smirked as she strolled in, tying her apron loosely around her waist. “I’m here for my cut of the souffle profits. I figure I did half the work, might as well reap the benefits.”

Agatha gave her a flat look. “You want a commission for lying to a table of pretentious morons?”

“For handling a table of pretentious morons,” Rio corrected. “Which, I might remind you, resulted in extra dessert sales. You should be thanking me.”

Agatha huffed a dry laugh, shaking her head as she reached for a clean plate. “You’re unbelievable.”

“And yet, here you are plating me a souffle.”

“It’s not for you,” Agatha snapped, but even as the words left her mouth, she was dusting the top with powdered sugar, adding a dollop of crème anglaise with ease.

Rio raised an eyebrow, watching her. “Uh-huh.”

Agatha ignored her, setting the plate down on the counter between them. “You want some? Take it. Otherwise, get out of my kitchen.”

Rio picked up a spoon and leaned against the counter, breaking into the souffle with deliberate slowness.

“So,” she said, savoring the bite. “Was it hell without me for those few hours?”

Agatha crossed her arms. “You mean the hours when I wasn’t being pestered by a glorified food courier?”

Rio grinned. “I take that as a yes.”

Agatha rolled her eyes, but she didn’t walk away.

Instead, she watched as Rio took another bite, tilting her head sightly in consideration. “It’s good,” Rio admitted, licking a bit of sugar from her thumb. “Light, fluffy, just the right balance of sweet.”

Agatha smirked, unable to help herself. “Of course it is.”

Rio met her gaze, something unreadable flickering behind those dark eyes. “Cocky.”

“Confident.”

A beat of silence stretched between them—one of those rare, loaded moments where neither of them had another quip ready. The kitchen was quiet now, the chaos of service a distant memory.

Rio’s lips quirked. “Guess I’ll have to keep coming back just to knock you down a peg.”

Agatha smirked. “Bring it on.”

Rio set down her spoon and gave her a lazy salute before turning toward the door. “Night, Chef.”

Agatha didn’t respond right away. She just watched as Rio disappeared through the swinging doors, leaving her alone with a half-eaten souffle and a lingering sense of something she wasn’t ready to name.

She exhaled, shaking her head, picking up the spoon and digging into the souffle.

Damn her.

The dinner rush had hit full force.

Again,

Agatha was in her element, barking orders as the line moved with razor-sharp precision. Pans hissed, flames flared, and the scent of butter, garlic, and seared meat filled the kitchen. The rhythm was perfect—until the doors swung open, and Rio waltzed in like she owned the place.

“Chef,” Rio drawled, already insufferable. “We have a little situation at Table Twelve.”

Agatha didn’t even glance up from the scallops she was plating. “Let me guess. They want to know if we can ‘whip up’ something off menu because their cousin’s new girlfriend has a very specific aversion to flavor?”

Rio smirked. “Close. They sent back the halibut because it’s ‘too fishy.’”

Agatha froze. A slow, dangerous inhale.

“Too fishy,” she repeated.

“That’s what I said,” Rio replied, resting a hip against the counter like she was enjoying this way too much.

Agatha set her tongs down with exaggerated care. “Did you—” she took a moment, pinching the bridge of her nose “— explain to them that halibut is, in fact, a fish?”

“Oh, I did,” Rio said, expression unreadable. “But apparently, they were expecting something ‘lighter.’”

Agatha opened her mouth, then closed it, pure murder flashing through her veins.

“Fine.” She turned, scanning the line for her sous chef. “Alice. Re-fire the halibut. Less lemon, extra butter, tell them it’s a special preparation.”

Rio’s smirk deepened. “So, you do like my tactics.”

Agatha pointed her tongs at her. “I am choosing to tolerate you.”

“Admit it,” Rio said, backing toward the door. “You’d miss me if I wasn’t here.”

Agatha turned back to her station, rolling her eyes so hard it nearly hurt. “The only thing I’d miss is the silence.”

Rio laughed, slipping out of the kitchen, leaving Agatha cursing under her breath—but also, maybe, slightly entertained.

Maybe.

With the halibut situation handled, another wave of orders rolled in, and Agatha slipped right back into her rhythm, her hands moving on instinct, plating, seasoning, calling out tickets.

But amid the chaos, she did something no one noticed.

At least, not yet.

Between firing dishes, she grabbed a small saucepan and pulled a few ingredients from the prep station—ones she hadn’t called for. Shallots, thyme, a splash of sherry. A pinch of something most of her cooks wouldn’t even think to use here.

She worked fast, blending, tasting, adjusting. A few minutes later, she plated a small portion —nothing extravagant, just enough to test. Without a word, she set the dish aside at the far end of the counter, near the expo station. No explanation. No announcement.

She barely thought about why she was doing it.

Service continued, the energy surging and dipping as the night stretched on. Eventually, the dinner rush slowed, giving the staff their first real breath of air.

That’s when Rio walked in.

She wasn’t waltzing in this time—no annoying smirk, no snarky comment about Agatha’s lack of joy. She was just tired, rolling her shoulders, fingers loosening the knot of her apron as she headed for her break.

Agatha caught her gaze before she could leave, giving her a pointed look toward the plate of food.

Rio paused, blinking at the plate Agatha had set aside. “What is it?”

“Just eat, Vidal.”

Rio arched a brow but didn’t argue. She grabbed a fork, twirling a bite into it before taking a taste.

The moment it hit her tongue, something shifted in her expression.

She chewed slowly, considering. Then, after a long moment, she set the fork down.

“…That’s good.”

Agatha smirked. “Obviously.”

Rio shot her a look but didn’t argue. Instead, she took another bite, this time slower. Thoughtful. “It’s…different. I don’t think I’ve had anything like it.”

“That’s the point.” Agatha leaned against the counter. “I’ve been playing with the balance— trying to pull in deeper flavors without weighing it down.”

Rio nodded, still eating. “Well, whatever you’re doing, it’s working.”

Silence stretched between them—not uncomfortable, but not the usual battleground of bickering. It was quieter, more focused.

Agatha cleared her throat. “I’ll refine it.”

Rio smirked. “You just want another excuse to make me taste-test.”

Agatha rolled her eyes. “Please. If I wanted real feedback, I’d ask someone who actually knows food.”

Rio took another bite, grinning. “Sure.”

Agatha didn’t reply. She just watched as Rio finished the plate, barely leaving a trace behind.

Somewhere in the back of her mind, she told herself it was just a test recipe.

Nothing more.

It happened again.

And again.

At first, it was subtle. An extra plate left on the counter at the tail end of service. A dish Agatha had been “testing” that just so happened to be waiting when Rio walked in for her break. No announcement, no invitation—just good left in the same damn spot, as if it had magically appeared there.

Rio noticed, of course.

She didn’t say anything at first. She just ate. No obnoxious comments, no snarky teasing— just a fork scraping the plate, quiet appreciation wrapped in something unspoken.

But by the third or fourth time, she had to call it out.

“You know,” Rio mused one night, leaning against the counter as she picked at a bowl of something Agatha had thrown together—definitely not a test recipe anymore, “for someone who claims they don’t like me, you sure as hell keep feeding me.”

Agatha, who was busy wiping down her station, didn’t look up. “You keep eating it. Maybe you’re the one who likes me.”

Rio snorted. “Oh, I know I like you, Chef.” She took a bite, exaggerated and slow. “I just think it’s funny that you pretend you don’t like me.”

Agatha’s knife hit the cutting board a little harder than necessary. “I’m not pretending anything.”

“Right.” Rio gestured at her meal. “And this is purely professional.”

Agatha finally looked at her, deadpan. “It’s a shift meal.”

Rio held her gaze, an infuriating smirk playing at the corner of her lips. “Sure, sure. A shift meal. Custom-made. Never repeated. Always plated like it’s going out to a VIP.” She stabbed at her food with her fork. “Totally normal kitchen behavior.”

Agatha rolled her eyes, turning back to her station. “You’re insufferable.”

“And you’re devoted to my dining experience,” Rio teased, but she kept eating. And Agatha —without thinking—watched her take every bite.

She shook herself out of it, forcing her focus back to her knives.

It was just a shift meal.

Nothing more.

…Right?

The kitchen was in its usual controlled chaos, a well-oiled machine moving through the second wave of dinner service. Agatha was mid-plating a perfectly seared duck breast when the doors slammed open.

Rio stormed in.

Not her usual cocky saunter, not her infuriating, teasing smirk. No—this was different. Her eyes were wild, her breath uneven, and her hands shoved into her hair like she was barely keeping herself from tearing it out.

Agatha barely had time to register it before Rio was pacing, muttering something sharp under her breath, fury vibrating off her in waves.

Agatha put down her plating tweezers. “What the hell happened?”

Rio let out a sharp, humorless laugh, throwing up her hands. “Oh, you know, just got ripped apart by some self-important, money-dripping jackass at Table Four who apparently thinks I should be grateful to serve his wife’s gluten-free, flavor-free, personality-free meal.” She turned to Agatha, eyes glassy with barely restrained rage. “He called me incompetent because his own dumb ass ordered the wrong thing and then said I was lucky he wasn’t the kind of man to demand I be fired.”

Agatha’s blood ran hot.

She clenched her jaw so tight her teeth ached.

“Who.” It wasn’t a question. It was a demand.

Rio scoffed, shaking her head. “Doesn’t matter. Jen already handled it. Comped their meal, kissed their ass, the usual—”

“Who.” Agatha’s voice was sharper this time, cutting through the air like a blade.

Rio blinked, startled by the force behind it.

Agatha was already untying her apron, tossing it onto the counter. She wasn’t thinking—only moving, only seeing red.

But Rio caught her wrist before she could storm past.

“Agatha.”

The way she said it—firm, low, almost pleading—made Agatha pause.

Rio shook her head. “It’s not worth it.”

Agatha turned to her, furious. “The hell it isn’t. No one talks to my staff like that.”

Rio gave a sharp, bitter smile. “I’m not your staff, Chef.”

Agatha’s stomach did something weird at that, something tight and tangled. But she shoved it down.

She exhaled sharply, trying to cool the fire in her veins. Instead of walking out, she turned back to her station and grabbed a plate.

She worked fast—instinct taking over, hands moving with purpose. Fresh pasta, a sauce she wasn’t thinking too hard about, a dish pulled from muscle memory and something deeper. Within minutes, she set it down on the counter, sliding it toward Rio.

Rio frowned, still catching her breath. “What—?”

“Eat.”

Rio blinked. “I—I’m not hungry—”

“Eat.”

Something in Agatha’s tone left no room for argument.

Rio hesitated, then reached for the fork. She twirled a bite, lifted it to her lips—

And her eyes fluttered shut.

She exhaled slowly, shoulders easing for the first time since she stormed in. She chewed, swallowed, then let out an almost reluctant laugh. “You really do this every time I’m upset?”

Agatha shrugged, wiping down her station like this wasn’t a thing. “You storm in here like a damn hurricane at least once a week. It’s easier than listening to you rant.”

Rio scoffed but took another bite.

And another.

And with every bite, Agatha watched her settle—watched the storm in her shoulders ease, the sharp edges of her anger smooth out.

Finally, Rio set down the fork.

“Thanks, Chef.”

Agatha didn’t respond right away. She just glanced at her, something unreadable in her expression.

Then she turned back to her station. “Get out of my kitchen.”

But Rio didn’t move right away. She just stood there, looking at Agatha a moment longer, something shifting in the air between them.

Then—finally—she walked out.

And Agatha?

She stood there, hands gripping the edge of the counter, jaw tight.

Because if she wasn’t careful, she was going to make something for Rio again.

And this time, it wouldn’t be just a shift meal.

Service was over.

The last plates had gone out, the kitchen was scrubbed down. Most of the staff had already tricked out, leaving only the dedicated few who took their time unwinding after a brutal shift.

Agatha stood at the counter, wiping down her knives with methodical precision. It was a ritual—clean, sharpen, store. She liked the quiet of the kitchen after hours. No yelling, no tickets flying in, no servers barging through with complaints about fish being too fishy.

Of course, just as she thought that—

The doors swung open.

Rio.

This time, she wasn’t carrying plates or relaying another ridiculous customer request. She was back in her usual stance—apron untied, posture loose, that ever-present smirk playing on her lips.

“We’re heading out for a drink,” she announced. “Alice, Billy, a couple of the line cooks. Figured I’d ask before we left.”

Agatha barely looked up. “Not interested.”

Rio huffed a quiet laugh, stepping closer. “Come on, Chef. One drink? You do drink, don’t you? Or do you just absorb nutrients through sheer force of will?”

Agatha shot her a look. “I’m perfectly capable of drinking. I just prefer not to waste my time with bar chatter and whatever cheap liquor Billy insists is top shelf.”

Rio clicked her tongue, crossing her arms. “See, this is why people think you hate fun.”

“I don’t hate fun,” Agatha replied, placing her knife back in its roll with an almost exaggerated sense of calm. “I hate being tired and dealing with idiots at the same time. Drinking with coworkers is both.”

Rio exhaled, tilting her head. “You ever just—relax?”

Agatha gave her a pointed look.

“This is me relaxing.”

Rio studied her for a second, as if trying to find a crack in the armor. When she didn’t, she just let out a breathy chuckle and shook her head.

“Your loss,” she said, turning for the door. “Try not to overwork yourself, Chef.”

She pushed through the swinging doors, disappearing into the front of house.

Agatha let out a breath she hadn’t realized she was holding.

For a moment, she thought about grabbing her coat. Following them. Just one drink. Just to see—

She clenched her jaw.

Instead, she turned back to her knives, methodically finishing her cleaning.

It was better this way.

Two Weeks Later

The kitchen was already heating up, the dinner rush settling in like an incoming storm. Pans sizzled, knives clattered against cutting boards, and the steady hum of coordinated chaos filled the air. Agatha was in her usual position at the pass, calling orders, checking plates, making sure no one on her line slipped up.

Then Jen walked in.

And Agatha knew, immediately, she wasn’t going to like whatever came next.

“Chef.”

Agatha didn’t look up, focusing on plating a delicate filet of branzino. “If this isn’t critical, save it.”

Jen, to her credit, didn’t waver. “It is.”

Agatha finally glanced up. “Then say it and move.”

Jen exhaled. “We have a food critic in the dining room.”

Agatha’s spine straightened. “What?”

“Table Four. Just got sat.”

Agatha’s fingers tightened around her plating tweezers. “Do we know who?”

Tonight had to be perfect.

Reaching for the ingredients.

Starting to cook.

This was going to be fun.

Against her better judgment. Against every instinct that told her to play it safe and stick to the tested, refined, untouchable menu.

Stupid.

Table Four.

Service wasn’t over.

For now.

He loved it.

Didn’t let herself spiral into why this mattered, why she cared.

For once in her life, someone had truly seen her.

And she wasn’t about to let them walk away.

Straight to her.

Agatha exhaled sharply—part irritation, part amusement—and slid onto the stool next to her. “Guess I deserved that.”

The guy beside them made a noise of protest, eyeing Agatha with a mild scowl. “Uh, I was talking to her—”

Agatha didn’t even glance his way.

“She’s not talking to you,” she said flatly.

Rio smirked—just a little—and finally turned to face Agatha fully, resting her chin against her palm. “So. To what do I owe the honor? Didn’t think you were the type to show up anywhere that wasn’t your kitchen.”

And then, meeting Rio’s gaze, she sighed.

“I was wrong,” she admitted. No pretense, no bravado—just quiet, begrudging honesty. “I shouldn’t have snapped at you like that. You saw something I didn’t, and instead of trusting you, I lashed out.”

“Yeah. You were a real piece of work.”

Agatha rolled her eyes. “Are you gonna make me grovel?”

Rio hummed, pretending to think it over. “I mean, it would be fun.”

Agatha groaned. “For you.”

Rio laughed then—an actual laugh, something warm and real—and Agatha felt something loosen in her chest.

A beer was set in front of them.

Agatha picked hers up, holding it up just slightly. “Let me buy your drink.”

Rio eyed her for a moment. Then, with a small, knowing smile, she clinked her bottle against Agatha’s.

“I’ll allow it,” she said, taking a sip.

And just like that, the tension between them shifted—subtle, unspoken, but no longer on the edge of breaking.

Four shots in. Two more beers.

Agatha was buzzed.

Not stumbling, not slurring—but that sweet spot where her shoulders had loosened, her head felt light, and her usual sharp edges were a little softer, a little easier to ignore.

Across from her, Rio was leaning against the bar, animatedly recounting a story, hands moving expressively, her lips curved in that mischievous, knowing way that usually drove Agatha up the wall.

Except—

It was—distracting.

Instead, she was trying very, very hard not to stare.

Rio’s nose crinkled when she laughed, the kind of full-bodied, uninhibited sound that wasn’t forced or polite—just real. Her dark eyes sparkled under the dim bar lights, and her hair, slightly tousled from the long shift, framed her face just right.

She was—

Shit.

Agatha swallowed, looking down at her drink, gripping the bottle like it might save her from wherever the hell her thoughts were trying to drag her.

Because no.

This wasn’t happening.

She did not get distracted by Rio. She did not get caught up in how the woman’s voice sounded warmer when she was tipsy, or how her hands looked when they moved through the air, or how—

“—and then,” Rio continued, laughing, completely unaware of Agatha’s slow spiral, “this asshole has the nerve to tell me I should ‘smile more’ after making me run back and forth for thirty minutes trying to get their ‘perfect’ meal.” She scoffed, shaking her head. “I swear, if I didn’t need this job—”

“You could’ve let me handle them,” Agatha muttered, before she even realized she’d spoken.

Rio blinked, surprised. “What?”

Agatha took a slow sip of her beer, shrugging like she hadn’t just let something slip. “You should’ve sent them back to the kitchen. I’d have made sure they never wanted to eat here again.”

Rio smirked, tilting her head. “Are you saying you’d have defended my honor, Chef?”

“Don’t be ridiculous.” Agatha scoffed. “I’d have defended the integrity of my food.”

Rio laughed again, shaking her head. But this time, there was something different in her expression—something quieter.

She leaned in slightly, elbows on the bar. “You’re staring.”

Agatha felt heat rise to her face. “I am not.”

Rio arched a brow, amused. “Oh? Then what’s that look, huh?”

“A look of someone regretting every decision that led her to this moment,” Agatha deadpanned, tearing her gaze away.

Rio chuckled. “Sure, Chef.”

But as she took another sip of her beer, there was something knowing in the way she smiled.

She should have stood up, muttered some excuse about an early morning, and walked out before she said or did something she couldn’t take back.

But she didn’t.

She stayed.

And worse—she kept watching Rio.

It was the way she leaned in, the way the bar lights traced over the sharp lines of her face, the way her lips curled just so when she smirked, like she knew exactly what she was doing.

Agatha’s grip tightened around her beer bottle.

Rio noticed.

“You know,” she said, voice lower now, smooth and teasing, “for someone who supposedly can’t stand me, you sure are sitting awfully close.”

Agatha’s mouth went dry.

She should have come up with something sharp, something dismissive—should have done a lot of things. But Rio was close enough now that Agatha could smell her—something faintly sweet under the scent of liquor and a long shift, something dangerous—and Agatha, for the first time in a long time, didn’t know what the hell to do.

“So are you.”

Agatha didn’t move.

The air between them shifted, tightening, crackling, like the split second before a knife met flame.

And then—

A phone shoved itself directly between them.

“Holy shit, you guys, the review just dropped!”

Agatha jerked back like she’d been scalded, pulse spiking as one of the servers—Charlotte, she thought hazily—wedged herself right between them, oblivious to whatever the hell had been about to happen.

Rio blinked, a little dazed, but recovered faster. “Wait—already?”

Charlotte grinned. “And it’s good.” She waved the phone in front of them. “No, scratch that —it’s really fucking good.”

Agatha, still reeling, snatched the phone, squinting at the article displayed on the screen.

It took a second for her brain to catch up, to process the words in front of her.

Then—

Shit.

It was good.

Better than good.

The critic wasn’t just impressed—he was glowing.

And at the very end, just as Agatha’s eyes skimmed the last few lines, she saw it:

“A dish with heart, executed to near-perfection. One of the finest meals I’ve had in the city this year.”

Heart.

Agatha’s stomach turned.

She set the phone down carefully, exhaling through her nose, trying to steady herself.

Rio was watching her, something unreadable in her expression.

Charlotte, oblivious, was still grinning. “So. Shots to celebrate?”

Instead, all she could feel was the way her heart was still pounding—

Not from the review.

Not from the success.

But from Rio.

And that was a problem.

It wasn’t her instinct that had won them this.

It had been Rio’s.

She’d been the one to push her, to demand that she take a risk, to challenge her in a way no one else ever had.

And it had worked.

Agatha had spent years believing she knew best, believing no one could tell her how to run her own damn kitchen. But then Rio—loud, insufferable, too-close Rio—had come waltzing in and dared to be right.

The thing she had been avoiding, ignoring, stomping down every time it tried to crawl to the surface.

Had been for a while.

And that—

That was a problem.

Because this? The heat curling in her stomach, the way her fingers itched with the urge to reach out, to pull, to see—this wasn’t some fleeting thing.

This was dangerous.

Didn’t make a scene. Didn’t announce her departure. Just slid off the barstool, dropped a few bills onto the counter, and slipped through the chaos, heading straight for the door.

Before she did something she couldn’t take back.

Before she let herself want something she wasn’t supposed to have.

She had to.

The night air was crisp, biting against her skin, but it wasn’t enough to cool the heat still simmering in her chest. She needed distance, space, something to shake the weight of realization pressing down on her ribs.

But—

“You’re really just gonna leave?”

Agatha’s steps faltered, just for a second, before she forced herself to keep going.

Rio’s boots hit the pavement behind her, quickening to catch up, her arms crossed tight against her chest as the midnight chill seeped through her thin jacket.

Rio scoffed. “Could’ve fooled me.”

She was mad.

At herself.

Couldn’t.

Chapter 2: In The Weeds

One Week Later

It was distance.

It was Agatha’s fault.

She didn’t like the distance.

She didn’t like it at all.

Then—

“Shit—”

A sharp inhale. A flinch.

Agatha was already moving.

“Let me see,” she ordered, voice tight.

“It’s nothing,” Rio muttered, but Agatha ignored her, pulling her toward the sink.

Agatha turned the faucet on, guiding Rio’s hand under the cool water.

“Christ, you’re a stubborn idiot,” she muttered, grabbing a clean towel and pressing it against the wound with a firm hand.

Rio scoffed. “Yeah, because you’re one to talk.”

Agatha should have let it go. Should have focused on wrapping Rio’s hand and getting back to work.

She had missed this.

And that scared her more than anything.

Rio’s breath hitched—just barely—but Agatha caught it.

The water ran cool over Rio’s hand, swirling pink as it carried away the blood. Agatha focused on it, on the way her own fingers held firm, grounding them both in the moment.

“What happened the other night?”

Agatha froze.

Just for a second. Just long enough for Rio to notice.

She swallowed, grabbing the clean towel again and pressing it back against Rio’s palm. “I needed space.”

Rio huffed out a humorless laugh. “That’s bullshit.”

Agatha finally looked up.

It was a mistake.

Because Rio wasn’t just angry. She wasn’t just irritated or hurt or any of the things Agatha had convinced herself she would be.

She looked—

Disappointed.

Like she had expected more from her.

Agatha exhaled slowly, shaking her head. “I don’t have a better answer.”

Rio’s jaw tightened, but she didn’t pull away.

Agatha finished cleaning the wound, grabbed the first-aid kit from the shelf, and began wrapping Rio’s hand with careful, precise movements.

“I’m sorry,” she murmured.

Rio didn’t say anything right away.

Didn’t yank her hand back.

Didn’t accept the apology either.

Then—

“Chef!”

The call snapped through the air, sharp and urgent.

Agatha closed her eyes for a beat, securing the last piece of bandage before finally releasing Rio’s hand.

“Go get a clean towel,” she said quietly. “Keep the pressure light.”

Then she turned.

And just like that, the moment ended.

Easier than thinking about the way Rio’s voice had sounded when she asked what happened. Easier than thinking about the way she’d looked at Agatha like she was expecting—hoping— for a better answer.

Rio stepped into the kitchen.

No plate in hand.

Not on break.

She was just there.

Rio stopped.

Turned back, something flickering across her face, unreadable.

Agatha let out a breath.

Shit.

No going back now.

----------------------

The kitchen had finally gone quiet.

Until she stepped into the alley.

And saw her.

Standing there like an idiot, gripping her bag just a little too tightly, realizing—

Agatha followed.

Not uncomfortable, exactly.
