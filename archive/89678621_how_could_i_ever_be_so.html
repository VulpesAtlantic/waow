how could i ever be so dumb to believe i'd be the one you would adore

If there’s one thing Rio is always willing to do, it’s wait for Agatha Harkness.

They’ve been best friends for fifteen years, and Rio has never known Agatha to be on time to a damn thing. Doctors have claimed ADHD, Agatha has claimed no one is worth her time; Rio knows it’s somewhere in the middle. She knows this because whenever Agatha is late to something having to do with her – like this dinner catch-up date, for example – she rushes in in the most apologetic way she can muster.

“Rio, I’m so sorry,” Agatha huffs, slamming her overstuffed tote bag on the ground and collapsing into her chair. “I had meetings all morning and press all afternoon. It’s been a whirlwind.”

“You don’t have to apologize.” Rio might sound magnanimous to others, but in all honesty she really didn’t mind the quiet time alone with a glass of wine. She’s been people watching – two tables over, there’s a girl who can’t stop talking about marriage and a guy who looks ready to bolt. Suckers.

The waiter comes by again now that Agatha’s here to catch her drink order, and Rio places an order for toasted sourdough since she knows Agatha likes the bread here. She gets a soft smile flashed at her in return which warms her chest.

“So,” she starts, once it seems like Agatha’s calmed down some, “pre-release is going well?”

Agatha sighs heavily. “So far. We’ll see how Tuesday goes, but Lilia is predicting bestseller list for at least a couple of weeks.” She swirls her wine around in her glass. “If anything, I’m… nervous about how the audience will react.”

Agatha is the only person Rio personally knows making good use of her English degree. She started writing novels when they were in college together, and a couple of months before graduation one of her intricate crime thrillers got a positive read from a small-time publisher who saw big potential. Lilia instantly became something of a mother figure to Agatha - who won’t admit it, but desperately needed one – and has advocated for her strongly; now on her ninth book in eleven years, Agatha is the figurehead of Lilia’s publishing house and somewhat of a household name among bookish people who really like murder.

The newest book, Rio has been told, is a bit of a departure. Agatha’s been known for writing crime fiction her whole career, and frankly no one knows more about murder or how to set up a mystery than Rio’s best friend. Even back in Writing 101 in a windowless classroom in the depths of Tolley Hall, Agatha was something of a genius. But she’d never pivoted from her dark, troubled protagonists and bloody crime scenes and sexy femme fatales.

Until now.

Agatha Harkness has written a romance.

Rio hasn’t gotten a look at the manuscript yet – which isn’t uncommon, as Agatha is intensely private with her work until she feels it’s at its absolute best. It’s not like there isn’t sex in any of her previous books, but there isn’t really romance, at least not the touchy-feely kind that is purportedly the center of this book. Agatha hasn’t told Rio much about it, just that it’s different, Lilia thinks it could be her biggest one yet, and she’s nervous. Which is very, very hard to get Agatha Harkness to admit to.

“I’m sure they’re going to love it,” Rio says honestly, reaching across the table to grab Agatha’s hand. Agatha looks down at where their fingers link with apprehension. “Everything you write is fantastic. Your readers know that.” Agatha blushes. “And, there are so many people who don’t read thrillers – this could open you up to a whole new audience.”

“Or completely alienate the one I already have,” Agatha grumbles, chin in hand. Something about the way she says it, or maybe the way her eyes flick to Rio’s for a brief moment, feels pointed. But before Rio can question it, Agatha is snapping out of it and shaking it out. “Okay. I’m tired of talking about me-”

“Which never happens,” Rio points out good-naturedly.

“-so it’s time for you to dish about the date with Angie.” Agatha raises her eyebrows around a mouthful of wine.

Rio leans back, eyes to the ceiling, and blows out a heavy breath. “It was a non-starter.”

“Non-starter as in awful, or non-starter as in…”

“As in we did not actually go on the date. Yes. That’s correct.”

Agatha slams her wine glass on the table and leans forward, staring at Rio intently. “Why not.”

It takes Rio a second to come up with an acceptable answer, eyes flitting across Agatha’s face and searching the room around them for an escape. “I guess I just… had a feeling it wasn’t going to work out.”

Rio’s snarky-yet-eager coworker, Angie, has been trying to get in her pants for months. It started with making her bouquets out of the extra cuttings at the nursery, spiraled into bringing her coffee almost every morning, and eventually landed with a thud at “let’s go on a date.” Rio hemmed and hawed about scheduling conflicts for as long as she could before very politely indicating to Angie that she might, perhaps, not be all that interested in a relationship, since she might, perhaps, be very interested in someone else.

Angie had taken that with the grace of a gazelle and moved on. Rio has been sick to her stomach for about three days now.

“That’s good,” Agatha says obliquely, hard to read. “It’s good to follow your gut on things.”

“It just didn’t feel like she was… what I was holding out for, you know?”

“That’s good,” Agatha repeats.

Rio’s known among their friends for being somewhat of a romantic recluse. She’s had one serious girlfriend and doesn’t really get down for the whole casual hookup thing. Part of it is her genuine happiness at being by herself, and part of it is that the one person she wants more than anything in the world, the person she’s loved for a decade and a half, has never shown an ounce of interest in her whatsoever.

Rio is pretty sure Agatha is the love of her life. Agatha is the only person she feels safe with, the only person who likes all the idiosyncrasies that are Rio. Agatha listens to Rio’s rants about pollination patterns and genuinely likes watching her play video games while she lays on the couch with her head in Rio’s lap. Agatha has never laughed at Rio for missing a social cue or getting a little too excited about something. Agatha listens and cares. She doesn’t make Rio itchy when she touches her. She’s the most beautiful person Rio has ever met in her life, and she deserves endless amounts of love that Rio knows she could easily give, if only Agatha wanted that kind of love from her.

But Agatha is the exact opposite of Rio in many ways, and that includes being a serial haver of one-night stands. Rio doesn’t really mind except for the jealous churning in her gut whenever Agatha mentions another late-night date, but it thankfully doesn’t come up the way it used to when they were roommates right out of college. Nowadays, Rio can put Agatha’s location pin (lingering on another downtown lesbian club) out of her mind and go to sleep dreaming of Agatha’s soft hair in her face and tiny snores in her ear.

She would say something. She has, in fact, come very close to saying something many times. But the fact of the matter is, Agatha is too important to Rio to lose. Maybe it’s selfish. She’s glad to admit it. But if telling Agatha how much she really loves her means Agatha is no longer a part of her life, then that’s not a bargain she’s willing to take. No matter how much it hurts.

So she turns down girls that aren’t Agatha and pretends like the person she’s waiting for isn’t sitting across from her, shoving sourdough and butter into her mouth.

“What?” Agatha asks, knowing Rio isn’t judging her but keeping up with the act anyway.

“Nothing,” Rio sighs, and takes another sip of wine.

---

On Tuesday morning, Rio queues up outside her favorite local bookstore with everyone else and waits to get her very own copy of Agatha’s latest book.

Normally, Agatha sends her advance copies with signatures and little messages (“don’t sell it,” she’ll say, and Rio will say “I would never”) for Rio to read on day one, but Agatha must really be nervous about the genre switch-up, because none of their friends got an ARC reserved. Rio doesn’t mind. In fact, it’s kind of cool to wait in line with the rest of Agatha’s fans and see how excited people get about her. It makes Rio kind of stupidly proud.

“I heard it’s, like, totally different,” a pack of girls in front of Rio are whispering. “Apparently it’s this, like, torrid lesbian love affair.”

“About time,” one of the girls sighs. “I wanna know what she fantasizes about.”

Rio makes a face. That’s not for them to know.

“I’m so excited,” another one of the girls says. “We so need solid lesbian representation from an author who actually knows how to write.”

Rio smiles to herself. Agatha’s not exactly the activism kind, but she cares deeply about the community around her and donates a hefty chunk of her book profits to queer causes. The very idea of this book being representation for the young queer girls in her audience is going to make her very happy.

Rio takes out her phone and texts Agatha. Heard there’s some girls kissing in this one. The baby dykes are getting excited.

It takes Agatha less than a minute to respond. I hope they get something out of it. A beat, then Even if they’re not the intended audience.

Rio snorts. What, you wrote the lesbian romance for the straight guys?

No, Agatha says, but doesn’t elaborate. Rio shrugs and puts her phone away – release days are usually busy for Agatha, and she might not have time to text right now.

The store ordered twenty-four copies of the book, and Rio is the nineteenth person in line to be handed her fresh hardback. It’s delightfully solid in her hands (Rio’s always liked physical books), with a glossy cover and beautiful black lettering. The book is called The Road, and the cover features a top-down view of an expanse of green grass with a prominent cluster of vibrant purple, yellow, and white flowers in the center. It’s understated and beautiful.

Rio texts Agatha a selfie of her and the book pressed to her face outside the store. Look who’s coming home with me!

Agatha texts back just as Rio has hopped on the bus towards her apartment. I’m really excited to hear what you think of it. In a separate text, she sends You, specifically.

Haha, because I’m always your harshest critic

It takes until Rio has gotten home and is kicking off her shoes for Agatha to text: You might be, after this one.

Which is not ominous at all, in Rio’s personal opinion.

---

It’s not that Rio deliberately took the day off to read her best friend’s new book. It’s just that she happened to have a choice of whether to work on Tuesday or Wednesday this week and she happened to pick Wednesday because, well, Tuesdays are historically known as better for relaxation. Or something.

Anyway, Rio reads all of The Road in a day.

It is an experience.

At first, she can’t put it down because it’s so good. Agatha’s heavy, rhythmic prose, so well-tailored to describing a shootout or a brutal stabbing, works remarkably well with the yearning and tension in the story. As always, her vocabulary is impeccable and her sense of style is so clear.

But as Rio turns pages, certain details start to feel oddly familiar. In a slightly terrifying way.

The book is about an artist named Agnes who, nearing her 40s, is starting to feel adrift in her love life. She harbors a relentless crush on her former college roommate, who she’s still close with, until a wrong number call accidentally reveals her feelings. She runs from the aftermath, and throws herself into planning her abusive mother’s funeral, refusing to acknowledge what’s happened, until her friend finally confronts her about their mutual feelings. In the end, the artist is able to accept love, even if she can’t fully accept herself.

It’s both tragic and sweet and it’s also disturbingly familiar.

Rio and Agatha were college roommates, and they’ve stayed friends for years. Rio works with plants, just like the love interest in the book, who is a florist – a florist named River. A muscular florist with big brown eyes and short, dark hair. The florist has a tattoo of an azalea flower on her upper arm. (Rio has to consciously avoid looking at her own arm, where a matching tattoo has lived since her early twenties.) The artist has unruly brown hair and a nose she hates, but the florist insists is beautiful. They have a group of queer friends, most of whom have been with them since college. The artist has a successful career but a tumultuous love life; the florist is content with her job, content being alone, and always makes time for the artist. They have a standing weekly dinner date that the artist refuses to read into. They go to the beach on the weekends, and the artist always stays in the lounger and reads while the florist goes splashing around in the water, coming back and getting the artist wet. And most importantly, Agatha’s mother died about a year ago, and it was the one moment in her life when Agatha wouldn’t let Rio hold her up.

At the end of the book, after being ignored by the artist for almost three months, the florist finds her at a gallery opening and confesses her undying love. She needs the artist to know that she will always, always love her. The artist breaks down sobbing right there and kisses the florist in front of everyone. They walk home together in the rain.

Rio reads all 400 pages of the book in one go. She sets it down and stares at the wall for about thirty minutes. Then she grabs a piece of paper and a pen and cracks it open again, making a list of everything that reminds her of her and Agatha.

By the time she’s finished the book again and the clock has switched over past midnight, Rio is absolutely certain: this book is about her and Agatha. Or at least, heavily based on them.

It’s not terribly uncommon for details of Agatha’s real life to creep into her writing, but usually in small ways only her friends would be able to pick out, as sort of inside jokes. This is something else entirely. And there’s too much here – more than Rio can even take in with her spiraling brain – for it to not be intentional.

What this means – what this means is a fully incomprehensible idea for Rio. Something she genuinely can’t even fathom. Watching her own feelings – her own habits reflected back at her so clearly in writing is somewhat akin to waking up from a horrible dream only to realize it was a memory. Agatha has noticed the way Rio always compliments her hair when she’s having a bad day. She knows that Rio loves her nose. She’s picked up on the fact that Rio will never let a day go by without texting her a red heart, just to remind her someone loves her.

(Also the fact that the florist has a penchant for roguelike video games, a reptile fixation, and is a clean freak everywhere except her own bedroom.)

All of these things stare back at Rio from the page in black Baskerville type that before long starts swimming in front of her eyes.

Agatha knows that Rio is in love with her.

It’s what the book is saying. There’s no other way to interpret it.

Rio leaves The Road on her kitchen table and paces for nearly an hour, glancing at it every so often like it might grow legs and start to perform a mocking dance at her. How long has Agatha known that Rio is in love with her? Why hasn’t she said anything? Drawn any boundaries? Why would she share she knows covertly, through a book of all things, instead of just talking to Rio? Is it because she can’t bring herself to talk about it out loud? Oh god, does Agatha hate the idea of Rio loving her, and is trying to humiliate her as punishment? There’s no way anyone other than their close friends would be able to draw the connections, but that would be enough to send Rio screaming for the hills anyway. Hell, she’s already contemplating a spontaneous move to Alaska. Do they have books in Alaska? Fuck.

Rio worries all through the night and into the early morning. She makes herself sick enough that she calls out of work, feigning a stomach bug. The only conclusion she can come to, after reading the book a third time and cocooning herself in bed all of Wednesday, is that Agatha definitely knows that Rio is in love with her, and she really wants Rio to know that she knows.

What Rio can’t for the life of her figure out is why.

And until she does, she doesn’t think she’s going to be able to say one normal word to Agatha. Probably ever again.

---

Rio manages to avoid Agatha for four entire days. No texts, no calls – at one point on Thursday, Agatha asks what she thought of the book, and she agonizes over a response before finally putting her phone down in her office and ignoring it all day. Best case scenario, Agatha wants to have a conversation that is making Rio sweaty just thinking about; worst case scenario, she’s waiting for Rio to admit she read it so she can mock her in real time.

Meanwhile, the book takes off like a wildfire. Rio catches glimpses of Agatha on talk shows, podcasts, ads, and the rest, talking about The Road. She mutes any clips as soon as they start playing and stops opening Twitter. She genuinely can’t handle it.

The world seems to love The Road. Critics call it a modern romantic masterpiece, and Agatha’s horde of fans – except the stupid straight men – hail her as the lesbian icon they always knew she was. In any other world, Rio would be beyond proud – and a part of her, the stubborn part that will always, always love Agatha, is. But she still can’t look at the book’s cover without getting sick to her stomach.

Rio is thankfully spared from agonizing over what to do about their weekly Sunday dinner by the fact that their friends are hosting a party for Agatha’s book release. Unfortunately, Rio’s presence is expected at said party. She might be able to get away with staying home and pretending she’s sick, but she knows Jen would see through that excuse immediately and besides, it would only raise more suspicion. Best to pretend, she decides, like everything is normal – and if she sees Agatha there, which she surely will, to beeline in the other direction.

Rio shows up at the party late with a bottle of wine. Rosé, which is Jen’s favorite. She intends for it to be an apology for ignoring her friends all week. It appears it is not needed.

When Jen opens the door to find Rio on the other side of it, she frowns. “You look like shit.”

“Thanks.” Rio hands over the wine. “Is it, like, winding down, or…?”

“No, it’s just kicking off,” Jen deadpans. “But thanks for asking.”

Rio glances over Jen’s shoulder, but doesn’t see waves of chestnut hair or hear a distinct laugh. Jen must take pity on her and steps forward into the hall, pulling the door mostly shut behind her. “She’s here, but she’s been swarmed by people all night.”

“Have you read it?” Rio swallows roughly.

Slowly, Jen nods. “Yeah. I did.”

“It’s…”

“Yeah.” Jen drums her fingers on the bottle. “It’s a really good book.”

“Yeah, it is.” Rio’s voice sounds a little wet, and she hates herself for it.

“Just come in,” Jen says finally, kicking the door back open with her foot. “Everything’s going to be okay.”

“How the hell do you know that?”

“I just have a feeling.” The weird, secret smile Jen gives her is not encouraging, but Rio steels herself and steps into the space anyway.

Jen parties always remind Rio of college – loud, dark, crowded, sweaty. Easy to hide in. She arms herself with a mixed drink that is strong, but not as strong as she really wants, and posts up in a corner, where Alice finds her not a moment later.

“Hey, dude.” They bump cups. Rio readies herself for Alice to say something about the book, but Alice – as always – knows better, and just leans against the wall beside Rio. “How’s business been?”

Alice keeps her in benign conversation for a while, until Rio’s nerves start to calm. There’s been no sign of Agatha, not through the throngs of people, and even though Rio is still itching to go home at least she hasn’t had to face her worst fears.

Until Jen calls Alice away for a pool game against Kara and Lena, and the crowd parts just enough to reveal Agatha, reclined on one of Jen’s vintage leather couches with Lilia perched beside her, looking…

Absolutely miserable.

Lilia, ever the mother, is leaning in to whisper something in Agatha’s ear, but she’s focused on her drink. Rio watches as she closes her eyes and takes a long, deep breath. When she opens them, she’s looking right at Rio across the room. Their eyes locked on each other. They can’t let go.

It feels like an eternity that Agatha just stares at Rio, lips parted. It feels like something out of a romance movie. But it’s not – it’s not The Road, Rio reminds herself, it’s just real life – and real life fucking sucks. Agatha abruptly stands up and storms away, into the kitchen and out of Rio’s sight. Lilia catches sight of her across the room. She smiles apologetically, but makes to follow Agatha, leaving Rio alone.

Rio downs the rest of her drink and mills through the party for a few minutes, trying to catch on to the flashes of Agatha’s hair, trying to make eye contact just one more time. But Agatha evades her, and eventually she gives up and slips quietly out the door of Jen’s apartment without anyone noticing.

It’s probably for the best if Agatha feels bad about what she did. At least Rio isn’t suffering alone.

---

The Road stays atop the bestseller list for a whole month, double Lilia’s two-week prediction, and shows no signs of disappearing. First Agatha’s dedicated fans, many of them queer women themselves, embrace it – then her wider fanbase, then romance readers, then the public at large. It’s at the front of every bookstore, on every library’s new reading list. Copies are being bought up faster than they can be printed and Rio starts to feel like she should sell hers and turn a decent profit. It would get the damn thing out of her house.

But also, she can’t stop looking at it, face down on the coffee table where she left it after reading it the fourth – no, fifth time. She’s gone from hating it to being oddly comforted by it. For the five or six hours it takes her to read it through, she can exist in a world where Agatha really does love her. Where things work out for them, and life is so beautiful and perfect that Rio can wake up one day and kiss Agatha just because she wants to. It becomes a horrible, dark fantasy, and very quickly one that Rio would rather be living in instead of her miserable day-to-day.

She goes from avoiding Agatha’s internet presence like the plague to being super invested. She watches every interview she can get her hands on and trolls forums and internet communities dedicated to the book that quickly pop up. She listens over and over again to Agatha describe the writing process – intensely emotional – and the reason she wrote the book – something personal that she refuses to dive deep into, no matter who she’s talking to.

“I never intended to completely switch genres,” she says on one podcast that Rio is listening to while doing maintenance at work, “and I plan on writing more crime fiction eventually. But this story wouldn’t leave me. In a lot of ways, I was living it every day. I couldn’t escape, and so the only way I could process it was through writing. And it turned into what is, thankfully, a wonderful book.”

“So do you consider yourself similar to the main character?” the podcast host asks.

Agatha hesitates. “In some ways, sure, I suppose. And there is definitely a lot of the story that is influenced by my personal life. But I wouldn’t say I’m a carbon copy of Agnes.” She takes a deep breath. “In a lot of ways, she’s sort of like an aspirational figure to me.”

Right, Rio thinks bitterly, the version of you that could possibly fall in love with me.

As a product of these interviews, and all of Agatha’s hinting, the internet starts spiraling worse than Swifties in the comments of a cryptic Instagram post. Agatha’s social media is pruned for clues about women in her life. People start theorizing that she might be engaged, or even married already – that The Road is a secret love letter to someone she’s kept hidden.

Little do they fucking know. At least Rio keeps her own social media presence very minimal, so they’re not likely to find her anytime soon – since the tattoo on her arm would be a dead giveaway.

Angie asks her one day if she’s read The Road. “It’s so dreamy,” she sighs, dumping roses on the edge of rot into a trash bag. “The love confession at the end is everything I’ve ever wanted.”

“Well, it’s just fiction,” Rio grits out. “Real life doesn’t usually work out like that.”

---

Rio hasn’t seen – or spoken – to Agatha in a full month when Jen and Alice ask her out to dinner. The first thing she says to them upon sitting down at a corner table in their favorite Koren BBQ place is, “so you picked me in the divorce?”

“You’re admitting you’re married?” Alice elbows Jen for the snarky remark.

“What she means,” Alice sighs, “is that no one’s picking anyone. We just missed you, that’s all.”

Rio sits with that for a moment. “You don’t think I’m awful, even after reading it?”

Alice and Jen exchange a look. “Why would we?” Jen asks.

“I mean…” Rio sighs, poking at the meat sizzling in front of her just for something to do with her hands. “The book is just… It feels like she’s yelling at me. Like she’s pointing out how… suffocating I am and trying to send me some kind of message.”

The table is quiet for a beat. Jen is making a face that Rio cannot possibly interpret – somewhere between confused and very, very amused.

“Do you think…” Alice clears her throat and starts again. “Sorry, when you read that book, did you think Agatha was mad at you?”

In a very small voice, Rio says, “I thought she hated me.”

Jen makes a sound like a goat being kicked in the stomach. Rio thinks at first she’s choking, then realizes it was supposed to be a laugh.

“Rio,” Jen says in a choked voice, while Alice tries to swat at her to be normal. “What possibly gave you the impression that she hated you?”

“I’ve spent so long loving her from afar, I guess I just… forgot what it looked like from the outside.” Saying it out loud is both freeing and desperately terrifying, but Alice and Jen nod along as if nothing about this is new to them. Rio wonders exactly how many people had noticed her feelings for Agatha before The Road came out. “She wrote all these things about me – I mean, not me, but it’s basically me – and I just seemed so suffocating. And too much for her. I don’t know why she would do it unless she was, you know, trying to send me a message. So I backed off.”

Alice and Jen nod as they process this. “Had you ever considered that Agatha wrote all that because she was trying to tell you something about her?” Jen asks, not unlike a teacher asking a toddler to solve a complicated math equation. “About herself, not about you?”

“I…” In reality, no, Rio had not thought of that. Probably because after over a decade, the concept of Agatha loving her in any way more than friends had simply fled her mind entirely. “What did you guys think, when you read it?”

“That Agatha picked a very public method of confessing her love for you,” Alice says bluntly, chewing on a piece of steak.

Rio sits with that for a second. Mentally combs through the pages of The Road for evidence to back up their claim.

“Whatever it means,” Jen points out, “you have to talk to her about it. Neither of you are doing well just ignoring each other.”

“She… she’s not okay?”

Jen reaches across the table to rub Rio’s hand. “She puts on a brave face for the public, but she’s been awful, Rio. She’s been sad.”

After dinner, Rio runs home, sits down on the couch, and reads The Road for the sixth time. She had been so focused on the way she was depicted she had never even thought about it the opposite way – how Agatha was depicting herself. She has a notebook out again, this time paying attention to the things that Agatha says about River – about her.

Agnes talks about how River has held her up for as long as she’s known her. How she’s been a companion and a friend and a partner in crime whenever Agnes needed it most. She mentions how River was the first person to say “I love you” to her, because it came so easily, even though Agnes didn’t know people could really feel that way about each other. She spends a whole page describing how River looks right when she wakes up, the shape of her eyes and the curve of her nose. She fantasizes about kissing River, about being held by her, about falling asleep in the same bed. Agnes tells River she’s the most beautiful person she’s ever met, not just outside, but inside – that she’s true, thoughtful, caring, funny, and individual, so comfortable being herself it makes Agnes jealous. She writes letters to River in her head she’s too afraid to let her hear.

Agatha lays it all out on the page, and for the first time, Rio starts to wonder if the fiction is all that fictional.

She feels so fucking stupid.

---

Rio falls asleep on the couch with The Road splayed over her face. She wakes up to a text from Agatha – noticeable from the Darth Vader music Rio put in years ago as a joke

There’s no words in the text – just a link to a webpage advertising a first-come, first-served talk and signing for The Road at the same bookstore where Rio first picked up her copy. It starts in an hour.

Rio is up and getting dressed within seconds. This is the first she’s heard from Agatha since she first read the book, and cryptic as it is, it’s contact. It’s something.
