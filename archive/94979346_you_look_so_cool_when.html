***

When the FBI’s Behavioral Analysis Unit is summoned to New Jersey to investigate a series of violent murders, Special Agent Rio Vidal becomes fascinated by the neighbor of one of the victims, who seems to have all the information they need to solve the case hidden somewhere behind her mischievous smile. Even as the team closes in on the unsub, Rio finds herself drawn further into Agatha's orbit - but she's becoming less and less sure the woman is all she pretends to be.

Chapter 1: set a fire in my head

It's Friday night, and Rio has her briefcase packed with case files and her jacket halfway on when Jen sweeps by her desk and dashes all hopes of a quiet weekend at home in her tiny apartment near Quantico. "Got a new one. Three so far, all in New Jersey. Local PD wants us ASAP."

Rio groans and throws her jacket back down onto her desk, following Jen up the stairs into the briefing room. Alice and Darcy are already there, and Billy trails in with Lilia shortly thereafter. Darcy passes out copies of the case file, then stands in front of the T.V. screen while the rest of them settle in for the presentation.

"Three men in New Jersey in three weeks," the faces of the three men in question pop up on the screen, "all mutilated in the same way before being shot once at point blank in the head and left near police stations around Newark. No witnesses."

"Mutilated how?" asks Alice.

Darcy's face twists in displeasure, visibly cringing as she clicks the remote to change slides, and photos from the coroner's report appear. "Oh, my friends, in the way all men are most afraid of."

"They were castrated?" asks Rio.

"Uh, actually, they were emasculated," Billy pipes up. "Since the, um, penis was cut off as well."

Alice sucks a sympathetic breath in through her teeth.

"What about victimology?" asks Lilia, bringing them back to the task at hand. Darcy looks relieved to be able to click to the next slide.

"The first victim was Charles Monroe, 47. He was a family court lawyer who mostly did custody cases. He didn't come home work three weeks ago and his body was found the next morning. His wife didn't even file a missing person's report. She said he usually stayed at work late and she didn't think anything of it until she woke up and he still wasn't home. Local PD found the body in the park across the street from the sixth precinct."

Rio skims over the information they have on Charles in the file. It looks like there had been ligature marks around the wrists and ankles.

Darcy clicks her remote again. "Next victim, George Cramer, 29, found a week later. He was a school counselor in Westview, which is just outside Newark. He lived alone, but he did have a girlfriend who says he never showed to a date the night he was killed. He was left in front of the fourth precinct."

The coroner's report for George looks identical to the one for Charles - both tied up, tortured, and killed by a single .35 caliber bullet to the base of the skill.

The third victim proves to be similar. Rio notes that all three victims are white. "The last guy was hidden in a dumpster near the fifth precint instead of left out in the open, so he was only found a couple of hours ago. This," Darcy clicks forwards, "is Stephen Halpert, 57-year-old real estate mogul and something of a local celebrity. He owns probably half of Westview. He was actually reported missing, when his wife noticed this morning that he never came back from a late dinner with friends."

"Westview, where George Cramer worked?" says Jen. "Does Charles Monroe have any ties back to Westview?"

Darcy shakes her head. "Not that I could find, but I'll keep looking. No one can hide their secrets from me for very long. One more thing," she flips to the final slide. "When local PD found the bodies, they all had their eyes covered by a blindfold."

"Hmm," says Lilia. "The emasculation speaks to pure sexual sadism, but blindfold indicates something else. Remorse, maybe, not wanting his victims to see him? We need to know what connects these victims besides geography and race." She snaps her case file shut, and the rest of them follow suit, standing. "Darcy, keep looking for a connection. Wheels up in 30."

***

By the time they get into the air, Darcy has the connection they were looking for. "What do you got for us, Darcy?" asks Alice as they answer her video call on the plane.

Darcy peers out at them from the low resolution screen. "Hey guys, so, get this. Charles Monroe is handling a custody case in Westview right now. Apparently it's been going on all year, and his wife says he drives out there at least once a week to talk to his client."

"What's the client's name?" asks Lilia.

"Mmmm," the sound of typing, "Wanda Maximoff. She started divorcing a guy named Sherwood Ellis last year, and apparently he's been dragging it out."

"Kale and Kaplan, We need to know more about how Charles Monroe spent his time in Westview, so we should look into his movements there. Credit card receipts, ATM use, that sort of thing. I want to talk to George Cramer's girlfriend and Stephen Halpert's wife, as well."

Jen nods, and Rio tries not to laugh when she sees Billy scrawl his latest orders into the little notebook he carries around. Rio, who has been thumbing thoughtfully through the coroner's report, pipes up, "It can't be pure sexual sadism, right? We'd see more evidence of torture. But it's just the emasculation. And the unsub doesn't hold them for very long."

"Yes, we'd usually expect to see more evidence that the unsub tried to prolong the suffering. If he really wanted, he could have let them bleed out from their injuries. There must be a reason he uses a .35 to end it," agrees Alice.

"Are we sure it's a 'he'?" asks Billy.

"Women don't usually torture at all," says Lilia. "They usually kill for a specific reason, not just to fulfill an urge. There's something almost ritualistic about these. The murders have been Thursday nights for three weeks straight, now, so we have six days to catch this guy."

"I'll go talk to the coroner tomorrow morning," volunteers Rio.

Lilia nods. "We'll all go to the crime scene as soon as we land. Local PD has been asked not to touch anything until we arrive."

"I'll start looking for anywhere the three victims could have crossed paths in Westview1" chirps Darcy, hanging up.

Lilia looks them all over. "Try to get a little shut eye if you can," she advises. "This will be a long night."

***

Lilia's prediction is right. They'd driven straight from the airport in Newark to the crime scene, an alleyway behind the fifth precinct. There, laying haphazardly in the dumpster, is the naked, emasculated corpse of a man with a ragged cotton blindfold tied around the eyes.

After spending a while analyzing the crime scene, standing around the dead body like it didn't used to be a living, breathing person, they all make their way to the hotel they've been provided. Rio breaks off for her room without saying goodbye to the others, and soon she's sitting on her hotel bed in her pajamas surrounded by the spread-out pages of the case file.

Something about this is sitting strangely with her. The disposal sites of the bodies, the lack of additional torture. It feels professional. Practiced. Almost like this isn't the unsubs' first time. She calls Darcy before she realizes she's doing it.

"BAU's employee of the year speaking," greets Darcy.

"Do you ever sleep?" asks Rio. She swears she's never called Darcy during an active case and gotten a voicemail, even at the oddest hours.

"After the photos in the coroner's report, it's gonna take me a minute before I feel like getting any shut-eye. But I know you didn't call me to chat about my excellent sleep hygiene."

"No, I want you to run a search through VICAP and look for any cases on the east coast involving emasculation or castration of men ages twenty to sixty. I don't think this is the first time this unsub has killed."

"Ah, you want me to look for his practice runs." Rio can hear Darcy's typing start up. "This is gonna take me a while. I'll hit you back tomorrow." The line goes dead.

Rio huffs, blowing a loose strand out of her face and falling back into her bed. We need to figure out what the victims have in common besides geography. There must be something.

***

The next morning, Rio goes to the coroner's office. The coroner is a serious woman named Monica Rambeu, and she lays the facts out concisely.

"The victims were conscious but restrained, based on the intensity of the ligature marks. They all struggled against the bindings. I did find traces of barbiturates in their systems, so I believe they were drugged before they were tied up. I haven't been able to determine how the drugs were delivered to their systems yet."

Rio nods, jotting this down on her memo pad. "And the emasculation? How was that done?"

"It's strange," begins Monica. "Even with how much the victims were stuggling, the cuts are clean. One for the penis, a second one to remove the scrotum and testes. Probably a curved knife of some kind."

"They could have bled out and died from just that, right?"

"Yes," agrees Monica. "But it would have taken a while, at least a couple of hours. Based on the volume of blood remaining in the victims, they were killed within minutes of their emasculation."

Huh, thinks Rio, that's definitely not how a sexual sadist would handle it.

"And the gunshot?"

"Clean, one tap. Straight through the brain stem. Whoever did this knows their way around a gun and has a solid understanding of anatomy."

***

She takes these findings back to the team and is sent with Billy out to Westview to interview George Kramer's neighbors. His girlfriend had been a Newark native, so she'd been somewhat of a dead end when it came to investigating Cramer's life in Westview before his life had been unceremoniously ended.

She and Billy search Cramer's house first, looking for evidence of anything untoward. Their search doesn't turn up anything except a VCR player and recorder accompanied by an unusually large collection of VCR tapes, most of which claim to be recordings of Wheel of Fortune airings. Strange, but not damming in any way, and not something Rio thinks will help get them any closer to the killer.

She splits off from Billy after they search the house, each taking a neighbor to either side for interviews. The house Rio approaches is a light tan, with baby blue trim and red brickwork along the lower half of the exterior. Rio has barely knocked when the door swings open and Rio swears her heart stutters.

The woman who answers the door is around Rio's age, maybe an inch shorter than her, with wild hair that's been pulled into a bun and stormy blue eyes peering curiously at Rio from over a strong nose and red-painted lips. She's in loose black slacks and a white blouse that buttons all the way up her neck. She is also, Rio thinks privately to herself, breathtaking.

"Hi, I'm Rio Vidal, BAU." Rio flashes her credentials. "Can I come in and ask a few questions about your neighbor, George Cramer?"

The woman drags her eyes slowly and unapologetically all the way down Rio's body and all the way back up, biting her lip. Rio fights the shiver this induces. "Okay," says the woman at last, stepping aside to gesture Rio through the doorframe. She doesn't move as far as she should, though, and Rio's shoulder brushes against her chest as she squeezes past. It sends sparks dancing down Rio's arm and she grits her teeth.

"Can I ask your name?" she tries, as she's led over to a couch and armchair. The house they walk through is full of dark oak and covered wall to wall in paintings and decor, a sharp contrast to the suburban pastel exterior.

"Agatha Harkness," the woman introduces herself. "Can I get you a drink, Agent Vidal?"

"No, thank you," says Rio, and Agatha seems to take that as permission to settle into the couch. Rio takes a seat in the high-backed armchair opposite her.

"Not even later this evening?" says Agatha, smirking.

Rio chooses very pointedly to ignore this. "Mrs. Harkness-"

"Miss. I never made the mistake of marrying," corrects Agatha.

"Miss Harkness," Rio tries again. "How much did you know about George Cramer?"

Agatha leans back on the couch, legs spread. Rio tries desperately not to stare at the confident way the other woman occupies the space, like she owns not just this house but the whole town, or even the whole world. "Not much, really. I moved here about a year ago, to support my friend through her divorce. Never saw much of old Georgie. He was always in his house with those VCR tapes of his."

"Tell me more about the VCR tapes," prompts Rio.

Agatha shrugs. "Not sure there's much to tell. He was constantly getting blank tapes delivered. I only know because the postman is a gossip and I'm usually out working on my garden when the mail comes."

"You mentioned you came out here to support a friend. Which friend?" Agatha's comment had set off an alert in Rio's head.

"Wanda Maximoff." Rio was right. "She's got this nasty custody battle going on, so she can't afford a nanny and a lawyer at the same time. I watch her boys after school during the week."

"And during the rest of the week? Do you work?" Rio tries not to make the question sound accusing.

Agatha laughs at that, a joyous kind of cackle. "God, no, can you imagine? I'm a trust fund baby. Mother dearest kicked the bucket ages ago and I've been irresponsibly spending what she left me ever since. I'm sure she's furious about it from wherever she is in hell."

Hmm. Agatha seems particularly gleeful about her mother's death.."Where did you live before you came to Westview, then?"

Agatha doesn't answer that immediately, and her piercing eyes roam Rio's face like she's searching for something. She must find what she's looking for, because she slowly sits up, resting her forearms across her knees as she leans towards Rio across the coffee table. It's as though she's about to impart a secret. Rio can't help how she leans in a little in response.

"You know, Agent Vidal," she draws Rio's title out, almost mockingly, "these are usually the kind of questions I make a girl ask me over dinner."

Rio can feel herself flushing. "I'm just trying to get a better sense of how George Cramer fit into Westview, that's all."

Agatha keeps Rio fixed with her smouldering gaze for a moment longer before leaning back again. "By asking me about my personal history? Interesting technique, Agent." Agatha blinks, and the intensity seems to leech out of her stare as quickly as it had come. "Then again, what do I know? I'm not the FBI agent here."

Rio tries desperately to get their conversation back on track. "Miss Harkness, I'm not sure if you're aware, but your friend Wanda's lawyer was found dead three weeks ago. We suspect the same man killed your neighbor George last week, and another man named Stephen Halpert yesterday."

"How do you know it's a man?" Not the question Rio was expecting.

"We've constructed a profile. Based on statistics and past cases, we profile this kind of violence as having been done by a white male. We're still nailing down the age range."

Agatha looks almost... amused at this. "And by kind of violence, you mean the kind the media is reporting?" She drops her voice into a scandalized whisper. "Emasculation?"

"Uh, yes."

Agatha sighs. "Hmm, and I suppose that kind of thing comes from some weird sexual urge to dominate, or something? I'm not really up to date on my true crime these days."

"Something like that," says Rio. She suddenly remembers the implied question Agatha had neatly sidestepped. "Were you aware of Charles Monroe's death? Did Wanda talk to you about it at all?"

"Oh, that. Yes, I had to spent three days consoling her. Apparently he'd told her only a week before he died that he thought they had a good chance of resolving the whole mess with full custody of both boys in her favor. Now she'll have to start all over again. I know her soon-to-be-ex-husband must be pleased about that."

"You mean Sherwood Ellis?"

"Yes. He's a walking monument to man's hubris, in my opinion. Far too high of an opinion of himself. He wants the boys to himself so he can parade them around like trophies." Agatha looks genuinely disgusted at the thought. "I can't say I'd cry if he was next on the victim list."

Rio fights the urge to laugh. Agatha hadn't even said it like it was joke. "Well, that list won't get any longer if we do our jobs right." Rio stands, deciding she has what she needs, and any further information she gets from Agatha isn't worth the way the woman's gaze and words seem to superheat Rio's skin no matter how much she tries to ignore it.

Agatha takes the cue and peels herself languidly off the couch before she leads Rio back towards the front of the house, brushing a hand on Rio's lower back as she ushers her through the door and into the crisp autumn air. Rio turns back to the door once she's standing on the porch again, fishing a business card out of her pocket.

She hands it off to Agatha, saying, "Please give me a call if you remember anything that might be useful."

Agatha takes it and makes a show of tucking it into her pants pocket. "I absolutely will, Agent Vidal," she husks in a low voice; the hunger that Rio had seen flickering in her eyes has returned. "And if I remember something that I'm not sure is very useful?"

"Call anyways," says Rio, before she can think better of it. "Better safe than sorry."

"Excellent," murmurs Agatha. "See you around, Agent Vidal." The door swings shut and leaves Rio staring at her own reflection in the stained glass window, which gazes back with a thousand-yard-stare.

Jesus christ, she thinks, before calling Darcy.

"Hey, Darce, any additional hits on VICAP for gential mutilations?"

"Nope, sorry, Rio. Nothing with the blindfolding aspect, at least. There were a handful near Boston, Massachusetts, but they were from nearly fifteen years ago. Local PD suspected a local woman, but she died before they could bring her in for questioning. Apparent heart attack."

Rio runs a hand down her face. So, the unsub had done this without any amateur practice runs? It doesn't sit right with her. "Okay, well, thanks. I think I might have a new lead anyway. Can you run a full background check on Sherwood Ellis?"

"Your wish is my command. I will return with news." The line goes dead.

***

Based on the ties they've established between all three victims and Westview, the decision is made that same day to relocate the investigation to the Westview police station. Everyone drives down to meet Rio and Billy there that evening. The chief of police, Harold Proctor, seems happy enough to give them the space. Rio gets the feeling Westview doesn't deal with murders very frequently, and the chief seems out of his depth.

It's dark out by the time the team convenes in front of their murder board, bouncing ideas around as they try to connect the various pieces into a coherent picture.

"Okay, so, our first victim spent a lot of time here working the custody case," starts Rio, "for Wanda Maximoff, who is divorcing Sherwood Ellis. Our second victim, Cramer, was a school counselor here- Hey, Darcy?"

Darcy's voice filters through the speakerphone at the center of the police station conference room they've commandeered. "What is up, my guy?"

"Was Bill Cramer a counselor at the same school that Wanda and Sherwood's kids go to?"

The sound of fingers on a keyboard is followed by, "Can confirm. Billy and Tommy Ellis, both age 12, both attending Westview Middle School where Bill Cramer worked as a school counselor until his body was found naked and afraid at a bus stop last week."

"Is there any record of Cramer meeting with the kids?" asks Alice.

"Hmm, school records are usually kept pretty secure, but," there's a pause as Darcy does what Rio is sure are borderline illegal things on her computer, "yep! Sure enough, it looks like he was meeting with both boys individually at least once a week. To, let's see... counsel them about their parent's divorce."

"That doesn't seem too out of the ordinary," says Rio. "Cramer's neighbor watches Billy and Tommy, and she told me the divorce proceedings are going poorly. She seems to think it's all Ellis' fault."

"Cramer's neighbor?" Jen fixes a sharp gaze on Rio. "She knows Maximoff and Ellis?"

Rio rubs the back of her neck, fighting a flush at the reminder of the brunette from this morning. "Maximoff, mostly. I got the impression she's an old friend of Wanda's. She told me she watches the kids as a favor, since Wanda is apparently spending all her money on lawyers and can't afford the childcare."

"Ellis doesn't help out?" asks Jen, raising an eyebrow. The unspoken implication being, why would a man petitioning for custody refuse to watch his own children?

"Apparently not."

"Huh. That guy seems like a piece of work," observes Alice, leaning back in her chair with her arms behind her head. "Do we know anything else about him?"

Darcy pipes up. "Yes, we do! Rio asked me to run his background earlier."

"Okay, hit us."

"Sherwood Ellis, 45, married fourteen years ago to one Wanda Maximoff, 34. Originally from Britain, he works in real estate - hey, just like our third victim! - and has money to burn as a result."

"And the divorce?" asks Lilia. "What's the status of it?"

"Well, Wanda filed a year ago, citing extreme cruelty as the reason." Darcy sucks a breath in through her teeth. "Ooh, nasty. It looks like she's been in the ER a dozen times in as many years, usually for a broken bone or two. She must have finally had enough last year. She showed up with a broken collarbone and a black eye and asked to have it documented by the hospital as domestic abuse. The divorce was filed a week later, and she took the kids and moved in with her brother across town. Ellis has been dragging the whole thing out ever since."

"Has anyone spoken to Maximoff yet?"

"No, local PD didn't even realize Monroe was her lawyer until I dug it up."

"And do we have any idea how close the divorce was to being resolved?"

Rio can answer that one. "Wanda's friend said Monroe had implied he had a way to force the verdict in Wanda's favor, but Agatha didn't know what it was."

"Agatha?" Jen's look from before is back.

"Agatha Harkness, the woman watching the Ellis twins," Rio clarifies. "Cramer's neighbor."

"Okay, so Sherwood Ellis. A history of violence and at least a tentative connection to all three victims," says Alice. "And a motive to eliminate at least one of them. With Charles Monroe dead, he probably has a better chance at winning custody of the twins."

"But why the genital mutilation?" pushes Jen.

"Well, the name for what he did is emasculation," posits Billy, "so he could be expressing a real or perceived sexual impotence. Having a wife and children is a societal marker of a man's success and virility. The threat of losing his family could make him feel powerless as a father and a husband. He could have felt that Monroe was contributing to his loss of power by being Wanda's lawyer."

"He feels metaphorically emasculated by the victims in some way, so he literally emasculates them?" summarizes Alice.

"Exactly." Billy nods, satisfied with his theory. "With his wife out of the house for a year, he wouldn't have anyone to take his violent impulses out on. He'd no longer be able to sublimate his psychotic urges."

"That's all well and good, but what reason would he have to kill the kid's counselor?," Jen points out. "And don't forget the blindfolds, or Stephen Halpert - he doesn't have any apparent connection to the divorce besides working in real estate alongside Ellis."

"Darcy, see if you can find any connections between Halpert and the divorce, specifically. I also want access to the notes from Cramer's sessions with the Ellis children," demands Lilia.

"I'll look into Halpert, but I already tried to find those notes, boss," admits Darcy. "I think Cramer might have kept them on paper or something, because I'm finding zilch in his computer or on the school's fileshare network. "

"Well, do what you can," Lilia waves a dismissive hand that Darcy can't see, but Darcy seems to sense it somehow and drops off the call without another word. "Billy and Jen, I want you to find and talk to Maximoff and Ellis tomorrow. Vidal, you'll go talk to the neighbor again. Find out what else she knows about Sherwood Ellis. Alice, you'll be with me; I want to go to Westview Middle and take a look at Cramer's office."

Rio tries to be totally normal about the fact that she's going to have to see Agatha Harkness again, and she's not sure she succeeds based on the searching glances Jen won't stop throwing her way, but they're released into the cool Saturday night air before Jen gets the chance to say anything.

Rio decides to walk to the motel the team has relocated to instead of drive. Westview really is a tiny town, and it's barely a mile to her accommodations. She pulls up the maps app on her phone and sees there's a pizza place on the way to the motel. Perfect.

She has just stepped into the buzzing fluorescent light of the pizza shop when her phone rings. Unknown number. Rio steps right back through the door, standing to the side of the entrance in front of a flickering neon 'OPEN' sign, and answers.

"This is Agent Vidal," she greets.

"Agent Vidal, hi," comes a breathy voice through the phone. Rio's stomach drops like she swallowed a rock. "I hope it's not too late for me to be calling?" The voice tilts up coquettishly and Rio swears her knees go weak for a moment.

"Ag-" Rio clears her throat and tries again. "Miss Harkness, hello. It's no trouble, I'm just leaving the precinct. How can I help?"

"Well, you said to call if I thought of anything, and I did think of something, about the newest victim, Stephen Halpert? I saw him on the evening news and it made me remember. Do you think you could come over and I can tell you?"

"Um, like, right now?" chokes Rio.

"Only if you're not busy, Agent" purrs Agatha. Jesus, talk about tonal whiplash. "I'm only a couple of miles from the police station, after all."

"Is there a reason you don't want to talk about it over the phone?" Being there during the day was bad enough - Rio doesn't think she'll survive Agatha Harkness now that the sun has set.

"Yes. It's kind of a complicated... situation. I think it might be better if I give a more," Agatha pauses as though looking for the right word, then continues in a low tone, "hands-on explanation."

Rio, tilts her head back at the sky, fighting a groan, praying to a god she is certain has long since abandoned her. Before she can decline, and suggest they meet tomorrow, Agatha goes in for the kill. Well, the metaphorical one.

"Please, Agent Vidal? I'd feel so much better knowing I've done everything I can to help the investigation."

The almost whining tone Agatha adopts when she says Rio's name shoots straight to her core. Her eyes land back on the pizza shop she's standing outside of, and she makes an impulsive decision. Agatha's house is in the opposite direction as the motel, but she should be able to make the walk from here in twenty minutes. Lilia did tell me to talk to her again, she reasons with herself, the devil on her shoulder cheering her on.

"Alright. I can be there in half an hour."

"Excellent. Don't keep me waiting, Agent."

***

Rio shows up to Agatha's door exactly half an hour later with a pizza in hand, feeling a little stupid. What the hell is she doing, showing up to the house of someone connected to the case with fucking pizza like some kind of first date?

Rio knocks twice, firmly, and before she can second guess herself, the door swings open. Rio swallows heavily when she takes in the other woman.

Agatha is in a silk floral print nightgown, hair from before loose around her shoulders and draping all the way down to her waist. Her face is bare, and whatever shoes she'd been wearing when she greeted Rio earlier have been discarded, and Rio realizes she's now a few inches taller in her boots.

"Agent Vidal, hi." Agatha glances down at the pizza box between them. "I see you brought dinner," she smiles.

"Uh, yeah, I was kind of in the middle of getting it when you called, so I just thought..." Rio trails off, free hand rubbing awkwardly at the back of her neck.

"How thoughtful of you. Please, come in," Agatha steps aside and gestures Rio into her entryway. Just like she had that morning, she doesn't leave enough space, and Rio's shoulder drags deliciously across the front of Agatha's body as she tries to enter without dropping the pizza.

They head towards coffee table, and Agatha gestures for Rio to sit, making her way on to the next room. There's the sound of glass bottles being shuffled around, and Agatha calls from the kitchen, "Can I get you a beer, Agent?"

Rio almost calls back, 'Can't, I'm working,' but she stops herself, thinking. Agatha may be related to the case, she reasons, but there's no indication the woman is responsible for the killings. All signs point to it being a man, probably Sherwood Ellis. It should be perfectly fine to discuss the case casually, especially as a way of getting more information about Ellis. Besides, being in Agatha's house is making Rio's every nerve buzz like a live wire, and she could use something to take the edge off.

"Sure!" she ends up shouting back. The devil on her shoulder cackles in triumph while her angel starts considering various forms of self-detonation.

Agatha emerges back into the living room with a beer in each hand. She walks over to the armchair Rio has once again claimed and holds one out to Rio. Rio takes it, and feels a buzz when their fingers brush.

Agatha grabs a slice and falls back into the couch. Her robe, which had apparently not been tied, falls open slightly to reveal a purple silk nightgown that ends halfway to Agatha's knees. Jesus. Is this how she usually dresses for company?

Rio works her way through half a slice of pizza and a third of her beer before she feels put together enough to start the conversation without her voice cracking. "So, what did you want to discuss that you couldn't talk about over the phone? About Stephen Halpert."

Agatha, who has been nursing her beer with nary a bite of her pizza, sets the untouched slice back in the box and leans forward. Her robe falls open a little further, exposing the bare expanse of her chest. Rio tries very hard not to look.

"I didn't know it was him until they showed his face on the news tonight, but Stephen Halpert is Wanda's real estate agent. Wanda wants to move out of Pietro's house."

Rio polishes off the slice in her hand, chewing as she thinks, then washes it down with her drink. "And you couldn't tell me this over the phone because..."
