Agatha was halfway through grading a stack of freshman essays when her phone started buzzing under the clutter of papers. She blinked, bleary-eyed and slow to react, her brain still fogged from too many hours of red ink and mediocre thesis statements. The buzzing cut off, then started again. She shoved a folder aside, the pages slipping off her lap, and finally unearthed her phone.


1 missed call from Rio Vidal 🔪

2 missed calls from Unknown Number

1 message from Rio Vidal 🔪

Agatha stared at the screen, suddenly wide awake.

 

She hadn’t spoken to Rio in months. The breakup had been… civilized, friendly even, but they hadn’t drifted into a post-relationship friendship. If anything, they’d left things in that strange liminal space where you know someone intimately but aren’t supposed to feel anything for them anymore.

 

The message notification hovered like a trap her thumb was magnetized to. She tapped it first, heart in her throat.

 

Rio Vidal 🔪

Rio Vidal has made an emergency call. This location was sent because of a hard fall detection.

 

It was the default SOS location alert. Her breath hitched. Rio’s Apple Watch must have sent Agatha an automatic emergency signal. 

 

Agatha froze for only a second.

 

She hit Call Back on Rio’s number immediately, pacing before the first ring even finished. Her heart thundered in her ears. After a few agonizing rings, someone answered—not Rio.

 

A woman’s voice. "Oh, thank god you called, honey."

 

Agatha stopped dead in the middle of the room. "W-Who is this? What happened? Where is Rio?"

 

“She fell from a ladder—she was trying to finish something in the house, and then—” the woman exhaled, her words tumbling out quickly. “We’re on our way to the hospital. I think her watch must’ve sent out an alert. I kept her phone in case you called her first.”

 

Agatha’s body was already moving before her brain could catch up—pulling on a jacket, grabbing her bag, slipping on shoes with her phone still pressed to her ear.

 

“We’re just arriving at St. George’s Medical. Do you know where that is?”

 

“Yeah,” Agatha said, almost on reflex. “I know it. I’ll be there in a few.”

 

“Good,” the woman added. “By the way… what’s your name? You’re listed on her emergency contact just as ‘Wife.’”

 

Agatha stopped short. The word hit like a sucker punch to the chest, knocking all the air out of her lungs.

 

Wife.

 

The silence stretched too long.

 

"Honey? Are you still there?" the woman prompted again, voice soft with concern.

 

Agatha swallowed. “Yeah. Sorry. I’m Agatha. I’ll be there as soon as I can.”

 

Agatha’s brain finally caught up with her body, snapping her into motion. She grabbed her keys, her phone charger—anything she could think of in the seconds it took her to bolt out the door and throw it all in her bag. 

 

She moved on instinct more than planning, repeating St. George’s, St. George’s like a mantra under her breath as if it could tether her to reality.

 

She tried to stay calm on the drive, white-knuckling the steering wheel, obeying traffic laws but pushing the limits. Her breath came shallow, her heart pounding in her chest with the rhythm of every imagined worst-case scenario. By the time she stepped into the hospital lobby, she was running entirely on adrenaline and anxiety.

 

"I'm looking for Rio Vidal," Agatha said breathlessly as she approached the reception desk.

 

The woman behind the desk, unbothered and almost too calm for the chaos churning inside Agatha, looked up with mild curiosity. "I'm sorry, ma'am, are you part of her family?"

 

Agatha barely hesitated. She took a breath, eyes flashing with urgency. "Yes. I'm her wife."

 

She didn’t even think about it—just said it. It came out sharp and true, and maybe it was her tone or her wild eyes or the fact that she looked like she might tear through the walls if she didn’t get an answer immediately, but it worked.

 

The receptionist blinked, nodded once, and said, “Room 1004,” pointing down the hallway.

 

Agatha didn’t wait for anything else.

 

She practically bolted, her boots echoing against the linoleum, lungs tight, breath coming ragged as she reached the door marked 1004. She grabbed the handle and hesitated for a single beat before pushing it open.

 

But it wasn’t Rio.

 

There was only an older woman sitting in a chair by the window, her coat draped over her lap, a half-empty styrofoam cup in her hand.

 

Agatha froze. For a moment, she thought she must have gotten the wrong room and started to back out silently, hoping not to disturb her.

But the woman looked up just in time and caught her.

 

“Oh, are you Agatha?” she asked gently.

 

Agatha blinked, startled. “...Yes?”

 

“I’m Lilia. We talked on the phone…”

 

Realization swept over her like a second wave of shock. Agatha nodded numbly.

 

Lilia rose from the chair, slow but steady, and walked over to her. “They took her for some tests. Just to be safe.”

 

Agatha’s mouth opened, but no sound came out. She pressed her lips together and nodded again.

 

Lilia glanced toward the empty hospital bed, then back to Agatha. Her expression softened. 

 

“She came by today just to take one last look at the house before declaring the project done. We’d finished everything, more or less. She said one of the lights in the library was flickering, probably a wiring thing. Nothing big.”

 

She paused to take a sip of drink before continuing.

 

“She took the ladder upstairs while I was in the kitchen making us some tea,” she said, voice quieter now. “And then… I heard this loud thump. I called out to her, but she didn’t answer.”

 

Agatha’s stomach turned.

 

“I ran to check and saw her on the floor. Unconscious. Ladder tipped over.”

 

Lilia grimaced faintly, and that told Agatha more than any detail might have. Blood. She didn’t have to ask.

 

 “I… I didn’t move her. I called emergency right away, and then I look for her phone in her bag and it was locked, but I saw her emergency contact was listed there”

 

Agatha nods, her throat with a lump too big for her to be able to say anything.

 

Lilia gave her a sympathetic nod. “I figured someone named ‘Wife’ was worth calling.”

 

Agatha let out a shaky breath, her body only now beginning to realize it had stopped moving. Her legs wobbled beneath her, and she reached for the back of a chair to steady herself.

 

“They took her for a scan and x-rays. Said it was standard procedure, considering the fall. I’ve just been waiting for someone to come, and… well, now you’re here.”

 

Agatha looked at the empty bed, her hands clenched at her sides.

 

“Thank you,” she said, voice tight.

 

Lilia smiled kindly, stepping back to give her space. “Of course, honey.” She gave Agatha a light pat on the shoulder, and Agatha had to bite hard on her bottom lip to keep from crying.

 

“Do you want me to stay with you until they bring her back?” Lilia asked gently.

 

Agatha didn’t really know what she wanted. Her insides were a chaos of panic, dread, and guilt, but she couldn’t justify keeping Rio’s client at the hospital just because she was unraveling.

 

“No, it’s okay,” she said quickly. “I’ll just wait here for her. But thank you for, you know… everything.”

 

Lilia looked like she wanted to argue, like maybe she didn’t feel great about leaving Agatha in that distressed state behind. But instead, she nodded and gave Agatha’s shoulder another squeeze.

 

“Okay, honey. You’ve got my number. Text me when you have any updates, or if you need anything. Really.”

 

“Will do,” Agatha said, forcing herself to smile. It wobbled at the edges.

 

She didn’t say anything else. Just sank into the chair closest to the hospital bed, her coat still on, hands gripping each other tightly in her lap. Her eyes stayed fixed on the door, as though sheer willpower could make it open sooner.

 

But it didn’t.

 

The hour that followed felt like it bent time into something cruel and slow. Every sound in the hallway made her sit up straighter. Every passing nurse made her heart leap into her throat. She felt like she was going to explode, like the waiting was pressing down on her from the inside, stretching her skin too tight.

 

She didn’t know what to do with herself. She couldn’t read. Couldn’t scroll through her phone. Couldn’t even breathe properly.

 

Just wait.

 

Finally— finally —the door opened, and a man in scrubs stepped inside, holding a clipboard.

 

“Are you miss Vidal’s wife?” he asked gently.

 

She was on her feet before she even registered it. “Yes. I’m her—yes.”

 

He offered a kind smile. “I’m Dr. Stranger. I was with Miss Vidal during the initial assessment and scans. I wanted to update you.”

 

Agatha nodded, barely managing to keep eye contact. “Okay...”

 

“The good news is her scans came back clear. No signs of internal bleeding or skull fracture. She does have a mild concussion, and a laceration at the side of her head that needed some stitches. She was unconscious briefly, so we’re going to keep her overnight for observation.”

 

Agatha swallowed hard, gripping the back of the chair to steady herself. “Okay. That’s… okay.”

 

“She also took a hit to her left side during the fall,” he added. “We suspect a minor hairline fracture in one of her lower ribs. The X-ray confirmed it’s non-displaced, which is good. It’ll be painful, but with rest, ice, and some light compression, it should heal on its own in a few weeks.”

 

Agatha blinked, nodding slowly, trying to hold all the information in her spinning head. It felt like too much and not enough at once.

 

“She’s still with the nurses, but she's awake right now and we’ll be bringing her back here shortly. I’ll give you more details and instructions for aftercare once she’s discharged tomorrow, assuming everything goes smoothly overnight.”

 

“Thank you,” Agatha said softly. 

 

He gave her a nod and slipped back out, leaving her once again in the quiet room, only this time with the promise that Rio would return soon.

 

She sat back down, her knees weak with the weight of it all—relief, fear, the image of Rio lying unconscious at the base of a ladder looping like a film reel in her head.

 

Agatha leaned forward, elbows on her knees, and dropped her face into her hands. Her breathing came fast and shallow as she coaxed herself not to fall apart completely. 

 

Not now. Hold tight. She’s okay. She’s okay.

 

She closed her eyes and focused on the rhythm of her breath, the sterile scent of the hospital, the hum of distant beeping monitors. Her pulse finally began to slow. And then—

 

She heard it.

 

That unmistakable voice.

 

Muffled, but bright. Light. Teasing. Rio.

 

Agatha lifted her head, heart thudding, as the nurse rolled a wheelchair into the room. And there she was.

 

Rio looked like hell.

 

Her hair was pulled back oddly, and a buzzed patch peeked out from behind her left ear, covered by a clean white bandage. Agatha assumed that was where they’d stitched her up. Her flannel button-down was crookedly fastened, sleeves messily pulled up, stained with faded blood, and the top three buttons gaped open. Her favorite pair of jeans—the ones Agatha had sworn were going to give out any day now—were still somehow clinging to life.

 

But none of that mattered.

 

Because Rio looked up, saw her, and smiled—that smile. Lopsided. Showing the cute little gap between her front teeth. It was familiar in the most painful way.

 

“Baby, you’re here,” Rio said instantly, her voice thick with warmth and slurred just enough to betray the medication in her system.

 

Agatha’s throat clenched. She blinked rapidly, eyes stinging. “I am,” she managed to say, standing quickly to help the nurse transfer her from the wheelchair to the bed.

 

Rio looked at the nurse as they guided her toward the bed. “That’s my wife,” she said proudly. “She’s a college professor. Teaches literature. Uses words like ‘didactic’ in casual conversation. So hot.”

 

Agatha’s eyes widened. “Rio.”

 

“She gives lectures that make you feel like you’re about to be set on fire emotionally,” Rio continued dreamily. “One time I sneaked out and sat in one of her classes just to listen and ended up crying over Jane Eyre.”

 

The nurse snorted a laugh. “She’s been talking about you nonstop,” she told Agatha. “I think she was more worried about you than her own injury.”

 

“She pretends she’s not soft, but she is,” Rio went on, completely ignoring the conversation and leaning heavily into the nurse’s hands. “She cries reading when she thinks no one’s watching. She even keeps emergency tea in her desk drawer and everything”

 

Agatha was now visibly flushed. “I—Is she on a lot of medication or something?” she asked the nurse quickly, flustered.

 

“She had some anesthetic for the stitches and we gave her pain meds for her ribs,” the nurse confirmed with an amused smile. “So yes, she’s a little high. But the sentiment seems genuine.”

 

Agatha sighed deeply, then gently took Rio’s hand once she was on the bed. “You’re embarrassing both of us.”

 

“You love it,” Rio whispered, curling her fingers loosely around Agatha’s.

 

Agatha groaned, half-laughing in disbelief. “Stop talking.”

 

“Can’t. You’re cute,” Rio said matter-of-factly.

 

“I’m going to let you two have a moment,” the nurse said tactfully, turning toward the door. “I’ll be right back to hook up the IV and vitals monitor.”

 

“Tell her she’s pretty before you go,” Rio added, waving vaguely at Agatha.

 

“Rio, shut up,” Agatha said, putting her hand over Rio’s mouth.

 

The nurse gave Agatha a smile. “You’re very pretty, ma’am.” she said and Agatha could feel Rio’s lips curling into a smile under her hand.

 

The door clicked shut behind her.

 

Agatha took her hand off and sat on the edge of the bed, giving Rio a stern look—one that held no real bite. “You are so lucky you’re already injured.”

 

Rio gave her a woozy smile. “You’re right I’m lucky, because you're here.”

 

Agatha’s expression softened in spite of herself. Her fingers brushed over Rio’s wrist. “I’m here, love. You’re okay now.”

 

“Good,” Rio murmured, already beginning to drift. “Stay close? Just till I fall asleep…”

 

“I’m not going anywhere.”

 

“Even if I keep talking?”

 

Agatha smirked. “Then I might consider sedating you myself.”

 

Rio giggled. “I love when you get all mean, you're so hot”

 

Agatha shakes her head, trying not to smile at her ex-girlfriend's antics. She leaned in and pressed her lips gently to Rio’s temple, just above the bandage. “Go to sleep, baby.”

 

And for the first time since the phone rang, or maybe since their last goodbye months ago, she let herself breathe again.

Agatha was reluctant to leave Rio alone, but the nurses had assured her Rio wouldn’t be awake for a few more hours—between the concussion, the painkillers, and the stress, she was out cold.

 

So, Agatha went home.

 

She moved with efficiency, deliberately avoiding thinking too much. She took the fastest shower of her life, pulled on clean clothes, and packed a small overnight bag for them both. A few changes of clothes. Toothbrushes. Deodorant. She purposefully avoided Rio’s hoodie she stole years ago and still slept with and the pile of T-shirts she’d never given back. 

 

She fed Señor Scratchy and gave the bunny a few soft head pats as he chewed indifferently on his hay. “Mommy will be back tomorrow,” she whispered. “Try not to destroy the rug.”

 

When she returned to the hospital, the corridor was quiet, the fluorescent lights humming. She made her way back to the room with a sense of low, vibrating urgency. Not panic, just a weight in her chest that hadn’t lifted yet.

 

When she slipped inside, Rio was stirring. Blinking slowly as she tried to focus, her hand twitching weakly on the blanket. 

 

And then her eyes found Agatha.

 

“W–what are you doing here?” Rio asked, voice groggy, small. Confused. There was something in her face—something raw and unguarded—that made Agatha feel like she was the one with bruised ribs.

 

“Taking care of you, obviously,” Agatha said, keeping her tone steady, matter-of-fact. Like she hadn’t been half out of her mind earlier.

 

“Why?”

 

That one word made Agatha flinch inside. Not visibly—she had practice holding herself together—but it stung. She made her way to the bed and sat gently on the edge, taking Rio’s hand in hers.

 

“Because you’re hurt, Rio. And apparently I’m still your emergency contact, so of course I would come.”

 

Rio didn’t look convinced.

 

Agatha traced light circles against the back of her hand, grounding herself in the quiet rhythm. “I would’ve come either way.”

 

Rio opened her mouth to say something, but just then, there was a soft knock at the door and a nurse stepped inside, smiling warmly.

 

“Hi, sweetheart,” she said to Rio as she came over, checking her vitals. “How’s the pain?”

 

“Everything hurts,” Rio mumbled.

 

“Well, that sounds about right.” The nurse offered a sympathetic chuckle as she prepared the IV and adjusted a few monitors. “Let’s get you topped off with some meds, okay?”

 

She turned to Agatha then, eyes brightening a little. “And you must be the wife! It’s so nice to finally meet you.”

 

Agatha froze for a second. Rio made a strangled sort of sound and sank a little deeper into the pillow, flushed all the way to the tips of her ears.

 

“Oh my god,” Rio muttered.

 

Agatha cleared her throat, trying not to look as amused as she felt. “Yeah, I’m Agatha. Nice to meet you too.”

 

The nurse glanced between them and smiled again, either oblivious or entirely aware. 

 

“You’ve got a sweet one here. Talked our ears off about you earlier. The entire ER staff knows you're a professor with a terrifying death glare and perfect nose.”

 

Rio groaned, covering her eyes with the arm that wasn’t hooked to the IV.

 

Agatha was so enjoying this. “That… sounds exaggerated.”

 

The nurse grinned and headed toward the door. “I’ll leave you two alone. She should be more comfortable soon. Buzz if you need anything.”

 

The moment the door closed, Agatha turned back to Rio with raised eyebrows and a smirk she couldn’t hide if she tried.

 

“Well,” she said lightly, “you certainly gave this whole hospital a show.”

 

Rio peeked at her through her fingers. “I’m never going to recover from this.”

 

“Oh, I’m certainly not letting you off the hook,” Agatha said teasingly, leaning back in the chair. “You seemed pretty committed to the whole ‘my wife’ narrative earlier. I'm honestly surprised you’re still keeping up this old bit, after all, I don't remember either of us signing those papers”

 

Rio groaned, but quickly started laughing, quiet and rasping, but real. “Shut up. I was concussed. And high.”

 

“Sure,” Agatha replied, but her voice was softer now. “Still. Must’ve been convincing. I got waved through without question.”

 

Rio let her arm fall to her side, turning her head just enough to really look at her. “So you also told them you were my wife, Ms. Harkness?”

 

“Of course I did, how else would they let me through?”

 

The silence that followed was charged. There was a lot left unsaid between them. Things buried under shared glances and long silences and old hurt they hadn’t unpacked.

 

But they didn’t go there.

 

Rio shifted a little on the bed, wincing slightly. “So. Did you bring me a change of clothes, or am I rocking the ‘blood-stained lumber lesbian’ look another day?”

 

Agatha huffed a laugh. “You say that like it’s not your usual brand.”

 

Rio gave her a crooked smile. “It usually doesn't involve blood”

 

Agatha shakes her head, smiling. “I brought clothes. And snacks. And toiletries.” 

 

“My hero!” Rio said, putting her free hand over her heart.

 

Agatha rolled her eyes, but she was smiling.

 

Rio blinked slowly. “Any chance you brought me one of those t-shirts you stole?”

 

Agatha shrugged. “Nope, they're soft and I’m keeping it”

 

There was a pause.

 

Rio smiled again, smaller this time, but warmer. “Thanks for coming.”

 

“No need to thank me,” Agatha didn’t look away. “It's my duty as your wife, after all.”

 

The smile Rio gave her was tired but sincere, and Agatha couldn’t help but smile back. The weight of everything between them hung there in the quiet, unspoken but present. 

 

Neither of them moved to unpack it. But neither one looked away. The monitor beeped softly in the background. The air between them was still.

 

After a moment, Agatha tilted her head slightly. “Do you want me to call the nurse to help you clean up?”

 

Rio hesitated, her expression faltering, and for a second she looked so vulnerable it made Agatha’s chest ache. “I-I guess… I don’t know if I can do it by myself.”

 

Agatha’s voice was gentle. “Or I can help you. If you want me to.”

 

There was a beat of silence before Rio nodded, a little embarrassed, eyes dropping to the blanket.

 

They didn’t say anything as Agatha got up and quietly gathered the overnight bag, pulling out a clean shirt, some soft sweats, and her own silk robe—Rio’s favorite, even if she’d never admitted it out loud. Agatha set the items down in the small bathroom before she returned to the bed, helping Rio shift carefully and slowly to her feet.

 

Rio winced as she moved, her body stiff and sore, and Agatha automatically adjusted her hold, slipping an arm around her waist. She helped her shuffle to the bathroom, making sure the IV line didn’t tug too hard, and guided her inside.

 

Once there, they both hesitated.

 

Rio leaned against the counter, cheeks pink, not quite meeting Agatha’s eyes. “This is… weird, right?”

 

Agatha gave a small, almost fond smile. “We’ve done worse.”

 

That got a soft snort from Rio, and Agatha took that as permission to move gently. She helped Rio out of the crumpled, stained shirt—carefully, so it wouldn’t brush against the bandage on her head—and tried her hardest not to linger on her body. But when her eyes caught the blossoming bruises across Rio’s ribs and along her hip, her breath hitched. 

 

She wetted a towel and wrung it out, stepping back to softly wipe her clean where she could, moving slowly and with care. Rio barely reacted—just the occasional sharp inhale or stiffening when Agatha accidentally brushed a sore spot. She kept her touch light, murmuring a quiet “Sorry” whenever Rio flinched.

 

It was quiet. Strangely intimate. A kind of stillness that felt fragile, like neither of them wanted to break it.

 

When she finished, Agatha gently brushed out Rio’s tangled hair with her fingers, then a wide-toothed comb. She braided it loosely, in a way that wouldn’t bother the bandage on the back of her head.

 

“I look ridiculous,” Rio muttered, dryly amused.

 

Agatha rolled. “You look lucky enough that you didn't have to get your whole head shaved”

 

“I bet I would look hot with a buzz cut,” Rio teased and Agatha shook her head.

 

Even though she was pretty sure that Rio would look hot either way.

 

She helped her into the soft sweats, then draped the silk robe over the top part of her body, clasping it in a way that would protect the naked skin under it, but not hurt the sore body. 

 

The fabric caught the light, and it looked almost out of place in the hospital bathroom, but it made Rio look a little more like herself again. Something about it made Agatha feel steadier, too.

 

She guided her to the sink, standing behind her as Rio leaned forward, brushing her teeth with Agatha’s help—Agatha holding the cup of water, handing her the brush, steadying her when she swayed a little.

 

Then she helped Rio back to bed and tucked her in gently, her movements careful, practiced. She kissed her forehead without thinking. Just a featherlight press of lips against skin.

 

By the time Agatha returned from brushing her own teeth, Rio looked drowsy again, her head against the pillow, one hand curled near her chest.

 

“Thank you,” Rio said softly—so quietly that Agatha almost missed it.

 

“It’s no problem, baby,” Agatha replied, her voice a whisper. The pet name slipped out like muscle memory, her hand cupping Rio’s cheek in a soft caress.

 

Rio closed her eyes at the touch. Agatha let her hand linger for a second longer before sitting back down in the chair.

 

“I wish I had my Switch,” Rio mumbled after a pause, eyes still closed.

 

Agatha let out a quiet laugh. “Sorry. I should’ve gone to your place. But I wasn’t sure if you’d changed the door code. And I didn’t want to waste time getting there and back, or leave you alone for too long.”

 

Rio opened one eye, looking at her with a sleepy kind of calm. “I haven’t changed the code.”

 

Agatha tried not to let that land too heavily. Because if Rio hadn’t changed the code, it was still 0129. January 29th. The date of their first ever date. The night Agatha had worn that purple skirt that Rio loves and Rio had shown up with a bouquet of peonies and mumbled something about “flowers that last longer than roses.”

 

She pushed the memory down.

 

“We can pick your videogame up on our way to my house tomorrow,” Agatha offered, nonchalant.

 

Rio blinked. “What do you mean?”

 

Agatha looked at her like it was obvious. “Rio, I’m not letting you stay alone while you’re recovering. Besides, your building doesn’t have an elevator. You shouldn’t be going up and down those stairs like this.”

 

She expected an argument. A joke. Some resistance.

 

Instead, Rio just smiled at her, slow and easy. “Okay, Agatha.”

 

Agatha blinked. “Okay?”

 

“Yeah.” Rio’s voice was soft. “Okay.”

 

They looked at each other for a moment, the silence between them comfortable now. Almost familiar.

 

“How is work? Besides giving you injury, I mean” Agatha asked teasingly.

 

Rio rolled her eyes. “Work is fine . That house I fell in? It was supposed to be the final walk-through before I signed off. Custom-built library, open concept everything, reclaimed wood from an old barn—looked great until I noticed a flickering light. I went up to check the wiring myself, because I’m meticulous and don’t trust subcontractors, and… well, gravity had opinions.”

 

Agatha gave her a look. “You’re not supposed to be on ladders, Rio.”

 

Rio grinned sheepishly. “Yeah, I remember someone saying that every time I ignored OSHA standards in our old place.”

 

“And you used to tell me you were part spider.”

 

“Well, apparently I’m not. I’m part dumbass.”

 

Agatha huffed, unable to hide her smile. “You are full dumbass. With a mild concussion and a bruised rib and eight stitches.”

 

“Seven,” Rio corrected. “The nurse said seven.”

 

“Oh, excuse me, only seven. So much better.”

 

They both laughed, more gently this time. It felt natural. Like they hadn’t spent the last eight months pretending the other didn’t exist. 

 

“What about you?” Rio asked after a beat. “Still making undergrads cry?”

 

Agatha snorted. “Only the lazy ones. I’m teaching a seminar on witches and women in literature this semester. Finally convinced the department to let me do it.”

 

Rio’s eyes lit up, genuine interest shining through. “You’ve been talking about that class since before we even started dating.”

 

Agatha smiled. “I know. It’s... good. Really good, actually. The students are surprisingly engaged. I had one of them write a fifteen-page paper on the evolution of the witch figure from Macbeth to Practical Magic. It was so pretentious. I was proud.”

 

Rio laughed and then winced, holding her side. “Ow, dammit. Don’t make me laugh. My ribs disagree with this conversation.”

 

“Sorry, sorry,” Agatha said, trying not to smile even harder. “You okay?”

 

“I’ll live,” Rio replied with a little breathless grin. “Nothing worse than when Señor Scratchy bit me.”

 

Agatha narrowed her eyes. “Señor Scratchy has never bitten anyone unprovoked.”

 

“He glared at me every time I went into the kitchen.”

 

“He’s protective,” Agatha said, placing a hand over her heart. “And he has excellent instincts.”

 

Rio just shook her head, that crooked smile lingering. “Missed that little guy.”

 

Agatha looked down at her hands for a second. “He missed you too. I told him I’d be back tomorrow. I think he knew something was wrong.”

 

Rio’s gaze softened, but she didn’t say anything. Agatha stood up and adjusted the blanket over her legs.

“I’m sorry,” Rio murmured again, her voice thick with vulnerability. Agatha’s chest tightened painfully at those words, her heart aching with longing and regret. She fought the urge to jerk her hand away, as if doing so might shatter her completely, because in that moment, she felt like she might.

 

“This isn’t how I wanted to have this conversation—never how I imagined standing here again with you,” Rio continued, her voice trembling with raw emotion. “But please, could you still hear me out?”

 

Agatha forced herself to lift her gaze, but the instant she did, a wave of regret washed over her. Rio’s eyes — those incredible, unwavering wells of kindness — had always been her weakness. Even now, despite everything, they held a silent, haunting plea that she couldn’t turn away from. Her heart clenched with longing and pain. She nodded desperately, her voice trembling and caught in her throat, afraid that if she spoke, her emotions would spill out and shatter what little composure she had left.

 

“Agatha… what the hell happened to us?” Rio’s voice wasn’t raised, but the words hit harder than if she’d shouted. They landed like a stone in Agatha’s gut, brutal in their honesty, slicing through the quiet with surgical precision. There was no blame in them, only a raw ache, the kind that made Agatha want to hide and confess in the same breath.

 

She inhaled sharply, her throat tight, and exhaled like it physically hurt to speak. “I—I don’t know, okay?”

 

Her shoulders curled inward as if trying to make herself smaller, like the weight of Rio’s gaze was too much to bear.

 

“I was so scared of what could happen if we stayed together… We were fighting so much, Rio,” she whispered, the words falling like ash between them. “We were angry all the time. Or exhausted. Or pretending we weren’t both hurting. I—I knew at some point I was going to end up hurting you worse than I already was.”

 

Her voice cracked at the edges, like an old film strip stretched too thin.

 

“I couldn’t bear the idea of losing you… not because I messed up and couldn’t fix it… but because I stayed. Because I put my weight on something already falling apart. I thought if I let it go first, it would hurt less.”

 

She shut her eyes, as if it might protect her from what she was saying—or from how Rio might look at her afterward. But she could still feel the bed dip slightly as Rio shifted closer, and then a hand came to her cheek. Not forceful. Not demanding. Just there. Warm. Steady. Forgiving in a way Agatha didn’t feel she deserved.

 

The softness of it cut deeper than anything else. Rio’s thumb brushed lightly along the edge of her jaw. 

 

“You don’t get to do that,” she said. Her voice was quiet, but it carried so much weight. Like a verdict. Like truth wrapped in grief. “You don’t get to make that decision for both of us.”

 

Agatha’s eyes opened, slow and reluctant, glassy with unshed tears.

 

“Things were hard. Yes,” Rio continued, unwavering. “I won’t pretend otherwise. We were exhausted. We were stubborn. We stopped listening and started trying to win. And yeah, we said some things I’d give anything to take back.”

 

She paused, a breath catching in her throat before she forced herself to go on.

 

“But I still believed in us. Even then. I still wanted to choose you. I wanted us to keep choosing each other—over and over, no matter how hard it got. And you didn’t even give me the chance to try.”

 

Agatha flinched, almost imperceptibly. She tried to pull away—not out of rejection, but instinct. A need to run. But Rio didn’t let her go. Her fingers stayed steady, grounding her.

 

“You were so afraid of hurting me,” Rio said, “that you ended up hurting us both. You thought walking away would be mercy. But it wasn’t. It was devastation.”

 

Agatha could feel something inside her breaking open, something that had stayed sealed all those months like a tomb. And now Rio’s voice was reaching inside it, warming it back to life.

 

“You were my soft spot, Agatha” she said, voice thick with emotion. “The one place that made me feel safe in this world. Where I could land, no matter how high or far I fell.”

 

The silence that followed wasn’t empty. It thrummed with everything they’d never said—until now.

 

“I meant it,” Rio whispered, her hand sliding down to rest gently against Agatha’s thigh. “All those times I told you I loved you. Every single one. You’re the love of my life, Agatha.”

 

Her voice cracked now, and it made Agatha blink, made her reach for something to hold on to.

 

“That never changed,” Rio said. “I didn’t need eight months apart to figure it out. But those eight months—they showed me what my life looks like without you. And it’s… god, it’s fucking awful. I wake up reaching for you. I keep hearing your laugh in the crowd. I cook something and look up, expecting you to walk into the room and steal a bite. I’ve been living with this… phantom pain. Like losing you cost me a limb.”

 

Agatha couldn’t speak. Couldn’t move. The ache in her chest bloomed so big it nearly swallowed her whole.

 

“I kept breaking,” Rio finished, eyes shining. “And the worst part is, I got used to the pain. But I never stopped wanting you back.”

 

That was when Agatha’s tears finally spilled over.

 

Her breath hitched, shoulders trembling as silent, devastating sobs escaped her lips—not loud, not wild, but gripped with such profundity that it felt like her very soul was breaking free. It was a quiet storm, heartbreaking and pure.

 

Without hesitation, Rio responded, pulling her into her embrace with fierce tenderness. Her fingers weaved into Agatha’s hair, anchoring her as Agatha clung to her, trembling as if she feared losing her again. As if her heart might shatter in the trying to hold on.

 

It was the second time that day Rio held her like this. The second time she had to be the strong one, even though she was the one who should’ve been resting. Healing.

 

“I’ve missed you so much,” Agatha choked out, her voice thick from anguish. “I tried fooling myself—I told myself I’d get used to the silence, that I’d move on, that I’d be okay. But I was lying all along. I never was.”

 

Rio pressed a gentle, trembling kiss to her temple, her grip unwavering.

 

“But I never stopped loving you,” Agatha continued, her voice raw, saturated with tears. “I just… learned how to hide it better. That's all. I was never truly okay.”

 

They remained locked in that embrace, the heavy silence finally broken by the truth that had been buried so long—at last spoken aloud, and shared between them. Rio pulled back just enough to softly wipe away Agatha’s tears with her sleeve, as if wiping away the ache itself.

 

“I know, cariño,” she whispered, her voice husky with pain. “Me too.”

 

And at last, Agatha felt the crushing weight inside her begin to lift, no longer feeling like she was sinking into an endless ocean, drowning in despair. No, now she felt lighter, freer. 

 

Like she was floating, weightless.

 

“I know we still have so much to say,” Rio murmured after a beat, her words barely more than a gentle breath between them, “but can we… just for a little while, put this aside?”

 

Agatha nodded before the words could even form in her throat. She didn’t trust herself to say much—not when everything in her ached with the weight of the day, and all she really wanted, desperately, was to hold Rio again like she used to.

 

To press her lips to hers and forget, even if only briefly, everything they’d endured until they got here. She leaned in slowly, her forehead touching Rio’s, breath mingling in a shared, tender space.

 

She hesitated, not closing the distance, lingering there—one breath away, lips parted in silent invitation, waiting for Rio to choose.

 

And Rio surged forward, capturing Agatha’s mouth in a kiss that was soft at first, slow and cautious. But then, it deepened—fierce and desperate, as if she feared Agatha might slip away again if she didn’t kiss her hard enough, fast enough, with the urgency of longing. Like she was making up for every lost day in a single, searing touch.

 

Agatha melted into it, into her, carefully grasping at Rio’s waist, softly threading her hand through Rio’s hair, pulling her close and closer still, chasing that familiar taste of her.

 

But then she felt Rio flinch, a sharp, involuntary twitch of pain, and Agatha pulled back immediately with a laugh that was half fond, half exasperated.

 

“Okay,” she whispered, breathless and smiling through her tears, “I think we’ve had enough strenuous activity for one day, baby.”

 

Rio groaned softly, but her grin was unmistakable—full of love, teasing, and something infinitely more fragile.

 

“Yeah, yeah,” she said with a mock sigh, “God, you’re no fun.”

 

Agatha arched an eyebrow, a playful glint in her eye. “Hey, I’m just trying not to break you before we even get back together.”

 

They both laughed and it felt like what had been cracked open between them was carefully starting to mend together again.

 

Agatha reached for the covers and helped Rio settle in, tucking her in gently like she used to. She turned off the bedside lamp, then slid in beside her, the mattress dipping as she got comfortable.

 

Rio reached out, curling into Agatha’s side instinctively, head on her shoulder, hand resting over her heart. Agatha pulled her close and kissed the top of her head.

 

They didn’t need any more words right now. There was just the quiet rhythm of their breathing, the warmth of each other’s bodies, the steady beat of Agatha’s heart under Rio’s hand.

 

Tomorrow will be another day, but that night, for the first time in months, they allowed themselves to fall asleep in each other’s arms—peaceful, whole, home.
