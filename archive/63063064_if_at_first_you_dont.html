The first time Rio proposes, it’s entirely on accident.

They’re dumb twenty-something-year-olds in college, so of course Agatha doesn’t take her seriously. That’s fine. It’s to be expected, even. Rio knows, with a certainty carved down to the place where she’s raw, unfinished, where the ancient Greeks used to say their gods tore whole souls in two, cursing mortals to spend their flickering, candle flame lives searching for the other halves of their spirits, that she’ll get the chance to ask Agatha this question again.

 

(And again, and again, and again.)

 

 

They meet in freshman year, at a trivia night being hosted by bar that doesn’t look too closely at anyone’s IDs.

 

Rio, even while being three sheets to the wind, manages to carry her team through a botany-based section called Unusual Edibles. Though after that, she’s lost, offering random answers with more enthusiasm than accuracy as the team two tables down quickly takes the lead in Which Witch Said This? and Baddies Through The Ages.

 

They’re being led with almost military efficiency by a dark-haired girl with the bluest eyes that Rio’s ever seen in her life. She never thought she’d be the kind of person to think in literary clichés, but she kind of wants to drown in them.

 

“What?” 

 

Rio turns to find Billy giving her an oddly questioning look. 

 

Oh, shit. Did she say that out loud?

 

“Yes,” Billy continues, taking shameless advantage of Rio’s alcohol-slowed reaction times to pat her on the head without getting his arm ripped off. “And you’re still speaking out loud, in case you weren’t aware.”

Or of approaching Agatha Harkness, who Billy says he knows from a shared history class.

 

So, while Agatha’s team loudly celebrates their win, Rio wobbles over with every intention of introducing herself to the undisputed victor of trivia night at Josie’s.

 

“Hi,” she says, after tapping the other girl on the shoulder and watching her turn with unblinking focus. Then Rio sticks out her hand, where a single purple iris has sprouted to life in the center of her palm, and attempts to make her case. “You’re really smart. This is for you.”

 

Agatha laughs, though not unkindly. It’s more of a surprised burst of sound. Rio mentally congratulates herself at the sight of Agatha’s smile. The flower she’s still holding practically glows as the fingers of Agatha’s left hand dip down to brush the petals. At first, her touch is tentative, inquisitive. Hesitant in a way that makes Rio wonder if the other girl is unused to receiving gifts. Rio decides to work on correcting that in the future. When the flower turns out to be just a flower—albeit a flawless, magically produced specimen of its kind—Agatha takes it from Rio with a nod.

 

“Thank you,” Agatha replies, slightly hoarse from shouting answers and cheering with her team. Rio’s gaze ping-pongs between her mouth and her eyes, unable to decide which perfect feature deserves her attention more. She feels downright entranced. Logic dictates that Rio should go home right now and check herself over for signs of accidental spell-casting. Magic, and not just an inadvisable level of alcohol consumption, might be messing with her brain. It’s happened before: a wannabe sorcerer in General Casting once shot a spell off wrong and basically concussed Rio into only being capable of speaking in Pig Latin for hours. “Rio Vidal. And you’re Agatha Harkness,” Rio declares, just the right side of intoxicated to think that this is a very smart fact to prove she knows—and feeling vaguely as though she deserves some sort of reward for saying it.

 

The corners of Agatha’s eyes crinkle.

 

“That I am.”

 

 

Rio proposes for the second time on graduation day.

 

It’s hot as balls in the stadium, and Rio’s last name means that she’s going to be stuck waiting around longer than most. Her only saving grace is the knowledge that the G’s are coming to a close, which means that Agatha’s going to be accepting her diploma soon. In a room this large, Rio’s distance from the stage basically reduces it to a big watercolor blur—but that’s what the Jumbotrons are for. A bunch of cameras are aimed directly at the main action, feeding into several live displays being filmed and broadcasted at various angles. The wonders of modern technology. 

 

(Rio would’ve army-crawled up to the front to watch otherwise. She’s not going to miss a second of Agatha’s walk.)

 

From the moment that Agatha appears on one of the screens, Rio leaps to her feet and starts screaming like her life depends on it, hooting and cheering for all she’s worth. She doesn’t stop until someone physically drags her back into her seat. Even then, Rio’s unrepentant. 

 

Agatha’s a vision to behold.

 

(Of course, this is putting it lightly. Rio’s of the firm belief that poems should be written, odes should be sung, and dramatic duels involving pistols and the possibility of facing a bloody death at dawn should be fought in the name of how unfairly divine Agatha Harkness looks at any given point in time.)

 

Killer heels, perfect hair, purple dress peeking out in flashes beneath the black university graduation gown. Mouth crimson-painted and grinning, the blue of her eyes almost entirely obscured from how wide her smile is, stretching practically ear to ear. Just before she leaves the stage, Agatha winks and blows a cheeky kiss towards one of the cameras. Rio’s heart cartwheels gleefully at the gesture, blowing air horns and popping bottles of champagne, battering itself against bone. 

 

They’re done. They’ve graduated. They actually fucking made it. Agatha’s going to use her fancy law degree to get a job as a high-powered attorney somewhere in the city and Rio’s going to follow like the loyal hound she is. She’s more than content to sit back and bask in the glory of watching Agatha Harkness make her mark on the world.

 

“Dude,” comes the exasperated voice of the kid who’d yanked her down. Mark? Mick? Wait, she’s got it—Max. Rio glances him as he motions towards the ground. “Can you chill out?”

 

A mossy carpet of flowers has sprung to life on the hardwood flooring, forming a circle at least twenty feet wide with Rio sitting damningly at its epicenter. Azure bluets, purple asters, Celandine poppies. Serenity, faith, brighter days ahead. Rio’s happiness and hope, spilling out for the world to see.

 

“Oh.” 

 

Well, she’s not going to apologize for it. Love is never going to be something that Rio will allow herself to feel shame for. She does, however, concede to willing the magical display out of existence with a wave of her hand—though not before plucking and pocketing a few of each bloom.

 

Afterwards, Rio doesn’t bother sticking around to shake anyone’s hands or say goodbye. Everyone that she cares about is going to stay in her life, and she’ll see most of them later tonight at Josie’s. Rio makes a direct beeline for the parking lot instead, where she finds Agatha waiting for her. 

 

Agatha’s sitting on the hood of Rio’s car, swinging her legs and smiling impishly as she watches Rio approach. When Agatha hops back down to the ground, brandishing her diploma excitedly, Rio just can’t help herself.

This—here, now, held safely in the hearth-fire warmth of Agatha’s tender gaze, a flower favored by the sun—is the truest place where Rio belongs.
