Summary

“Oh this is too good,” Jen sips her drink in amusement. “Agatha Harkness. Jealous. I never thought I’d see the day.”

“I’m not jealous,” Agatha grinds out.

“Sure, you just look like you want to strangle that woman with Rio with your bare hands for no reason.”

Agatha would very much like to do that, but not because she's jealous. Definitely not.

Rio stumbles out of a bar in Boston and crashes into a lamppost with a groan. Hela chuckles behind her and leans against the wall of the building.

“What the hell was in that drink?” Rio’s vision is cloudy and she feels like her mind has been submerged in fog.

Hela shrugs, “Just regular Asgardian brew.”

They both know it's a lie. Death wasn’t susceptible to any normal alcohol in any realm. Rio glares at her friend, “You poisoned me.”

The Goddess of Death scoffs, “Please, you’ll be fine.”

“This is why I don’t go to Asgard.”

“You used to before a certain annoying mortal witch made you obsessed with earth.”

Rio growls through her drunk and dizzy state, “I told you never to mention her.”

Hela rolls her eyes, “As if she’s not on your mind 24/7.”

The green witch laughs, “Jokes on you, it’s 25/8.”

“You’re so drunk.”

“And whose fault is that?” Rio stumbles toward the street.

Hela sighs and follows Death, “Where are you going?”

“Do you think texting Agatha still counts as not showing her my face?” Rio looks at Hela with so much hope that the Goddess of Death almost feels bad. Gross.

“I thought we weren’t talking about her.”

“But I miss her!” Death pouts. “Let me find a phone.” The green witch wanders into the streets to find a phone to steal off a nearby human.

Hela reaches out and grabs her lovesick friend. “Oh no you don’t. I’m not dealing with you scaring the living daylights out of more mortals on the last day of the year. And you’re not going to text her!”

Rio huffs and sits on the city curb which is covered in snow and dirt. Hela wrinkles her nose and just stands above Death with her arms crossed.

“Why doesn’t she want me?” Rio asks her friend brokenly.

Hela sighs. She had heard this question at least 133 times in the last three centuries and she was really running out of bs answers at this point. She wishes she could personally go and threaten Agatha Harkness herself, but she’s sure Rio would permanently banish her from hell if she did, so she was stuck being the personal therapist for Death itself. Not exactly what Hela signed up for when she betrayed her father to become Goddess of Death.

“Because you make yourself too available.”

Rio holds her head and leans against a trash can. “What’s that supposed to mean?”

“You always run after her over the smallest crumbs. Now she counts on that. You have to let her come to you.”

“Ha, she’s not going to do that. Besides, she said she never wants to see me again.”

Hela rubs her forehead, “This is why I gave you that drink. It’s New Years Eve, Rio! You could have any woman you want! Hell, there were like ten women throwing themselves at you in that bar!”

Rio shakes her head, “I don’t want them though.”

The Goddess of Death huffs and pulls out another drink concoction from her pocket. “I really didn’t want to do this.”

Death eyes the drink warily, “What is that?”

Hela shrugs, “Something I stole from Heimdall.”

“Why is it rainbow colored?”

“It’s gay.”

“Ha ha. I’m not drinking that.”

“Funny you think you have a choice.”

“Hela.”

The Goddess of Death holds out the drink. “Consider this payback for all the relationship counseling I’ve given you.”

“You told me to murder Agatha like an hour ago.”

“Exactly. Fantastic advice. Would solve all your problems.”

“It really wouldn’t.”

“Just drink the drink.”

Rio frowns at the suspicious bottle, “What will it do to me? It won’t make me forget her, will it?”

“No.”

Rio looks at her friend skeptically.

Hela rolls her eyes, “Even I’m not that cruel. No matter how much easier that would make my life.”

“What will it do then?”

“Just drink it. I swear it’s for your own good.”

Death frowns at the bottle.

“Don’t make me call a celestial here to force you to drink it,” Hela threatens.

“Ha, as if they can make me do anything.”

“Rio.”

“Fine!” The green witch reaches up and grabs the drink from her friend. “But if this kills me, I’ll kill you.”

“Sure thing.”

Rio huffs and looks at the bottle. She feels a familiar feeling of despair and loneliness in her chest. Another year gone without the love of her life, but this time it feels so much worse than past years. This time it feels permanent.

She downs the drink in one gulp.

Immediately her vision blurs and she sways back and forth on the curb until she falls with her face against the pavement. The last thing she sees is Hela giving her a wave before darkness overtakes her.

-------------------------- Rio wakes up with a groan, feeling like she smashed her head through a couple thousand walls and then went to a Lorna Wu concert right after. She blinks and tries to take in her surroundings. There’s a concrete house frame around her and she sees outlines of graffiti on the walls. She shakes her head in disbelief as she realizes where she is. Heimdall’s magical juice had sent her to The Scarlet Witch’s old house in Westview of all places.

Death doesn’t know whether to laugh or cry. She settles for vomiting all over the Scarlet Witch’s old front steps. Fitting. She really should get rid of this inconvenient human form now that Agatha no longer wanted to see her, but she just couldn’t bring herself to make the change. Now she was paying the price.

“Oh my goodness! Are you okay dear?”

The green witch blinks and looks up to see the one and only Sharon Davis looking down at her with concern. Her head spins with confusion. Has she gone back in time? Was this some illusion? A false memory?

Sharon reaches out, “Here let me help you up.”

Rio lets herself be guided to her feet. Though to be fair she’s sure she would’ve immediately keeled over again if not for the other woman steadying her slightly. The green witch nods her thanks. She almost feels guilty knowing she has to reap Sharon’s soul soon, but she had learned long ago that it didn’t do her any good to feel that way.

“You’re new to town aren’t you?”

“Uhh.” The green witch hesitates. She wouldn’t exactly call herself a resident of Westview.

“Moved in over there?” Sharon points at a house down the street and Rio’s imaginary heart stutters. It was Agatha’s house.

“Moved in?”

“Oh it’s okay you don’t have to act coy dear. I’m not prejudiced or anything. Plus Agnes has been a way nicer neighbor since you arrived.”

“Agatha.” Rio corrects absently, head reeling. She lived here?

“Who?”

The green witch ignores the question, too confused to explain anything to Sharon.“I better be going. But uh, thank you.”

“No problem dear. Oh and Agnes said you liked flowers so if you ever want to garden with me I’d love the company!”

Death stares at the elderly neighbor. “She said that?”

“Yes, she’s quite smitten with you. It’s so endearing.”

Confusion continues to swirl in Rio’s head. Maybe she had gone back in time to when Agatha was still under Wanda’s spell? That made sense if Sharon was still alive and referring to her as Agnes, but she doesn’t think Agatha would be talking so nicely about her even under that spell and she knows for sure she didn’t live here during that time.

“Right, well, maybe I’ll stop by to garden sometime.”

“That would be lovely.”

A pang shoots through Rio’s chest as she nods and turns away. That was a neighborly interaction that she knew she would never have. Almost as if she and Agatha were a real couple settled in the suburbs. She wonders if Heimdall’s drink was actually a torture potion to show people what could have been. Hela was so going to pay for this.

She half walks, half drags herself to the house at the end of the street. Her heart beat is pounding in her ears by the time she reaches the door. She hesitates for a second, unsure if she wanted to know what laid on the other side. She had a feeling it would break her heart even more, but that had never stopped her with Agatha before, and it wasn’t going to stop her now.

She pushes the door open.

“You’re home early.”

Rio stares at the back of the woman who sits on the couch reading a spellbook. She knows it’s Agatha. No other being in the universe made her feel like this. Yet something feels very wrong.

Agatha pauses at the unusual silence and sets the book down to stand up and look at the woman in the doorway. When they lock eyes, it feels like they’re meeting for the first time all over again.

“Rio.”

There’s so much emotion laced into the word that the green witch almost stumbles backward. She can’t remember the last time Agatha said her name like that. With so much love and understanding and acceptance. And yet she can tell by the look on the purple witch’s face that Agatha knows something is off too.

Rio hesitates, trying to find the right response. “Agnes.”

The spirit witch scoffs, “That spell was broken long ago.”

Death frowns in confusion, “Then why did that gardener lady call you that?”

Agatha smirks, and hell if this is an illusion Rio wonders how the creator managed to encapsulate the woman in front of her so perfectly. “Got to keep up some air of mystery.”

Rio shakes her head and looks around the place. It’s then that she notices the details. Spellbooks are scattered throughout the living room and Agatha’s many coats are draped on various chairs. Yet the main things that catch her eye are the well-kept plants in the window (Agatha couldn’t keep plants alive to save her life) and the green hoodie on the table (something she’s sure Agatha would never be caught dead in). Did she really live here?

The spirit witch seems to notice Death’s confusion and slowly walks around the couch to be a few steps closer. Rio hates the way her stomach flips. She also hates how Agatha looks at her like she’s a wounded animal to tiptoe around. That was a new look, and Rio had already decided she didn’t like it.

The purple witch gives Death a once over. “You look…different than I expected.”

Rio shifts uneasily under the scrutiny, “What’s that supposed to mean?”

“It means you look like shit.”

Despite the situation, Rio pouts, “You’ve never complained about how I look before.” Agatha chuckles, “I'm not complaining. Just stating facts.”

Death frowns and looks down at herself. It was true, she did look like shit. Which makes sense considering in the last few hours she’s been poisoned by her friend, wiped out on a disgusting city curb, and thrown up in the middle of The Scarlet Witch’s dumpster house. She flushes in embarrassment and raises her hand to call on her magic to clean herself up.

“Wait, don’t.” Agatha holds up her hand.

“Don’t what?”

“Change.”

Death’s eyebrows furrow. “Why?”

Agatha just studies her like she’s an artifact at a museum. “Because this look tells a story.”

“And what story would that be?”

“Well, I was kinda hoping you’d tell me that.”

Rio scoffs, her patience starting to wear thin. “What is this Agatha? Some kind of hex you and that boy have imprisoned me in?”

The purple witch raises an eyebrow, “Billy is dead.”

Death blinks and frowns. That didn’t sound right. It wasn’t how the story went. She would know. She had practically written that story. “Then what is this place? Do I…live here?” The green witch hates the hopefulness in her own voice.

“Yes and no.”

“Helpful as always,” Rio rolls her eyes.

“You know, for a cosmic entity you’re being a bit dense,” Agatha says playfully.

Rio grits her teeth. This woman was the most infuriating being in the universe. “Wow, insulting my appearance and intelligence in the span of two minutes. Low blow. Even for you.”

The purple witch raises an eyebrow at the genuine hurt in the green witch’s voice. It cuts to her chest like a knife. She hadn’t heard Rio that hurt by one of her quips in a while, which meant the woman in front of her was still feeling really wounded and vulnerable. Guilt gnaws at the inside of Agatha’s chest knowing she’s the cause of that, but she needs to keep pulling on the thread. “Did you prefer when I blamed you for Nicky?”

Death flinches at the name. “What do you mean when? You still do!”

Agatha winces. So her theory was true. She hadn’t forgiven Rio yet. And now here the green witch was, looking like absolute death because of it. “I don’t.”

Rio scoffs, “Cut the bullshit Agatha. The last thing you said to me was you never wanted to see my face again!”

“I said that?”

The sound of genuine confusion and regret in the purple witch’s voice makes Rio pause. What the hell was going on? “Yes you said that! What is this? Do you have amnesia or something?”

“No.”

Death throws her hands up in annoyance.

“Rio, do you really think I’m her?”

The green witch freezes at that and squints at the woman in front of her. What was she implying? That this Agatha was an imposter? Someone else just playing the part? Surely Death would’ve seen through that. She was one of the most powerful beings in the universe and she’d like to think she knew the woman she had pined after for centuries well enough to know that Agatha was very real.

“I know you’re Agatha Harkness. No one else makes me feel like this.” Rio mumbles the last part, as if it was only meant for herself.

The purple witch softens at the words, “I mean yes, I’m Agatha Harkness. But I’m not your Agatha.”

“My Agatha?” Rio scoffs. “What is this, some joke that you’ll never be mine?”

The spirit witch sighs and resists the urge to reach out and touch the green witch’s face. She decides to just come out with it. “No, I am yours Rio. In every universe.”

Death’s eyes widen as she stumbles back and hits the wall. Okay maybe she had been being a bit dense because of course this was another universe. That was the only logical explanation for how Agatha was treating her right now. The one in her universe wanted nothing to do with her and never would.

“You’re definitely not mine in my universe.” It comes out more brokenly than Rio intends.

Agatha winces, “Give it time.”

“What, three centuries not enough?”

“If I told you I never wanted to see you again, I’m definitely regretting it.”

“I severely doubt that,” Rio deadpans.

“Wouldn’t I know a bit better than you?” Agatha retorts.

Death frowns, “I know you better than anyone, Agatha.”

“That’s true about most things, but not this.”

Rio scoffs and looks around the room. “So we’re together in this universe?”

The spirit witch hesitates, knowing the answer would hurt the woman in front of her. “Yes.”

Death laughs emptily as she feels pain and jealousy coursing through her chest. So even in other universes she gets to be happy. It’s just hers where she’s miserable. She always thought that the idea of her and Agatha being together in another universe would make her feel better, but turns out it just makes her feel infinitely worse. “How long did that take?”

Agatha grimaces. “Still centuries.”

“Stubborn in every universe.”

“Would you want me any different?”

Rio doesn’t answer because they both know the truth. She’d never change anything about Agatha Harkness.

“Look Rio, you just need to give other me a little push.”

“I’ve given you plenty of pushes over the centuries.”

“Okay fine, maybe a big push.”

Rio scoffs, “How do you even know? Have you been to other universes before?”

“No but-”

“So how do you know what you feel in mine? Maybe this is the only one where you love me!”

The words seem to hang in the air for a minute. Rio feels pain eating at her chest while Agatha feels guilt. Why did she put the green witch through this much hurt in every universe?

“It’s not the only one where I love you.”

“You don’t know that.”

“I do! I know this,” Agatha gestures between them, “Spans not only time, but timelines. I know there’s nothing else like this for me in any universe, and deep down, you know that too!”

Rio deflates a bit. Sure she always thought what she and Agatha had was more than just in their timeline and being a cosmic entity magnified that feeling. But she didn’t see any hope for them in her universe anymore.

Agatha sighs and clenches her fists to stop from reaching out to the woman in front of her. She’s sure she loves every Rio in every universe, but this Rio wasn’t hers to love. “You just have to make me see.”

Death scoffs. “How, when you don’t even want to see me again?”

“Why are you listening to what I want for once?”

“Because after three centuries of clues I finally decided to take the hint!” Rio feels her anger rising to the surface along with a flurry of other emotions. She realizes she should be having this argument with the other Agatha, not this one, and yet somehow this still feels cathartic.

“Well undecide that!” The purple witch yells back. Apparently they were good at fighting in every universe too.

“Why! If you want me like you claim, why don’t you chase me for once?”

“I will if you just give me a push!”

“How do you know that!”

“Because that’s what happened between us here!” Agatha gestures frantically around.

Rio pauses, “Really?”

The purple witch bites her lip and nods.

“What happened?”

Agatha wrings her hands a bit, suddenly feeling exposed. “Oh you know, I got the Darkhold. Then lost it. You got pissed. I told you I wished I hadn’t lost it just to stay away from you. You stopped pursuing me and told me you were giving up your job as Death and leaving the mortal world. I panicked. And now we’re here.” She gestures to the house in Westview.

“I said I would leave the mortal world?” Death asks in surprise.

“Mhmm.”

“Is that the push you’re telling me to give other Agatha?”

The purple witch’s eyes widen, “Preferably not. Because I can most definitely tell you that it would make her go borderline insane.”

Rio shrugs, “Doesn’t sound so bad.”

“And nearly rip apart the fabric of the universe trying to stop you.”

“Ah.” Rio sighs in understanding. She didn’t really want to deal with the cosmic fallout she’s sure would come from that ordeal. “So what do you suggest then?”

Agatha thinks for a second and tilts her head, “Some good old fashion jealousy couldn’t hurt.”

The green witch raises an eyebrow, “You want me to make you…jealous?”

The purple witch shrugs non committedly, feeling weird at recommending that the woman she loves make her jealous.

“How?”

Agatha gives her a strange look, “Have you seen yourself? Just go to a bar I’m at or something.”

Rio smirks at that, “I thought you said I looked like shit.”

The purple witch rubs her forehead, “You’re going to hold that over me forever aren’t you.”

“In every universe.”

The two women share a soft smile and lock eyes. Rio knows then that Agatha was right. It was the same for them in every universe. The only difference was the level of tragic their love story was in each one.

Agatha breaks the eye contact and looks toward the door. “You should go. You’ll be home any minute.”

The green witch tilts her head, “What if I want to meet other me?”

The purple witch gives her an unamused look, “Do you really want to see what happens when you find out that another you has been here?”

Rio grimaces. She had a point. Agatha wasn’t the only one in their relationship with a jealous streak. “I don’t even know how to get back.”

Agatha smiles softly. “You’ll figure it out.” Rio can tell the words have a deeper meaning than just getting back to her universe.

The green witch nods and moves toward the door, then pauses and turns back. “I’m glad you’re happy here. And for what it’s worth, I love you in every universe too.”

Agatha feels tears in her eyes and blinks them away. “I know.”

Rio turns and exits the house, forcing herself not to look back. A part of her wished she could stay in this happy ending, but as much as she wanted that, she knew she couldn’t. Despite everything, she wanted the woman back in her universe that she had all that tragic shared history with. The woman that had been the source of all the pain and happiness she had ever felt. Her Agatha.

For a moment she wonders how she’s going to get back, but suddenly she feels herself fading and realizes the potion must have the ability to know when the consumer is done needing its effects. She wakes back up with a jolt and a groan as she realizes she’s back on the curbside in Boston where she passed out earlier.

Hela peers down at her with an amused smirk, “Did you find what you need?”

Rio groans, “Was that your plan all along?”

“My plan all along was to get you to get over her but that hasn’t worked in the last 300 years so I figured I may as well try to help you get her back.”

Death feels a vague rush of gratitude for her friend, but still scowls. “You’re going to pay for this hangover you gave me.”

Hela laughs, “Sure, tell me that at the wedding.”

“Agatha and I are already married.”

“At the second wedding.”

Rio rolls her eyes and gets shakily to her feet.

“Where are you going now?”

Death smirks, “To make my wife jealous.”

Agatha couldn’t believe she let Jen talk her into this. She had been perfectly content to spend New Years’ Eve alone with her large selection of alcohol, but the potions witch had shown up at her house and all but dragged her out with Lilia and Alice to a bar in New York. Needless to say, it was an absolute zoo.

Now, she was no stranger to crowds or bars. She had navigated her fair share of wild nights over the centuries, but generally those also involved a certain green witch. Whether she was trying to find new witches to kill or women to flirt with to piss Death off, Rio had always been hot on her heels. She’d never admit it in a million years, but the most thrilling part about going to bars was the inevitability that her wife would always show up and drag her away from her latest target, just for them to fight about it in the alley behind the bar and all the way to the nearest motel room. But now, all of that history is gone.

She hadn’t seen Rio in months, and while it certainly wasn’t the longest they’ve gone without seeing each other thanks to the Darkhold, it feels like the most permanent. She’d spent every conscious minute regretting telling Death to stop pursuing her, and most of her unconscious minutes regretting it as well. She thought that was what she wanted, but turns out she has no idea what to do with her life now that the one constant she had ever had was gone. She couldn’t tell Rio any of this though. Sure, Agatha is being prideful about it, but another part of her thinks that maybe it's just better this way. If they’re not together, they couldn’t hurt each other. No matter how often her coven says that is the most ridiculous thing they’ve ever heard.

Jen sets a shot in front of Agatha. “Drink Harkness. I can’t stand to see that mopey look on your face a second longer.”

Alice nods, already partially drunk. “Yeah seriously, I thought you of all people would like the bar scene. Plenty of easy targets to manipulate. I’m sure you could find one or two witches in the crowd to kill.”

The purple witch gives the others an annoyed look but downs her shot all the same. If she’s going to have to endure life alone from now on, she may as well enjoy the small things in life like the copious amounts of alcohol she’s sure none of them would pay for tonight. “My serial killing days are behind me.”

“I’m telling Billy he made you soft,” Lilia smiles as she sips her martini.

“Please, she just doesn’t want to see the psycho,” Jen laughs, earning a glare from the coven leader.

“No, pretty sure it’s the exact opposite,” Lilia corrects. “She’s scared of how much she actually wants to see Rio.”

“Avoiding trauma for the win!” Alice holds out her glass for a toast to Lilia and Jen.

Agatha grits her teeth, “Change of plans. I’m killing all of you tonight.”

Jen shrugs, “At least then we get to witness the reunion. Nothing like seeing Agatha Harkness lose her mind over Death.”

The coven leader scowls and tries to bite back the flush on her cheeks, “I don’t lose my mind over her.”

Lilia chuckles, “Billy told me you ate the coven list I gave you just to avoid her which is even crazier considering she wasn’t the black heart.”

“Yes well, she had just shown up at my house and tried to kill me with a knife. Anyone would want to avoid that.”

“Nah, sounds hot.” Alice shrugs as she takes another beer. “I wouldn’t mind if she did that to me.”

“Here here,” Jen and Lilia hold up their drinks in agreement.

Agatha clenches her glass so hard she’s surprised it doesn’t break. “Can you all not?”

“Not what?” Lilia asks innocently, a layer of amusement in her tone.

Lust after my wife, Agatha almost says, but she stops at the last second, realizing that is exactly what her coven wanted her to say. They had been trying to get her to face her so- called feelings for months now, and it was honestly getting exhausting. She didn’t have feelings for Rio. And she definitely wasn’t jealous that her coven seemed to appreciate Death’s appearance far too much. She just, didn’t like people objectifying others, yes that was it.

The spirit witch stands up abruptly, “I’m getting another drink.” She disregards the snickers the other witches send her way as she goes to the bar.

“Old fashioned,” Agatha nods to the bartender.

“On me.”

Agatha nearly groans at the sound of a man sidling up to her side. She taps her fingers on the bar impatiently.

The man looks her up and down, “I’ve been watching you all night.”

The purple witch rolls her eyes. She had heard that line plenty before, but the words sounded like nails on a chalkboard coming out of this man’s mouth. There was only one person she truly wanted to be watched by, and it wasn’t him. She remembers how in the past, Rio would already be here by now, scaring the man away with a dagger to his kidney. Agatha had secretly welcomed that. Even in their most bitter moments, neither of them had any time to spare for the men that would hit on them. Besides, she got far more of a rise out of her wife when it was women flirting with her anyway.

The green witch is nowhere to be found now though. Oh how the times had changed. Because of you, Agatha thinks in disdain. She’s not used to admitting to herself she caused something. Actually she actively avoids doing that, but it’s starting to become harder and harder to do so nowadays.

She gives the man a hard glare, “I’ll say this only once. I would strongly advise that you leave.”

The man is undeterred, “Come on. Just one drink.”

“Leave.”

“Why? Are you married or something? Or into women?”

Agatha grabs her drink from the bartender and turns fully to face the man. Purple dances in her eyes. “Yes to both. But even if not, I still wouldn’t be interested in you.”

The man frowns and goes to grab her arm, but Agatha anticipates the predictable move and sends a wave of purple at the man’s head. He falls unconscious at the bar, unnoticed by the rest of the crowd around them.

The spirit witch smirks a bit and glances at the other side of the bar which is now unobstructed by the man. Time itself seems to stop as she takes in the view. A woman, a blonde woman, was sitting across the bar, casually talking to a redheaded woman who was clearly overly interested in the blonde. Agatha doesn’t recognize the face, and she doesn’t much care for blondes, but something about this one makes her freeze. She couldn’t understand it, but something about the blonde just screamed Rio, even if nothing about her looked the same.

She blinks and shakes her head, almost feeling disgusted with herself for feeling so strongly about some random woman across the bar. As much as she’s loath to admit it, this type of feeling had only ever been reserved for her estranged wife, so why was she suddenly feeling it now? Sure the woman was beautiful, but it was the aura around her that just seemed absolutely magnetic without even trying. Just like Rio. Maybe her coven was right. Maybe she had lost her mind.

Her eyes widen even further as she sees the blonde lean in closer to the idiot redhead and stick her tongue in her cheek suggestively. She then fiddles with something in her suit jacket that Agatha can just barely see across the bar. A flower. Holy shit, it was Rio.

For a second she feels joy at seeing the green witch after so long and also relieved she hadn’t just gone crazy over some random blonde for no reason. But soon all that energy gives way to absolute rage. How dare Rio show up here after all this time looking like that? How dare she pretend not to notice Agatha’s existence? And how dare she openly flirt with some other woman? Just because Agatha said she never wanted to see her again, doesn’t mean Rio could be with anyone else.

She glares at the two women getting far too close to her liking and tries to will Rio to look at her but to no avail. She grits her teeth and stalks back to the table with her coven.

“Woah Harkness! What happened in the five minutes you were gone?” Jen asks. “You look like you want to level this place.”

Agatha slams her glass down on the table, “Rio is here.”

That sobers everyone at the table up a bit. “Really? Where?”

Agatha points at the blonde across the bar.

The rest of the coven gives her a strange look.

“Wow you’re more lovesick than I thought,” Jen comments. “That is not Rio.”

“It is.”

“She’s blonde,” Alice states.

“She can shapeshift.”

“Of course she can,” Lilia mumbles.

“How do you know it’s her?” Alice asks.

“I just do.”

“True love.” Jen snickers.

Agatha glares, “I’m going to kill her.”

“Just proving my point.”

“Did she say anything to you?” Lilia interjects before the purple witch can hurl her shot glass at Jen.

“No.”
