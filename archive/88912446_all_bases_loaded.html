Chapter 1: Practice

Agatha Harkness hates two things: Rio Vidal and the heat.

Unfortunately, both were currently present.

The harsh midday sun beat down on the Coven’s practice field, baking the dirt beneath Agatha’s cleats. Sweat clung to the back of her neck, turning her brown hair shades darker. Around her, every member of Salem’s softball team looked miserable, like they’d rather be anywhere else than here right now, all of them waiting for Lilia to finish discussing pitching strategies with Kate.

Agatha resisted the urge to roll her eyes. Surely being Salm’s pitcher in the State Softball League would mean Kate should know what she’s doing.

Agatha has been playing softball for almost fifteen years now, having started at age five when Evanora Harkness decided she had better things to do than look after and spend time with her own daughter on Saturday mornings.

Evanora had expected her to drop the sport after a week, but much to her surprise and horror, Agatha had stuck to it, at first out of spite, but now because it matters to her.

Softball is the one part of her life that Agatha has had complete control over, she knows exactly what the team needs and she knows who to boss around to get things done.

That was until Rio Vidal joined.

Rio arrived at the start of last season and all of a sudden, everyone forgot about everything Agatha had done to help make the team what it is today.

Agatha’s gaze drifted to her left where Rio herself stood, chewing loudly on a piece of bubblegum and messing with her glove, going through the motions of breaking the leather in, despite it already looking ancient.

God, she’s annoying. Agatha thought. Who even chews gum during practice? And that loudly too, it’s disgusting.

“Alright ladies, batter up.” Lilia calls, her voice cutting through the chatter and Agatha's thoughts.

Kamala stepped into the batters box, tapping the end of her bat to the plate before settling into her stance. Agatha got ready at her position at shortstop, halfway between second and third base, her eyes roaming around the infield, watching the others set up. Kate positioned herself at the pitcher's plate bending before winding her arm up and throwing a perfect underarm pitch.

The ball flew through the air before cracking cleanly against the bat, which sent it flying the opposite direction into the shallow centre field.

“Second!” Wanda shouted, but Agatha was already moving.

Her cleats dug into the dirt as she sprinted to second base, never taking her eyes off the neon yellow ball as it arced through the air.

Her glove was outstretched, ready to catch, and right when she knew she got it, there was a shout from somewhere to her right.

The next thing Agatha felt, instead of the ball in her glove, was pain erupting in her body as something solid slammed into her.

The impact knocked the air from her lungs, sending her sprawling into the ground, as dust kicked up around her. The only thing she could hear though, was the ringing in her ears from the force of the impact.

“Jesus-” she wheezed.

A second body hit the ground beside her and Agatha didn’t even need to open her eyes to know exactly who it was.

Oh that fucker.

“Vidal, what the fuck.” She snarled, shoving herself upright and brushing dust off of her jersey from the tumble.

Rio was already sitting up, rubbing her shoulder, likely sore from where it hit Agatha square in the ribs, before shooting Agatha an irritated glare.

“It was my ball, I called it.” Rio argued, pulling herself up to her full height, two annoying inches taller than Agatha.

“It’s not your ball if you call it a second before bulldozing me!” Agatha shot back. “Who even lets such brutes onto the playing field?”

“I cover second base Harkness, the ball was mine.” Rio snarled, stepping closer to Agatha who bristled.

Who does she think she is?

“How can the ball be yours if I was already there?” Agatha shouts now, throwing her hands up in the air and rolling her eyes.

Rio scoffed ready to respond, while the rest of practice had frozen around them, everyone watching them argue once again.

“Here we go again..” Agatha heard Alice say from the outfield as Jen shook her head beside the shorter woman.

“Can the two of you wrap it up? It won’t kill you to go one practice without starting an argument,” Jen called out to the two of them.

They both turned to her with identical expressions of outrage, talking over one another as they tried explaining to Jen.

“It clearly wasn’t my fault-”

“I’m second baseman-”

“LADIES.” Lilia’s voice bellowed across the field.

The entire field fell silent and the two of them turned to see Lilia striding across the green, practically seething. They slowly stepped away from one another, chests still heaving from the argument before turning to face Lilia properly.

“What was that?” Lilia asked, and when both of them opened their mouths to argue she held a hand up. “No, I don't want to hear excuses, I don’t care who’s catch it could’ve been, neither of you caught it.”

“I don’t care who was right or who’s ball it was.” Lilia continued. “I care that in a championship game that’s two runs scored while the two of you are lying in the dirt screaming at each other.”

The words hung in the air as both of them exchanged glances before frowning and looking away.

“You two are the most talented people I’ve seen on the field in a long time.” Lilia sighed. “But talent won’t win us championships.”

Her gaze swept over the entire team, everyone now watching intently. “A functioning team is what will win us a championship.”

Lilia pointed between the two of them.

“The two of you have been at each other’s throats since Rio joined the team. I’ve ignored it because I thought it was a settling period and you’d grow out of it, but it’s clearly gotten worse.”

Rio looked away first.

“Coach I-”

“No.” Lilia interrupted. “Listen very carefully because I’m only going to say this to you both once.”

She waited until they both looked at her again.

“We have the state league in three months. You two play side by side, but if you can’t trust each other enough to call a fly ball without ending up in a pile in the grass, then I don’t think you can play beside each other when it matters.”

Agatha felt her stomach sink as she heard Rio intake a sharp breath of air.

“If I see either of you pull something like this again, you’re both benched for future games. Now go run ten laps and reflect on your actions,” Lilia said pointing at the warning track.

Agatha let out a loud sigh before turning on her heel and jogging to the warning track that circled the field. She heard Rio behind her mutter something that sounded suspiciously like a curse word in Spanish under her breath and she rolled her eyes.

The two of them ran in silence, neither of them speaking for nearly half a lap. The only sounds were the rhythmic thumps of their cleats against the dirt as they both slowly started speeding up, unconsciously racing each other and refusing to come last.

“So.” Rio eventually broke the silence, panting slightly. “Are you planning on apologizing to me?”

Agatha barked out a laugh, throwing her head back and speeding up to overtake Rio.

“For what?”

“For running into me, for being the reason neither of us got the ball.”

Agatha scoffed, the absolute audacity of this woman.

“I was already running for the ball, you bodyslammed me!”

“You weren’t watching where you were going!” Rio shot back indignantly.

The jog Lilia had intended them to run had turned into a full sprint by now, neither of them willing to give the other the satisfaction of winning their mental race. There were no more words exchanged between them, both of them focusing their energy into not falling behind the other.

Agatha could see Rio out of the corner of her eye, her jaw set and her dark hair curling slightly at the end where some flyaways had escaped her ponytail. Rio was focusing intently on the track in front of her, speeding up slowly, as if Agatha wouldn't notice that.

Agatha surged ahead, and Rio, seeing the movement sped up too.

For the next three laps they kept trading the lead, each refusing to let the other stay ahead for too long, and by the time they crossed the sixth lap Agatha could see the rest of the team watching them during their water break, talking quietly, likely about how the two of them are now full out racing each other instead of reflecting on their actions as Lilia had intended.

Agatha could hear Rio wheezing slightly beside her, and by lap eight the silence was finally broken again.

“You know…” Rio gasped, “This would really… be a lot easier… if you admitted… that you were wrong.”

Agatha scoffed. “You’re delusional. You’re barely breathing and you’re delusional.”

Rio huffed, rolling her eyes and speeding up as they finally rounded the corner for their last lap, both of them too exhausted now to continue on with their insults, just running quietly beside each other, the only sound being their cleats in the dirt and their loud panting.

When they finally crossed their tenth lap, both of them slowed down to a walk, hands braced on their knees as they caught their breath, wheezing loudly.

Lilia glanced up from where she was staring at her clipboard, looking at the two of them.

“Are you two done?”

“Yes coach.” They replied simultaneously.

“Good.” Lilia pointed to the dugout. “Get some water then I’m going to talk about our game we have next week in Worcester.”

Agatha grabbed a bottle from the cooler without another word and right as Rio reached for one, their hands brushed.

Agatha snatched her hand away immediately like the touch burnt her, moving away from the cooler and letting Rio dig around for her own bottle before walking away.

As Rio turned away she brought the bottle up to her mouth, spraying a stream of the water into her open mouth, some of it dribbling down her chin onto her jersey.

What the hell.

Agatha felt something stir low in her belly at that view. Annoyance. She’s obviously annoyed that Rio refused to just sip normally from the bottle, that's all it could possibly be.

There’s no need for her to pour the water into her mouth like that. Agatha thought furiously, chugging from her own bottle. Fucking Vidal just has to be different all the time.

She finally puts her bottle down before jogging to Jen and Wanda, refusing to acknowledge Rio who’s laughing with Natasha and Hela.

She’s not even that funny, why do the other two look like she’s told the best joke they’ve ever heard?

“Alright ladies, gather around,” Lilia calls them.

“As you all know we’re going to be spending the night in Worcester in three days, and I have created room assignments for you since some of you argue too much,” she looks pointedly at Agatha and Rio, while the rest of the team snickers.

“And others don’t get enough sleep when together because they're too excited about the sleepover,” she continues, now turning to Kate and Kamala who look sheepish.

Lilia flips her paper on her clipboard, and all the girls shift forward in anticipation at who they’re going to be roomed with, before she starts calling out names.

“Yelena and Kate.” Kate blushes slightly while Yelena smiles at her.

God everyone can see Kate’s fat crush on the blonde.

“Natasha and Hela.”

Both of them smirk at each other while Rio groans quietly.

Ha, serves her right for being an ass.

“Kamala and Wanda”

Okay, not a problem, Jen’s still there to room with Agatha

“Jen and Alice.”

Oh no. Oh absolutely not.

“Coach what!” Agatha bursts out. “You just said that Vidal and I argue too much, why’re you putting us in a room together?”

“Yeah I don’t want to be in that room,” Rio adds on indignantly.

“Too bad ladies.” Lilia responds without looking up from her clipboard. “You two need to fix your problems and clearly nothing else is working out so now here we are.”

Agatha can’t believe it. Today might’ve just been the worst day of her life. Not only did she get bodyslammed by the biggest idiot on the team, she also had to run ten laps with said idiot, and now she has to share a room with her for their next match? Things could not possibly get worse than this.

God, Agatha Harkness hates Rio Vidal.

Three days later Agatha walks to the practice arena and sure enough, the bus is right there, waiting for her, almost taunting her. 

 

Her teammates were already there, most of them rushing around and gathering last minute items they might need for the game tomorrow. 

 

Natasha and Alice were wrestling with a crate full of their helmets, trying to shove them into the bus storage amongst everyone's duffles while Kamala is digging frantically through her open duffle 

 

Agatha throws her duffle into the storage right after the helmets before making her way to where Lilia’s standing, occupied with her checklist.

 

“Boo.” Agatha whispers, sneaking up beside Lilia, causing the older woman to jump and swear, clutching her chest.

 

“Agatha what the hell.” Lilia complains, exhaling heavily and shaking her head.

 

“Is there absolutely nothing I can possibly do to convince you to change the room assignments coach?” Agatha asks, batting her eyes and reaching out to fiddle with the hem of Lilia’s shawl that’s draped over her shoulders.

 

“You know that flirting with me will never work.” Lila bats Agatha’s hands away from her shawl, looking back down at her clipboard for a second before looking back at Agatha with a startling sincerity in her eyes.

 

“Rio’s really not that bad, you know.” Lilia’s eyes bore into Agatha. “I think you just need to give her a chance and you two would get along like a house on fire.”

 

Agatha huffs. Why would anyone think that?

 

“We would not.” She snaps back. “We argue too much and Rio’s way too cocky and annoying and-” She cuts herself off with a growl, not able to articulate just why rooming with Rio would be a terrible idea.

 

“Just get on the bus.” Lilia sighs looking back down at her clipboard and leaving Agatha no room to argue back. 

 

Fine I’ll get on the stupid bus, damn.

 

Upon boarding the bus Agatha notices that most of the seats are already taken, save for two around the middle of the bus, so she slides into the window seat before putting her headphones on, ready to relax for the next one and a half hours. 

 

Soon everyone was piling onto the bus, sitting together and joking around. Agatha ignored most of them, not willing to take her headphones off yet, when there was a tap on her shoulder and she turned away from the window to face Rio's smirking face. 

 

“God, no, anywhere but this seat, please.” Agatha groaned as Rio laughed, dropping into the seat beside Agatha.

 

“Don’t worry I won’t talk to you.” She reassured with a smirk

 

Agatha scoffed, rolling her eyes and turning back to the window. 

 

The bus ride was fairly uneventful, the only problems Agatha had being having to sit next to Rio, and Rio talking to everyone on the bus. Honestly at that point she might as well talk to the driver too.

 

It started with Alice asking if anyone wanted to play Heads Up on somebody’s phone.

 

“I’m in,” Kamala announced immediately, practically climbing over Kate, to join in.

 

“Ow, relax we all want to play!” Kate complained, moving aside so Kamala could sit in the aisle seat. 

 

Before long, half the bus joined in, shouting clues over one another and dissolving into laughter every time the guesser got it wrong. 

 

Against Agatha’s better judgement, she found herself listening in anyways as Jen propped the phone up for her turn.

 

“Animal!” Kamala shouted out unhelpfully.

 

“Winter Animal.” Kate added.

 

“Penguin?” Jen guessed, frowning.

 

“Bigger!” Rio exclaimed.

“Polar bear?” 

 

The whole bus cheered at the correct answer, with Jen flipping the phone up for the next card. Beside Agatha, Rio laughed, loud enough that Agatha instinctively glanced over.

 

Her head was tipped back against the seat, eyes squeezed shut, and one hand clutching the armrest for balance. 

 

It was so loud. 

 

Honestly, did she really have to laugh with her whole body? 

 

Agatha looked away again, staring back out of the window.

 

“You know.” Rio said after a moment. “You can join us.”

 

Agatha didn’t bother taking off her headphones before replying.

 

“I’d rather walk to Worcester.”

 

“Suit yourself.” Rio shrugged, turning and going right back to shouting out hints for Jen’s next round.

 

Soon Kamala appeared again, this time holding a packet of sour gummies.

 

“Snack?” She offered, holding the pack out between Agatha and Rio.

 

Agatha took one while Rio took three.

 

“Greedy much?” Agatha muttered, side-eyeing Rio’s handful.

 

Rio looked down at her gummies before rolling her eyes and dropping one back into the bag Kamala was still extending.

 

“Happy now?” She asked Agatha, showing off just the two gummies remaining in her hand.

 

“Very.”

 

Kamala blinked, looking between the two of them before slowly retreating, choosing not to get involved in whatever that was. 

 

The bus soon settled again as people drifted into smaller conversations. Alice and Natasha had started exchanging music tastes, Kate was trying and failing to convince Yelena to stop throwing crumpled candy wrappers across the aisle at Hela and Wanda. Jen turned, catching Agatha’s eye from three rows ahead and mimed you okay? 

 

Agatha responded by pointing at Rio and rolling her eyes, to which Jen snorted loudly, turning back around. 

 

By the time they crossed into Worcester, most of the team had quietened down. 

 

Kamala had fallen asleep against the window, her mouth hanging open enough for Kate to take some admittedly diabolical 0.5’s that would definitely surface again, most likely on their group chat as stickers.

 

Alice and Natasha were now whispering a game of twenty questions between them.

 

Even Rio had stopped talking. 

 

Agatha glanced sideways. 

 

The other woman was staring absentmindedly down the aisle, likely looking out of the bus’s windshield. Her arms were crossed over her chest and the gum she’d been chewing since Salem had disappeared. 

 

Peace at last. 

 

The bus rolled off the highway a  few minutes later, winding through Worcester;s streets before pulling into the circular driveway of a nice hotel a short walk from the Softball stadium.

 

The brakes hissed loudly as the bus came to a stop.

 

“Alright everyone!” Lilia called, standing in the aisle. “Grab your bags, dont leave anything behind and remember, you represent Salem. I don’t want any complaints from the hotel staff or other guests.”

 

Groans filled the bus, the girls knowing this meant absolutely no monkey business between rooms. Everyone started standing and reaching up to the overhead compartments for their backpacks and pillows. 

 

Agatha waited until the aisle cleared before stepping out of the bus into the warm afternoon air, taking a deep breath and stretching after the long ride. 

 

Just one night of sharing a room with Rio Vidal, what’s the worst that could happen?

 

A lot apparently. 

 

The second they got their keycards from Lilia and went up to their room their bickering had already begun.

 

“I want the better bed, whichever one that may be.” Agatha declared, speeding up slightly, walking faster through the hotel hallway.

 

“Not if I get there first.” Rio taunted, picking up her pace so she was marginally faster than Agathat.

 

This naturally led to them all out sprinting through the hall until they reached their door, slamming it open and falling into the room from their momentum. 

 

The room itself wasn’t anything special, just two narrow double beds with a bedside table wedged between them. There was a desk beneath the window, a closet tucked into the corner and a washroom just barely big enough for one person to fit.

 

Agatha immediately untangled herself from Rio and dived towards the bed closer to the window shouting in triumph as Rio groaned, dropping her bag onto the bed beside the bathroom wall. 

 

“You are so unbelievably childish.” Rio complained.

 

“You are so unbelievably slow.” Agatha countered, already unzipping her duffle and peering in at her clothes. 

 

Rio rolled her eyes hard enough that Agatha was slightly surprised they didn’t fall out of her head before placing her small suitcase down on the floor beside her bed.

 

“Whatever helps you sleep at night.” Rio muttered.

 

Agatha ignored her, kneeling to unzip her duffle and begin unpacking. 

 

Or at least, she tried doing that.

 

Rio’s suitcase was sitting directly between the two beds, blocking almost half of the already limited floor space. 

 

Agatha stared at the suitcase before turning her gaze to Rio who was seemingly unaware of the issue. 

 

“...Move your suitcase.”

 

Rio didn’t even look up from her unpacking, replying “It’s fine where it is.”

 

“No it isn’t.” Agatha countered.

 

“Yes, it is.” 

 

“It’s in the middle of the room.”

 

“It's on my side of the middle.” Rio pointed out

 

Agatha frowned, staring down at the floor.

 

“There are sides?” She asked.

 

“There are now.” 

 

Agatha looked down again at the invisible line across the floor, supposedly separating their sides of the room.

 

“You’re over the line.” She declared

 

Rio blinked, pausing from where she was pulling her jerseys out for the next day.

 

“No I’m not, how much space have you given yourself on your side?” She asked skeptically.

 

“Enough.” Agatha sniffed. 

 

Rio scoffed, before walking to her suitcase, but instead of pushing it further from Agatha’s side, she nudged it closer to where Agatha was standing.

 

Agatha gasped, rolling her eyes. 

 

“Did you seriously just move it closer?” 

 

“What’re you going to do about it, Harkness?” Rio goaded, smirking at her.

 

Without breaking eye contact, Agatha used the toe of her sneaker to shove the suitcase back across the invisible line, the plastic scraping loudly against the floor. 

 

Rio gasped dramatically, dropping down to inspect the face of her suitcase.

 

“What is the fuck wrong with you?” She snarled out, seeing faint the scratches on the plastic.

 

Agatha shrugged innocently.

 

“Wouldn’t you like to know?” she asked, turning and flipping her hair over her shoulder as she moved back to her duffle to continue unpacking. 

 

Rio shook her head before grabbing her toiletry bag. 

 

“I’m taking the first shower.” 

 

“What? Why?”

 

“Because you just kicked my suitcase.”

“So? We should do rock paper scissors to see who gets to go first.” Agatha complained.

 

Before she could take a step towards Rio though, the other woman disappeared into the bathroom, slamming the door shut, the lock clicking into place a second later. 

 

Agatha scoffed. 

 

“Coward!” She shouted at the closed door, knowing Rio would hear her.

 

The sound of running water answered her. 

 

 

Later that night, after a team dinner and meeting, by the time the two of them got back to the room Agatha was exhausted.

 

The day’s travel finally caught up with her, and all she wanted was uninterrupted sleep before tomorrow's game. 

 

She changed into an old Salem softball T’shirt, brushed her teeth, and collapsed into bed with a deep sigh. 

 

Click.

 

A warm pool of light flooded the room.

 

Agatha cracked an eye open, looking toward the other side of the room where the source of that light was sitting back against the headboard, one knee drawn up and supporting a paperback book she’d pulled from her backpack.

 

Agatha frowned.

 

“Can you turn the light off? Some of us are trying to sleep.” 

 

Rio didn’t even glance up from her page. 

 

“I’m reading.” 

 

“We have a game tomorrow.” 

 

“I’m well aware.” 

 

“Great, well I’d like to sleep.” Agatha scoffed.

 

Rio slowly lowered her book, fixing Agatha with an unimpressed look.

“Oh, I’m terribly sorry sharing a room is such an inconvenience for you.”

 

Agatha didn’t like where this was going.

 

“I forgot that the world revolved around you, Your Highness.” Rio continued. 

 

“Well, someone has to keep it spinning.” Agatha snorted, cutting in and interrupting Rio.

 

The other woman stared at her for a beat, her tongue poking her inner cheek, before she let out a disbelieving laugh. 

 

“You really hear yourself and think you’re God’s gift to Earth, huh?” She asked.

 

Agatha scoffed again, rolling her eyes so hard it almost hurt.

 

“Just turn the light off Rio.” She groaned, rolling over, her back facing Rio, who sighed, but slid a bookmark between the pages before snapping the book shut.

 

Click. 

 

Darkness finally settled over the room and a few quiet seconds passed until- 

 

“Thank you.” Agatha mumbled. 

 

“What was that? Did I just hear the great Agatha Harkness thank little old me?” Rio gasped, sarcasm colouring her voice.

 

“Jesus fuck, stop talking, you’ve ruined it now.” Agatha grumbled, while Rio snickered quietly to herself.

 

Silence soon settled over the room, the only noise being the air conditioner and the occasional rustle from the sheets. 

 

“Night Agatha.” Rio muttered, rolling over. 

 

“...Night.” Agatha responded into the darkness before finally letting sleep overtake her.

 

Rooming with Rio still sucked, but maybe it wasn’t all bad. Who knows what tomorrow will bring?
