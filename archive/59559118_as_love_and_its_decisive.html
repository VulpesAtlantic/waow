Rio had never known rest. No slumber had befallen her since the beginning, since Adam’s first breath. She’d been the snake to whisper in Eve’s ear, to tempt her and doom each of her successors. There was no Satan, unless He was Her–and she didn’t take herself for a devil. Death, as it were, had always just been , no rhyme, no reason, cruel and unyielding. She didn’t question her purpose, the reason behind the actions she was so compelled to take, and everyone was better off.

Since the first day, the moment her new-dead eyes opened to an infinite darkness, she’d been alone. Sometimes she wandered, travelling from place to place to instil chaos and calamity, but only ever to return home and await its inevitable reward. Even as her collection grew, as the number stretched into the billions and further, no one would ever notice anything missing when she was done. She didn’t deal much with the physical, instead she collected only souls–the one thing no one would miss. They appeared at her doorstep in anticipation of her welcome, waiting for her to deliver them to the rest of the unfortunately fated.

There were times when she would venture out not to set in motion one of her divine catastrophes, but instead to see them come to a close. Empires fell before her eyes, entire cities collapsing at her feet leaving in their wake thousands upon thousands of souls for the taking. She liked to wander the empty streets and pluck each one from its host, revelling in the destruction all around her. Why shouldn’t she be the first to walk the ash-covered streets of Pompeii? It was her that nudged Mount Vesuvius into eruption, after all. And, after she’d whisper so sweetly in the captain’s ear to ignore the passing ships’ warnings, what would stop her from roaming the halls of the sinking Titanic?

She felt things such as those, large scale events that would yield many fatalities, ones that would fill her foyer with meandering souls, lost and awaiting direction. Rio always had an idea of when they would be coming–she could anticipate them after so many centuries of practice–, and she still felt a thrill when they finally did. It was rare that she was ever taken by surprise, but not unheard of.

The Salem Witch Trials had been impossible to miss–she’d felt the magic swell beneath her skin each time one of her kin was tied to a post and blasted to dust. It was almost daily, if not more, that she would find the soul of yet another damned witch at her feet. It was routine, it was normal . Until, of course, she felt the magic of eight witches sucked from the atmosphere, funnelling violently toward one. It was a disturbance, to say the least, not a problem by any means, but certainly interesting. Donning her cloak, Rio ventured up to investigate–she’d fetch these souls herself

She emerged from the dirt with very little elegance, her bones cracking grotesquely as she righted herself on two feet. The woods were dark, the moon hidden behind clouds and the canopy high above her, but she knew where she was heading. She trudged carefully through the tall grass and littered twigs for a short while, following the tug of magic at her navel, until she came across a clearing. A large post was erect in the centre, looming and terrifying to everyone but her–she had arrived.

From the trees, she scanned the expanse, and her eyes landed on a young girl, likely no older than 18. She was standing among bodies, all eight that Rio had sensed, and she looked rife with magic. It danced across her skin, little tendrils of fading light slithering along as she drank it in. Rio didn’t need to wonder what had happened–she could feel it, her own green tingling in her fingertips at the sight of such greed. She, too, dealt in excess.

Rio worried for a second that the girl might spot her through the dark, that her eyes might find her just behind the treeline. She had no idea what would happen if she did–the last time someone laid eyes on her, she was forced to take them down, too, as was the desire of something outside of herself. Of course, she understood how bad it would be to have rumours spreading of a delightfully beautiful woman prowling between towns feasting on dead bodies, but she hated having to be the one to exact such a will. Everyone who was misfortunate enough to lay eyes on her alluring form at the wrong time, to gaze at her inviting face when she was at work, was met with a swift demise, their necks smiling red with a deft slice of her dagger, quick and clean.

Rio watched with rapt attention as the girl approached one of the bodies and reached down. Her hand disappeared into the layered fabric around the corpse-woman’s neck and tugged –Rio worried for a moment that the brittle body would break with the force of her hand. When she stood, she held something shiny in her hand that Rio couldn’t quite see, perhaps jewelry of some kind. She didn’t have much time to look, anyway, because a moment later the girl flew away, purple smoke trailing behind her as she shot into the night sky.

For just a moment, Rio searched the clouds for any sign of her, for a glint of purple in the dark, captivated by her power, but found nothing. No stars, no moon, no lavender haze to be seen, just black on black on black. She ignored the disappointment she felt in favour of stepping out from the darkness of the trees and toward the fallen coven, eyeing them with intense interest–she could tell from afar just how drained they were, how desperately devoid of life their bodies would be, but the sight from up close was another thing entirely.

She peered down at the first, then the second, then each one that followed as she rounded the circle. She could barely distinguish one from another, they were so depleted of anything lifelike. It was strange to see such complete emptiness, their corpses all hollow save for the souls she was about to reap, and she couldn’t say she’d seen anything quite so withered in such a large quantity in her time. She’d known the girl had stolen their power, but she had taken their life forces along with it, drained it from each of them until there was not a drop left inside.

Despite the peculiarity of the sight before her, she performed her duties as usual. One by one, she leaned over each woman’s body and took a lock of their hair, slicing it from their heads with her dagger, to release their souls across the river. She felt each one ghost past her face on its way, a gentle thank you for the care and attention, and then they were gone.

Rio felt the girl’s magic again only a day later. It was like she’d been fine-tuned to pick up on it after the previous night, and it beckoned her in like a magnet. Instead of resisting, she let herself be pulled toward it, crawling up from the ground in Salem once again.

She stumbled right into the periphery of a violent witch fight. The girl– woman , she realized, in the daylight–was fighting three witches, all much older than she, and she was winning . It didn’t surprise Rio, no, not after what she’d seen the night before, but seeing it in action was certainly something.

For a young witch to be so powerful at such a young age was quite uncommon, even for a blood witch, which Rio assumed she was. Her purple was strong, and with it she was about to pull the streams of yellow right out of her unwitting opponents. It was cruel and unfair, and Rio couldn’t take her eyes off it.

She felt the magic in the air thrum with renewed energy when it began, the woman’s body glowing a blinding purple everywhere the offensive magic was reaching her. Horror etched itself across the witches’ faces when they realized what was happening, but by then it was too late–less than a minute later they were in a heap on the ground, drained and dead.

The young witch looked quite proud of herself then, glancing down at her glowing hands to watch the magic seep into them. Rio was confident that she’d never seen a witch with such outstanding abilities ever –they might even rival her own. The magic she’d been imbued with at the moment of her conception, at the same moment as the Earth’s first breath, might be threatened by an 18 year old Salemite. As Rio watched from her hiding place, she found herself jealous–jealous of the knowledge that must be required to create such growth, to instil such power in someone so young.

Sure, she was as old as life itself, but, somehow, she remained so desperately young in all the ways that mattered. Amortality granted her eternal existence, but robbed her of time –it moved through her, around her, forcing her to stay still and watch everything pass her by. Wisdom wasn’t imbued onto her as many may insist, she wasn’t governed by rational thought, after all. Instead she operated on impulse, the pull from deep in her stomach, the innate feelings that were so natural to her she didn’t need to question their correctness. That was not knowledge, not truly, and though she learned through observation, there was so much she could not yet fathom.

Observing such a beautiful display of simultaneous mastery and potential reminded Rio of how little she knew. The intricacies of her abilities were truly unknown to her, as were many things, and she ached to know them all, ached to learn and understand and think . She had so much to learn, so much that could never come passively.

The fleeting press of her hand to the side of someone’s head as she pulled it aside to make room for her knife was not touch , not affection, not reciprocal or warm. She yearned to be held, to be thought of fondly like she often saw mourners do–when they crouched next to the dead’s soulless husks and sob, pulling their lifeless form into their chests for one last moment of contact. Companionship, trust, love … she craved them with a hunger so strong she wondered if anything could ever sate it. In her not-heart of hearts, she knew she was not all cruelty and misery.

This woman, this witch , this formidable force of magic in front of her… she could be what Rio was missing. She had no way of knowing if it was the will of the universe, if it was truly the way things should be, but it certainly was her will; the pull she felt toward her was undeniable, as strong as the very essence of time and space that brought her into existence.

“Do you kill everyone so indifferently?” Rio asked as she emerged from the treeline, announcing her presence. She looked down at the corpses on the ground and could feel them reaching for her. “So cruelly ?”

The woman turned, her eyes blazing with fiery glee. “Of course.” Her hand lifted, fingers drenched in purple magic, as she spoke. “Just as I will with you.”

Purple magic hit her with such strength that it pushed her back a few steps. As she stumbled, an amused smile teased at her mouth at the woman’s aggression, at her self-assuredness. Rather than answering her with a blast of her own green, both due to lack of practice and worry of causing any undue harm, Rio took a step forth and extended her hand. In it, a flower materialized, which she promptly offered to the stunned witch before her.

“A green witch,” she murmured, staring down at the blossoming pink petals. She picked it up then, admiring it momentarily before speaking again. “This won’t stop me from killing you, you know.”

Rio laughed. “You need me to blast you back for that, and I won’t.”

The woman froze in place at her words, a shocked look on her face, perhaps surprised she already knew her trick. Rio just laughed again and got to work, leaning over the body nearest to her with her dagger drawn. In plain view of the witch staring right at her, she let the blade cut through a lock of each witch’s hair, then placed them all in her pocket.

“You’re Death ,” Agatha said completely incredulously, her entire face contorted in shock.

“Smart girl.” Rio turned and smiled brightly. “Call me Rio,” she said, holding out a hand for the witch to shake.

“Rio?”

“Spanish for river, like the river that divides your world from mine.”

The witch ignored her offered hand. “Rio…”

“And you are…?” The woman turned on her heel then, stalking away determinedly. “Covenless?” Rio filled in herself, chasing after her with glee. “Power-hungry?”

The witch stopped with a huff. “Listen, if you’re not gonna blast me then go away.”

“Sweetheart, you don’t want me to blast you. It would kill you to take my power.”

“And you’re not here to kill me?”

The confusion painted across the woman’s features at her question was mirrored in Rio’s own head. She hadn’t really thought any of this through, so not used to acting on her own wants. Nothing turned in her stomach as she pursued this witch, as she pestered her and let herself be known, though, no sourness curdled her insides at the interaction, so she must not have been doing too much wrong. Or, she hoped, at least.

“No,” Rio shrugged. “Will you tell me your name now?”

The woman rolled her eyes. “Will you leave me alone if I do?”

“For now.” Rio extended her hand once more and smiled.

The woman took it. “Fine. Agatha Harkness.”

Victory . “Wonderful to meet you, Agatha Harkness.”

For the first time in all of history, Death found herself compelled by something new. Not only was she fulfilling the divine desire of the universe, collecting souls to be returned to the essence of space and time, but she was pulled, too, by the undeniable magic of one Agatha Harkness. She came prancing out of the trees to care for Agatha’s victims only seconds after their corpses hit the grass, nearly humming to herself with the joy of having bodies all but dumped into her lap each time.

“Don’t you have people to tend to?”

It was the first time she’d spoken to Rio in weeks, though she’d been trailing after her power- hungry murder spree day in and day out since their meeting. Preferring to flee at the first sight of Rio, Agatha never stuck around long, so the question took her by surprise.

“I am,” Rio shrugged.

Agatha scoffed. “What the hell does that mean?”

As she reached under her cloak for her dagger, Rio sighed. “I’m sort of everywhere,” she said, gesturing her hands around in the air. “My essence is, at least, I don’t have to be there for every death, that would just get tedious.” She leaned down to take a lock of hair from the nearest body, taking a brief look at it in the light before shoving it in her pocket. “I can wander as I please.”

She looked up to see Agatha’s brow furrowed. “And the hair?”

“My collection?” Rio smirked. “What about it?”

“Why?”

Rio pondered for a moment–it was just another one of those things she never questioned. She knew, of course, that the detachment released the souls from their hosts, but why she held onto them was a mystery, still. “I suppose I’m just sentimental like that.”

Agatha laughed, and the sound was music to Rio’s ears. “I wouldn’t think Death got sentimental.”

“There’s a lot of things about me that would surprise you.”

Agatha seemed thoughtful after that, though Rio only meant it as a joking prod. She finished her job in silence, relieved at the silence around her once she stowed her blade, waiting for Agatha to fill the silence.

She never did, just walked away deeper into the forest. Now would usually be the time when Rio disappeared back into the dirt, into the decay and rejuvenation of the Earth itself, but something gave her pause. Agatha seemed to be softening on her, less repulsed by the idea of her company. Something strange swelled behind Rio’s sternum, something she’d never felt before, and the peculiarity of it was enough to break her routine.

Into the lush greenery, she followed after Agatha. Damp grass gave way underfoot as she went, each blade whispering to her, reaching up to her green magic like it was beckoning her forward, so bright and rife with life she would have stopped to feel it under her palms any other time. Then, though, she was far too preoccupied in her pursuit of the purple witch ahead.

For miles, they walked, Agatha moving steadfastly forward without a glance back. Rio tailed her, matching her pace from a few yards back, never getting closer, but never letting herself stray. Only when Agatha stumbled over a branch at her feet with a pathetic ‘ oof ’, did she finally acknowledge Rio’s presence again.

She tumbled to the ground and groaned, turning her head to glare at Rio at long last. “Are you ever going to stop following me around?”

Rio bit her lip, thrilled for some reason by her frustration. “Are you going to stop leaving a trail of bodies for me to clean up?” She asked in response, eyebrow quirking up.

Agatha groaned again and pushed herself back up to her feet. “I thought your… essence… or whatever , could deal with them,” she spat, leaning down to dust the clumps of dirt from her skirt.

“That’s no fun when I can watch you take them down instead.” Agatha gasped and stood at once, eyeing Rio with a strange look. “What?”

Agatha began to nod slowly, her eyebrows furrowing like she was contemplating something. “So you like watching me? That’s what this is?”

A laugh erupted from Rio’s mouth then, and she nodded, too. “Something like that.”

The inklings of something more had been nagging at Rio’s psyche for a long while. It was just them, a unit, against anything that came their way. Death and her companion; a witch and her familiar.

It was a stark difference from the way they were at the start, when Rio was still trailing after Agatha like a lost puppy, waiting to be able to clean up the mess she left in her glorious wake, and she wasn’t quite sure how they’d gotten there. Somehow, slowly, they just stopped denying the convenience of it all, stopped resisting the inevitable.

Rio had never known friendship, or, well, anything to do with the interpersonal, but she did know that she felt a certain… affection… for Agatha that was as foreign as it was right . Just as she never questioned the demands of the universe, she didn’t question the warmth in her chest for the witch at her side. She never thought twice about the beds they shared nor the lingering touches of their hands, never pondered the meaning of the embraces they shared late at night nor the occasional brushing touch of Agatha’s fingertips to her thigh.

“What does love feel like?” Rio asked, speaking her question into the night air in the dark of a bedroom that wasn’t theirs.

The word was used often, she’d heard it from the mouths of mourners and dying alike. Whether through tears or agony, through despair or fleeting hope, it always had a weight, a significance she couldn’t understand from watching alone. The sound of it in the air didn’t do it justice, couldn’t tell her why it was often the last words spoken between shaking breaths.

Agatha bristled at the question and turned right over in her arms, her eyes boring into Rio’s through the darkness. “Why do you ask?”

“I’m not sure,” Rio replied earnestly. “I’ve just heard the word so many times, and I just realized I don’t know what it is .”

Lips twitching into a gentle smile–the same one she always had when she got to explain a human concept to Rio–Agatha sighed. “Love is the feeling you get when you’re close to someone,” she said simply. “You can love a friend or your family, which is just caring deeply, usually.”

She trailed off, which confused Rio immensely because she could tell Agatha wasn’t done speaking. “Or?”

“Hm?”

“You have more to say, Agatha, I can tell,” Rio prodded, offering her an encouraging smile.

Agatha swallowed. “Sorry,” she said quietly. “You can also love someone differently than that, romantically .”

She let the word off her tongue slowly, like she was waiting for Rio to confirm or deny her knowledge of it, so she nodded. “And that love is quite warm, and usually physical, too.”

Rio let her explanation sink in, repeating her words as she digested them. If love was warm and caring, if love meant physicality, then she loved Agatha Harkness. As she pondered the notion of loving someone, the idea of Death caring so deeply for something that it bloomed into love rattling around in her head, her chest swelled. Agatha was always the answer, she’d come to learn, so it would only make sense for her to love her too.

“I think I love you, then, Agatha.”

Something achingly familiar crossed Agatha’s face then, an expression Rio had seen many times. Softness, such desperate gentleness, coloured her features as it often did, a look of need making a home in her eyes. Only then did Rio understand what it was, a look of love so strong she was shocked she had ever mistaken it for anything else.

When Agatha finally spoke, it was with a voice so hushed Rio had to strain to hear it. “I love you, Rio.”

Then Agatha did something Rio had only ever seen from afar: she brought her hands gently to the sides of Rio’s face and leaned in until they were breathing the same air, until they were so close the only way they could be closer would be to consume each other, and then she did .

Agatha’s mouth covered Rio’s, her lips so exquisitely plush and tender Rio was sure she’d never felt anything like it. It was over as soon as it began, chaste and quick, but the feeling lingered. Even when Agatha turned back over and nestled back into the space under Rio’s arm, the tingling in her lips, the pang of desire deep in her chest remained. Death never slept, but Rio got as close as she had ever gotten then, lulled into a state so serene that all she could do was close her eyes and feel it.

“Are you going to kill me?” Agatha asked one night, like it was something she’d neglected to wonder until just that moment, her voice earnest as she brought her hand to Rio’s chin. “Someday, I mean.”

Agatha was sitting in an armchair next to a fire, neither of which were hers. They belonged to her most recent victim, as did most of their temporary abodes–they were on the move far too much to find one place to settle.

Rio was kneeling on the worn rug at Agatha’s feet with her head resting on her thigh, but she let her face lift with Agatha’s gentle unspoken demand. “I don’t think I can.”

Agatha’s brow furrowed. “I’m not immortal.”

Rio smiled softly, her confusion as charming as her soft touch. “No, mi amor, there are some things that I have come to learn from no source in particular, the divine wisdom of the universe, perhaps, that forbids me from causing your death.”

“What does that mean ?” Agatha looked intrigued as she leaned in, her mouth hovering just before Rio’s.

“Just that I love you.”

Agatha bit her lip. “Say it again.”

“I love you, and I will never love anyone else.”

It was true, so desperately true. To be loved by Death should be impossible, and, yet, it was not. Rio felt it burning from inside her, a fire stoked only by Agatha, a fire that hadn’t existed before her, and would not exist after her. It was a dreadful thought, that of potentially being separated from her equal, from the only being that could understand her, but it didn’t deter her from basking in the feeling. She would stomach the despair of eventual loss, if it ever came to it, in exchange for however long she may have with Agatha. Her fingers were crossed that it would be eternity.

The kiss Agatha pressed to her lips was full of a familiar hunger; it made Rio ache all over, an empty, needy feeling with no beginning or end. Agatha’s hands were both on her face then, holding her so dearly between warm palms, and her tongue slid into her mouth. Rio let her have her way, she always did. She sat still at Agatha’s feet and surrendered, giving and giving and giving into her every unspoken demand. It was more than just devotion that brought her to her knees, so much more. After an eternity of taking, of enacting the will of the universe without question, it was a relief to be needed –needed by something real, something she could touch, something she could hold in her hands without fear, and god did it feel good.

Agatha’s hands circled to the back of Rio’s neck, sending a shiver down Rio’s spine as she let out a soft moan. “Agatha.”

A smile pulled at the corners of Agatha’s mouth at her desperate plea, her mumbling into her lips. “I know,” she said softly. “Be patient.”

Rio moaned again, throatier and louder, but it was muffled by yet another press of Agatha’s mouth to hers. As desperate as Rio was, she knew Agatha was, too–she needed this as much as Rio did. She would never admit it, but Rio could tell she needed the control, the power that never threatened to evade her. Rio’s submission ensured her something she could never be truly certain of outside of themselves; Agatha didn’t need to fight for this power, not when Rio surrendered it so freely. It was safe, familiar, and so fine-tuned Rio wasn’t sure how she ever survived without it. To be equals with Agatha Harkness was the single biggest honour Rio would ever experience.

“You don’t have to do this, please, Rio .”

Agatha sounded so defeated, so anguished as she weeped that it made Rio’s chest collapse in on itself. Her stomach lurched, nausea that shouldn’t have even been possible boiling inside of her. She ran her tongue over her teeth as she swallowed around the thick lump in her throat, biting back tears with everything she had in her.

Nicholas was already so small, barely a toddler, and he looked even smaller, somehow, cradled against Agatha’s body as he was. His little chest heaved with labouring breaths, each one more shallow and more difficult than the last. Rio could make it all end, relieve him of the pain he so clearly felt even a few minutes earlier to spare him the misery, but she knew Agatha would never forgive her. As difficult as it was, she resisted.

“You know I do, mi amor.” The words tore her up inside. She was fighting tooth and nail to keep it together, to follow through while every piece of her was crying out against it, while her heart was before her begging her to spare him.

“You can fix him, you know you can!”

“It’s not that simple.”

Another sob wracked Agatha’s body, the sheer force of it doubling her over at the waist. She pressed her face, wet with her soul-crushing devastation, into Nicky’s tiny stomach, letting his clothing catch the tears as they fell. Rio just watched, allowing as much distance as she could stomach, and allowed her own tears to fall silently at last.

If she were anyone else, she would be allowed to hold that child. If she weren’t herself, she could be right next to Agatha with tears of her own. But she was not anyone else, and she was herself–she was Death . She didn’t blame Agatha for keeping her at arm’s length, but it stung anyway.

Eventually, she would have to close the distance between them. When Nicky took his last, shaking breath, she would be forced to breach the invisible wall dividing them to help him crossover, and there was nothing she could do about it. Rio would run to the ends of the Earth to save the boy; she would sacrifice her own eternal existence just to stop Agatha’s tears, but it was not her will . She felt deep in her gut that feeling she never had to question, and this time she did .

Rio pushed back against the tug in her navel, in turmoil over the demand coming from something much bigger than her or Agatha or the both of them combined. Even with the infinity of their love, the shared devotion strong enough to break even Death’s apathy, she couldn’t find it in the universe to deny the impulse.

Nicky was growing weaker by the second, and Rio knew it was time. She slipped her hand under her cloak for her dagger, drew it, and on shaking feet she stepped toward Agatha. Leaves crunched underfoot, and Agatha’s head shot up just as she crouched down in front of them. She shrieked as Rio’s dagger shone in the light, the blade glinting as she lifted it between them.

“Please don’t,” Agatha pleaded.

Rio couldn’t hold her eyes as she shook her head. “I’m so sorry.”

She placed a kiss to Nicholas’ forehead, cringing as she heard his lungs struggle to inhale, then, with more gentleness than she had ever mustered, picked up a section of his dark hair. With her dagger, she sliced clean through the cluster of fine young hairs, twisting them between her fingers as she lifted them to the light. She had a place to store these, a collection of billions across the river, but taking his back there felt deeply wrong.

Agatha’s eyes bored into her, Rio didn’t need to look to know it. She felt the scathing heat of her gaze, the unbridled anger radiating off her. Never, not even in the beginning, not in their entire time together had Agatha ever looked at her with such disdain, with such injury, and it was all her fault.

Remorse was not something she felt often. In fact, as the guilt washed over her at Nicky’s last breath, she was suddenly sure she’d never felt it even once before. It was a terrible feeling, one that clawed at her from the inside so insistently she was sure she’d be hollow within the hour, leaving room for the shame to expand and eat her alive. The idea of simply adding to her collection of souls, of his hair–the only part of him that would never decay–being mixed in with everyone else’s made her sick , so, for the first time since she came to be, she bent the rules.

The lock of hair didn’t disappear beneath her cloak with her dagger. Instead, she finally found it in her to look up at Agatha. Her mother’s locket dangled around her neck, catching Rio’s eye at once. Reluctantly, she reached forward, ignoring the way Agatha tensed at her incoming hands, and opened it. Within it, she placed the magically bound lock of her son’s hair, the only thing she could give her of the boy she’d just had torn from her life so cruelly. If anyone should keep it, it should be Agatha. The action was almost certainly forbidden, a slap in the face to the way of the universe, but whatever punishment might ensue would be easier to endure than the look of betrayal on Agatha’s face.
