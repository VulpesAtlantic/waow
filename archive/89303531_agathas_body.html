Summary

Rio's been obsessed with Agatha since the first grade, ever since she decided to take her in like a stray. They're an unlikely pair, Agatha being the popular queen-bee and Rio being the artsy-nerd, but they just work.

When they both head off to college together and -though it's ill advised given Rio's head-over-heels in love with her straight best friend - they decide to get an apartment together rather than stay in dorms. Eager not to miss out, Agatha drags Rio to a party, but after a strange society of girls approach her, she disappears.

When she finally resurfaces, Rio can tell straight away that something is very, very wrong.

Chapter 1: Sandbox Love

There's some things in life that are doomed before they even begin

Rio, an only child with an unsettling energy, and a single mother who's only income came from tarot and psychic-mediumship readings? She never really stood a chance in the American public school. She mostly liked her own company, anyway, so it wasn't a huge deal, but she couldn't say it didn't hurt having no friends in the first grade. It hurt even worse that she was being bullied regularly, but couldn't tell her mother as it was mostly due to her reputation in town. For a place so seeped in rumours of satanism, the occult and witchcraft, WestView really was quite a religious place. It was probably people's way of comforting themselves that if they went to church, if they prayed, if they believed in god, that none of that stuff could get to them. 

It was just a day like any other. She was minding her business, lost in a world of her own as the other kids shrieked and chased each other around, when she came across a beautiful little green caterpillar slowly creeping over the bark of the big tree on the edge of the school playground. It was speckled yellow with black stripes, stretching and contracting along the wood and Rio couldn't take her eyes off of it. She quickly grabbed a stick from the ground nearby, encouraging the creature to climb on so she could inspect it more closely. 

"Hey there." Rio grinned, holding the stick close to her face, watching it crawl around. "You're a big guy." 

The caterpillar's head picked up a little and turned as though it could hear her and she giggled. She'd watched Alice in Wonderland with her Mom a couple of weeks ago and she can picture smoke blowing from its mouth as it asks her "Who are you?"

"I'm Rio." She whispered, holding her finger out to the stick. It crawled over her little index finger and up over the back of her hand. The sensation tickled; like pins and needles across her skin. "I think I'll call you Scratchy."

"Hey look, the witch's kid's talking to bugs!"

Rio looked up from where she sat cross-legged on the grass to see Ralph and his friends, chuckling to themselves, standing right over her. Ralph was the absolute worst of all the kids in her class. The rest of them whispered behind her back; not necessarily caring if she heard, but not necessarily eager to say it to her face. They, like their paranoid parents, probably worried Rio would hex them or something. Ralph never had that fear. 

Rio said nothing. She found it best in these situations to just let them play out until he got bored. Giving him a reaction usually just prolonged it. 

"What've you got there, freak?" He approached, crouching down to her level, then cringed when he caught sight of the creature, circling Rio's wrist now.  

Again, Rio ignored him, hoping he'd just leave her alone. Then his hand shot out, enclosing her new friend in his fist. Rio scrambled to her feet to chase him as he ran, holding it out of her reach. 

"Let him go!" Rio cried, swinging at him, but his friends grabbed her and held her in place. Ralph laughed monstrously, dangling it from his thumb and index finger in a pincer grip as it writhed around, angling it towards the girls who'd started to watch the spectacle. They all screamed and giggled as he paraded it around like a ringmaster in a circus. 

"Hey Ralph, she looks scrawny today. Do you think her mom couldn't feed her lunch again?" One of the little goons said, still gripping her tight. Rio's lip trembled and tears started to well up in her eyes.

"Is that why you were playing with this." Ralph sneered, holding the caterpillar towards her. "Nice and fat, isn't it? Was this gonna be your lunch, freak?" 

He walked closer, arm extended, the creature still squirming around in his grip. "Well go on then. Eat it."

Rio felt sick. Ralph was always mean, but surely he wouldn't actually make her eat that thing. He nodded to his friends and they wrestled her to the ground, answering her question. Turns out he absolutely would do that. Rio yelled and kicked and writhed to get free as Ralph lowered the bug to her face. The whole playground had erupted into a chorus of nervous muttering and laughter. She sealed her mouth closed and clenched her eyes shut. 

Then, so quickly Rio had barely registered what happened, there's a loud thwack noise. She felt the grip on her arms release and heard the crowd around them all gasp, then erupt into chanting.

Fight! Fight! Fight! Fight!

She finally opened her eyes to find Ralph on the grass, a girl with long, silky brown hair straddling him and pounding his face with a bloodied fist. His hand was lying open, the caterpillar undulating across the grass to freedom. The boys who had been holding Rio, ran over to try to pull her off of him, but to her credit it took them a decent amount of time. The kid managed to get at least another five strikes in before a teacher came sprinting over, wrangling the girl like a rabid dog. It's only then that Rio got a good view of her and Scratchy's saviour.

It was none other than Agatha Harkness. 

Agatha was actually an incredibly popular girl for only having had attended the school for a couple of months. Her parents were rich, moving in from some place in a neighbouring city. There wasn't a kid in her class that didn't try to invite her to play with them at recess.

Well, there was one kid. Rio. 

Rio, who had been so completely captivated by the girl when she'd first been introduced to the class that her throat felt tight. Rio, who knew that Agatha choosing to befriend her would mean certain social-suicide. Rio who never really felt nervous around other kids because she was so used to rejection, and was immediately terrified of receiving it from Agatha. 

It wasn't until they were sat, shoulder to shoulder in the hallway outside the principle's office that Rio finally spoke to her for the first time. She could hear yelling from through the door as Ralph's parents are likely being told about the history of torment and terror he'd inflicted on other students, but typically Rio. Agatha's parents, as it turned out, had a lot of sway with the school, and if they said that Ralph started the fight, then Ralph started the fight.

She sat, staring down at Agatha's knuckles, still bloody and sore. 

"You didn't have to do that. I was fine." Rio whispers nervously, feeling guilty for being the reason Agatha's bleeding. 

The girl turns, raising an eyebrow at her, a look of mild disgust on her face, like although she'd done this huge thing for Rio, she wasn't sure she even liked her yet. "You wanted to eat a caterpillar?"  

Rio shakes her head hurriedly. "N-No. Of course not, but-"

"Well then I obviously had to do it, Stupid." She rolled her eyes, laughing a little. Rio felt her face flush in embarrassment. 

"I just mean I'm sorry you got hurt because of me." Rio said quickly, desperate to save face. 

"That kid had it coming." Agatha shrugged like none of this meant anything, inspecting her knuckles the way Rio had seen teen girls inspect their manicures. Like she was proud of it. "You want to make it better?"

Rio nodded dumbly, eager to do anything if it meant making this girl happy.  Agatha extended her hand out like a princess in a Disney movie.

"Kiss it better." She smiled. It wasn't a mean request, she wasn't teasing. It was like a test of loyalty - of willingness. 

Rio was only too happy to oblige, grabbing Agatha's hand in hers and lowering her mouth to her knuckles, kissing over the bloodied, raw skin like it was nothing. Whatever ritual she'd just conducted for Agatha's approval, she'd clearly succeeded, because Agatha grinned seemingly thrilled with Rio's compliance. 

"All better." She giggled. "Rio, was it?"

Rio nods eagerly again. "Vidal. Rio Vidal."

The door to the principle's office opened and the principle can be heard, still arguing with Ralph's parents.

"Honestly, Mrs Bohnner, I think it's extremely small-minded of you to perpetuate those rumours for your son. I can assure you, little Rio Vidal is not a witch, she's a quiet, well-meaning child that-"

He's cut off as Ralph's parents get an eyeful of Rio, blood staining her mouth and chin, dark hair matted with mud and grass from the ground, and they scream.

Agatha cackled in delight from her seat, which only seemed to add to the horror of the scene.

Some things are doomed before they start, and even before that day, Rio was certainly doomed to follow Agatha wherever she went.

It was senior year and their usual Friday night sleepover. They almost always spent them at Rio's, despite Agatha's room being far larger, unless Agatha's mom was out of town. This was because Agatha's mom didn't approve of Agatha hanging around with a girl like her, given the reputation she had as the daughter of an unholy witch. She was one of those religious-types, fit right in with the rest of WestView, really, but Agatha's mom was the only creature on earth that Rio knew was capable of scaring Agatha. 

Right now, Agatha was lying across her bed in front of her, absentmindedly painting her nails lilac. She had on a tank that rode up, revealing a few inches of her lower back. The shorts she had on were what was really killing Rio - grey, retro-style athletic shorts with white trim that sat so high on her ass they were basically underwear. From there down it was all legs.

It wasn't a secret that Rio liked girls. She didn't exactly tell people, but she had a girlfriend for a while - Natasha - before she'd moved out of state. Agatha had hated her, naturally, probably because Rio's attention wasn't on her twenty-four hours of the day. To be totally honest, her relationship with Natasha was strained enough thanks to Agatha seemingly perfectly timing phone calls, like she just knew when they were messing around in Rio's room. Rio would always answer. Agatha was her best friend after all. If she also had a giant crush on her? Well, isn't it a right-of-passage?  To crush on your straight-best-friend?

"You still haven't told me what you thought." Agatha comments absentmindedly as the couple in the cabin in the woods on screen start having sex - a tell-tale sign that the killer is about to break in and slaughter them any moment. 

Rio peeled her eyes from Agatha's ass, suddenly aware of where they'd landed. "Thought about what?" 

"The pep-rally today. What did you think?"

Rio smiled. Agatha was fishing for compliments again because it's another day that ends in 'Y'. 

"You were amazing. You're always amazing."

Okay so, despite the fact that Agatha's status in school protects her from bullying, there were some whispers. If Agatha's heard them, she never said anything. Rio was a little too obvious in her obsession with her best friend, so much so that the whole school were under the belief that Rio must've cast some kind of spell to make her agree to hang out with her.

Sure, they were an unlikely pair - Rio got near-perfect grades, loved focussing on her art, spent most of her teen years awkward-looking  dawning braces and big glasses - and Agatha was...well she was Agatha. But in the last year though, Rio had swapped the glasses for contacts, lost the braces and her acne cleared up. She had been getting more - unwanted - attention from boys and Agatha clearly hated it. She practically tore some guy's dick off at lunch for looking her up and down the wrong way. Either way, regardless of the fact she's no longer an awkward, gangly nerd, a hot nerd was still a nerd, and high school is a cruel place. So most people thought it had to be some spell, or Agatha was keeping her around out of pity. 

"Jen said I was a beat off." Agatha huffs, extending her nails to check her work. 

"You weren't. You looked really good." Rio reassures. 

Agatha suddenly swivels to look at her. "Hey, do we have an English assignment due Wednesday?" 

"Monday." Rio corrects. In the background, sure enough, the couple on screen are slaughtered, fake blood pouring by the bucketload on to the wooden floor. 

"Shit." Agatha groaned. She rolled over, seemingly satisfied that her fingernails were dry and crawled towards Rio, who nearly passed out at the image. "Rio could you do it for me? Pretty, pretty please? I haven't caught up on MacBeth yet." She was on her hands and knees, pouting up at Rio who was sitting against the headboard. 

"It's Hamlet." She croaked out. 

"Sure. That's what I said. Will you do it?"

Rio pretended to think about it but she already knew the answer. She's only human. "Sure. But you owe me."

"Thank you, thank you, thank you!" She squealed, coming up to sit by her. "I actually have some really good news."

"You break things off with Ralph?" Rio muttered bitterly. 

Agatha laughed. "For the seventh time? Yeah actually, last week. He cried. That make you happy?"

Rio rolled her eyes. "Why do you even date him? You don't even like him."

"Yeah, but it's all about power, Rio. Star of the football team? Parents rich enough to buy and sell the country? The school watch me rinse and repeat and toss him like garbage and it only makes me look better. Untouchable."

That pissed her off a little. Agatha did this all the time growing up - reaching for status, for power. Like she could trick the world into giving her whatever she wanted. 

"Okay? And we leave high school in the next few months so what is it all for?"

"You don't understand." Agatha sighed. "If you can control the way people view you, you can have whatever you want. Most of the school will be going to Hawthorne anyway, so it translates. That's...actually what I want to talk to you about."

Rio had already gotten her acceptance letter to Hawthorne, the university that was practically in WestView for how close it was. She'd gotten her acceptance letter to everywhere, actually. She had planned on skipping town, leaving this place behind, but Agatha had her heart set on Hawthorne, and Rio had her heart set on Agatha. 

"I got my letter. I'm in" Agatha announced with a squeal. 

"You did!? That's amazing!" Rio grinned with her.

Agatha knelt beside her, taking her hands in hers and Rio struggled to fight a blush. 

"You're coming with me right?"

There's not a corner of the Earth that Rio wouldn't follow Agatha, so absolutely she'd be attending Hawthorne. 

"Of course I am." 

"Good. Because there's not a chance in hell I'm using a communal shower."  She gagged, making Rio laugh because Agatha used communal showers all the time. She was a cheerleader. 

"What does that have to do with me?"

Agatha blinked at her like she'd just been asked something insane. "Uh...because we'll get an apartment?" 

"Agatha, I don't have any way to afford that."

Agatha grinned wolfishly. "We'll just use my trust fund until we get into the swing of things. We'll both get cute part-time jobs or something. Come on Ri, do you really want to spend the first year in a cramped little bedroom with me?"

Yes. Fuck yes. 

"No. You're right, but is that even allowed?"

Agatha brought her legs underneath her to sit cross-legged. "It is at Hawthorne. They don't have the dorm-space. And don't even ask about if my mom would be cool with it, of course she wont, but she's barely in town anymore and she'll gladly let me use my trust fund to pay for my own apartment. So? What do you think?"

An apartment with Agatha? Cooking dinner together? Getting to stay up in the living room watching movies every night? Yeah, there's no way she'd turn that offer down. 

"I think we need to start apartment hunting." 

Agatha launched forward, wrapping rio in a hug that sucked the air out of her lungs. She could smell Agatha's lavender shampoo and feel her lips against her shoulder. 

"God people are totally going to think we're...y'know."

Rio's stomach filled with dread. She was pretty sure she knew where Agatha was going with this, but the fear of being caught still caught her off guard every time. 

"We're what?" Rio asked as Agatha pulled away.

Agatha smirked, then crudely made scissor gestures with both hands and mashed them together. The gesture set a spike of heat to Rio's lower belly at the image of what she'd implied, but mostly just made her embarrassed. 

"Why? Because I'm into girls and we're apartment sharing?" Rio forced herself to scoff. "That's dumb."

Rio nearly choked on air at that. What kind of a fucking question was that?

"Oh get over yourself, Agatha. You're not my type." Rio lied, chuckling for effect.

"Not mine."

Agatha's frown intensified into a scowl. "Why not? What's wrong with me?

Jesus, and people said Rio was the needy one.

Holy shit. Rio could not handle this conversation right now. She can already feel her face glowing in the dark of the room, lit only by the dull light of the slasher on the TV. 

"Uh, well I wouldn't know."

Agatha's eyes lit up, a grin splitting her face in an instant.

There will be plenty of gothy lesbians like Nat flooding to Hawthorne to study witchcraft if that's what you're into. You'll be drowning in p-"

"-Thanks, Agatha." Rio cut her off before she could finish that sentence. 

"Any time" Agatha winked. "Now, gimme your laptop I want to apartment hunt."
