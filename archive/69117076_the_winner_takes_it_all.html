It landed somewhere way out of bounds. 

 

“Second set, Maximoff.” 

 

That was that then.

 

Rio grabbed a change of clothes and headed to the bathroom, a member of staff joining her in the tunnel to escort her. She would be allowed three minutes to use the toilet and a further two to get changed, giving her a total of five minutes alone away from the crowd, away from Agatha, to clear her head. 

 

The second the bathroom door clicked shut, she stripped off and wiped her sticky limbs and torso with a towel, quickly peeing and washing her hands before throwing on a fresh pair of shorts and a clean shirt.

 

She took a deep breath, braced her hands against the counter and locked eyes with herself in the mirror. 

 

At times like this, it helped to recall her very first memories of learning tennis. All those hours she had spent on the court after school. The mental lessons were just as important as the physical ones. She remembered her mami kneeling down in front of her, taking Rio’s small hands in hers, looking up into wide, watery brown eyes and telling her to never give up. Even when it felt like you had lost all hope, if you looked hard enough, you could always find it again. It was never over until it was over.

 

Get out of your head, Rio told herself. You will not let her beat you. Not today. You may not be able to take her girl, but you can take this. You love this game. You always have, Forget about who’s watching. All that matters is you, her and the ball. 

 

She exhaled slowly, splashed her face with cold water, and gave herself one last look.

 

You’re Rio fucking Vidal. You can do this. Get out there and make your mami proud. 

 

There was a knock at the door, signaling her time was up. Feeling more settled and with renewed motivation, she stepped out of the bathroom and followed her chaperone back out onto the court. There was an enthusiastic roar from the crowd as she reappeared, but she kept her head down, focused. 

 

“Third set. Vidal to serve.”

 

Rio hopped from foot to foot, tapping the ball against the ground with her racket, the bright yellow a vibrant contrast against the red clay. 

 

The chants began again, calling out Rio’s name.

 

“Ladies and gentleman, silence please.”

 

Rio readied herself. She bounced the ball three times, kept her gaze fixed on it as she launched it skyward, and then thrashed it over the net. 

 

She could feel the strength of it as she struck, and Wanda’s eyes widened in surprise as the thing came hurtling towards her, landing right in the corner of the box. It was the perfect placement, tricky for the redhead to catch, especially at that speed. Wanda moved to reach for it, but she was too late. 

 

Fifteen - Love. 

 

Rio stuck her tongue in her cheek, fighting off a smile. The game was back on. 

 

For the rest of the set, she didn’t take her foot off the gas for a single second, entirely zoned in, entirely in control. She had forgotten about the disaster of a second set, about why she was even stressed. She was having fun. 

 

Wanda seemed to be in total disbelief at the turnaround, frustration bubbling over on more than one occasion, growling and muttering to herself as she lost point after point. Rio wouldn’t have been surprised if she threw her racket, she had done it at the US Open last year. Her PR team had likely given her a warning after that because she hadn’t done it since, but it was obvious she wanted to, smacking her leg with the racket and gritting her teeth.

 

Soon, Rio was leading by 5 games to 1, a mirror of Wanda’s performance in the second set. The score was at 30 - Love. Two more points and Rio had done it. 

 

Sweat dripped down her temple, the sun still beating down on them. They hadn’t even been playing for 90 minutes yet but they were both clearly exhausted. It was a matter of whether Rio could hold her nerve just a little longer. 

 

She delivered another powerful serve. Wanda returned it. Rio sent it back down the line. Wanda slid to reach it, her form shaky, and Rio seized the moment, smacking it across to the other side. It landed right on the line.

 

40 - Love.

 

Wanda marched up to the umpire, eyes wide with rage, gesturing furiously.

 

“Miss Maxmioff is requesting a review.”

 

Was she fucking kidding? It was clearly in. It could’ve been touching the line by a millimeter and it still would’ve been in, but it had been firmly on it. There was no doubt about it.

 

The umpire fiddled around on her little tablet, tapping it a couple of times, and a few moments later a digital replay appeared on the screen. Both players turned to watch. The display showed the ball’s trajectory and then yep, it landed right on the line. The crowd cheered. Wanda huffed and spun on her heel, returning to her position. 

 

Rio rolled her eyes. Clown. She was clearly just trying to interrupt Rio’s flow, but Rio was unshakable at this point. She was so fucking close. One shot away. 

 

The umpire hushed the crowd. The resulting silence was deafening, thousands of people holding a collective breath. Rio’s pulse thudded in her ears. She inhaled deeply, visualizing the ball soaring across the net. 

 

She looked over at Wanda, meeting her eye, and then lifted her gaze higher, up to Wanda’s team. To Agatha. She just wanted one last look before this was all over in more ways than one.

 

The sun had moved over the course of the match, the other woman now cast in shade, sunglasses sitting atop her head. She had taken her jacket off, leaving her in just a white tank top, long, dark waves falling around her bare shoulders. Without her sunglasses, it was undeniable that she was watching Rio, those piercing blue eyes meeting hers across the room. She nodded. A silent, subtle encouragement.

 

Rio knew what she had to do.

 

She bounced the ball. Tossed. Served hard and fast. Wanda returned it with force, but adrenaline was pulsing through Rio’s veins and she moved at speed, catching it easily and sending it back. Wanda slammed it back over, and Rio was running, sliding, fuck it was just out of reach, she wasn’t going to get there and–

 

“Out. Vidal wins two sets to one.”

 

The crowd went wild. Rio let herself flop to the ground, laying on her back with her arms stretched wide. She had actually fucking done it. She had defeated Wanda. Again. 

 

She basked in the feeling for all of about ten blissful seconds before reality came crashing down on her. The applause faded into the background. The match was done. She would be going back to her hotel room soon. She would shower, get ready, head out to this party, find Agatha, ask to speak with her. And then she would end it. Fuck. She closed her eyes and dug the heels of her palms into her eyelids.

 

As she dragged herself to her feet, the noise came back into focus. She waved to the crowd before running over to the net to shake Wanda’s hand. The redhead gave her a tightlipped smile, clearly upset, but Rio envied her. It didn’t matter that she was technically a winner, the thought of losing Agatha was tearing at her chest. 

 

Wanda gave her speech first, thanking Rio for challenging her, pushing her to be better, before thanking various members of her team and “last but not least” her fiancee for always supporting her. Rio tasted bile in her throat. 

 

Rio then gave a very short speech in English, reluctantly thanking Wanda for keeping her on her toes, as well as Alice and Lilia, followed by a few words in Spanish, thanking the crowd for their support, and her mami too, for everything she had taught her. 

 

After she had lifted her trophy, there was just the press conference standing between her professional duties and her sorry state of a personal life. She took her seat and adjusted the mic as the host did the introductions and asked the journalists to raise their hands to speak. 

 

Everyone was eager to congratulate Rio on her second clay win, making remarks about her ‘dominating the court’ and having a ‘great start’ to the season so far. There were a few questions about specific moments, about what went wrong in the second set, about how she managed to recover from that. She kept her answers brief as always, not wanting to give too much of herself away, to be perceived in such a public forum.

 

After about ten minutes, the host gave her a nod to signal that she could wrap it up after this last question. 

 

“Rio, congratulations on another clay title,” the journalist said, a Brit by the sounds of it. “With your success in Stuttgart and now Madrid, it’s obvious that you’re a very strong player on this surface. Some might even say that the Roland Garros title is a guarantee if you continue playing like this. What do you make of that?”

 

There was a hushed silence in the room, the only sound being the click of cameras. 

 

Rio cleared her throat. “Thank you, that’s very kind,” she replied evenly. “But I’m very aware that I can’t ever get too comfortable. Maximoff is a tough opponent and she doesn’t go down without a fight as we’ve seen. I’m enjoying this winning streak, sure, but just like in life, there are no guarantees in this game. You never know what’s around the corner. All good things must come to an end, right?” She gave a final nod. “No further questions, thanks.”

 

With that, she pushed back her chair and stood, following the host towards the exit.

 

It was time to go rip off that damn bandaid.
