Written in the Stars

Rio couldn’t stop looking at the clock. All of her work was done for the day, so she was just stuck sitting at her desk, tapping her foot and clicking her pen. Even though she knew it was just her impatience, she couldn’t help but feel that time seemed to be moving criminally slow for the last half hour.

Normally, Rio wouldn’t have minded killing time until it was time for her to leave. She was actually somewhat of a professional at acting like she was doing very important things while in reality she had a game of minesweeper pulled up.

Today was different though because A. M. Harkness was having a book talk and signing at a nearby bookstore. In fact, it was a bookstore Rio frequented, by the name of The Witch’s Corner and owned by a woman named Lilia. Rio wasn’t sure if bookstore owners were supposed to have favorite customers, but she liked to think Lilia had a soft spot for her. She always let Rio know about new books she was ordering, setting aside ones she thought she’d like, and she also let her know about tonight’s signing before anyone else, reserving her a spot. 

As soon as the clock turned to five, Rio was up and out of the office before anyone could even blink. The event wasn’t until six, and the bookstore was only a fifteen minute walk, but Rio couldn’t help the anticipation that was building up inside her. 

Stepping into the bookstore, Rio couldn’t help but deeply inhale and sigh. There was nothing like the smell of books, and that, coupled with the warm lighting and colorful decor, made The Witch’s Corner one of Rio’s favorite places in the city.

Rio found Lilia easily. Tucked away in the back corner, Lilia set up a table with A. M. Harkness’s latest book, The Darkhold Diaries. Just the sight made Rio’s heart jump a little. Soon enough, she’d meet the genius behind this series. 

“Rio, I’m glad you made it! Lilia said when she spotted her. 

“I had some time,” Rio said lightly. Understatement of the year. She would have killed someone to make sure she made it. “How’d you get her to agree to this anyways? Isn’t she pretty private?” Rio may or may not have spent several hours stalking A. M. Harkness’s socials, which consisted solely of an Instagram account that really only posted about her books. 

“We’re old friends,” Lilia informed her with a secretive smile.

Rio’s eyes went wide. “Seriously?”

“Something like that,” Lilia said. She seemed pleased at her little mystery, and as badly as Rio wanted to know what she meant, she didn’t think she could politely press for more information. Lilia patted her shoulder and said, “Now, dear, the talk doesn’t start for another half hour. Why don’t you walk around a little while? I promise to save you a seat in the front row.” 

Rio didn’t really want to, but she also didn’t really have an excuse to say no either. Managing a small smile, Rio wandered off with a gentle push from Lilia. 

Despite already being here for A. M. Harkness’s books, Rio still found herself in the adult fantasy section. She attempted to look at other books, but Rio ended up plucking A. M. Harkness’s debut book. Even though she knew it wasn’t her best book, it still held a special place in Rio’s heart. It came out when she was in college, and it had instantly become a comfort read for her. A coven of witches, disastrous and beautiful and everything a young Rio had needed at a time when she felt so alone.

Rio was thumbing through the book when she felt someone join her in front of the bookshelf. She glanced up absently, only to do a double take. The woman standing next to her was gorgeous, blue eyes and sharp features. Long, brown waves fell down her back, but what Rio really noticed was the collection of gold earrings adoring her ear (five, if she counted correctly) and the fact that her black shirt was sheer enough to tease at the bra underneath. Then, as if to fully put Rio in her grave, the woman was also wearing glasses. It was as if someone had plucked this woman from Rio’s fantasies. 

Embarrassingly, Rio didn’t realize she was staring until the woman turned her head and met her gaze, blue eyes bright even in the dim lighting in this part of the bookstore.

“What do you have there?”

Rio nearly dropped the book at the sound of her voice. It should be illegal to look that good and have a voice like sin. Hands clutched around the cover, Rio showed it with an awkward smile. “Um, just a book,” she said.

The woman smiled and teased, “In a bookstore? Who would have thought?” When Rio let out a self-deprecating laugh, the woman held out her hand. “I’m Agatha.”

“Rio.”

Agatha’s hand was soft and warm, and Rio had to make a conscious effort not to be weird and hold on too long. Rio went right back to holding the book, unsure of what else to do without something keeping her hands occupied. 

“That’s an A. M. Harkness book, right?” Agatha said. She stepped closer, tilting her head to get a better look. “Are you a fan?” 

“Yeah, I’ve followed her work for a while now,” Rio answered with a shrug, trying to act casual. No sense in letting a total stranger know what a total geek she was over these books. “She’s probably one of my favorite authors. I’m actually here for the book talk and signing for her latest book.”

Agatha wore an odd smile at this information. If Rio didn’t know any better, she would’ve said she looked smug. “That sounds exciting,” Agatha hummed. “And how are you liking her latest series?”

“I like them,” Rio said, smiling at the thought of the recent books. “They feel like a natural progression from her other books, a little more mature, a little more complex, but they still have that same charm, you know? A found family and all of the possibilities with magic.” She blushed at how she just gushed over the books, but Agatha simply looked charmed. Testing the waters, Rio added matter-of-factly, “Well, that and the two main women of this series are definitely in love with each other.”

Agatha let out a bark of laughter, only to cover her mouth. Rio grinned at the unintentional reaction, raising an eyebrow. Agatha smiled and shook her head, asking, “What makes you say that?”

“Whatever straight people at the publishing house are proofreading this series may not see it, but trust me when I say that those women are in love,” Rio said. It was a common conversation Rio had with her other friends who read the series too. “The subtext is all there if you’re not blind. Or straight.” Agatha seemed to be looking at her with renewed interest, and Rio shifted uneasily under the attention. Smiling hesitantly, Rio said, “Plus, you know, witches.” 

Agatha laughed again, but this time it was softer. Still amused but almost affectionate. Then again, Rio was probably projecting her own hopes onto Agatha, imagining a universe where this gorgeous woman would actually be interested in her.

“It’s up to the reader’s interpretation, I suppose,” Agatha said. She sounded contemplative, almost wistful. 

“Well that’s the magic of it, isn’t it? Pun intended.” Rio grinned when Agatha rolled her eyes. 

Rio ran her finger along the edge of the book’s spine, debating whether to ask Agatha for her number, but before she could, Lilia was walking up to them. Weirdly enough though, she had her eyes on Agatha instead of Rio.

“Agatha, your table is ready,” Lilia said.

“Table?” Rio repeated, eyebrows furrowing.

Lilia stared at Rio blankly for a moment. She nodded slowly. “Yes,” she said, “For the signing.” 

“For the…” Rio trailed off. Her mouth dropped open as the pieces fell into place. A.M. Harkness. A as in Agatha. Agatha who was standing right in front of her, listening to her babble about her own books. “Kill me now,” Rio whispered.

Agatha wore a shit-eating grin as she asked, “Now why would I do that?” 

“To spare me the absolute mortification of this whole conversation,” Rio groaned. She all but shoved the book onto the shelf and buried her face in her hands. “Put me out of my misery, Lilia. Just take me out back.”

“I’m enjoying this though,” Agatha said, amusement dripping from every word. 

“At least one of us is,” Rio sighed. Her hands dropped back to her sides as she dared to look at Agatha again. Her grin was still wide, her face was still attractive, and Rio was still hoping the earth would open up and swallow her whole. “I’m such an idiot, explaining your own book to you. You must think I’m so pretentious. And stupid. God, I’m stupid and pretentious.”

Agatha chuckled and placed a hand on her arm. “No, I would say I’m feeling rather magnanimous about the misunderstanding.” 

“Magnanimous,” Rio said faintly. God, her hand was warm. There was too much going on. Her head was going to explode. “Good word.” 

“Well, I am an author,” Agatha said before adding playfully, “Your favorite author.”

Rio let out a weak, borderline hysterical laugh. “Right, so you haven’t changed your mind about killing me?” 

“Not a chance,” Agatha replied.

Lilia, who’d been watching the back and forth with wide eyes, finally said, “Agatha, you really do need to come to the table and set up.”

Seeing the opportunity to escape this horribly embarrassing situation, Rio said, “That’s my cue to leave.” Rio spun to leave, but she didn’t realize just how close she was to the end of the bookshelf. When she turned and took a step forward, her forehead clipped the edge of the wood with a dull thud. Rio blinked dazedly, hand flying to the wound, muttering, “Oh my god. Death would be kinder than this.”

Rio took an unsteady step back, but then Agatha was there, hands on her shoulders. “Are you okay?” Agatha asked, looking genuinely concerned. It would’ve been exciting to see her so worried if it weren’t for the fact that she only looked like that because Rio was a walking disaster.

“Doing great,” Rio said dryly. 

“Are you sure?”

Rio stopped breathing as Agatha’s hand gently brushed over her forehead. She was close enough that Rio could smell her perfume, spiced and musky, and become painfully aware of just how sheer her shirt was. 

“Yep,” Rio said. Her voice was barely more than a squeak. 

Agatha didn’t look like she believed her, but she let her hand fall back to her side. Rio missed the touch immediately. “Okay, if you’re sure,” Agatha said. She waited a moment then continued, “I’ll see you at the signing then.”

Rio didn’t breathe again until Agatha was out of sight. Then, she all but collapsed against the bookshelf and buried her face in her hands. She met her favorite author for the last decade, only to make a total fool of herself. She must have done some really awful shit in a past life. 

After taking a few more deep breaths, Rio made her way back to the corner that the talk and signing was. As promised, there was a chair saved for Rio front and center in front of the table Lilia had set up. Agatha was already there, laughing at something Lilia said, and Rio found herself astounded by just how attractive she was all over again. No one should be allowed to be that hot and that talented.

When the talk finally began, Rio wondered if she would be too distracted to actually listen to what Agatha was saying, but it quickly became clear that wouldn’t be a problem. Absolutely stunning face and voice like velvet Agatha had, but both were almost eclipsed by the excitement and passion she had when talking about her books. Her hands gestured with every other word, and her eyes were alight with joy. Rio couldn’t have looked away if she tried.

For almost an hour, Rio listened to Agatha explain her writing processes, discuss her characters, and tease at future books. It was everything Rio could have hoped for in a book talk from her favorite author. It was almost disappointing for it to end, except for the fact that it was now time for the book signing.

Rio lined up with everyone else, ending up in the middle of the line. As she glanced up and down, Rio noted that the fans were predominantly women, ranging from teenagers to women a little older than herself. It made Rio laugh to herself because of course this would be Agatha’s fanbase. Rio could only wonder just how many of these women were also crushing on the author now. 

The line moved steadily, and Rio watched Agatha interact with the other fans while she waited. While polite and kind, there was also a coolness to Agatha as she spoke. A sense of mystery, if Rio had to describe it. Like there was a veil between her and whoever she was talking to. It made sense to keep some distance with fans, but Rio found herself curious. 

When it was Rio’s turn, she blushed at the sly smile Agatha wore upon seeing her. 

“Well, if it isn’t my biggest fan,” Agatha teased.

“I never said that,” Rio protested.

Agatha tilted her head, blue eyes burning into her. “So, you aren’t my biggest fan?”

Rio opened her mouth then closed it. Her entire face felt like it was on fire. “I’m a fan,” she said lamely. 

“Yes, you are,” Agatha agreed. Her voice was practically a purr, and it took everything in Rio not to collapse against the table. “How did you like the talk?”

“It was amazing.” You were amazing, Rio almost said.

“I’m glad you enjoyed it,” Agatha said. She scribbled something in the book and snapped it shut. She held it out to Rio, voice softening as she said, “Thanks for coming, Rio.”

Rio barely managed to say thank you before she shuffled off. She made it a few steps before she remembered to check what Agatha wrote.

Rio, 

It’s an honor to be your favorite favorite author If you’d like to continue our conversation from before, give me a call.

212-666-1031

Agatha’s signature followed the note, but Rio only had eyes for the ten numbers. She blinked, but they were still there in bold, black ink. Slowly, Rio looked over her shoulder to where Agatha was still signing books. As if sensing her gaze, Agatha looked up and smiled. Then, she winked, and Rio wondered just how many times she could feel like she might die tonight.

. . .

Rio called Agatha later that night after downing some whiskey for courage. The phone rang long enough that Rio wondered if she’d been given a fake number, but then Agatha’s voice came through, saying, “Hello?”

“Hey,” Rio said dumbly, still surprised it was actually Agatha on the other end. “It’s Rio.”

“Rio, I’m glad you called,” Agatha said. Her voice softened from its polite greeting, molding into something warm. “I’m sorry we didn’t get to talk more at the event.”

“Oh, it’s fine,” Rio said. “You were literally doing your job.”

“No, no, I insist I make it up to you,” Agatha said, sounding deeply pleased with herself, and Rio couldn’t help but laugh when she realized why Agatha said that in the first place. “How does dinner sound? This weekend?” 

“That sounds great,” Rio said.

“I’ll text you the details then.”

Rio nodded absently, unable to stop the giddy smile spreading across her face. There was a natural pause, but she didn’t want the call to end yet though. Biting her lip, Rio said, “You know, it was pretty bold of you to just give me your number. What if I was a crazy fan or something?”

Agatha’s responding laugh was low and rich as she said, “Considering you didn’t even recognize me, I think it was safe to say you weren’t a stalker.”

Rio’s cheek reddened all over again at the reminder. She cleared her throat and said, “Yeah, well, you don’t exactly post a lot of photos of yourself.”

“Looked me up, did you?”

Rio bit back a curse at letting that slip. She ran a hand through her hair, trying to figure out how to get herself out of this one, but she ended up saying, “Maybe.”

“Well, now I’m at a disadvantage, ” Agatha said, and Rio almost let out a hysterical laugh. There was no way to hear this conversation and think Agatha was the one at a disadvantage. “Will you share your instagram with me, so I can do my own bit of research? Just to make sure you aren’t a crazy fan.”

“I guess,” Rio said, even as she mentally panicked over what was on her profile. What was the last thing she even posted? “I’ll text it to you.”

“Perfect,” Agatha hummed. “Now, as much as I’d love to keep teasing you, I have to call my agent. I’ll see you this weekend?”

“This weekend,” Rio agreed.

As soon as the call ended, Rio collapsed onto her couch, staring up at her ceiling. She couldn’t believe that just happened. She also couldn’t believe that she had a date with Agatha Harkness, one of her favorite authors, this weekend. How was any of this real?

Shaking her head, Rio pulled up her instagram, giving it a cursory look to make sure there wasn’t anything embarrassing. She didn’t use it much, so her most recent posts consisted of a cup of coffee, a photo of her and Alice hiking, and a cat she’d seen around her neighborhood. It could be worse, she decided.

Rio sent the link and went to shower. When she came back, there were two texts from Agatha. The first was a name and address to a restaurant downtown along with when to meet her there. The second was a screenshot from Rio’s instagram, a year ago according to the post date at the bottom. It was from one of her hiking trips with Rio standing by a lake in only leggings and a sports bra.

The text read, This is what you were hiding under those office clothes?

Rio laughed and replied, That much skin is usually frowned upon in the corporate setting.

Agatha’s reply was quick. Well, just so you know, I wouldn’t be opposed to that much skin for our date.

I’ll keep that in mind.

Rio bit her lip, cheeks rosy and smile threatening to overtake her entire face. She knew exactly what to wear now.

. . .

As she got out of her car, Rio couldn’t help but run a nervous hand over her clothing. After getting Agatha’s reaction to her photo, Rio chose a black jumpsuit for the date with cut-outs on the sides that teased her abs. It was showier than she would normally go for on a first date, but considering the fact that she was going out with a famous author, she figured she could get away with it. 

Rio waited for about five minutes before Agatha stepped out of a cab, looking absolutely stunning in slacks and a low-cut, sleeveless blouse. Her hair was also pulled back into a ponytail, giving her a clear view of all of her piercings. God, how was Rio going to survive tonight?

Agatha smiled as she walked up to her, saying, “I see you took my words to heart.”

“Do you approve?” Rio replied, doing a slow spin. When she faced Agatha once more, she blushed at the unadulterated lust on her face. 

“I certainly do,” Agatha murmured. She slipped her hand into the crook of Rio’s elbow and tugged. “Come on.”

Once Rio moved past the fact that she was eating in a very expensive restaurant with her favorite author, it became easy to fall into conversation with Agatha. While Agatha was brilliant in her writing, it became abundantly clear that her wit and intelligence transcended her writing. She kept Rio on her toes as they talked, never letting Rio go without fully explaining herself, even when she agreed. 

It was the most engaged Rio had felt on a date in a long time, and Rio was almost afraid by just how smitten she was by the time dessert came out.

Digging into her mousse, Agatha said, “So, be honest. When did you start reading my books?”

“Is this just some way for me to stroke your ego?” Rio replied.

Agatha’s face was overly innocent as she said, “Well, I can certainly think of a few things I’d like you to stroke.” Not expecting that, Rio choked on the wine she’d just taken a sip of. While she coughed, trying to breathe, Agatha smiled wickedly. “Seriously though,” she said once Rio had calmed down. “I’m curious. I don’t get to talk to a fan like this all the time, you know.”

“I sure hope not,” Rio said under her breath. She picked at her slice of cake, mulling over how honest she should be. After a moment, she shrugged and admitted, “I picked up your stuff in college. Your stuff, especially the earlier books, was exactly the kind of thing I needed at that time, and well, I just followed your work after that.” 

“College, so… A decade?”

“About.”

To Rio’s surprise, instead of gloating, Agatha looked flushed and shy at the realization. She fiddled with her spoon, looking out at the restaurant instead of at Rio. “It’s easy to forget sometimes,” Agatha said quietly. “A book gets popular, goes to the top of lists, but it’s not the same as meeting someone face-to-face and having them tell you they love it.”

Rio smiled and reached out, holding Agatha’s hand. She waited until she looked at her to say, “Agatha, I love your books. I have for so many years. They’re brilliant, and you’re brilliant. I hope you realize just how much comfort they’ve brought me, even at my lowest.”

Agatha’s blush worsened, making her entire face turn a pretty shade of pink. Her eyes met hers for a long moment before they skittered away. “You don’t have to try so hard to get into my bed.”

Rio laughed and let it go, pulling back her hand. “Good to know.”

After dinner, the two of them started aimlessly walking until they found a coffee shop that was still open. While Agatha got a coffee, Rio ordered a chamomile tea. At Agatha’s amused look, Rio explained, “If I have coffee now, I’m not getting any sleep tonight.”

“I miss those days,” Agatha said with a laugh, “when a single cup of coffee would actually do something.”

“And just how many cups have you had today?” Rio asked, raising a brow.

Agatha smiled and shrugged, “Enough to give you a heart attack.”

As the two of them sat on one of the couches, arms pressed together, Rio realized she could have talked to Agatha for forever. The other woman was just so charming, so smart, so funny. The craziest part was that she seemed to think the same of Rio, constantly prodding her to share her thoughts and opinions on whatever new topic they landed on. 

That was what made it so disappointing when the coffee shop finally closed, and they had no choice but to each call a car.

“I had a lot of fun,” Rio said shyly as she stepped closer.

“I did too,” Agatha said. She placed her hands on Rio’s chest before she slid them around her neck, pulling her even closer. Rio bit her lip as she stumbled into Agatha, using her hands on Agatha’s hips to steady herself. Their lips were just inches away as Agatha whispered, “Thanks for not being a crazy fan.”

“Thanks for not being an asshole author,” Rio replied.

They shared a grin, and when their lips met, Rio was unembarrassed to admit she felt fireworks. 

When she would tell Agatha this, months later, Agatha would blush and roll her eyes, complaining about cliches. But then, she’d curl up in Rio’s arms, head tucked on her shoulder, and say, “There’s one cliche that isn’t so bad though.”

“And what’s that?”

“And they lived happily ever after.”

Rio smiled and kissed her forehead. "No, that one's not bad at all."
