“Okay,” Rio replies, in a voice that barely sounds like her own.

 

Agatha leans into the touch. “It won’t happen again.”

 

Rio still has questions, but now is not the time to ask them. It’s late, and her exhaustion leaves no room to keep searching for answers. She wants to go to bed, to be held, to fall asleep in the arms of the woman she loves so much that she can’t catch her breath enough to say it.

 

 

On the day of the anatomy scan, Agatha drives them over to the birthing center herself.

 

Agatha’s been concerned about their privacy from the start, and as heavy-handed as she can be about it sometimes, it’s one of the things that Rio can’t fault her for. Thanks to Jen’s personal involvement in every aspect of Rio’s care, they get to come in early on a slow Sunday morning. 

 

Rio dozes off in the passenger seat as Agatha steers them through traffic, soothed by a combination of the blasting AC and the snugly warmth of her favorite oversized hoodie. Jen had told her to dress for comfort today, since she’ll be lying on an exam table with her belly out for anywhere up to an hour or possibly even longer, and she’d happily complied. And now, between stewing in the strange, stilted silence that’s fallen over them since Friday and sleep, unconsciousness is the far more preferable option.

 

The ultrasound starts off well enough—Rio gets on the table, drags the stretchy band of her skirt down to sit below her bump, lets Jen slather warm gel all over her lower belly. Agatha sits on Rio’s other side, never straying out of reach. It’s a good thing she does, because Rio makes a desperate grab for her hand the moment that Jen picks up the wand.

 

“You’re with me,” she blurts out, well beyond caring about how her voice shakes or the way that Jen goes suddenly, carefully still in the periphery of her vision. Agatha stares up at her with wide eyes. “Right?”

 

“Yes,” Agatha breathes, fingers squeezing back equally hard. “I’m with you, Rio. I promise.”

 

“Okay.” Rio offers her a watery, trembling smile before turning to give Jen a small nod. “Sorry. We can keep going. I’m ready now.”

 

Jen—who Rio makes a mental note to find the best thank you for guiding me through pregnancy gift ever before she gives birth—doesn’t miss a beat.

 

“No apologies necessary. Let’s get started.”

 

The rest of the appointment goes by smoothly. 

 

Jen has Rio change positions when she needs her to, pokes gently at her tummy to get Sprout to move, has her sit up and walk around when Sprout gets shy and starts hiding away. She checks on the baby’s brain, heart, umbilical cord, major internal organs, and more, all the while talking Rio and Agatha through each of her assessments as they come. Sprout looks healthy and active, and Jen confirms that their measurements all appear to be on track for how far along Rio is. As for Rio, Jen informs her that her placenta is nicely positioned towards the back of her uterus, that her cervix looks good, and that the volume of her amniotic fluid appears normal. 

 

They get to listen to Sprout’s heartbeat again, and Jen marks a few images on the machine for printing.

 

“Are we looking to discuss the baby’s gender today, moms?” Jen asks towards the end of the appointment, wearing an expression of practiced neutrality. “I did get a pretty good look from the scans.”

 

Agatha, who must be in danger of losing the whole hand by now, doesn’t bat an eye as Rio’s fingers tightens around hers once more. She’s stunned to realize that they haven’t discussed this before. Why didn’t they discuss this before?

 

Does she want to know? Does Agatha?

 

Rio shoots her the kind of panicked look that wouldn’t be amiss on the face of a deer caught in the path of a speeding car. Agatha reaches out to rest her other hand on Rio’s thigh, right by the bend of her knee.

 

“There’s no wrong answer here, sweetheart. If you’d like to know, I would too. If you want to be surprised, so do I.”

 

“I—well—yes.” Rio’s cheeks burn as she stutters. Again, she turns to give Jen another nod. “Yes. I want to know.”

 

Jen’s face breaks into a soft, sincere smile.

 

“Congratulations, you two. You’re having a girl.”

 

 

Rio’s too keyed up to sleep on the drive home.

 

She fidgets in her seat, in the elevator, in the bathroom while she changes out of her gel-stained skirt and top. She fidgets all the way over to the kitchen, where she finds herself tearing into a box of Honey Nut Cheerios just to give her hands something to do.

 

A girl. 

 

They’re having a girl. 

 

It’s their daughter that Rio’s been poking at when she studies her bump in the mirror, their daughter who’s been making Rio crave sour Skittles like there’s no tomorrow. It’s their daughter that loves moving around more than usual whenever Rio laughs or when Agatha rests a glowing hand low on the swell of her belly to siphon energy towards her. It’s their daughter that Rio’s carrying so close to her heart, growing bigger and stronger day by day.

 

They’re having a daughter together, but they’re also caught in this weird limbo where they’re not talking to each other and Rio just can’t help it—she starts bawling into a bowl of dry cereal at the thought. That’s how Agatha finds her, bent over the counter and crying so hard that there’s a real risk of her ending up dry-heaving into the sink.

 

Agatha doesn’t hesitate to pull Rio into her arms. This, of course, only makes Rio cry harder.

 

“I don’t want to fight,” Rio weeps, burying her face against the side of Agatha’s neck, all semblance of composure gone. “I don’t, I don’t, please I don’t—”

 

She chokes on a sob, clinging to Agatha like the last safe port in a storm.

 

 

“You want to know why I shot you down like that,” Agatha says without preamble. “Before.”

 

They’re lying in bed together, legs and limbs tangled, with Rio resting as much of her body against Agatha’s as humanly possibly without climbing fully on top of her. Agatha had brought her here when she’d calmed down enough to breathe without struggling, sweeping her up into a carry before Rio could insist on walking. Even as tired as she is, Rio stirs at Agatha’s words.

 

“Yeah,” she admits, squirming back enough to be able to meet Agatha’s gaze evenly. She leaves her left hand where it is, resting above Agatha’s heart. “I do.”

 

“What do you know about covencraft?”

 

Rio shrugs. It’s not a topic that she’s formally studied. As a child, she knew that her family was her coven. Now, it’s just her, Lilia, Alice, and occasionally Lorna who participate in a few coven-ish traditions.

 

“The basics, I guess? Most covens are made up of family and friends. Witches are stronger together than we are apart,” Rio recites, calling on as much as she can remember from the few classes that had mentioned it. “We mourn together, we celebrate together. We’re meant to share blessings and burdens alike and all that jazz. Why?”

 

“Bigger covens, ones that have established themselves beyond a handful of families interconnected by blood or other bonds, are a little different. They’re also very rarely lost. Witches are constantly being found, apprenticed,” Agatha’s gaze falls to Rio’s rounded midsection as she continues. “Or born. Historically, these kinds of covens only die out for a small number of reasons. Disease, disaster, and dissent are the most common.”

 

“Okay. And you suspect the Boston coven of… what, exactly?”

 

“Dark magic.” Agatha says it like she’s making an observation about the weather. Rio, meanwhile, almost recoils in shock. “I looked into their history. They haven’t welcomed a child or kept a living apprentice in almost fifty years.”

 

Rio’s stomach sours.

 

“So you think they’re killing people.”

 

“No. I think a curse is killing them. I think they dabbled in magicks far darker and far greater than their capabilities, and I think it’s cost them everything.”

 

The wheel turns, puzzle pieces falling neatly together in the forefront of Rio’s mind.

 

“The auction is a trap.”

 

Agatha nods.

 

“Yes. There’s a chance that the curse dies out with them, and in that case, maybe the remaining coven members really do want to make sure that all they’ve learned continues to live on in some manner. Or they’ve gotten desperate enough to make a last-ditch attempt to save their own skins by sacrificing others.”

 

Unbe-fucking-lievable. So much for the bonds of sisterhood and the witches’ way.

 

“You can’t be the only one thinking this. Other coven leaders and company heads with enough resources and knowledge must’ve put the dots together too.” Agatha nods in the affirmative. Rio frowns. “Then why the hell is anyone going to Boston?”

 

“Hubris. People are greedy and foolish. Every single one of those fools wants power, no matter the cost.” The turbulence in Agatha’s eyes shifts into odd pensiveness. “My mother was the same way. She never loved me, but she did value me. I had round-the-clock private security until I was twenty years old. That’s when she thought I could be trusted to protect myself.”

 

Rio stays silent, sensing more to be said.

 

“The first kidnapping attempt that I was old enough to remember happened when I was four.” Agatha’s gaze turns cutting. Rio’s heart comes to an awful, lurching stop. Every drop of blood in her veins begins to freeze over as Agatha continues to speak. “Then when I was six. Again at seven. Twice when I was nine. The attempts didn’t stop until I proved that my power belonged only to me. Violently. Besides us and Jen, no one knows what our child is. Siphoning is a rare enough ability that most of the people who even know that she’s mine are probably assuming she takes after you. But trust me when I say that anyone eager enough to go to Boston for that grimoire is equally willing to kill over the chance that our daughter is like me.”

 

“I won’t risk that—I won’t risk either of you—for anything,” Agatha finishes, sounding far, far away.

 

The edges of Rio’s vision take on an ominously gray tinge. She fights against the wave of dizziness threatening to wash her away, breathing in through her nose and out through her mouth just as Lilia had taught her to back during the daily meditation phase that she’d dragged Rio through for months during her early teenage years. She feels herself somehow go even limper than she already was before, drowning in a bedspread that suddenly feels oceanic in its vastness.

 

A firm hand settles on her cheek, the press of it warm and grounding. Rio moves—no, is moved, pulled close to the source of the warmth and held there tightly.

 

“—safe, Rio. You’re safe. I’m so sorry. I won’t let anything happen to you, sweetheart. Come back to me, please. You’re both safe with me, I promise. You’re safe. You’re safe. You’re safe.”

 

Recognition, at last, manages to carve through her grief-stained fear.

 

Clarity trickles back in through the panicked fog that had descended over her, blanketing the world and muffling her senses. Rio recovers enough to wiggle the fingers of the hand still resting on Agatha’s chest, tapping out a message she hopes that Agatha’s heart will understand.

 

I’m sorry. I’m here. I know.
