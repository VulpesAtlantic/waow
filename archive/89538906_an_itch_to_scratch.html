An Itch to Scratch

by RhodiumDark

Summary

Two years after disappearing from the world, former Secret Service agent Rio and her wife Agatha have built a quiet life on a remote island.

So when Agatha starts acting suspicious, Rio is certain she’s close to losing her wife to the life they left behind.

But the real threat is something she never expected....Agatha wants a bunny.

Notes

A one-shot set after Master of Restraint (with notable exceptions)....if you haven't read that, stop what you are doing, block out the extra several hours and enjoy: https://archiveofourown.org/works/85952646/chapters/227232951

Two years later

Rio didn’t want to admit it, but she could tell Agatha was restless.

It had been two years since they left the US for their own slice of paradise. The first six months were spent settling into island life - and fucking. A lot of fucking.

After everything, neither of them had been in any hurry to do much else.

Then came their engagement. Like most things in their lives, it moved quickly. A week after Agatha proposed, they stood barefoot on their stretch of beach with a local Taunga officiating. Their only guests were their flock of chickens and the occasional crab wandering along the sand.

Agatha found a simple white dress in a tourist shop the day before and Rio wore a white linen button-up with matching linen pants. It was perfectly intimate and exactly what they wanted.

Now two years had passed. Two years since they disappeared off the map after changing the course of American history.

For two years, they felt on top of the world.

Their house had become more like a home. Rio’s favorite shells lined the bookshelves, collected from countless walks on the beach. Rio had added a garden to the little plot of land, the volcanic soil was ideal for vegetables. Agatha added a few herbal plants alongside Rio’s tomatoes, grown with herbal remedies in mind from the books she’d bought at the local shops. Rio took up drawing, Agatha took up painting, filling the walls of their home with artwork. The woven rugs had worn patterns from their footsteps, the near constant feeling of sand embedded into the fibers.

Everything felt peaceful. Impossibly perfect.

But then Rio started noticing little signs from Agatha.

—

Rio woke up to find Agatha’s side of the bed empty. She reached across the mattress, her fingertips brushing on cool sheets. Cold. Agatha hadn’t been there for hours.

With a small sigh, Rio stretched before rolling onto her back. Beyond the bedroom window, the sun had only just risen, the ocean shimmering with the early morning light.

She pulled on an oversized sweater to chase away the morning chill.

It wasn’t like Agatha to get up this early.

Sure, the first few months there, she would get up before Rio. She would jog for miles along the beach or swim against the waves until her arms burned. Old habits from her assassin days to stay in shape. The routine faded with time, replaced with lazy mornings in bed and casual walks in the sand.

The house was quiet. Agatha wasn’t in the bathroom or reading curled up in an armchair by the front windows. The only sounds were the ceiling fan turning continuously overhead and the waves breaking against the shore.

Rio made two cups of coffee, balancing them carefully as she stepped barefoot onto the sand.

“Agatha?”

She found her perched on a rock near the waterline, knees tucked beneath her, a faded purple blanket wrapped around her shoulders.

“Hey,” Agatha said, looking up as Rio offered her a mug.

Rio frowned. There were dark shadows beneath Agatha’s eyes. Her hair was windblown, like she had been sitting there a while.

“You okay?”

“Couldn’t sleep.”

Rio settled onto the rock beside her, pressing into her shoulder. They watched the waves in silence, sipping their coffee as the warmth seeped into their bones.

“Bad dream?” Rio gently probed as the silence stretched on.

Agatha shook her head.

“Then what’s got you awake before the chickens? They didn’t even get the chance to squawk at you this morning.”

Agatha offered a small smile. “Couldn’t turn my brain off.”

Rio hummed. She knew the feeling. Most nights when the job was too intense, the danger too close, she slept in short, fitful bursts. Now the island seemed to have seeped into her bones. Most nights, the chirping cricket and Agatha’s soft breathing next to her quieted her mind.

“Thinking about something?”

“A lot of things,” Agatha offered.

Rio tilted her head, waiting. They weren’t afraid of silence. It was a comfort that came with knowing someone so completely - words stopped feeling necessary all the time. They could sit in each other’s presence and be content.

This felt different. Like the words were sitting just under the surface, waiting to bubble up.

They never came.

Instead, Agatha shifted closer, resting her head against Rio’s shoulder. She closed her eyes to the sounds of the beach.

Rio slipped an arm around her waist and kissed the top of her head.

“Whenever you’re ready,” she murmured.

Agatha only nodded.

—

“I want to go to the island tomorrow,” Agatha mentioned two days later.

Rio glanced up from where she was trimming the tomato plants in the garden.

They didn’t keep a strict schedule for when they ventured to the main island. Usually they went together, taking turns driving the boat. Once they got to town, they would often wander to different shops, meeting back together over lunch or coffee.

But this time, they had only been there a few days before. Their fridge was well stocked, chickens well fed. There was no incoming storm or anything that would require supplies.

“Anything in particular you need?” Rio asked. “I’m sure someone from the village is headed over. They could grab it for us.”

“No,” Agatha replied. “I want to go myself.”

Rio studied her for a moment. “Okay,” She smiled. “We can make a day of it, go to that little cafe you like.”

—

Once they cleared the dock, Rio eased back on the throttle. The boat settled into an easy glide over the glassy water, leaving only a gentle wake behind them.

The morning sun was warm on Rio’s shoulders, promising an even hotter afternoon. Further out to sea, a pod of dolphins surfaced before disappearing beneath the waves again.

“Do we think we could grow carrots in the garden?” Agatha asked, her eyes fixed on the horizon. She trailed her fingers through the water as she spoke.

Rio shrugged. “I don’t see why not,” she replied. “But you hardly eat them.”

Agatha hummed thoughtfully, not turning around. “No.”

Rio waited for the rest. But it never came. She looked over at her wife, who seemed utterly absorbed in watching the ocean drift by.

“I think we could try to grow avocados too,” Rio added. “I know a few locals have had good luck with them.”

Agatha thought for a second, then her eyes grew wide. “No, no,” she shook her head. “Not avocados. Never avocados.”

“Right,” Rio questioned. “That’s because…” She trailed off, hoping Agatha would finish the sentence.

“I’ll help tie the boat!” Agatha jumped up, taking a giant step onto the dock as they approached.

Apparently the discussion was over.

Agatha had the boat tied and she was halfway down the dock before Rio even stopped off of it.

“I’m going to swing by the bookstore,” Agatha called over her shoulder. “Coffee in an hour?”

Rio frowned. She loved that bookstore. Agatha knew that. They usually wandered through it together, disappearing into various aisles before finding each other again to show off whatever they’d discovered. It wasn’t like Agatha to go without her.

“Umm, yeah,” Rio called back.

Agatha lifted a hand in acknowledgement but never slowed her pace.

Rio told herself not to read into it. People were allowed to run errands. Agatha could browse a bookstore without her. It wasn’t a big deal.

Except as Rio wandered through various stores absentmindedly, she kept thinking about Agatha. She found herself staring through shop windows, unfocused with no plans to purchase anything.

With nothing else to do, she found herself headed to the bookstore. The familiar bell chimed as she opened the door.

Agatha stood at the counter, leaning onto the wood and chatting with the clerk.

“…about two weeks,” Rio overheard the clerk say. “It has to be special ordered.”

Agatha lowered her voice. “That’s fine.”

The clerk scribbled onto an order form, sliding it toward Agatha. She signed it, handing it back to him before she turned toward the door where Rio stood.

Their eyes met across the small store.

For the briefest moment, surprise flickered across Agatha’s face before it disappeared into an easy smile. “There you are.”

“Here I am,” Rio quipped back. “Find something?”

Agatha let out a loud sigh. “Nothing special.”

—

Now Rio was sure there was something going on with Agatha. This all felt strangely familiar, like when they first met. Full of secrets. Every answer to a question was too vague, too laced in mystery.

A tiny thread of doubt wove into Rio’s chest. She hated how those instincts returned.

Two years of peace and her mind was already racing toward the worst.

She sat across from Agatha at a small cafe overlooking the harbor, absently stirring cream into her coffee while she watched her wife work though a stack of newspapers she’d collected on the walk over. The round table was far too small for the giant stacks of papers, they practically overflowed off the edges. Rio scooted her coffee toward herself, again, as Agatha opened another newspaper, covering more of the table.

She had gathered a local paper, two from nearby countries and three from the US.

Agatha’s brow was furrowed in concentration, flipping through the pages. Her eyes scrolled down the page, circling various sections.

It wasn’t unusual for Agatha to browse the papers, reading little stories here and there about politics. She would laugh and make a quip about stories she thought might be The Coven, then toss it to the side, changing the subject. She would never dwell on it.

Rio never asked why Agatha kept looking, why she brought herself back to that place again and again. It always seemed like more of idle curiosity than longing.

Until today. Today, Agatha looked through each paper carefully, examining the stories like she was looking for something. Or someone.

Paradise had given them both peace, but now Rio was worried it took away that part of her that made her feel alive. It would explain her behavior the last few days.

Agatha wasn’t happy. She wanted to go back to the adventurous life she once had.

Rio watched her flip from page to page, making tiny notes and circling various sections.

That had to be it. There was no other logical explanation Rio could think of.

Rio leaned slightly over the table, trying to see what Agatha was so focused on.

The table wobbled beneath the shift of Rio’s weight. Her coffee lurched toward the edge.

“Rio!” Agatha dropped the paper, her arm shooting out to catch the tipping cup. But it was too late.

The mug tipped over, hot coffee spilling out, instantly soaking into the newspapers and dripping off the edge of the table.

“Shit,” Rio lunged for a handful of napkins. “I’m so sorry.”

Agatha stared down at the rapidly spreading stain. She sighed, pulling the newspapers off the table and shaking them out slightly.

“You’re mad,” Rio dropped back into her chair.

“No,” Agatha shook her head. She folded up the newspapers, standing to toss them in the trash. “I’m sticky.”

“I wasn’t trying to…”

“Let’s just go home,” Agatha cut her off, her voice clipped.

—

Rio debated calling Lilia. Maybe she would know what to do. Maybe The Coven needed one last job - just enough to scratch whatever itch Agatha couldn’t seem to shake. The thoughts lodged themselves deep in Rio’s brain like a splinter - persistent, annoying and refused to leave.

Maybe after a job, they could go back to normal. They could go back to laughing naked as they swam in the ocean, warming themselves up on the beach afterward. They would make dinner together again, Agatha trying and failing to follow a recipe.

Rio couldn’t focus on the movie playing on the tv. They had settled on the couch dinner in hand while a nonsense romcom played. Rio isn’t even sure why they bothered. The movie was far too straight for both their tastes.

Rio spent the time watching Agatha’s foot bounces endlessly against the floor next to her. Her food was as untouched as Rio’s, simply pushed around her plate with her fork.

It was getting to be too much. It had been weeks now - early mornings without Agatha in their bed, growing stacks of newspapers and unfocused conversation where Agatha was off in her own world.

Rio tried to ignore it. Tried to excuse it away. But she couldn’t anymore. She couldn’t live like this. She needed to fix whatever this was that was making Agatha turn into someone else entirely.

“You’re restless,” Rio said at last.

“What?” Agatha looked up from her plate.

Rio took a slow breath. “Like I’m not making you happy anymore.”

“Rio.”

“I see you looking over the newspapers, Agatha. Like you are looking for a reason to go back to all of it…to them.”

At that, Agatha burst out laughing. Not a subtle giggle, but a full-bodied, breathless laugh. She doubled over until she had to wipe tears from the corners of her eyes.

“You’re laughing at me,” Rio huffed.

“I am,” Agatha managed between breaths.

Rio crossed her arms. “You told me you were done but you want to go back! I just know it.”

That only made Agatha laugh harder.

But Rio persisted. “I know you said you would be happy being retired, but if this isn’t the life you want…if I’m not the life you want…you need to tell me. I can’t keep doing this Agatha. I feel like I’m married to the shadow of you. And I don’t want that. You don’t want that. If we need to go back to The Coven…or another job…” Rio was rambling now. “Maybe Lilia can find something for us.”

“Oh,” Agatha gasped, pressing a hand to her chest. “Oh, this is going to sound so ridiculous now.”

Rio shook her head. “I swear to god Agatha, if you…”

“I’m trying to get a bunny,” Agatha cut her off.

Rio swore she heard her wrong. She was prepared for excuses about needing more adventure, needing to feel the excitement of a case again. She was prepared to hear that the island felt too small and the world too big to be just sitting here on the beach day after day. She was prepared for a lot of things. But not…this.

The last few weeks replayed in her mind. The questions about carrots, the book that was hard to get.

“Like the animal?” She asked.

“I’m not sure what other kinds of bunnies there are.”

“What about the newspapers?” Rio wondered out loud.

“I was looking for pet listings.” Agatha said sheepishly. “Turns out there aren’t many of them, especially for rabbits.”

Rio stared. “I believed you were planning to rejoin an international assassination network…because you wanted a rabbit.”

Agatha scoffed. “And you say I’m the dramatic one.” She rose from the couch, grabbing their plates off the coffee table. She headed into the kitchen like the conversation was over. But Rio’s mind was still reeling.

“Why didn’t you tell me?” Rio called out to her retreating form.

Agatha stopped mid-stride, her back still turned to Rio. She muttered “dunno” under her breath and kept walking until she reached the kitchen sink. She slipped on rubber gloves, starting the water and soaping up the plates left there.

Rio came up behind her, wrapping her arms around her and placing her chin on her shoulder. “I know there is a reason.” she murmured into her hair. “Please tell me.”

Agatha gently let go of the plate, letting it splash into the soapy water. “Why do you say that?”

“Because you hate doing dishes.”

Agatha sighed, pulling off the gloves and spinning to face Rio. “It’s going to sound stupid.”

Rio smiled softly at her. “Nothing you think is ever stupid.”

“Fine,” Agatha smiled sadly. “When I was little…I always wanted one.”

Rio stayed quiet, just watching her as she spoke.

“And you know my home life wasn’t exactly stable.”

Rio nodded.

“Then I became…well.” She waved a hand dismissively. “An assassin’s schedule isn’t exactly conducive to being a pet parent. And now…” She continued. “We have a home, a garden. You have your chickens and insects. I just thought…” Her voice trailed off.

“You’re going to put him in our bed, aren’t you?” Rio joked.

But Agatha looked serious. “He isn’t a bug, Rio. He’d be my baby.”

All this time, Rio had been terrified that Agatha was looking backward, but she was really looking forward, toward their future.

“You really want this, don’t you?”

“I’ve done my research,” Agatha tilted her chin up. “The right habitat, diet, vet care we can provide.”

“We’re getting the bunny,” Rio said definitively.

“Really?”

Rio nodded.

Agatha put her hands in her, squeezing gently. “Thank you,” she whispered softly. “But it turns out, it won’t work out.” She bit her lip and Rio swore she was trying not to cry.

Rio furrowed her brow. “Why not?”

“It’s impossible,” Agatha shrugged like that solves it. “They aren’t native to the island and it’s not like there are just pet stores to wander into.”

Rio shook her head then leaned forward to kiss the very tip of Agatha’s nose. “My wife wants a bunny.”

“She does.”

“Then I will find a way to get her a bunny.”

—

Turned out, Rio should not have made such hasty promises.

Agatha was right. Getting a bunny was fucking hard.

The nearest veterinarian was on the Cook Islands, which was 19 hours away by plane. He wasn’t even sure the last time he had cared for a bunny.

“Maybe 10 years ago?” He explained on the phone.

Rio pinched the bridge of her nose. “Years?” Rio asked again.

“Basically extinct here. There was that one guy who kept one as a pet for a while.”

That led Rio on a wild goose chance of phone calls. That guy’s rabbit died two years earlier, but he’d gotten it from a man on another island.

Turns out the second guy hadn’t bred rabbits in over a decade, but he knew someone who might.

That someone turned out to raise goats.

Rio almost said yes but remembered they eat everything, including the carrots Agatha had been caring for in preparation of their new arrival.

Another local mentioned Rabbit Island off the coast of Oahu, explaining that a bunch of rabbits were released in the late 1800s. Rio’s excitement quickly died when she found out they disappeared in 1970’s. Rio was tempted to take the 20-hour journey just to check for herself, then she learned it’s illegal to even land on the island.

Back to square one.

The next morning, she had spread a paper map of the South Pacific across the dining room table. Islands had been crossed out, circled and scribbled over with increasingly desperate notes.

Agatha wandered in, coffee in hand.

“What are you doing?” She peered over Rio’s shoulder.

“Research.”

“For life as a pirate?” Agatha took another sip.

Rio looked up at her. “Yo ho.”

Agatha laughed before her eyes settled on the various notes scribbled across the map. She dropped into the chair next to Rio. “This looks impossible.”

“Not impossible,” Rio looked over the map again, crossing out another route. “Just considerably more difficult than I imagined.” She sighed. "Apparently there are a few disadvantages of living so remotely.”

Agatha offered her a sad smile. “Maybe it isn’t meant to be.”

Rio’s head snapped up. “Don’t you dare.”

Agatha leaned back in her chair and crossed her arms.

“I mean it,” Rio pointed the end of the pen at her. “I will get you a bunny, even if it means flying back to the States and smuggling one home.”

“You? Back in the US?” Agatha tilted her head and lifted an eyebrow. “I can see the conspiracy theories already.”

“Then I’ll dye my hair. Get some botox. Maybe filler.” Rio rubbed a hand across her forehead. “I think all this sun is giving me wrinkles anyway.”

“All this…for a bunny?” Agatha asked.

Rio reached over, lacing their fingers together.

“For you, my love. Anything for you.”

—

It had been another month without any real leads. Rio didn’t want to admit defeat but it was feeling more and more likely. Every possible lead she could think of had been exhausted - every neighbor, vet and cargo captain that might come across a rabbit knew of her pleas.

Finally, one afternoon a monsoon swept through the island. The rain was relentless, turning the beach into a blur of grey. Choppy waves crashed onto the sand but they were barely heard over the sounds of the thunder.

Rio was standing in the living room, looking out the window at the garden. She was so focused on making sure the tarp protecting the garden was secured that she almost missed the knock on the front door.

An elderly fisherman she’d never seen before stood on the porch, his clothes soaked through from the rain. “I heard you’re looking for a rabbit.” When he smiled, rain droplets fell from his beard.

Rio sighed, waiting for the disappointment that followed. “...yes.”

“I’m sorry I couldn’t get here sooner,” he apologized, removing his hat as he did. “I was out to sea for a while and then the storm pushed us toward the island for the day.”

Rio opened the door wider. “Do you want to come in or…”

The man shook his head then wiped the rain from his eyes. “This is just a short visit. I wanted to let you know that I have a niece over in New Zealand,” he pointed down the beach like he could direct her there. “She volunteers for a rabbit rescue.”

“Oh,” Rio tried not to sound too excited.

“You know they are considered pests there?”

“So I’ve heard,” Rio muttered.

“Anyway, she told me they’ve got this little fella, really tiny and undernourished. She’s been caring for him for a while now, getting him healthy.”

By this point, Rio was sure he came all this way just to tell her a cool story about the rescue, maybe ask for a donation for his niece.

But he continued, “I’ll be passing through one of their ports next month. She thinks he’ll be ready for a new home by then.” He hesitated. “If you still want one.”

“Yes!” Rio blurted out. Then shook her head, trying to compose herself. “I mean, of course. We would love to take him in.”

Then she turned toward the house, calling out toward the living room. “AGATHA!”

Agatha appeared seconds later, holding her toothbrush in one hand and looking alarmed. “What happened?”

Rio tilted her chin up, gesturing toward the man at the doorway.

“You must be the mama.” The old man smiled when he saw her.

“I’m sorry?” Agatha asked, looking toward Rio for an explanation. “I found you a bunny,” she said proudly.

Agatha’s eyes grew wide.

—

“My love, you have to stop biting your nails,” Rio reached out, lowering Agatha’s hand from her mouth.

Agatha took to biting her knuckles instead.

They stood out on the beach, shoulder to shoulder, watching the horizon. Rio pulled Agatha’s hand down from her mouth to thread their fingers together, giving her hand a squeeze.

“It’s going to be fine,” Rio reassured her. “He’s going to love us.”

“I just…” Agatha dropped Rio’s hand, turning to her. “But what if he doesn’t? Rabbits aren’t native to the island. He could hate the beach.”

“Then we build him a sand-free enclosure.”

Agatha studied her face. “He could hate the sun.”

“Then I build him some shade.”

They could see it now, a tiny boat in the distance, steadily making its way toward the island. Beside her, Rio felt Agatha’s breathing grow just a little quicker as the boat approached.

Agatha’s gaze dropped to the sand where she absently pushed sand around with her big toe. “He could hate the garden,” she mumbled half-heartedly.

Rio glanced sideways at her. “I hope he hates the garden. I don’t need him eating my azaleas.”

At that Agatha scoffed, “Those are my azaleas.”

Rio shook her head. “You only planted them. I’m the one who takes care of them.”

Agatha finally cracked a smile. “So teamwork?” Some of the tension finally left her shoulders.

“Teamwork.”

The boat slowed as it approached, killing the motor to float carefully into the shallow waters near their house. Rio was surprised to see more than just the older fishermen on board, but a girl no more than twenty standing beside him. Her blonde hair was in a braid, wind blown strands pulling out at the sides. She waved excitedly toward them, holding up a small pet carrier into the air and pointing at it repeatedly.

“There he is,” Agatha breathed to herself.

The fisherman hopped into the shallow water, boots splashing as he steadied the boat against the shore. His niece followed closely behind, stepping carefully off the boat. She cradled the carrier against her chest with both hands, protecting it from the waves.

As they approached, Rio could hear small chewing noises coming from inside.

“You must be Agatha,” the woman said excitedly.

Agatha nodded, biting at her lip. She kept her hands at her sides like she was waiting for permission to reach out to the carrier.

“He’s been through a lot, but he’s the sweetest little thing.” The woman looked down at the carrier. “He was found alone. Probably the runt of a litter.”

She handed the carrier over and Agatha took it gently. She steadied it against her chest before lowering herself to her knees in the sand.

“Can I?” She asked, her fingers hovering over the wire door.

“He’s all yours.” The woman smiled.

“Hi there,” Agatha whispered through the wire.

Rio held her breath for a moment. Agatha was looking into that carrier like it held her entire world.

Finally, a tiny pink nose appeared at the wires. It twitched once. Twice.

Agatha reached forward, unhooking the small wire closure to open the door. A pair of impossibly soft, white lopsided ears followed, along with two enormous dark eyes.

“Hi sweetheart,” Agatha breathed.

The bunny took a small, cautious hop forward. His front feet hit the stand and he immediately jumped back into the carrier.

“Sand-free enclosure it is,” Rio laughed.

Agatha looked over her shoulder with a triumphant grin. “I told you he might hate the beach.” Then she turned back to the carrier and held out both hands, palms up.

“It’s ok,” she murmured to the rabbit. “You don’t have to like the sand.”

As if he understood her words, the rabbit hopped forward, landing in her palms. It fit perfectly in her hands.

Agatha brought him closer to her chest, cradling him against her. She rested her cheek against his soft fur.

“He’s so small,” Rio added.

The bunny looked at Rio, swiping a paw toward her like he was protecting his new found home. Agatha laughed softly, trying not to startle him. He turned toward the noise, stretching his neck up toward Agatha’s face. His nose bumped hers but he didn’t pull away.

“I think he was trying to scratch you,” the fisherman’s niece chuckled. Rio almost forgot she was there, her eyes had been so focused on watching Agatha.

“I love you already,” Agatha whispered into his fur. The bunny settled against her with a tiny, contented sigh, as though he’d been looking for this home all along.

—

That night, after the fisherman and his niece had left, it was just the three of them, sitting on the floor, watching the small ball of fluff scurrying around.

“Am I being biased or is he the cutest bunny you’ve ever seen?” Agatha cooed, tilting her head.

“I have to agree with you.” Rio leaned her head on Agatha’s shoulder, stroking a hand through the rabbit’s fur. “He still needs a name.”

Agatha reached out, carefully picking up the rabbit and turning him until he faced her. “What do you think mister?”

She studied him for a second, his nose twitching next to her.

“Peanut Butter?” She asked, like he would respond.

He didn’t react.

Rio leaned over. “Señor, you want me to pick your name, don’t you?”

Agatha lowered him onto Rio’s lap. He turned in a small circle, before dropping dramatically onto his side. Rio sighed, smiling toward Agatha at the small act of acceptance.

Rio stroked down his fur before taking to scratch behind one big floppy ear. He leaned into her touch before relaxing completely. His eyes closed contentedly as she continued.

“He really loves those scratches,” Agatha pointed out.

“You do,” Rio spoke directly to him. “Don’t you Mister Scratchy Pants?”

Agatha made a face. “You are not naming our bunny child Scratchy Pants.”

“Mister Scratchy?”

“Ehhh,” Agatha thought for a second. “Señor Scratchy.”

“It’s perfect.”

They looked down at the small bunny, now sleeping happily in Rio’s lap. Agatha reached over slowly, brushing along his back once again.

“This was…a lot,” Agatha sighed, the exhaustion noticeable in her voice. “I made it harder than it needed to be.”

“Hey now,” Rio whispered, not wanting to wake their new addition. “You don’t have to apologize for your feelings.”

Agatha turned toward Rio more fully now, offering her a soft kiss. “But you don’t have to be afraid of losing me. Not to The Coven. Not to anyone. I know I scared you for a while but you should know, I’m not going back there.”

“Never?”

“Not if you don’t want me to,” Agatha shook her head.

Rio took a deep breath, looking straight into Agatha’s eyes. “What if I did?”

Agatha pulled back, furrowing her brow. “What?”

“I’m just saying, if The Coven ever called and wanted us back,” Rio’s voice dropped, sounding more dangerous. “Like old times…” Her fingers trailed along Agatha’s thigh. “Work and play. Would you take it?”

Agatha shivered at Rio’s fingertips trailing higher up her leg. “If you wanted to,” she sounded strained.

Rio pulled her fingers back suddenly, straightening her back. “Good,” she said.

Agatha narrowed her eyes at her. “What do you mean by good…”

“Because Lilia called.”
