Their two words could not be more different.

Two months after she broke up with Tina, and a month and a half since she’d left for college, she was in a thrift store with her new friend, Alice. They’d fallen in love with each other the moment they’d been assigned the same room and Rio recognised in Alice a quiet, determined spirit that had hidden fire and the dirtiest mouth she’d ever heard.

Alice being a closet case only made them closer. And Rio found a firmer friend in her than she had in anyone she’d ever met.

And now, in the little store in downtown Newark, there, hidden amongst the old shirts was a faded pink sleeve. Rio frowned as she riffled through the rail to get to it.

It shone like a beacon amidst the mostly subdued, mostly muted tones of the others.

And then: cowboys.

Something about it lit something inside Rio. That beacon again. She held the shirt up, looked at each little cowboy throwing each little lasso.

“That is the ugliest fucking shirt I’ve ever seen. You’ll never get a girl wearing that.”

Rio just laughed, now cradling the shirt to her chest as if someone were trying to take it from her.

She grinned at her new best friend, raising an arrogant brow in challenge: “Maybe I’ll get the girl with it.”
