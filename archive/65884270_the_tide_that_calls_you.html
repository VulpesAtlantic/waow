Her brain yells for air, her chest aches with agony.

Blades scrape against wood and skin as the fight becomes primal, boots slipping on wet boards, fists landing with raw force. One intruder makes a break for the gangway but is swiftly tackled by one of her deck-hands who appears from the shadows. It’s carnage. All she sees are blood-splashed faces in the dim light. And for the first time she entertains the thought Agatha had put in her head.

Would this be a nicer, safer life if it was lived on land?

She has no time to think it through. She has just about extinguished the fire, when another man aims at her, falling forward with a long blade, screaming. She manages to push his arm up and embeds her dagger right in his gut. He gurgles, hanging over her shoulder, his body going limp. He clutches at her for a moment, mouth agape and eyes wide with disbelief, before his knees buckle. Rio staggers back as his weight slips from her shoulder, dragging him toward the edge. The wind howls around them as the ship rocks beneath her - then he tumbles backward, vanishing over the railing like a discarded shadow. He falls overboard and into the black waves with a loud splash. 

In her ears Rio hears the blood rushing so loudly it almost drowns out the sound of the sea. She feels her heartbeat in the vein on her neck, all of her hairs there standing upright.

Agatha.

Rio turns around to where Frank is gutting the last attacker, knife deep in his stomach. 

“Where is Agatha?’, she yells in black, liquid horror.

“She came to warn us, I didn’t see her after that,” someone yells from above- and Rio rushes into the corridor.

“Agatha?”, she yells, “AGATHA!”

Hot, boiling fear pumps through her veins as she runs towards her room. The door is open, but Agatha isn’t there. She gets on her knees, looks under the bed, lifts the blankets, but there is no sight of her. She checks her own cabin next, screams her name into the emptiness. She isn’t there.

What if one of them has gotten to her? What if one got away with her, holding her hostage or even worse? She stumbles back out of her cabin and hits the wall with her back. Her side hurts. But what hurts even more is the thought of any harm coming to Agatha. It’s her fault that she is here, it’s her fault that she hasn't already gotten her home safely. Suddenly, the idea of Agatha being safely married somewhere else sounds like heaven. 

She stumbles on, checks the kitchen and Agatha’s mending room.

Nothing.

Only then does she crawl deeper into the hull, down to where they store the load. There is one room she hasn’t checked, and it isn’t even a room. It’s the broom-closet the men had confined Agatha in so many weeks ago. If she is still on the ship, it’s the only space she could be in. Rio opens the door carefully in case Agatha is ready to attack her with whatever is available to her.

But she doesn’t.

Agatha is hunched up in the left corner of the small space, tears over her cheeks, Scratchy between her knees and her chest, flush against her body, carefully kissing her chin as if to soothe her. She weeps and snorts when she sees Rio, shaking on the floor. Her legs relax as Rio stumbles and gets on her knees to take her into her arms. Stretching out her own, Agatha reaches and asks for it.

“You’re okay, you’re okay, you’re okay,” she slurs her words and buries her face in the crook of Rio’s neck, the bunny hopping down to escape them. 

“No it’s all fine, everything is fine,” Rio whispers and wraps one hand around the back of her head, gently caressing Agatha’s hair.

“W-what happened?” Agatha sobs, lifting her head, looking at Rio. But she doesn’t let her answer. Instead, she looks at her with glassy eyes then takes her face in both of her hands and pulls Rio’s lips to hers. 

It’s as if a firework is being lit somewhere, bright and sudden. Rio’s breath catches, her body frozen for a heartbeat before melting into the warmth of Agatha’s hands. The world narrows to the press of lips and the salt on their skin, the ache in her chest unraveling into something tender and electric. She’s dizzy, not from the fight or the blood still drying on her skin, but from the way Agatha’s touch feels like both a question and an answer.

But Rio barely has any time to think about what it might mean. At first, it feels like it is merely the new and overwhelming feeling of the kiss that sends her mind spinning. Only slowly does she realize that the pain in her side is not dry and dull, but shark and wet.

“It’s uhm- it’s all fine they… they’re gone now,” she answers, reaching for her hip, her hand coming back bloody.

“What? No-!”

It’s the last thing she hears before she collapses into Agatha and her world goes dark.

 

She dreams of less than nothing. Not even darkness.

Her mind is so empty, she isn’t even sure it is still existing, still human. She drifts - nothing below her and nothing above. The only thing she feels is the throbbing in her side, loud and anxious. She slips in and out of feverish consciousness, but even when she wakes, she never conceives anything at all.

She knows not how long she exists in this state. It might as well be death itself. If it is, it is good that the last thing she has gotten to do was kissing Agatha’s lips. It is the first coherent thought she forms. It carries her back into existence where a heavy, fluffy thing sits on her chest, head tilted, nose scrunched.

“She’s awake! Quick, get Harry, she’s awake!”

Rio hears someone shout beside her. A woman. Her woman.

Scratchy abandons his watch on her chest when Agatha drops a basket with dirty linen on the floor, rushing to her side.

“You’re awake,” she sighs, “finally!”

Rio looks at her confused. She feels dried, sticky sweat on her forehead, “h-how long did I sleep?”

“Three full days, Rio I was so worried,” Agatha swallows hard and places a cool, wet towel on her forehead, “you stupid fucking idiot, running around like a madwoman with your hip slashed open! The boys were cleaning up your mess for hours!”

Rio chuckles.

There she is. Furious and beautiful.

For a moment, she wonders whether Agatha will let her kiss her again, but then Harry barges in, along with the rest of her crew, one after the other sticking their heads in as she raises herself up on her elbows to smile at them.

 

Later, after Terry has made her soup, Agatha gently feeding it to her spoon by spoon, Harry takes a seat next to her, a frown on his forehead, but relief in his voice.

“They must have followed us all the way from Cartavegna. I wouldn’t be surprised if it were our very constituents. Probably just needed someone to transport the dagger out of the port…”

“The dagger?”

“Miss Agatha and Billy opened the crate. It’s a steel dagger, the hilt is wrapped in green leather,” he shrugs, “some nonsense poem was included… something… well it’s not important.”

“What is it?” she asks.

“Something about cutting the fabric between life and death,” he looks spooked, but then Rio knows he believes in the curses and omens of his homeland.

“What did you do with it?”

“We left the archipelago, but we did not meet another ship. Whoever wanted it delivered to them never showed their face. Miss Agatha wants to sell it to the highest bidder in Westport… but I would like to give it to the sea. Sink it in the deepest depths we know.”

Rio looks at him long and hard.

“My first priority is Miss Agatha,” she changes the subject. She can worry about the dagger later, “she has spent enough time with us. I-I can’t have her in harm’s way again,” she knows her voice breaks as she speaks. Any thought of danger coming to Agatha constricts her throat. 

“Captain…”

“No", she cuts him off, "tell Billy to chart us the quickest course to the nearest harbor. I want one of you to escort her home. We will pick you up when we reach Westport by sea.”

“Have you spoken about this to Miss Agatha,” he raises a brow.

“We kidnapped her and now we shall release her from her prison.”

“But-”

“I will not discuss this any further, Harry, please,” she sighs and closes her eyes, “spare me the grief.”

He looks down, but nods before he rises from the chair, “I shall let Billy know. If we sail through the night we can reach the coast by morning.”

“Thank you Harry.”

As he opens the door, she sees Agatha peeking through, fresh dressings in her arms, Scratchy hopping into the cabin ahead of her. Rio smiles at their inseparability. 

Agatha makes her lift herself up on the bed to inspect her bandages before she takes them off and washes her wound with salt water followed by clear liquor that makes Rio flinch and jerk with every time the liquid touches the open wound.

“We will make for the coast tonight. Billy is looking for a port that has roads to Westport. One of the men will travel with you for your protection,” she huffs through the pain, unable to look at Agatha.

She doesn’t respond.

“I’d like to pay you for your time here," she continues, "we look better than we have in years. I was hoping if all of this sounds more like honest employment-”

“Honest employment?” Agatha scoffs.

“I mean not for the- well… I meant for the sewing,” Rio goes red as a cherry. She turns her head towards Agatha, but when she does not look up, Rio stares into the candlelight instead.

“I’m not your whore.”

“I know,” she whispers.

She wraps the clean linen around Rio’s waist, pulls on the hard so they sit so tight Rio almost feels unable to breathe.

“I wanted this.”

“I assumed you did…”

“And now you want to send me home with a satchel full of coins so I can tell my mother I found honest employment on a pirate ship? Pardon - smuggler’s ship?”

Rio swallows hard again, “I wasn’t-”

“You’re pathetic. You disgust me,” Agatha whispers in the softest of tones before she ties the linen together, stuffing the knot into the uppermost layer, and leaves without looking at Rio again.

She doesn’t move at first. Only when hot tears begin running down her face does she carefully lay down on the side that isn’t injured and blows out the last candle, sobbing in the dark.

 

The wind is in their favor. It carries them ashore over the course of the night as they sail through the dark, the water restless underneath. The moon hangs low, indifferent to the ache in Rio’s chest. Each wave brings them closer to land, and closer to the moment she dreads. The sea, normally like an old friend, has turned into a cruel accomplice fueling her insomnia.

The men are out at the crack of dawn, working the sails to carry them safely into the harbor. When it is time, Billy helps her dress in a loose, white shirt. Her hair has come undone throughout the night. She leaves it open, falling over shoulders in dark curls as she steps outside into the harsh daylight with Billy’s help.

Agatha has packed all of her belongings into a single basket. It’s the two dresses she had sewn herself and what seems to be some provisions for the journey. They are already docked, the small ramp lowered onto the pier.

“Who is going with her?”, she whispers to Billy.

“Frank. He’s already found a carriage.”

She watches Agatha giving each of the men a tight embrace. She holds on to them, inspects their clothes one last time and jokes with them, a sad smile blooming on her lips. They each take their time with her. They each bless her, tell her to be good, wish her all the happiness in the world.

They step away when they have said their goodbyes. Rio can see that Harry ushers them below deck until it is only her and Rio left, the morning air making Agatha’s hair dance.

“Be well,” she says quickly, the basket in front of her in protection, “Billy will know how to dress your wounds, I’m sure you will manage.”

“He won't do it as well as you,” she offers. Her eyes dart to Agatha’s lips. She tries to look away as fast as she can.

“Mhm,” is all she replies before she lowers the basket so Scratchy can hop right in.

So this is it. This is goodbye, is it not?

Rio wants to hold her close, wants to feel Agatha’s body flush against her own one last time. But instead Agatha turns around and steps onto the ramp, taking slow, careful steps down. Torn between going back to her cabin to mourn and watching Agatha for as long as she can, Rio freezes in place. The wound on her side stings again.

“You know you ruined my life, do you?”, there is a tremble in her voice, but it is loud and clear.

Rio sighs and slouches her shoulders, “I am so sorry all of this happened to you, Agatha, I-I wish I could rewind time and make it so they never thought to take you,” she swallows hard.

To her surprise, Agatha begins to laugh. It is hollow and dishonest. Ridiculing and almost cocky.

“You sorry excuse for a captain. You can’t even look love in the eye even when she’s standing right in front of you. What a coward you are…”

Rio looks at her in shock.

“Tell me you want me to leave. C’mon, tell me you really never want to see me again!”

“It would be a lie!” Rio screams, not sure where the strength is coming from, “it would be a lie if I told you that!”

“Then don’t make me leave!”

“But you want to! You were abducted against your will, you had to live through a pirate attack, Agatha, please-”

“Do I look like I want to leave and go home to be married off to some disgusting old man?”, she yells. And apparently she has made her decision. She walks back all the way on board. Setting the basket down on the planks.

“I’d rather live through a hundred pirate attacks if it means I am a free woman. A free woman by your side!”

“You can’t mean that…” Rio murmurs, another swell of tears blooming in her eyes.

“I will not go,” Agatha sobs and her voice shivers, “I will not leave you.”

Rio’s lips part in shock, both cold and hot, until relief pushes through.

“Will you have me?” she asks, and she looks like a child, the terrifying Agatha Harkness finally unsure and terrified herself.

“Of course I will,” Rio whispers, almost no tone in her voice, but more sure now than she has ever been about something in her entire life. So instead of waiting for Agatha to come to her, she stumbles forward, right into her arms and kisses her like she is taking her first breath after surfacing from deep water - desperate, trembling, and full of life.
