Chapter 1

The steady hum of Salem Public School on a Tuesday morning slipped through the open door of the counselor’s office, where Jennifer Kale had been swiveling in her chair, blissfully zoned out, for quite some time now.

The faraway sounds of rustling leaves and birds chirping filtered through the crack in the old window, creating a pleasant soundtrack to her current musings.

Six weeks had passed since she took on her role as the new School Counselor, and she was doing a damn good job, all things considered.

The move up from New Jersey had been a long and taxing process. Although Salem was just a few hours away, the newness of it all took some getting used to. Hunting down an apartment in an unfamiliar city, entering a new workplace… starting from scratch, in many ways.

And yet, here she was, mid-October, finally starting to find her footing. She had learned her way around the sprawling maze of hallways, offices, and classrooms. Not to mention the students within them. From the eager preschoolers and gangly middle school preteens, to the seniors who were counting the days until they could leave it all behind and join the adult world—Jen had been there for all of them. Listening. Reassuring.

And let’s not forget one of her crowning achievements: bringing the decrepit Keurig machine in the staff room to its metaphorical knees. After weeks of spilled coffee and bitter, burnt-tasting brews that lingered on her tongue far longer than what was natural, she had finally bent the stubborn thing to her will.

Yes, she considered, she was doing quite alright. Had carved out a space for herself in this little microcosm of school life, even managing to build a solid rapport with students, parents, and teachers alike.

Well… almost.

In all honesty, there was only one little snag in her otherwise perfect rhythm. If she really had to complain (and she didn’t, mind you, but she’d like to anyway), there was just one thing that continued to disconcert her.

Well, not so much one thing as it was, quite frankly, two people.

But she wasn’t one to throw a colleague under the bus like that, no. No one would ever have to know that, in her mind, there were few people on this earth more irritating, more exasperating than —

"Ms. Harkness and Ms. Rio."

A voice cut through the quiet space.

Jen blinked, momentarily confused.

Shit, had she just said that out loud?

But the feeling of disorientation faded quickly as she realized a student was watching her expectantly from the open doorway of her office. She cleared her throat, shaking away the remnants of a rapidly spiraling internal monologue, and turned to face the boy.

It was Tommy Maximoff, her brain supplied helpfully, and she addressed him with an impressively cool, calm and collected response, as was her way.

"Wh—sorry, what did you say?"

Nice one, Jen.

"Uhm, yeah." the sixteen-year-old scratched his head. "I was just saying Ms. Harkness and Ms. Rio are arguing again, they said to come get you and uh—"

The kid looked bored of the situation in its entirety, which she empathized with, as there was only a modicum of professionalism left that kept her from letting her forehead smack into the wooden surface of her desk.

He went on, "Yeah, they want your opinion, I guess. But you seemed sorta spaced out when I came in here so if you want I can tell them you’re, like, busy cleaning chalkboards, or whatever."

Jen spluttered, searching for an appropriate response. Did this kid think she was the school custodian? They didn’t even have chalkboards here, probably hadn’t since before he was born.

"No, no. That’s sweet of you to offer, though, Tommy. I’ll be right there."

He nodded his head in acknowledgment and shuffled off.

Jen waited until his footsteps faded before gritting out, "Yes, I’d love nothing more than to play babysitter for two grown women who know I have much more important things to see to."

And yes, in case anyone should ask, the previous twenty-five minutes of staring blankly at the wall —considering her life’s choices—were an essential part of her workflow.

Everyone has their methods.

Reluctantly, Jen stood from her chair, summoned every ounce of patience she could muster, and strode toward the tenth-grade classrooms.

She passed the history room, where a group of students were dutifully waiting for their teacher to return. At the front, Natasha—Jen remembered—looked particularly stricken.

Jen stepped into the doorway. "Do any of you know where Ms. Harkness is?"

Natasha raised a shaky hand. "She’s across the hall. I’m so sorry—I just asked if she’d excuse my absence for a biology field trip." She bit her lip nervously. "I didn’t think it would be a problem."

Jen offered a reassuring smile. "Alright, thanks, Natasha. And don’t worry, we’ll figure it out."

Steeling herself, she turned to the biology room, hand hovering over the door handle.

God—or whoever’s listening—grant me strength .

Inside, Agatha Harkness and Rio Vidal stood nose to nose, voices hushed but sharp.

Agatha’s glare could melt steel, her irritation only rivaled by the sheer thrill of picking a fight.

Meanwhile, Rio—arms out, palms up—was simultaneously defending herself and placating the other teacher’s temper.

"It’s only half the period, Agatha." Rio’s voice was all practiced innocence. "And you haven’t even heard where we’re going."

Behind them, students watched the drama unfold with far too much interest. Jen, knowing how quickly things could go viral, cleared her throat pointedly.

"Both of you. In the hall. Now, please."

The look Agatha gave her was chilling. But, in the end, they traipsed out of the room like misbehaving children.

As soon as the door clicked shut, Agatha turned on Rio with a pointed finger.

"How many times do I have to tell you—" she barked, "not to call me that in front of them. It undermines my authority."

Before Rio could retort, Jen cut in. "Enough. Explain to me why you are disrupting your lessons right now."

That was all the invitation they needed, it seemed. Both women launched into overlapping defenses, voices rising as they spoke over each other.

Jen closed her eyes. Christ . Raising a perfectly manicured hand, she silenced them with a sharp look.

Then, pointing at Agatha—"You first."

With a roll of her eyes, Agatha sighed.

"Ms. Vidal here has planned a field trip next Thursday, which conveniently overlaps with my class. Almost half of my students will miss the critical lecture I have planned on the Salem Witch Trials and its manifestation in modern-day feminism."

Rio opened her mouth—but Agatha wasn’t finished. Not one to give even an inch, she silenced her with a glare.

"Not to mention," she continued loudly, "it was mere weeks ago that Ms. Vidal decided it was time for a class trip to the local zoo, which also happened to coincide with my lesson. An excursion whose educational merit remains… questionable at best."

Jen’s lips twitched. She could tell Rio wanted to argue—but Agatha’s melodrama was too entertaining to interrupt.

Jen watched in amusement as Agatha haughtily cast her long brown hair over her shoulder with a smug look of self-determined victory, as if it had all been decided now.

"Right," Jen said. "But as you know, Ms. Harkness, course-relevant field trips are excused absences. Is it unfortunate that it could take away from your time? Sure. But Rio is well within her rights to plan them."

Agatha scoffed. "Right, because that zoo day just screamed ‘course relevant’."

Jen knew she shouldn’t encourage her, but she couldn’t help but ask—"And why is that?"

Agatha crossed her arms. "Because the Maximoff boys spent the rest of the week showing everyone the pictures of them getting their faces painted by—" she lowered her voice, grunting the next words in a mock teenage boy impression—"like, the hottest zookeeper chick ever."

Jen’s shoulders shook with barely contained laughter.

Rio showed no such restraint. She cackled, playfully nudging Agatha’s shoulder.

"Hey!" She defended with a grin. "The face painting was animal-themed."

For her own sanity, Jen decided not to pursue that particular line of reasoning.

Rio then glanced pointedly at the small stuffed animal clipped to Agatha’s lanyard.

"Besides, I got you that cute bunny keychain from the zoo, which you didn’t seem to mind all that much."

Agatha, mid-glare, faltered. She glanced at the little rabbit, quickly tucked the entire lanyard into her shirt, and turned her head away, fighting off a blush.

Interesting.

The fight had gone out of her, it seemed. Jen realized the conversation had shifted from hostile to— well, whatever this was.

Jen was about to comment, but Rio—clearly sensing her victory—clapped her hands together.

"Anyways!" she beamed. "You’re gonna love the next one. We’re going to the teaching hospital in Boston—to the morgue. How wild is that?"

Silence echoed.

Jen stared.

"Sorry—you’re taking them where?"

"The morgue!" Rio chirped gleefully. "The kids will learn, up close, about human anatomy and nature’s cycle of death and decay."

Yes, it was a little charming seeing her passion for the subject shine through. No, Jen would never admit to that.

She caught the way Agatha’s expression shifted, amusement flickering behind narrowed eyes. She gave Jen a look as if to say, ‘are you hearing this right now?’, but she didn’t try to argue.

Because Rio had won this round. That trip did sound pretty fucking cool, and they both knew it.

Jen clapped her hands together. "Great. It’s settled. Rio, try not to traumatize anyone. Agatha, suck it up. And apologize to Natasha before she drops your class out of fear."

Agatha grumbled under her breath but turned away, returning to her classroom.

Jen and Rio watched her disappear inside.

"You do know it’s not actually the field trip that annoys her most, right?" Jen glanced at Rio. "It’s how much you’re enjoying pissing her off."

Rio flashed a sharp grin. Winked. "I know."

Three glorious, uninterrupted days passed before Agatha Harkness once again made her way onto the counselor’s list of concerns.

Jen wasn’t one for superstition, but she did briefly wonder if she’d been hexed in a previous life so thoroughly that its effects had bled into this one—in the form of one particularly stubborn history teacher. The woman did have a way of causing Jen headaches with an ease that bordered on the occult.

This time, it was Billy Maximoff.

It was a rainy Friday morning when he stomped into her office, report card in hand, his usual easygoing demeanor replaced with righteous indignation.

"This is so unfair!" he burst out, waving the offending document around. "She’s way too hard on us, Ms. Kale. And the amount of homework is insane."

Jen, accustomed to the occasional teacher-related rant, nodded sympathetically.

"I mean, come on." Billy huffed, slumping into the chair across from her. "Everyone says she’s the hardest grader in the whole school. We’re drowning in coursework while other classes are just watching movies. My friends in her AP class are out here writing, like, entire dissertations. For fun."

Jen had to bite back a laugh. Yeah, that sounded about right.

Still, she tilted her head, considering. "Alright," she said, "I’ll look into it. Was there anything else on your mind?"

Billy hesitated. "Uh. Yeah, actually," he said, fidgeting with the strings of his hoodie. "Could you… do something about my tardiness from yesterday?"

Jen’s brow quirked. "Billy."

"It was literally less than a minute after the bell rang," he argued, leaning forward in his chair. "And besides, Ms. Rio was there, and she totally sided with me! You can ask her."

Jen sighed. "Well, Ms. Vidal probably shouldn’t be ‘taking sides’ on matters outside her own class." Then, curiosity getting the better of her, "Why was Ms. Vidal in your classroom, anyway?"

Billy shrugged, not seeing how this pertained to his absence. "Dunno. She was just hanging out at Ms. Harkness’ desk, chatting with some of my classmates. Guess she lost track of time."

That… also sounded about right.

"So," Billy returned steadfastly to his argument, "the bell rings just as I’m running in and Ms. Harkness is already in a bad mood because Ms. Rio was sitting in her chair." He rolled his eyes. "She's, like, weirdly territorial about that chair. How is that my fault."

Jen was losing track of where this conversation was going, but Billy had more to say, interrupting her thoughts.

"And I tried to explain myself to Ms. Harkness—politely, you know." He stressed the word, then continued, "And she just looked at me and said, ‘Do you really want to keep poking the bear?'"

He threw his hands up. "Like, what does that even mean?"

Jen coughed into her fist to disguise her laugh at Agatha’s incredibly lame intimidation tactic. Of course she’d said that. In the most ominous tone possible, no doubt.

But Billy had made his case.

"Alright," she conceded. "Just this once, I’ll erase the absence. But next time? Be on time."

Billy beamed, practically jumping out of his seat. "Awesome! Thanks, Ms. Jen."

As her office fell silent again, Jen leaned back in her chair. She had lost count of how many students had brought similar complaints about Agatha’s class.

With a sigh, she made a note to take it up with Lilia in time for the staff meeting later.

The rest of the day blurred by in a mess of phone calls, admin reports, and last-minute emergencies.

By the time Jen’s alarm buzzed, signaling the start of the staff meeting, she was running on sheer willpower alone.

She finished up an email and hurried to the teacher’s lounge, eyes already locked on the Keurig— the lifeline that would get her through this.

The others were filing into the conference room when Jen opened the cupboard and found one single, cursed mug remaining.

"You have got to be kidding me."

She stood, staring blankly at the lone ‘World’s Greatest Grandfather’ mug.

With closed eyes, she allowed herself one deep, pitiful sigh before grabbing the stupid thing and jamming it under the spout.

As the coffee brewed, a small snicker sounded behind her.

Jen knew exactly who it was, as they were the very reason she had not known peace since first stepping foot into this school.

She turned, face to face with a smirking Agatha.

"Wow," she drawled, leaning against the opposite wall, "congratulations, old chap."

Jen inhaled deeply. "It’s funny, isn’t it," she remarked, her smile all faux-sweetness and bared teeth. "There’s usually twenty other mugs in here just… collecting dust. Almost like someone childishly hid them."

Jen narrowed her eyes. "You wouldn’t happen to know anything about that, would you, Agatha?"

Agatha gasped, scandalized. Like this was a complete and unfounded affront to her dignity. "Moi?" she crooned, eyes wide. Her face the very picture of deceit.

Just then, Rio skidded into the room, clearly having sprinted to be on time. She paused, catching her breath. Then, with one look at the silent standoff taking place, she grinned.

"Quit antagonizing Jen," she said, chiding Agatha like an underpaid babysitter, guiding her toward the conference room with a hand between her shoulder blades.

"Come on, Jen," Rio added. "We’re already late."

Jen grabbed her coffee and sighed, defeated, following them inside.

She slumped into an empty chair next to Principal Lilia Calderu—an enigma in her own right.

Jen admired her ability to run a tight ship while still knowing when to let go and let loose. The expectations of her leadership role were completely subverted by the bohemian outfits she floated around the halls in. Some called her kooky, but Lilia clearly embraced it—and was all the more respected for it.

The principal cleared her throat, and the room fell quiet.

"Alright, kids. I know it’s Friday afternoon, and we’d all rather get home on time, so let’s try to keep it short."

She went through her notes, quickly rattling off information about upcoming events, school board updates, and parent-teacher conferences.

Jen sipped her coffee idly, tuning back in as Lilia neared the end of her list.

"One more thing," she said, glancing at Jen. "It has come to my attention that there has been some confusion regarding our field trips."

Across the table, Jen caught Agatha smirking as she leaned in to whisper something about ‘frivolous class outings’ in Rio’s ear.

Lilia glanced at the two before clearing her throat again. "Having said that—" she held Agatha’s gaze pointedly—"I have gone through the records and see no issue. All guidelines are being followed, and I see no reason for further… disagreements."

Rio did a poor job of tamping down the snort that erupted from her.

She hurried to roll her chair out of Agatha’s reach, who looked seconds away from smacking her upside the head.

Rio took cover behind Alice Wu-Gulliver, the music teacher, who was laughing into her coffee.

Lilia sighed good-naturedly. "Please, children, behave yourselves."

Then, turning to Agatha—"You know, Ms. Harkness… it might do you good to consider taking a page out of Ms. Vidal’s book."

That stopped Agatha in her tracks.

"Lilia," she gasped in betrayal. "Tell me you’re kidding."

Jen was well aware that Agatha and Lilia had worked together for years—their mutual respect woven beneath all the sarcasm.

Which was exactly why Lilia could get away with pushing back.

"You know," Lilia said patiently, "Jen has spent a disproportionate amount of her time here fielding complaints about all the surprise tests, rigorous workload, and mediocre grades your students are treated to."

Agatha scoffed, but Lilia wasn’t finished.

"We know you’re passionate about history, and we appreciate it," she explained, tone kind but firm. "But keep in mind, this is high school history, not a doctorate program. You can go easy on them every once in a while. Let them have some fun."

Rio seized her moment.

"You remember fun, right, Ms. Harkness?" she grinned, teasing with more audacity than someone currently using their colleague as a human shield should really possess.

Agatha rolled her eyes so hard it looked painful. "It’s not my fault these kids can’t focus for more than a minute without a godforsaken Subway Surfers gameplay projected onto the wall behind me."

Jen choked on her coffee.

"A toddler could explain the Industrial Revolution more succinctly than some of these kids!"

And that started another wave of playful bickering between Agatha, Rio, and now Alice, who couldn’t resist joining in.

Jen glanced at Lilia, who looked like she was still processing the whole ‘Subway Surfers’ thing.

Letting the chaos across the table fade into background noise, Jen leaned closer to the principal and murmured—"I hear you’re quite the tea leaf hobbyist."

Lilia, delightfully strange woman that she was, didn’t question why she was being asked this out of nowhere.

"Tea leaf hobbyist and interpreter of the divine, yes."

Jen tipped her empty mug, allowing Lilia to divine the meaning of the coffee dregs.

"Might this be telling me to pull the fire alarm and put an end to my misery?"

Lilia, ever the enabler, hummed in thought.

"That’s not how tasseography works, and, as principal, I certainly don’t condone vandalism."

A beat.

"But who knows," she winked, "I could turn a blind eye just this once."

Jen huffed a laugh.

Across the table, Agatha was still gesturing wildly as she went on about ‘kids these days’, while Rio and Alice giggled like overexcited schoolgirls.

At last, Agatha turned to Lilia, head bowed. A tongue-in-cheek act of benevolence.

"Fine," she sighed dramatically. "I’ll… simplify things. To accommodate the delicate sensibilities of my students."

Lilia rubbed her eyes. "Not exactly what I was going for, but I’ll take it. Now, go enjoy your weekends."

As Jen made for the door, she saw Rio sidle up to Agatha and murmur, "Sooo… about my 'frivolous' number of field trips?"

"Shove it, Vidal." Agatha groaned, turning sharply down the hall.

Rio barked out a laugh, skipping after her gleefully.

Jen spent the weekend finishing up projects around her apartment and calling a few friends from New Jersey.

To her delight, she woke up early Monday morning feeling well-rested and accomplished.

She was in such a good mood, in fact, that she soon found herself at the staff room table, sipping coffee and having a downright pleasant conversation with Agatha Harkness.

And they say miracles never happen.

Jen had been surprised to find Agatha in the break room so early, long before most students would arrive.

She was sipping from a to-go cup, a crumpled wax paper bag and an untouched almond croissant on the table.

"Morning, Jen," Agatha croaked with a small smile and raspy voice.

She was wearing casual brown slacks and a loose-fitting white button-up, a few undone buttons revealing a simple gold chain that grazed her collarbones. Her long hair was swept back, glasses perched on her head.

It was rare to see her like this—presence still unguarded and placid with sleep.

Jen leaned against the counter as her coffee brewed—in a perfectly average mug, Jen noted, for the record.

"What are you doing here so early?"

"Well," Agatha hummed, stretching, "for some reason, I was woken up far earlier than usual today." "Couldn't fall back asleep, so I figured I might as well drive in early and get some lesson planning done…" she trailed off before admitting, "but I may have taken a detour past my coffee shop. And now I’ve been sitting here, head empty, devouring a bagel."

"Sounds like selfcare to me," Jen chuckled, pulling out a chair across from her.

Glancing at the table, she grinned. "Couldn’t resist the croissant either, huh? I know the feeling."

"Oh, that’s for Rio," Agatha said easily.

"Since I left early, we couldn’t carpool like we usually do. The croissant is just a ploy to keep her whining at a minimum."

She rolled her eyes in a show of exasperation, but the soft smile tugging at her lips made it come across as fondness.

"Are you meeting her here?"

Agatha nodded. "She’s doing kindergarten drop-off first," she said off-handedly. Then, with a suspicious look at the clock above the door, her brows furrowed.

"Come to think of it, she should be here by now."

Jen hesitated.

Drop-off?

She thought back to the email they’d received a few weeks ago about staff scheduling for the semester—she didn’t remember seeing Rio’s name on the rotation.

Maybe she’d volunteered to cover for someone?

And also, how close did they live to each other that daily carpooling even made sense?

Her train of thought was broken as Agatha sprang from her chair, looking suddenly alert.

"Jen," she whispered ominously, "she’s up to something. I can feel it."

The dramatic shift in tone was so abrupt that Jen wanted to laugh.

But Agatha was already half way out the door, heels clicking determinately.

And before Jen could stop herself, she was following.

A downright pathological curiosity, her mother would call it.

But so what if it killed the cat? Jen figured she must have a few of her nine lives left to spend.

Chapter 2

Jen caught up to Agatha, matching her quick stride.

As they turned the corner, the distinct rip of duct tape echoed from down the hall.

Agatha groaned, picking up the pace, desperate to see what horrors were unfolding within her classroom.

Jen and Agatha burst through the door with a dramatic flourish—the whole thing like a scene out of a buddy-cop comedy. Two idiots in an overblown crime busting operation.

Except the idiots were educated adults and the crime was… refurbishing?

Inside, Rio turned to them, grinning wildly.

She had tape stuck to her elbow.

"Hey guys!" she beamed, like this was all perfectly normal. "Thought I heard you talking."

Then, gesturing to the walls—"I figured I’d help Agatha pep up her classroom after Friday’s meeting. You know… encourage her to bring out the fun in learning."

One had already been plastered to the wall, and Rio was halfway through the next. She finished taping the last corners down and stepped back to reveal her work, completely unfazed.

A brightly colored poster with food-themed puns.

'You’re One in a Melon!’

'Donut Give Up!’

Jen blinked slowly.

"Oh! Jen, look—" Rio pointed at a cartoon salad. "Thought you might like this one."

'Kale ‘Em with Kindness!’

Jen sighed. Somewhere in the elementary wing, a wall was probably conspicuously bare. She was about to call Rio out for obvious theft before remembering that she definitely did not get paid enough to care.

Instead, she just groaned. "Please don’t drag me into this."

Agatha, who had until now been frozen in stunned silence, finally spoke.

"Oh my God," she said monotonously. "Jen, be a dear and find me a pair of scissors I can gouge my eyes out with."

Agatha blinked. "Or a spoon, even. I can make it work with a spoon."

Rio giggled, pure trouble.

"Come on," she said, dragging out the words. "The kids will love it. And maybe you can learn from it too!"

She grabbed another poster from the desk.

"Here." She unfurled it as she walked closer.

"Instead of permanent blindness and an eyepatch, you can express yourself in a more productive manner—" she held it up.

"With the help of this Feelings Chart."

With a glint of mischief, Rio goaded, "Come on Ms. Harkness… point to a feeling. Let it all out."

Agatha pressed her lips together in agitation—or was it amusement, Jen could never tell with these two.

She peered closely at the chart, as if really contemplating it.

Then, straightening, she remarked, "You know, I can’t seem to find the illustration for existential dread or homicidal rage anywhere on there. I think your poster is missing a few."

Jen clapped her hands loudly. "Okay!" she announced, backing away. "I think I’ll take mentions of murder as my cue to leave."

As she left, a rustling sounded behind her. She looked back just in time to see Agatha snatch the Feelings Chart from Rio’s hands and start rolling it back up.

Anticipating what was coming next, Jen power-walked out the door and down the hallway as voices raised behind her.

"What are you—oof."

A hollow sound of rolled up paper finding its mark echoed down the hall.

"You did not just bonk me on the head!" Rio squawked in disbelief.

A screech of laughter rang out in response.

"You should’ve seen your face," Jen heard Agatha say.

A moment of silence. Then—

"Hey, no. Don’t walk towards me like that, you creep. Let’s not—"

A chair scraped violently. A pair of heeled boots thundered down the hall.

Then, the squeak of sneakers chasing in pursuit.

Jen sighed, reminiscing about the much simpler time of ten minutes ago, when Agatha had contained an energy level more befitting of a Monday morning.

With her back still turned to whatever was going on behind her, she called out a weak, "No running in the halls, guys."

Just so, should anyone ask, she could say she’d tried.

Further down the hall, Alice had arrived and was leaning against the doorframe of the music room. She took a slow bite out of an apple as she watched the chaos unfold—like this was nothing out of the ordinary.

She turned her knowing gaze on Jen and smirked.

"So," she drawled, amusement dripping from every syllable, "how’s it going?"

Jen closed her eyes and breathed deep, willing herself into a state of serene acceptance.

On the exhale, she let out a weary, "It’s been a day."

Alice grinned. "It’s 7:48 A.M., Jen. Not sure we can call it a day yet. Might be jumping the gun a little on that one."

Jen gave a tired laugh, glancing down the hall, which was now empty. The teachers must finally have retreated to their respective classrooms.

She turned back to Alice with a raised brow.

"I’ll probably regret asking this," she started, "but what exactly is the deal with those two? I can’t tell whether they’re flirting or fighting half the time."

She rambled on, "Like, do they hate each other? Or do they just have really dysregulated ways of showing affection? Or maybe they’re just… weirdly overfamiliar colleagues?"

Alice snickered.

"All of the above," she said cryptically, taking another bite of her apple before pushing off the doorframe.

How incredibly helpful.

Jen decided not to dig any further, for now, as she made her way back to her office.

With a huff, she settled into her chair with a fresh coffee, her notebook, and the day’s to-do list.

…There might have been a long-forgotten almond croissant somewhere in the mix too.

So sue her.

The next few days passed in a flurry of meetings, emails, and behavioral assessments—not to mention the frequent drop-ins from overworked teachers and stressed-out kids.

It wasn’t until Thursday evening, as she slumped against the staff room couch, that she was able to catch a break.

She should’ve left hours ago. But in a rare moment of discipline, she’d resolved to clear out her overflowing inbox and send off the last permission slips before the weekend. Barring any major crises, she could take it easy the next day and enjoy a well-earned two-day hibernation thereafter.

And Jen got it. Agatha had quietly mentioned that she’d never had the prom experience. It hadn’t been some dramatic confession, just an offhand comment. But it was one Rio had clearly taken to heart. And now here she was—wearing jeans and a paint-splattered t-shirt—being crowned ‘prom queen’ by the mother of her child.

It was ridiculous.

Jen hollered louder, egging it on.

"Come on now, don't be shy," Rio teased from the stage.

Agatha shook her head but climbed the steps anyway, laughing as she went. "You’re insane," Jen heard her mutter.

"I do my best." Then Rio turned to the ‘crowd’ (their tired, unpaid volunteers) and beamed. "Fun fact, folks: Ms. Harkness here has never been to prom. Was never made prom queen. Isn’t that just absurd? I mean, look at her."

Agatha flushed. Then, before she could think better of it, she leaned in and pecked Rio’s cheek. The room let out collective oohs and awws, not used to seeing Agatha Hardass Harkness softened like this.

Jen beamed, wondering if her earlier chat with Agatha about ‘letting yourself be seen’ had gotten to her—if this was Agatha, leaning into vulnerability.

The kiss left a lipstick stain behind, and just like that, Rio—who had been basking in her own theatrics—fully lost her composure. She stammered, face pink, grinning like an idiot.

Agatha just smirked. "Cat got your tongue?"

"Shut up." Rio cleared her throat. Regained control.

"Right! The crowning ceremony." She turned to Agatha with a stupidly loving smile. "I will now place the sacred crown upon your head, milady."

Jen snorted. The ‘prom presenter’ voice was way off. At this point, Rio was veering straight into medieval decree territory.

On the stage, Rio mimed placing an invisible crown onto Agatha’s head.

A few people clapped. Jen yelled, "Speech! Speech!"

"Excellent idea, dear audience member," Rio agreed, then she held out the mic. "Tell us, beautiful Prom Queen, what are you thinking in this very moment?"

Agatha smiled. Eerily calm all of a sudden. She looked… certain.

Like there was really only one possible outcome to all of this.

"I think we should get remarried."
