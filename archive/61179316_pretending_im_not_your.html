Summary

Agatha's ghostly mother shows back up with her usual disapproving and demeaning attitude so Agatha devises a plan to spite her mother over the holidays.

There's just one problem with her plan though. She has to pretend to be happily married to her real estranged wife.

Chapter 1: The Proposal

Agatha had always hated the holidays. Okay, maybe that was a lie. She used to love them, back when she had a family to celebrate with. Now they just remind her of everything she had lost over the years, but deep down she still longs to be able to enjoy them with community. She’d be damned if she was about to admit that to Billy Maximoff though.

“Come on Agatha! Where’s your holiday spirit?” Billy sweeps around his mentor's house adding decorations. A minora here, a Christmas tree there, lights everywhere.

“Teen seriously, it is the day after Thanksgiving-”

“Which you didn’t celebrate.”

“I saw Thanksgivings in the 1600s," Agatha deadpans. "Not much to celebrate.”

Billy sighs, not in the mood for a history lecture. “But aren't there at least a few things you have to be grateful for?”

The purple witch pauses. Normally she’d say no without hesitation, but for the first time in a long time, she thinks she actually does. She wasn’t about to say that though. Plus, the one thing she really wanted at this time of year was still missing. “No.”

Teen rolls his eyes at the lie but grins when the doorbell rings. He quickly opens the door, ignoring the look of confusion from his mentor. The rest of the coven strolls in casually.

“What are you all doing here?” The coven leader says in a huff.

“Oh Billy said you needed help decorating!” Alice says excitedly, dragging in a long garland.

“I don’t.”

“Come on Agatha, even someone as heartless as you has to celebrate the holidays.” Jen chides as she carries in a box of cheesy Christmas products from her store. Lilia then waltzes in with some Sicilian decorations and starts to place them around the house.

Agatha groans, “You guys know you don’t live here right?”

“You haven’t stopped us.” Jen shrugs. “Besides, you could use some green and red in this house.”

“Or green and purple.” Billy comments with a knowing look.

The spirit witch glares at her mentee and the rest of the coven snickers at the comment. Having a coven was already awful, but having a coven that meddled with her love life was by far the worst. She really should kick them out.

“Yes, where is Rio? I feel like she’d like the festivities.” Lilia asks innocently.

Agatha grips the table in annoyance. It was true, Rio always loved holidays. In fact, it was one of the reasons Agatha used to love holidays a long time ago. The joy and wonder on Death’s face was truly beautiful to watch, but those days were long gone. “I don’t know where she is and I don’t care.”

“Yeah, one of those is true.” Billy mutters.

The mentor clenches her fist, “I swear Maximoff-”

Suddenly the lights start to flicker and a chill sweeps into the room. The house begins to shake and all the candles go out.

“Agatha! What are you doing!”

“It isn’t me!” The purple witch shouts, looking around the room in shock.

“Is it Rio?” Alice shouts over the noise. Death did have a thing for dramatic and terrifying entrances.

Agatha shakes her head. Somehow in her stomach she knew it wasn’t the infuriating green witch. She swears she’s not disappointed by that fact.

Suddenly white swirls appear around them that look very supernatural. The coven cowers together behind the couch.

“What is this?? A Christmas Carol?” Lilia screams.

It’s then that they hear it. A heartless and terrifying laugh that shoots right down Agatha’s spine. She’d know that evil laugh anywhere.

A second later, the ghost of Evanora Harkness stands in her living room.

“Mother.” Agatha spits venomously.

The rest of the coven look on in shock and fear. None of them were a big fan of the last time they had run into their leader’s mother.

Evanora grins wickedly. “Daughter.”

The purple witch sneers and stands up. She sweeps her hand around the house, turning the lights back on. “What are you doing here?”

“Oh well, since this boy here was kind enough to give me an entrance back into the mortal realm, I figured I’d come visit my daughter for the holidays.” The words sound innocent enough, but everyone sees the look of contempt on Evanora’s face.

Billy winces with guilt as he realizes what he’s done. He goes to say something but his mentor cuts him off.

“Get out.”

The ghost smiles, “Oh come on Agatha. It’s been what? 330 years? Isn’t it time for some mother-daughter bonding time?”

“Last time you saw me you told me I was born evil. So no.” The purple witch tries to say it in her usual confidence, but the coven can hear her voice trembling with pain and emotion. Despite their history with the coven leader, they all feel bad for her at the moment.

“Well, maybe you can prove me wrong then?”

Agatha hates that she hesitates. Rio and Nicky may be her deepest wound, but her mother was her oldest. No matter how much she fought it, she had always longed for her mother’s approval and love. It was why she was so excited to have Nicky, so she could be the mother she never had.

Evanora smiles as she dangles the possibility of her approval in front of her daughter, “I see you finally ditched that woman. Off to a good start already.”

Agatha growls at the mention of Rio. Sure she could deny their relationship and insult the green witch all she wanted, but not her mother. Never her mother. “I didn’t ditch her.”

“Hmm then where is she?”

“Busy.”

“I see. Maybe your time apart will be good. You can see you don’t need that woman and finally find a man who-”

“Rio and I are married!” Agatha interrupts furiously.

The rest of the coven raises an eyebrow at that, but none of them say anything, too eager for their leader to continue to put her mother in her place. Calling Agatha evil for being a serial killer was one thing, but the clear homophobia from Evanora was not cool with anyone in the room.

Evanora pauses in shock and disdain. “Well, you’re clearly unhappy being married to the woman if she’s not here-”

“She’ll be here soon!” The purple witch hisses.

“Do you have any idea who she is, Agatha!” The ghost sneers.

“Of course I do!” The spirit witch retorts back, wishing she could smack her mother right about now. “You’re the one that has no idea who she is!”

“I know she is inherently evil! And so are you!” Evanora points at her daughter. “And you two will always be cursed!”

The cruel words echo in the silence. Agatha just stands there slack jawed, eyes filling with tears she refuses to shed. For once, she’s thankful for Billy’s interruption.

“Uh, Agatha, do you want me to find that banishing spell for ghosts?”

The spirit witch considers it strongly, but her desire to spite her mother wins out. “No Billy.” She turns back to the ghost, “Mother, I ask, no, I insist, you stay for the holidays.”

Evanora tilts her head with a chilling smile. She knows what game her daughter is playing, she invented that game, and she’s fully looking forward to watching her daughter’s relationship with Death crash and burn like it was destined to. “It would be my pleasure.”

Agatha grits her teeth. “Good. Starting with dinner next weekend. Do let the door hit you on the way out.”

The ghost’s eyes narrow but she nods with one last scathing look at the coven and disappears.

When her mother is finally gone Agatha lets out a breath she didn’t know she was holding. The rest of the coven gather around her, all feeling like they should comfort her but not knowing how.

“Well, your mother is…” Lilia starts, searching for the right words.

“A bitch?” Agatha offers.

“Precisely.”

“I just need to prove her wrong about Rio and I.” The coven leader mumbles absently, still distracted by the whole ordeal.

“And we second that.” Alice nods.

“Right…but are you sure Rio will agree to pretend to be your wife?” Billy asks hesitantly.

“Rio and I are technically actually married.” Agatha shrugs, still not really understanding the implications yet. The rest of the coven raises their eyebrows but can’t say they’re all that surprised at the revelation.

Jen nods but points at the purple witch. “What Billy means though, is are you sure Rio will agree to pretend to be your…not estranged wife?”

Agatha finally picks up on what they’re getting at now. Shit. They were right. She hadn’t seen Rio in almost two months, and the last time she did she said she never wanted to see the green witch’s face again. She’d been regretting that request ever since, but now she’s kicking herself even more for it. Rio probably didn’t want anything to do with her, and even if she did agree to help by some miracle, they’d still have to sell their marriage to her annoyingly perceptive mother. Considering they had been at each other’s throats for centuries, she’s not sure how likely that would be. Still, she couldn’t let her mother win, which means…she had to try.

“I’ll…ask her.” Agatha winces as the words almost taste sour on her tongue.

“Oh, I have to see that.” Jen smirks.

“Absolutely not. Now everyone get out.” Agatha starts pushing her coven out the door. “You can come for dinner next Friday.”

“Uh, how are you going to even find Rio? You’re not going to kill someone, are you?” Billy asks nervously.

The mentor rolls her eyes, “I am literally more than 20 times your age. I know a lot more things than you ever will. Now go.”

She closes the door quickly before Billy can ask anymore questions. Then she sighs and sinks to the floor. She honestly wasn’t sure how to find Rio without killing someone, but supposes she has a few ideas.

She curses her family problems. Only Evanora Harkness could make Agatha Harkness ask her wife to pretend to be her wife. Her stomach is already in knots at the thought of seeing Rio again. She vaguely thinks she could’ve just said she was with some woman, any woman, other than Rio. It probably would’ve been easier to get them to agree to play the part. But her subconscious had never even thought about being married to someone other than Rio, so it’s the first thing that came to mind. She groans and tries to push the implications of that truth away. It’s not like she was still in love with the green witch, it’s just she had never had the time to think of marrying someone else. Yes, that’s it.

She stands up with a sigh and mutters a quick spell to ward off the ghost of her mother unless she was invited. The last thing Agatha needed was that ghost looking over her shoulder the rest of her life. Then she remembers who taught her that spell and groans again. Time to go find Death.

----------------------------- Rio runs her hand through her hair wearily as she returns to the mortal world after a particularly annoying soul ferrying trip of a group of idiots that died in a bar fight. Apparently some men still thought it was cool to call her “sweetheart” and “honey”. She really thought after millenia of this that something would be different now, but unfortunately not. She was quick to reveal her true form to them, and was instantly gratified by their screams of terror, but the interaction was annoying all the same. There was only one person in the universe that could get away with calling her pet names, but that person had asked to never see her again, so that didn’t make the day any better.

“Rough day?”

Speak of the devil.

The green witch freezes and slowly turns to where Agatha Harkness is leaning against the bar, hands in the pockets of one of her ridiculously fashionable coats. Rio hates how she instantly turns to mush at the pleased smirk on the woman’s face. She knows the purple witch is proud of sneaking up on her, usually it was the other way around. She also can still see the lingering contempt on Agatha’s face that had been ever present the last three centuries, but there’s something else there she can’t quite place this time.

Rio recovers quickly and leans against the bar with a casual smirk, though inside her heart is racing. She thought she’d never see this woman again, at least not for a very long time until Agatha finally let her out of the doghouse, but here she was only a few months later. The green witch is suspicious though, she’s sure she’s not out of the doghouse yet, which means the purple witch was here for some other reason.

“What is Agatha Harkness doing in a place like this?” Rio leans in flirtatiously, playing the game they’ve played hundreds of times. The purple witch rolls her eyes, but Rio notices how she absently smooths out her hair and jacket. The green witch recognizes the tell-tale sign of nervousness and straightens up a bit. “Is something wrong?”

Agatha can’t help but stare for a second, marveling at how even after all these centuries of fighting, the green witch could still sound so worried about her. It made her feel things she definitely did not want to feel at the moment. She opts for her go-to move when it comes to avoiding her feelings, deflection.

“Why would you think that?” Agatha’s eyes flash as she turns her body more to the green witch. She’s hoping she can count on her seduction techniques to distract from the fact that she was here to ask for help.

Rio looks her up and down, and Agatha hates how it makes her feel completely vulnerable and seen. It was more than just a look of appreciation for her physical appearance, though that was clearly part of it, but it was also a look of knowing. Of seeing through her bullshit in a way nobody else in the universe could.

The green witch shakes her head with a laugh at the deflection, then reaches behind the bar and grabs a bottle of whiskey. She conjures up glasses and pours them both a heavy pour. The purple witch grabs and drinks hers quite quickly.

“You’re not fooling anyone Agatha.” Rio sips her whiskey and studies the woman that she was still so in love with. “Last time you saw me you said you never wanted to see my face again. And while that may have been an exaggeration, we both know you’d rather throw yourself off a cliff than come seek me out, especially so soon. So that can only mean one thing. You need my help.”

Agatha’s eyes narrow in annoyance at the completely accurate statement from the green witch’s lips. She’d be damned if she admitted that though. Instead she just pours herself more whiskey and shrugs. She may as well cut to the chase. The sooner she could get this conversation over with, the sooner she could go back to pretending being here at a bar with her ex wife didn’t make her feel anything.

“My mother stopped by my house.”

Rio tenses at that, eyes flashing with anger and protectiveness. “Please tell me you banished her.”

Agatha winces, “Well…”

The green witch groans and pours herself more whiskey. “I’m going to give that kid a piece of my mind for allowing her back into the mortal world. That evil hag has been gone for centuries and some dumb teenager just let her back in through his hex.”

The purple witch fiddles with her glass and Rio relents a bit, realizing her anger wasn’t helping Agatha say whatever she needed to. “Okay, so your mother stopped by and what?”

The spirit witch bites her lip, “She wanted to…reconnect over the holidays.”

“Ha! You’re joking.”

“I wish.”

“And you agreed to that?” Rio asks in shock. “You can’t be serious. Why?”

Agatha shifts, “I don’t know. She said maybe I could prove to her I’m not evil.”

Rio deflates at that. She knew this was at the center of everything for Agatha. That no matter how far she ran or what she did, she still longed for her original coven’s approval, for her mother’s approval. No matter how many times Rio said she was better off without it and that she was more valuable than Evanora Harkness would ever understand. The green witch has the sudden urge to pull the other woman into her arms, but she doesn’t think that would be welcome so instead she opts for leaning slightly closer.

“Agatha-”

“You don’t have to give me the spiel Rio.” The purple witch gulps more whiskey. She knows the green witch is about to tell her she wasn’t evil and she deserved better than her mother but her heart was not ready to hear those words at the moment. Not from the woman she had been running from for three centuries, and definitely not from the woman she had just called evil herself months earlier. Her stomach twists at the memory.

Rio sighs, but doesn’t continue further. She hopes one day Agatha would see herself as she saw her. As someone who deserved the world and didn’t need Evanora’s approval, but it’s looking like she may have to wait another 300 years for that.

“Besides,” Agatha continues. “Proving to her I’m not evil is not why I’m here. It’s quite the opposite in fact.”

The green witch raises an eyebrow, “Are you returning to your serial killing ways, sweetheart? Because as much as I’d love that, I’m not sure it will make the point you’re trying to make to your mother.”

The purple witch shakes her head and ignores the pet name. “No, this has more to do with the other reason she thinks I’m evil.”

“Ah.” Rio says disdainfully, chugging more whiskey. She was well aware of Evanora’s homophobia too. “So you’re here because…”

“I need you to be my wife.” Agatha winces as the words leave her lips.

The green witch can’t help but raise an eyebrow and smirk at that. “My knowledge of human culture may be limited, but I don’t think it’s usual for someone to propose to someone they’re already married to.”

“Rio…” Agatha growls in annoyance, trying to fight the blush on her cheeks and look anywhere but at the smirk on her (unfortunately not ex) wife’s lips.

“Yes?” The green witch asks innocently.

The spirit witch grits her teeth. Of course the infuriating woman was going to make her spell it out.

“I am asking you...to pretend, to be my not estranged wife for the holidays.” Agatha quickly goes on, “To prove to my mother that I can be in a relationship with a woman and be happy.”

Rio tilts her head, her mind swimming at the sentence that just left the purple witch’s mouth. There was so much she wanted to say. Like how they wouldn’t even have to pretend if Agatha would just let her back in. She doesn’t say this though. As desperate as she was to have her wife truly back, she didn’t think now was the time to have that conversation. Not with Evanora looming.

Still, Rio can’t help but prod a little. “And you’re asking me because…?”

Agatha rolls her eyes at the clear attempt to get her to admit that she was asking Rio because she wanted to. While it was completely true that the green witch had been the only candidate to ever cross her mind to play this part, she was never going to say that. “Because she already knows who you are and it would piss her off the most.”

“Uh huh.” Rio says with a knowing smirk.

The purple witch scoffs, “If you’re gonna be like this I can easily find someone else to fill the role.” She turns to leave but she feels a hand on her arm jerk her back possessively.

“Don’t even think about it.” The green witch says in a low voice that rushes straight to Agatha’s core. The two stand there nose to nose in the bar, Death with an iron grip on the purple witch’s hand, brown eyes meeting blue.

Agatha, despite all logic telling her otherwise, leans into the ever present chemistry between them. She can smell the whiskey on Rio’s breath. It's as intoxicating as it is dangerous. She smirks playfully and traces her finger over the green witch’s jaw teasingly, trying to claim the upperhand. “You’re too easy, my dear.”

The green witch’s jaw clenches under Agatha’s finger, but she smirks and leans in even closer. Her lips ghost over the purple witch’s ear and she whispers in a low tone, “As if anyone else could play the part of your wife as good as me.”

Rio pulls back with a pleased smirk on her face and Agatha stares back, licking her lips. She realizes then just how bad of an idea this all was because it’s only been two minutes in and she already wants to break all her rules and kiss the stupid smirk off Rio’s face. She recovers at the last second though. She was Agatha Harkness after all, and she’d be damned if she lost to Death.

Agatha wretches her hand out of Rio’s gasp and pulls back, straightening her coat and trying to look unaffected. She’s not sure she succeeds, but the green witch looks distracted enough by the exchange that she hopes she doesn’t notice Agatha is equally distracted. “So it’s settled then. Be at my place early next Friday and we can go over the…logistics.”

Rio raises an eyebrow at the formalness of the woman’s tone, but nods all the same. Even if this was fake, spending the holidays with Agatha as her wife was everything Rio wanted. She figured she’d go along with anything at this point.

Agatha hesitates for a split second, suddenly not even sure if she wants to leave. It had felt so freeing sitting there in that dingy bar with Death , someone who understood all the intricacies of her relationship with her mother. She quickly shakes the thought out of her head. She couldn’t stay. That was stupid and would lead nowhere good. So she just nods once, then disappears in a cloud of purple.

Chapter 2: Ground Rules

“I see the coven has decorated.”

Agatha looks up from where she’s cooking at the stove and gives the green witch who just appeared an annoyed and unimpressed look. She tries to disregard the fact that Rio was dressed for the holidays in black jeans and a green flannel that she swears doesn’t look good at all.

Rio is far less shy about her staring as she takes in Agatha’s red blouse and dark slacks. Her already elevated heart rate tells her this fake(?) marriage was going to absolutely destroy her, but Death had always been a sucker for pain.

“Yes, Billy insisted against my will. Unfortunately it also attracted the attention of my mother.” Agatha shoves a tray of food into Rio’s hands as she continues to move casually throughout the kitchen.

The green witch frowns and bites back a comment about the son of another woman. She opts for putting the food down on the table instead and goes to carry over other dishes from the kitchen. Agatha pretends not to notice the ease in which they move around each other to prepare for the guests. Almost as if they were a real couple.

When the table is set, the purple witch leans against the counter. “Okay, so here’s the plan.”

Rio raises an eyebrow but leans against the opposite counter and beckons Agatha to continue.

“We just have to act like a happily married couple in front of my mother and hope the rest of the coven doesn’t give us away.”

“Right, wouldn’t want her knowing that we are in fact, not happily married,” The green witch mumbles.

The spirit witch glares, “That comment brings me to the second part. Ground rules.”

Rio rolls her eyes, “Since when do you care about rules?” The purple witch just glares some more so Rio sighs. “Fine. What.”

“No kissing.”

The green witch laughs, “Why? Afraid I have cooties?”

“Well, you are literally Death. So you probably do.”

“Hasn’t stopped you before,” Rio smirks. Agatha flushes at that but continues to hold firm. Rio crosses her arms. “How are we supposed to convince her we’re happily married if we don’t kiss? I’m pretty sure that’s something married couples do. In case you’ve forgotten.” The purple witch bites her lip. Oh how she wishes she had forgotten, but alas, she vividly remembers every kiss in her marriage the last three centuries.

“Fine. But only if the situation warrants it. No unnecessary kissing. Same goes for any display of affection.” Agatha knows Rio loves physical touch, and while she may have loved that in the past, she wasn’t mentally or emotionally prepared for what it would do to her right now.

Rio frowns at the rules obviously placed specifically for her but then shrugs, “Whatever. Good luck keeping your hands off me then.”

Agatha rolls her eyes and tries to ignore how the suggestive comment makes her feel. She’s really regretting all of her life decisions that led her to this point. “Also, no pet names.”

“Sure sweetheart.”

“Rio.”

“Fine. Agatha.”

The purple witch hates how her name still sounds oh so good on Death’s lips after all these centuries. She grits her teeth in aggravation and curses her mother for the millionth time for putting her in this mess.

“Is that all?” Rio asks with a slightly amused smile.

“What do you mean?”

The green witch smirks and tilts her head. “Well, you never said no sex-”

“That’s implied!” Agatha growls, fighting back a blush on her cheeks. Rio just continues to smirk so she rushes on. “And if we have to share a room, you’re sleeping on the ground!”

Death pouts, “That hurts my millenia-old joints.”

The purple witch shrugs, “Aren’t you a cosmic being or something? Surely you can handle some back pain.”

“That doesn’t mean I like it!”

“You’ve slept on the ground in the woods plenty of times, I think you’ll be fine.”

Rio smirks, “You mean we’ve slept on the ground in the woods plenty of times.”

The purple witch curses herself for walking into that one. “Well who knows where you slept before you met me.”

“I didn’t sleep.”

“So this whole debate is pointless.”

“You started it.”

“You’re a child.”

Rio goes to retort but then Señor Scratchy comes bounding in and jumps onto her legs. The green witch laughs and scoops up the bunny. “Hey there mister!”

It takes all of Agatha’s willpower not to think Death was cute in that moment. Instead she gives the woman what she hopes is an annoyed look and crosses her arms.

The green witch sighs, “Come on, if she’s gonna believe we’re happily married, I have to be friends with the bunny.”

Agatha relents with an eye roll, holding back her comment about how of course Rio was friends with the bunny considering she gave it to her. She can’t help but watch the green witch lovingly pet the animal. Death was so full of life. She once thought it was so beautiful and now…she still does. Shit.

Then suddenly she remembers the last and most important ground rule. “Wedding rings.”

The green witch looks up from the bunny and gives Agatha a curious, yet knowing look. “What about them?”

“You have to wear one.”

Rio raises an eyebrow, eyes glinting, “You act as if I just have one lying around.”

The comment makes Agatha genuinely freeze for a second. Did Rio not have her ring anymore? The thought makes her stomach clench miserably. She knows neither of them had worn their rings for centuries, mainly because of Agatha’s insisting that they were over, but she never thought Rio would get rid of it.

The green witch tilts her head at the look of barely concealed panic on Agatha’s face, not fully sure what it meant. Was she that worried Rio wouldn’t have a ring to sell this lie? Maybe. Was she worried Rio had gotten rid of it? The green witch doubts that since Agatha had been the first one to take off her ring the second everything went to hell between them.

“Luckily for you, I do just have one lying around.” Rio waves her left hand and her wedding ring appears on her finger. She notes the relief that flashes across Agatha’s face and glances at the purple witch’s bare hand. “Well, what about you?”

For a second, Agatha considers crafting an entirely new ring for this charade, but decides against it. Nothing she created could be more beautiful than the one Rio had given her all those years ago, and she needed to rub this in her mother’s face as much as possible. She conjures her ring and holds it up to the green witch. Rio looks slightly surprised, as if she hadn’t expected that Agatha had kept the ring after all these years. Agatha couldn’t really blame her since honestly she had tried to get rid of it many times over the centuries, but somehow it always ended up back in her pocket. She slips it on, hands shaking slightly at how the ring still fit so perfectly after all these years like it was still meant to be. She shakes the thoughts from her head and is relieved to hear the doorbell ring.

Rio glances at the door and puts the bunny down. Then she looks over at her pretend wife with a smirk. “Let’s put on a show, milady.”

Agatha sighs in annoyance at the pet name but can’t help smile a bit at the comment. If there was one thing Rio Vidal and Agatha Harkness were good at, it was putting on a show. She goes to open the door.

“Wow, you actually convinced her,” Jen says in surprise as she and the gang waltz through the door and look at the fake couple.

The coven leader shrugs, “I can be very persuasive.”

Rio smirks, “Nah, I’m just here to piss off Evanora.”

“We appreciate your sacrifice,” Lilia nods as she pats the green witch’s arm.

Agatha frowns at the comment but doesn’t retort. If there was one thing the coven could agree on, it was that her homophobic ghost mother needed to be put in her place.

“I brought cookies!” Teen holds out the plate excitedly. “They’re err, green.” He glances at Rio.

The green witch grabs the plate and points at the teen, “Don’t think for a second I’ve forgotten that you let that ghost back into the mortal realm.”

Billy winces, “I’m sorry! I didn’t know what I was doing!”

Death scowls but eventually relents. She never was one to hold grudges with children. If there was anyone she truly had a gripe with about this whole mess it was The Scarlet Witch, but that was a story for another day.

They sit down at the table and Lilia looks at the coven leader, “Nice ring.”

Rio smirks, “Thank you.”

“Did you make it?” Alice asks in surprise.

Agatha glares at the green witch, begging her not to answer. Of course, Rio does. “Yes.”

“Woah, can I see?” Billy leans across the table to get a glimpse of the ring.

“No.” Agatha scowls and pulls her hand back, barely resisting the urge to throw the ring across the room. Just because she was pretending to be married again doesn’t mean she was ready for all the feelings and questions that came with it.

“Aw babe, the kid’s just curious,” Rio teases.

“Don’t call me babe,” the purple witch hisses.

“Sorry, Agatha.”

“I hate you.”

“And yet here I am.” Death tilts her head cockily.

“Only because of some stupid eternal binding spell.”

“You guys did that? I didn’t think that was possible!” Alice says in shock. The bickering couple ignores her though.

“Which you suggested,” Rio smirks back at the purple witch. “Besides, we both know you never even tried to break it.”

Agatha grits her teeth. “Doesn’t mean I don’t hate you.”

“I would never think otherwise,” The green witch’s eyes glint with mirth. “Barcelona aside.”

The purple which bites her cheek to hold back a blush at the 20th century memory. Okay so maybe her and Rio had their moments over the years apart. What estranged couples didn’t? It doesn’t matter that Agatha had lost count of how many of those moments there were.
