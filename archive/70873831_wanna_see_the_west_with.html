CHAPTER 1 - Tallahassee

 

RIO

 

A half filled duffle bag sat on the bed, already stripped of the sheets and duvet, now hanging on the clothes line outside in the warm sun. That, a few boxes and the last bit of clothing was all she had left to pack for her move to the great Yosemite. 



Well that and her grandfather’s wedding ring. A simple gold band her grandmother gave her, which she kept tucked away in between her favorite sweaters for one day. For some day in the future, hopefully not too far from now, where she would meet the love of her life and promise to be hers forever.



To say that Rio was a hopeless romantic would be an understatement. Hell, she was a bona-fide fucking sap and there was no denying it. Though she didn’t exactly dream of having a traditional fairytale wedding, that was some other straight girl’s dream, she still dreamt of one day finding that one girl that she could love and cherish for the rest of their lives together. 



Rio consumed romance novels like they were manuals to her future love life and cried along with Meg Ryan movies until the ending credits rolled. Even some of her favorite songs seemed to be written just for her and her future love. 



But that was just the kind of love she had known all her life. Growing up, her parents were the picture of true love and happiness. Rio could listen to her father tell the story of how they met over and over again. 



As Gabriel liked to tell it, they met on a beautiful sunny Florida afternoon, at the bottom of the courthouse steps on her father’s first day as an official American citizen. After months of hard work, studying and finally passing the grueling interviews to prove his allegiance to a new country’s flag, he held his Certificate of Naturalization up in the air as he hopped down the stairs to celebrate his US citizenship. 



Her mother Ana, a clerk of the court at the time, was sitting on the courthouse steps enjoying her sandwich on her short lunch break, when a very excited Gabriel ran past her, celebrating loudly and joyfully hugging everyone in sight, yelling “God bless America!”. Before he could bend down and hug Ana, a total stranger, she stood up abruptly and shoved him off. 



“Not so fast, buddy.” She said, holding out her hands to stop him from pulling her into a hug. 



“Apologies, miss.” he said, removing his hat and when the two made eye contact the world around them stilled. Gabriel called it love at first sight. Ana called it a happy coincidence that she didn’t shove him off the stairs. Ana also didn’t know what to call the fact that she agreed to celebrate with him by joining him for a milkshake and a slice of pie at the diner down the street after her shift at the courthouse ended that same day. Maybe that was fate.



Whatever it was, after that day the two started dating and married soon after. Rio was born a year later and quickly followed by her two younger sisters Lida and Sofia. Rio grew up in a household filled with love. The love her parents had for each other was apparent in everything they did and in the way they treated each other and their children. Rio’s two younger sisters would agree, no one loved their mother like their father did.



That was what Rio wanted. Where others dreamed of becoming doctors and fire fighters, Rio dreamed of being in love, of spoiling a girlfriend or being loved and adored by a romantic partner. Sure she also had big career ambitions, that’s why she always studied her ass off to keep her scholarship and to graduate from college, but being in love and having a family of her own to love and care for, that was her ultimate goal.



Being a hopeless romantic could really suck sometimes. Throughout high school and college, she was always the giver in her relationships and she gave everything, time, affection, attention, and with Kayla she even gave money, but that last one was a mistake and a lesson she had to learn the hard way. So yes, being a love fool let her make mistakes and allowed her to spend her love on the wrong people but she owed that up to growing pains in her pursuit to find the one. The real one.  



After returning home to her parents from college after graduation, Rio decided that she had outgrown small town Florida and thought it was time to finally spread her wings. She applied for jobs at every national park the great states of America had to offer and when the position of Invasive Plant Management in Yosemite National Park opened up, Rio jumped at the chance. 



Over several Zoom sessions, calls and assessments, she passed the final interview and was set to start her new life in California in three short weeks. Rio was ecstatic. Her new adventure awaits, and what and who else? Well she’d find out pretty soon. The only thing between her and her new life was about 3000 miles and saying her final goodbyes to her family and friends.



Her mother helped her pack all her belongings up in what felt like a hundred boxes and bags and her father helped to make sure her car would be up for the long drive across the country and on the last night in her parents house, her family, friends and neighbors all celebrated with a going away party that brought tears to Rio’s eyes. She’ll miss Florida, and she’ll miss her family and her friends, but Rio had a good feeling, her life was about to change and leaving small town Florida was only the start. 



The next morning Rio pulled her car out of her parents’ driveway, packed to the brim and her insides feeling heavy with anticipation mixed with sorrow as her parents waved her off. Rio looked back in the rearview mirror with a teary smile, her new life stretched out before her. Every line blurring past her on the road spelled out, “This is your turn”. Yes, this was her turn to go out chasing the sweet call of finding love and adventure.



She had three weeks to get to California, and 3000 miles would take her only about a week if she was slow, but Rio planned on making the most of her road trip. And why not? She had time and nowhere to be for at least three weeks, so way before Rio left, she spent hours researching her road trip, marking out a map of everywhere she wanted to stop and everything she wanted to see and experience while traveling across to the other side of the USA. 



And most importantly, no road trip would be complete without an excellent soundtrack. Turning out onto the interstate, Rio inserted the mix CD she made for her road trip and her excitement grew with every drumbeat of the first song. She matched the drumbeat on her steering wheel and sang along loudly to Mr. Brightside as the road opened up before her. “But it's just the price I pay, destiny is calling me, open up my eager eyes 'cause I'm Mr. Brightside…”

Tomorrow she wanted to visit Tate's Hell State Forest for the day before continuing on on her adventure. Rio tossed her overnight bag on the second bed and went to inspect the bathroom. She suddenly thanked her sister for convincing her to pack her flip flops to shower in.



She changed into a fresh shirt, opting for a dark green button down flannel shirt, and swapped her sneakers for her boots and slung her bag with her book and her wallet over her head, to go out to see where she could find some dinner before getting some much needed rest. 



The cool autumn breeze blew past her as the leaves rustled around her. “A lil’ wind didn’t never hurt no one in Florida” her elderly neighbor used to say every year when the seasons changed. Rio agreed, summer in Florida was brutal and the turn of the season felt welcome.



A few blocks down from where she was standing, Rio saw a flickering of lights as the sign of a small diner beckoned her. She tucked her hands in her pockets and headed down the road, passing the gas station on her way. 



A bus was parked haphazardly across the entrance of the gas station, clearly broken down on its way into town and Rio ducked her head as she passed the small crowd of disgruntled passengers. 



She reached the diner and decided to take a seat at the counter since the booths seemed to be filling with some of the passengers from the broken down bus and since she was only one person, she decided it would be decent manners to not take up a whole booth for herself. 



“What can I get ya?” a perky waitress asked as she took a seat behind the diner counter.



“Just a black coffee for now, please.” Rio answered politely.



The waitress left the menu on the counter and Rio scanned it for something small and easy. She had some money, but she also had three weeks and Rio has never been one to splurge or spend money unnecessarily.



A small cup of coffee was placed in front of her and Rio placed her order for a toasted sandwich with fries. While she waited for her food, she pulled out her book and continued reading her latest obsession, The Notebook. It was cheesy, it was sappy, it was a cry your eyes out romance novel and Rio had read it twice already but who cared, she loved it anyway.



“The Notebook? Really?” a voice next to her snarked. 



Rio paused and watched as long fingers picked up a bunch of fries from her plate. She followed the hand to the mouth of its owner and blinked stupidly as she watched the fries disappear behind a set of perfectly shaped lips. 



Rio held her breath as her hands moved without her knowing, folding over the corner of the page where an old earmark was already engraved in the corner. She couldn’t look away from what she thought was the most beautiful woman she has ever set her eyes on. 



Piercing blue eyes narrowed at her as the long fingers moved back to her plate to steal another fry.

A flicker of concern washed over the woman’s face and Rio noticed her glancing back to her plate.

The waitress stood in front of her and she seemed annoyed, “If you’re not gonna order, you gotta go. We’re busy tonight with the broken down bus and all.”

“...a strawberry milkshake and a cheeseburger thanks doll.” she said to the waitress with a big fake smile, sitting back down next to Rio. 



When the waitress left to put in her order Rio sheepishly turned her head to the woman who was now trying her best to avoid eye contact.



“Most people would say thank you if someone offered to buy them food, you know.” Rio said, picking up her book again. She wasn’t going to read, but she suddenly didn’t know what to do with her hands.

Of course, Rio thought, “You could have just said Thank you, Rio. I appreciate the kind gesture, Rio. No need to be a bitch about it.”

“Bingo.” Rio whispered to herself, slowly opening the door and finding the bunny nestled in a pile of hay in a small enclosure.



“Hey buddy,” Rio smiled, and bent down to pick up the bunny, hitting her head on the edge of the table next to the enclosure. 



As she held onto the bunny, tucking him in under her jacket, she felt her head sting from hitting the corner of the table. The bunny squirmed a bit, so she decided that there wasn’t time to tend to her own wounds, she was rescuing a bunny for her new wife. She made sure the bunny was snugly tucked away and out of sight under her arm and headed back to the reception hall to get Agatha.



When she peeked around the corner, she saw Agatha still talking to the magician, who had his hand on her shoulder. Rio started fuming and made her way to the two, pushing through the small crowd of people around them.



“Excuse me,” Rio interrupted them, jealousy starting to boil over. How dare he touch her wife? And as if in joint protest, the bunny started squirming again, making her jacket bulge out visibly under her arm.



Agatha’s eyes widened when she saw it, and Rio realised they were sure to be caught out soon.



“Oh my god,” the magician said suddenly. 



This is it. Rio thought. This was how she’d end up in jail. Stealing a magical bunny in Las Vegas.



“Oh my god!” the magician yelled out again and Rio froze. “You are bleeding. I… I hate blood, I’m gonna be sick!” he said, folding over himself and stumbling away and leaving them and the stolen bunny behind.



For a few seconds they were shocked and silent. They were actually getting away with it… Agatha grabbed her hand and pulled her out of the theatre and around the corner, passing Jen and Alice who, obliviously stood and waited for them at the theatre’s snack counter.



Agatha dug the bunny out from Rio’s suit jacket and cuddled it against her face. “Hey baby! You’re safe now!” Agatha said. “Look, Rio. Our son! Mister Scratchy.”



Rio stood blinking and swaying a bit. 



“Seńor Scratchy,” Rio corrected. “He’s my son too, so he’s a seńor.” 



Suddenly Agatha grabbed her chin and pulled her face to hers. “Of course, my brilliant…” kiss “...beautiful, wife! You did it! And the bleeding was an excellent touch! Really sold it, and look, now our baby is safe!”



When Alice and Jen finally caught up to them again, Agatha hid the bunny back in Rio’s suit jacket and finally agreed to call it a night, but not after getting a little name tag made for Seńor Scratchy, their newly adopted (totally not stolen) son, from the coin press souvenir machine in the lobby of the casino.

“...and I guess the rest, you’ll find out once you finally get over your hangovers and your memories return.” Jen shrugged her shoulders.



“We’re… married.” Rio swallowed. “And I stole a bunny from a magician.” 



“And I won eighty five thousand dollars!” Agatha beamed, clearly not recognising the panic in the woman Jen revealed to be her wife.



“We got… m-married. Okay… okay… this is fine… like really okay.” Rio slowly put down her glass of water on the table and felt five sets of eyes follow her every move as she struggled to make sense of everything Jen had just revealed to them. She started getting faint flashes of the night before and felt herself get nauseous, “Sorry, I’ve gotta go.”



Rio got up and ran to their room, stumbling into the en-suite bathroom and heading straight for the toilet, where she hurled and heaved. Her head was killing her and now as things became more vivid and clearer she felt her heart beating out of her chest. 



Suddenly Agatha appeared by her side and held her hair back as the previous night's bad decisions spilled from within her. “Breathe, Rio. Please, just breathe okay, you’ll be okay.” Agatha said, still holding her hair and lightly rubbing her back.



Rio finally stopped heaving and settled on the floor, resting her head against the wall tiles, breathing in and out to try and to prevent the threatening panic attack from taking over.



She cupped her face with her hands as she started crying. “What the fuck did we do?”



Agatha moved to sit in front of her and gently pried her hands away from her face. “Rio. Rio, look at me please.”



Rio wiped her eyes on her shoulder, flinching at the sudden pain from the covered tattoo, and finally dared to make eye contact with Agatha, her wife.



“Are you going to be okay?” Agatha asked quietly, gently rubbing the palm of Rio’s hand, in an attempt to help calm her down.



Rio awkwardly met her eyes and nodded. She couldn’t yet find the words to speak or address the married elephant in the room.



“Rio, please. Talk to me.” Agatha quietly pleaded. “I know it’s a lot, and last night was a mist…”



But Rio knew what Agatha was going to say, and although she regretted a huge chunk of what apparently happened the night before, she couldn’t let Agatha finish her thought.



“No, Agatha. Don’t.” Rio interrupted. Her voice was slightly more hoarse than she herself expected. “Don’t say it was a mistake. Marrying you… I… it…”



Agatha took in a sharp breath and stared at Rio. Her brows were knitted together in a mixture of worry and nerves and she bit her bottom lip which Rio admittedly loved. 



“Unless you feel it was a mistake, I… don’t.” Rio said, carefully taking Agatha’s hand in hers and lacing their fingers together.



Agatha looked down at their hands, “You don’t?” she quietly asked and Rio couldn’t help but hear a glimpse of hope in her voice.



“I don’t.” Rio answered confidently. “I mean, we’ve got a lot to talk about and to figure out. Like how this will all work, and if we want to… I am way too hungover for this now actually.”



Agatha nodded and laughed and a tear dropped from her eyes. Rio gently caught it and wiped it from her cheek. 



“Just… I don’t regret marrying you.” Rio smiled. “I just regret not really remembering any of it.”



Agatha smiled widely, a few more tears fell from her eyes, and she leaned in to try and kiss Rio, but before she could, Rio stopped her with her hand over her pursed lips, “Before you do that, I really need to brush my teeth first.”



Agatha giggled and helped Rio get up from the floor, holding onto her hand for just a while longer. Rio appreciated the anchor and squeezed it lightly before moving to the sink to brush her teeth, while Agatha held her hand in hers



“I don’t regret it either, Mrs Harkness.” Agatha said to Rio’s reflection in the mirror.



“Vidal.” Rio said, correcting her around the toothpaste foaming in her mouth. She bent down to rinse away the toothpaste and pat her mouth dry with the small hand towel. “It’s Mrs Vidal. We’re The Vidals now, baby.”
