Rio's room was unrecognizable. Normally, it was neat as a pin, carefully and artfully arranged. Now, as she opened her bleary eyes into the morning sunshine, all she could see was a snowdrift of paper covering the floor of her bedroom.

Hello, I'm Rio Vidal. Welcome to my channel—

Hi! It's Rio! Thanks for watching my new video—

—Now that we're in our new home, the Death and her...fuck that's stupid NOPE

—and remember to like, comment, and subscribe!

She felt the faint scratchiness of a piece of paper beneath her and rooted it out. Whatever diagram or flow chart she had scribbled onto it late into the night was smeared beyond recognition. Rio sighed and put her face in her hands.

It was her own goddamn fault, really. For not only hitching her wagon to the Agatha Harkness express, but also for letting the fame, money, and, let's be real, free stuff that came from content creation get to her head. Now she was back home after her writing residency abroad, with no real job outlook, turning back to YouTube to pay her rent. Unlike some of the other writers in her residency, moving home was not an option. Her relationship with her parents was best described with the word "strained" and it was a rare occurence when her sisters responded back to a text or a phone call.

Rio stood up and gazed around the disaster in the bedroom. The suitcases in the corner were still not unpacked and their contents spilled across the floor, mixing in with the paper. She should really separate the two before she destroyed her entire laundry load by staining all the clothing with ink. With a groan, she began to sort out the papers from one another:

Possible channel names: Lady Death, Original Green Witch, using just my name is STUPID, Ma—

Pros and Cons for asking Lilia for a pity collab. Pros: Money. Cons: Agatha, seeing Agatha, hearing about Agatha, running into—

Video ideas:

teach how to make miniature magical greenhouse

talk about the sapphic history of giving violets

eat a deadly mushroom on camera and then purge it from my system (not clickbait)

Google price of kidney vs section of liver on the black market. Which is easier to part with.

Fuck all of this. Rio swept all of the loose papers into her hands and tossed them into her wastebasket. Good fucking riddance. She rooted under her bed, coming up with another snowdrift of papers. By the Mother, if she didn't—

Her phone rang and she dived for it as a distraction from her current situation. She didn't even look at the screen to see who was calling, which was a mistake.

"Hello?"

"Ok, is there like, an ad you need me to do or something?"

"Vidal." She could hear the faint squeak of Jen's computer chair. "Give me your new address. The car will be there at two for you."

"Uh, I'm busy at that time," Rio said, scrambling.

"No, you're not. You don't have a job." There was some of the real Jen peeking through, finally, as Rio irritated her.

"Are you implying there is a financial gain if I attend this meeting?"

"Yes, obviously."

"Then I suppose I'll be there. Thank you for sending a car. I'll be ready." She hung up the phone before Jen could get the final word in, which would absoluetly make her seethe.

Rio remembered when Jen has spent long hours debating over the color palette, before settling on lots of creams and wood tones, featuring pops of pink. Pictures from the history of Kale Kare Network hung on the walls, which spanned the full gamut from Jen filming skincare videos on her bedroom floor to starting her own network to support other witch creators to the entire coven onstage at the most recent Creator Awards. Rio knew she stuck out like a sore thumb in her grungy green and brown sweats amongst the calming vibe of the lobby. She could feel the receptionist's judgement and did her best to give off an air of not caring as she scrolled away on her phone.

Great. Great great great. Time to see her ex, who she had spent the last year trying to forget and get over. It was why she had waded out into the forest, hoping that the time away from all society would be beneficial. But no dice. How her ancestors must be laughing at her now.

The basement was the coolest room in Agatha's house.

Cool metaphorically as well as literally. It was always 20 degrees lower in there than the outside temperature, which suited Billy very well. His body still had not properly adjusted to the Los Angeles temperatures, even after living here for a year.

The basement also held Agatha's well of magical knowledge. It was meticulously set up, with all of her grimories lined up along one wall. She'd put in a potion station once Billy had moved in, so that he could practice, and there was a large floor space to draw chalk circles and runes. The entire space was protected in runes so strong that only he and Agatha could enter without trouble.

It was where Billy spent most of his time after school, reading through Agatha's books and taking careful notes in his journal. He could easily lose hours in the basement, only emerging when he was hungry enough to take notice.

"Teen!" Agatha called, from the top of the stairs.

Billy ignored her. He'd spread himself out on the floor, carefully copying out a chalk protection circle. If he rubbed the chalk a little more this way and that, he'd make the perfect rune. He grimaced in frustration.

"Teen!" Agatha's voice came again. "Teenager." Her voice was louder as she descended, though Billy could tell she paused right at the bottom. "The left line is wonky."

Billy sighed and smudged it out, chucking the chalk across the basement floor. It bounced and rolled. Scratchy, who liked to hang out in the basement with him, scrambled after it.

"You didn't have to smudge it out" Agatha huffed. "You could have fixed it."

"Rio's moving in, temporarily."

When he had first woken up to an empty house a year ago, he hadn't thought anything of it. Tommy must have had another track meet and his parents had left Billy home to sleep in. But then the hours had ticked by and they hadn't returned. When Billy had checked the garage, their cars were still there. Then he'd checked their rooms, only to find his parents usually facetiously made bed messy and Tommy's neatly made up, as though he'd never made it to bed himself. It was then that he'd panicked and called the police and then Agatha.

Agatha had arrived much faster than he'd expected her to ("I was in Massachusetts," is what she'd said when he'd asked). She'd examined the house thoroughly and had spoken to the police on his behalf. When his parents and brother hadn't reemerged in a week, she'd secured temporary custody and had brought him back to LA with her. There had been no sight of his family since.

But maybe, maybe, maybe today would be the day that he would see them. Setting the bowl filled with water down carefully, he cleared his mind and spoke the Latin phrase that would hopefully show him his lost family. But when the water rippled, all that there was to see was inky blackness. He slumped over sadly, releasing the spell so that the water cleared.

"Can you double check in case I did it wrong?" he asked. Agatha nodded and completed the incantation herself. Again, there was nothing to see except pitch black.

"We'll keep trying," Agatha soothed. On the other magics he was taught, she'd push and bicker with him, but in the search for his family, she never did. "Lilia may think of something. We'll find them someday."

"Ok," he responded, despondently.

Rio was experiencing about the weirdest case of déjà vu ever.

The master bedroom was the same. Same paint, same linens, same bed. The closet had been half cleared for her or maybe Agatha had just never moved her items over to the side Rio used to habitate. The bathroom was completely cleared of Agatha's toiletries, presumably moved to the hallway bathroom Agatha would share with Billy, so it felt more like a clean and empty hotel bathroom now. Scratchy's pen had been moved too, probably into the office. Same with the bookshelves that had once stood in the space between the closet and the bathroom. There was spots in the dust on the dressers that were shaped like the photo frames that Rio knew had once stood there.

She emerged from the closet to find Agatha standing in the doorway. "I might need to get in the closet occasionally," she said brusquely, "but I tried to make it so that I'm in here as little as possible."

Rio nodded. "Thanks." She opened her suitcase and carefully removed the knife sheath that was sitting at the top. She placed it carefully on the dresser where it would be safe before remembering to add, "And tell Jen I appreciate the help with getting the sublease taken care of."

"You can tell her yourself at the launch event on Friday," Agatha said.

"Right, ok." Rio said. She shuddered a little at the thought. She'd always hated the schmoozing events, showing up to see and be seen. At least she would be Agatha's arm candy, which meant that she could get away with hanging on the edges of conversations and sipping her beverage of choice. She probably wouldn't be able to get away with that this time around. She'd been gone a year. People would be curious and would have questions.

Agatha didn't seem to notice Rio's inner turmoil and merely said, "I'll let you get settled in. I'll send Billy up when lunch is ready."

"No," Rio said. "Thank you. I want to get 100% settled in. I'll eat later."

"Alright," Agatha said. She shifted, as though she wanted to ask another question, but when Rio looked at her expectantly, all she said was, "I'll leave you to it."

"Thank you," Rio said, again, and Agatha swung the door closed. The house was soundproofed well enough that she didn't hear Agatha walk away.

Her suitcases were fully unpacked and everything was arranged in the hour. She still had the bulk of her items in a storage unit, where she'd stashed everything before she left the country. That would need to wait until she moved into the next place. No sense in pulling everything out only to move it in a few months, once the fake relationship was up.

When she had finished her unpacking, she left the room and headed downstairs. There was no sign of Agatha or Billy. She pulled the refrigerator open and stared into it, letting the cold air wash over her.

"I made soup earlier."

The refrigerator door slipped from her fingers as she jumped and slammed shut. Billy stood there, smiling, in all of his emo glory. Dark eyeliner, a sweater when it was 80 degrees outside, painted fingernails. She let her eyes sweep over him from head to toe.

"It's vegan. And I made bread."

She could sense the over eagerness to please pouring off of him. Rio stared at him, which seemed to unnerve him. Maybe she should feel a little bad for picking on a teenager, but it was delightful to see him squirm.

"I'm not vegan," she finally said.

Billy winced. "I know, I just." He stuttered for a moment. "Wanted you to know. It's lentil."

Rio considered the logistics of ordering Uber Eats to the house just to see what he'd do. But considering there was only about $30 in her bank account, that would not be a smart move.

"Sure. I'll take some soup."

Billy exhaled in relief, then carefully slid past her to get into the fridge. "I'll just get it out and reheat it. Won't you sit down?"

"You don't need to treat me like a guest."

"But you are. I mean. You're not going to be here long term. You're just...staying for a bit. Right?"

Rio cocked her head. "Right." She could tell that he continued to be unnerved by her. Maybe the monotone? Or the unblinking stare? It was like he'd never met an autistic person before.

Billy didn't nuke the soup like she thought he would and instead slowly reheated it on the stove. When he brought the bowl over, he nearly dropped it. Only Rio moving quickly managed to save the bowl.

"Thanks," she said, once the bowl was safely in front of her. "You can go back to whatever you were doing."

"Ok," Billy said. He backed away slowly, like she was a ferocious beast that he had to keep an eye on the entire time he backed out of the door.
