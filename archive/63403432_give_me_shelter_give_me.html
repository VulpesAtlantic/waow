She turns to the window in an instant.

“Please my love, are you there?”, the tiny voice begs and Agatha shuffles off the bed and to the window in the span of a single second.

“Rio?”, she peaks through the slit.

“Thank God, I was so scared,” she squeezes her fingers in, reaching to touch Agatha, “are you alright?”

Agatha sobs.

“No I’m not, she’s locked me in, Rio, she’s not letting me out,” she whispers and touches Rio’s hand with hers, “there is a man that comes in the morning, she won’t allow me to turn him down.”

Rio’s eyes widen in the shine of her lantern.

“Please don’t let her give me to anybody else!”, she cries and holds on to Rio’s fingers, “please say you meant it! That you would be-”

She hears steps behind her, loud thuds on the wooden floor, and her mother yelling her name.

“Agatha, I swear by God, if anybody is in there with you!”, her mother is shouting behind the closed door, metal clacking as she scrambles for the keys.

The lantern has vanished into the night when Agatha turns back around. Rio is no longer beneath her window and for a moment, Agatha cannot say for certain that she had ever been there at all. Maybe her mind is tricking her now. Perhaps she is going mad after all.

When her mother opens the door, and Agatha has crawled back up onto the bed.

“I’m sorry mother, I must have talked in my sleep,” she whimpers and buries her fists in the blanket until her knuckles turn white.

There is something hateful in her mother’s eyes again, but nevertheless, she closes the door with a huff and walks back into the direction of her room.

Once more, Agatha silently cries herself to sleep.

 

The world is still and silent around her when she slowly comes to.

Dust dances in the dim beams of sunlight her windows let through. In the distance, she can hear the faint sounds of birds. Something throbs against the inside of her skull and her mouth is so dry, she feels thirst for the first time in days.

An hour passes before she hears a knock on the front door, and then another. That must be him. He might have come to take her, but she will not dress herself for him, she will not walk out of this room for him, she will not willingly go with him.

Only on the third knock can she hear her mother walk to the door. He must be earlier than expected then, if she is not yet up and ready to entertain him. Agatha closes her eyes once more, listens to them talk, mainly him, probably chattering down the same rehearsed speech they all hope will impress her mother.

It takes longer than usual though, and there is barely any reply from her mother. Opening her eyes, curiosity finally gets the better of her. Leaning against the door, she notices it is unlocked, her mother must have forgotten to shut it off last night. Silently, she presses down on the handle and steps out, carefully rounding the corner to get a glimpse of him.

Only it’s not a stranger – the face that smiles at her as soon as it sees her is Rio’s. She’s in a white shirt, black pants, and a black vest, a cap pressed to her chest with her left hand, a bouquet of fresh flowers in the other.

“-so if you are willing to give her to me, I promise I will take good care of her, she will want for nothing, I will lay the world at her feet,” Rio looks back at her mother, “tell her to wear her nicest dress and I will make her my wife this very hour.”

Her voice is gentle, deep and soft. Her hair is shorter again (she must have cut it herself), the shirt tight and high on her chest and she has stuffed something into her pants that mimics the soft bulge of a man’s. There is not a single thing that would tell of her secret, not a single hint she is not what she presents to be.

“That is,” Rio hesitates, “if she wants me too, and wants to come to Pennsylvania with me.”

The smile on her lips is warm and open. In the right light, it even looks as if her eyes glimmer a little when she addresses Agatha directly, “I will build you a beautiful house and I will plant you a garden full of strawberries and peach trees, Agatha, if you say yes.”

Agatha looks over to her mother, still wrapped up in an old morning cloth, bewilderment bordering on sheer shock in her face. She moves her lips but nothing comes out.

“I will take him, mother," she intercepts any protest that might be forming in her mother's head, "please I beg of you,” she breathes and swallows hard.

And her mother nods, barely moving her chin before Agatha can close the distance and fling herself into Rio’s strong, open arms, making her tumble out of the door and onto the porch.

“Make me your wife,” she hums into Rio's ear before she kisses it, “and give me shelter, give me warmth.”
