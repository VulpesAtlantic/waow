Summary

Agatha wakes up first. She doesn’t mean to watch Rio sleep, really, she doesn’t. But once it starts, she can’t seem to stop.

Forty-seven minutes pass.

She counts all of them.

And in that time, she spirals through the quiet panic of realizing just how much she cares, how much this means, and how terrified she is of the fact that this—Rio, the bed, the safety, the love—might actually be real.

When Rio wakes, she doesn’t need Agatha to explain. She knows. She feels it.

And Agatha, still buzzing with too many feelings and not enough defenses, blurts out the one truth she can’t seem to hold in:

“You make it better.”

And Rio… pulls her in like it’s the most obvious thing in the world.

Notes

This is one of those “you wake up and realize you’re in love and everything hurts” one-shots. Agatha doesn’t know how to process softness. Rio is half-asleep and emotionally clairvoyant. Nobody panics (except Agatha again). Enjoy.

The first thing Agatha noticed was the silence.

Not the kind that crept in after something loud—not the silence of a slammed door, or a fight, or some emotional fallout—but the soft, ambient kind. The kind that came with early morning light and unspoken permission to stay still a little longer.

She wasn’t sure what woke her.

No alarm. No noise. Just… something. Her body, maybe. The quiet shift of sleep into awareness. Her internal clock, that useless thing she still didn’t trust but kept listening to anyway.

It was early.

Too early for birds. Too early for traffic.

The world hadn’t started yet.

She blinked slowly into the half-dark, eyes adjusting to the grey light that bled through Rio’s curtains. The room looked different in the morning. Blurred at the edges. Like the sharp outlines of life hadn’t fully loaded yet.

And then she turned her head.

Rio was still asleep.

Agatha’s breath caught—not from surprise, but from the sheer, ridiculous tenderness of it.

Rio was on her side, facing Agatha. One arm curled under her pillow, the other tucked in close to her chest like she was protecting something invisible. Her mouth was parted slightly. Her lashes cast soft shadows across her cheeks. A strand of hair had fallen over her temple, and her nose wrinkled every few breaths like she was processing something in a dream.

It was obscene, how calm she looked.

Peaceful in a way Agatha didn’t know how to be even when she was awake, let alone unconscious.

Agatha lay very still.

Watching.

Not for any particular reason. Just… because.

It wasn’t the first time she’d seen Rio sleep, obviously—they’d been sharing beds for weeks. And Rio was a quiet sleeper, rarely moving, always generating more warmth than seemed physically possible. But something about this moment felt different.

Softer. Slower. Realer.

There was nothing performative about sleep. No filtered expression. No social mask. Just… being. Breathing. Existing.

And Rio—who was all structure and deliberation and neat, quiet habits—was completely unguarded.

It undid her a little.

Agatha exhaled through her nose, careful not to make a sound. The air in the room felt too delicate. Like if she moved too quickly, it would fracture.

She let her eyes roam.

Over the slope of Rio’s cheekbone. The delicate line of her brow. The faint indentation at her temple from the pillow. The subtle twitch of one hand under the blanket, fingers flexing slightly.

It had only been a few months.

Not long in the grand scheme of things. Not long at all.

And yet—

Agatha’s chest ached.

She’d known crushes before. Obsessions. Flings. People who made her feel alive for exactly one week and then evaporated like smoke.

But this?

This was something else.

She didn’t know how to name it yet.

But it was terrifying. And steady. And true.

Her hand itched to reach out and touch. To trace the line of Rio’s collarbone, to press her palm to her shoulder, to confirm that all this softness was real and present and hers.

But she didn’t move.

Instead, she lay there in silence and let herself feel it.

Not rush it. Not wrestle it into something manageable. Just… feel.

The fact that she was warm. That she hadn’t woken up in a panic. That she hadn’t bolted the second she realized she was still in someone’s bed.

That she was still here.

Still beside her.

The realization hit like a slow, low wave.

I am safe. Right now. Here. With her.

It wasn’t a thought. Not exactly. More like a sensation that settled under her skin and refused to budge.

Her throat tightened.

She’d never trusted mornings. Never trusted how vulnerable she felt at the start of the day, before her armor was up, before the caffeine kicked in, before she remembered how to be sharp and untouchable.

But here, in Rio’s bed, in this quiet cocoon of sunlight and soft breathing and tangled blankets— She didn’t feel untouchable.

She felt touched.

And for once, it didn’t feel like a threat.

Agatha had never believed in the whole "watching someone sleep" thing.

She’d always thought it was weird. Creepy. A trope written by people who had never experienced the sheer anxiety of waking up beside someone and not knowing who you were supposed to be in the first five seconds.

She didn’t get it.

Why would anyone want to look at another person that long?

Why wouldn’t it feel invasive?

Why wouldn’t you get bored?

But now—

Thirty minutes in, and she wasn’t bored. She wasn’t creeped out. She wasn’t even overthinking it anymore.

She was just… undone.

Completely, irreparably undone by the sight of Rio Vidal sleeping peacefully in a bed they both kept ending up in. Not just physically, but emotionally. Repeatedly. With choice.

Agatha had never had that.

Not really.

Sure, she’d shared beds. She’d done the tangled-limbs, stolen-sheet routine. But it had never lasted. The quiet always turned awkward. The affection always ran out by sunrise. There was always something to prove, something to fix, some unspoken clock ticking down to the moment they realized she wasn’t what they wanted.

She wasn’t soft.

She wasn’t easy.

She wasn’t calm, or quiet, or uncomplicated.

She was too much of everything and not enough of anything.

But Rio?

Rio hadn’t flinched once.

She hadn’t tried to mold Agatha into something else. She hadn’t pulled away when Agatha got sharp, or tired, or unbearably earnest in all the wrong moments. She just was—calm, grounded, methodical. A person with their own language of care. A person who used systems instead of sweet talk. Who showed love with planning blocks and post-it notes and an alarming capacity for sustained eye contact.

And for some reason—some completely absurd, cosmic, unfair reason—she loved Agatha.

Or if she didn’t yet, she was standing terrifyingly close to the edge of it.

Agatha stared at her sleeping face and thought, You’re ridiculous. You’re asleep, and I’m malfunctioning.

Because that’s what it felt like.

A slow, soft malfunction of every defense mechanism she’d ever spent a lifetime perfecting.

She used to be good at this. At flings. At boundaries. At escape plans. She used to keep her bags half-packed, her feelings tucked behind a hundred self-deprecating jokes, her goodbyes always within reach.

And now?

She’d spent almost an hour lying in bed, silently cataloguing the way Rio’s face changed when she dreamed.

That was… horrifying.

She was horrifying.

Who did this?

Who lay in bed and let themselves feel this much?

Her hands were warm under the blanket. She clenched them without meaning to.

She wasn’t scared of Rio. Not really. But she was scared of what Rio made possible. Of how easily she softened around her. Of how quickly her walls gave way when Rio looked at her like she was already enough.

She’d never trusted that before. Never believed someone could look at the whole of her—neuroses and snark and chaos—and not only stay, but want to stay.

And now here she was. Staring at a woman who had hyperfocused through an dinner and still come out the other side with a handwritten post-it note promising no work, no exceptions.

She didn’t know what to do with that kind of devotion.

So she spiraled.

Internally. Quietly. Elegantly.

Maybe she’ll wake up and decide this is too much.

Maybe she’s just good at systems, and I’m one of them.

Maybe I’m not scared of being abandoned—I’m scared of not knowing how to exist when I’m not.

She rolled onto her side just slightly, careful not to shift the bed.

Rio murmured something unintelligible and adjusted her position, face nuzzling deeper into the pillow. Her hair slid across her cheek. One hand flexed gently under the sheets, reaching in sleep for something invisible.

Agatha nearly cried on the spot.

She didn’t know if this was love.

She didn’t know if she could know that yet.

But whatever it was—whatever this quiet ache in her chest was becoming—it was bigger than anything she’d built her armor for.

And it was quietly, disastrously beautiful.

The worst part about remembering was that it never waited for permission.

It wasn’t dramatic. Not a cinematic flashback. Not some gut-punch of grief. It was just… quiet. Creeping. Slipping into the back of Agatha’s throat while she lay still and watched someone she loved breathe.

It was a blanket pulled tight—but too cold. A silence that buzzed like static behind the skull.

She used to sleep like this all the time.

Alone.

Not just alone in the literal sense, but in the existential one. The kind of alone that sank into your bones and made you forget what it felt like to be safe with another person in your orbit.

There’d been other beds. Other bodies. Other people.

But not like this.

Those nights had always been transactional. Careful. Laced with rules—don’t touch her after. Don’t talk in the dark. Don’t fall asleep too close or let your breathing sync up. And never, ever let them see you soft.

She’d gotten good at the script.

Flirt. Deflect. Disappear.

Repeat.

The first time she tried sleeping next to someone she actually liked, she hadn’t slept at all. Just lay there, eyes fixed to the ceiling, heart racing like she was under surveillance.

She’d counted the seconds between their breaths, convinced they’d roll over, change their mind, leave.

They hadn’t.

But it hadn’t lasted.

It never did.

She was too difficult. Too much work. Too much noise. Too everything.

So she’d gone back to sleeping alone. Safer that way.

She knew the rules in solitude. Knew where her edges ended. Knew how to press her hand to her own chest at 3 a.m. and whisper, you’re okay, you’re okay, you’re okay, until it felt mostly true.

That was easier than risking disappointment.

Easier than looking at someone and wondering when they’d stop looking back with softness.

And then—

Rio.

Agatha didn’t even remember how it started. Not the exact night. Not the first time they drifted off in the same bed instead of retreating to opposite corners of their routines.

It had been gradual.

A head on a shoulder. A hand on a thigh. A brush of limbs under blankets.

And then one morning, she’d woken up and realized she wasn’t scared.

She wasn’t waiting for the silence to turn sharp.

Wasn’t cataloging her breathing or planning her exit.

She’d just… slept.

With someone beside her.

With Rio.

Who slept like it was something sacred.

Like her body trusted the ground under it, the air around it, the woman beside it.

Agatha didn’t understand it at first. That kind of trust. That kind of peace.

But it pulled her in like gravity.

And slowly, night after night, she stopped resisting it.

She started falling asleep faster.

Stopped flinching at every shift.

Let Rio's warmth bleed into her bones like something she didn’t know she’d needed.

It was terrifying.

Because what happened when she got used to it?

What happened when she forgot how to sleep alone again?

What would she do with all the softness she’d stored up in the space between them?

The thought wrapped tight around her ribs.

But then she looked at Rio again—still asleep, still so gentle even in unconsciousness—and something broke in her. Gently. Quietly. Like a piece of armor sliding to the floor.

She was already used to it.

Already past the point of pretending this was casual.

And the truth was?

She didn’t want to sleep alone again.

Not ever.

And God, that terrified her more than anything.

Because now she knew what it meant to feel safe at night. To sleep beside someone and wake up still wanting to be near them.

To want more.

More mornings. More warmth. More Rio.

More of this life they hadn’t meant to build together but were absolutely building anyway.

And Agatha didn’t know what to do with that kind of longing.

Except hold it.

Hold it and hope it didn’t crack her open more than she could survive.

Agatha was still watching her.

She’d shifted slightly, propped up on one elbow now, head resting against her knuckles, breathing slow but shallow. The room was brighter—daylight finally filtering in through the curtains—but not enough to pull her out of the liminal haze she’d fallen into.

She didn’t want to wake Rio.

Not really.

She wanted to stay here in this strange, suspended place. Watching. Memorizing. Folding the moment into herself before the real world intruded.

But Rio moved.

Just barely.

A twitch in her brow. A slight adjustment of her head against the pillow. The hand tucked beneath it flexed once, a subtle signal of incoming awareness.

Agatha froze.

Watched.

Held her breath.

Rio’s eyes didn’t open right away. Her brows drew together, faint lines etching into her forehead. She shifted under the blanket, face twitching with the signs of slow re-entry—like her brain was clicking back into place one piece at a time.

Then she breathed in, deep and steady.

And let it go.

Agatha watched the motion. Watched the tension rise, hover, fade.

And then—

Rio’s eyes blinked open.

Just a sliver at first. Glazed and unfocused. Her gaze flicked toward the ceiling, then toward the shape beside her.

Agatha.

She didn’t speak.

Not yet.

But her eyes sharpened almost immediately.

And then she blinked again—fully this time, like the sight of Agatha made something settle.

Then, softly, still thick with sleep, “You didn’t sleep well.”

Agatha stiffened.

Rio didn’t sound accusatory. Didn’t even sound particularly awake. Her voice was low, rasped, somewhere between observation and intuition.

Agatha opened her mouth. Closed it. Tried to look somewhere else.

“You’re buzzing,” Rio murmured, eyes still half-lidded.

It wasn’t a guess.

It wasn’t even a question.

Just the kind of statement Rio made when she knew something. Deeply. Instinctively. With the kind of certainty Agatha didn’t think anyone had ever had about her.

She swallowed. “Sorry.”

Rio blinked slowly. “Don’t be.”

She reached out, groggy but precise, and rested her fingers lightly on Agatha’s wrist.

The touch was feather-soft. Not demanding. Not possessive.

Just… there.

Anchoring.

Agatha looked down at it. At Rio’s hand. At the faint warmth radiating from the contact like it was a lifeline.

“You’ve been awake awhile,” Rio said.

It wasn’t a question either.

Agatha nodded, throat tight. “Forty-seven minutes.”

Rio’s lips twitched, just barely.

“Very specific.”

“I counted.”

A breath passed.

Agatha felt ridiculous. Ridiculous and exposed and utterly transparent.

And somehow—Rio still hadn’t pulled her hand away.

She shifted onto her back, blinking up at the ceiling. Her other hand came up to rub gently at her eyes, like she was calibrating her brain before forming full thoughts.

“You’re not upset.”

Agatha didn’t answer.

Rio turned her head slightly, met her gaze.

“You’re… full.”

That was the word she used.

Not anxious.

Not overwhelmed.

Full.

Agatha blinked hard.

“Yeah,” she whispered. “That’s one way to put it.”

Rio didn’t push.

Didn’t ask why.

Didn’t demand a breakdown of what the fullness meant, what had woken her, what had kept her staring at Rio’s sleeping face like she’d been trying to translate it.

She just nodded.

Soft. Steady. Present.

And then she said, in the same quiet, calm tone she used for tracking weather systems and laundry schedules:

“You can tell me. Or not. I’ll still be here.”

Agatha’s chest cracked so wide she couldn’t breathe for a second.

Because that was it, wasn’t it?

The thing she hadn’t been able to name.

It wasn’t just that Rio saw her.

It was that Rio left room.

Room for the silence. Room for the spirals. Room for the messy middle of things Agatha didn’t always have words for.

She looked down at their hands. Still connected. Still real.

And for the first time all morning, she let herself believe it.

Agatha stared at Rio’s hand in hers.

It was warm. Slightly calloused at the tips. Not from hard labor or anything especially dramatic— just everyday life. Lifting mugs. Repositioning monitors. Tending to the handful of plants on the windowsill like they were quiet roommates. Holding her.

Agatha flexed her fingers slightly.

Rio didn’t let go.

“You can tell me,” she’d said.

“Or not. I’ll still be here.”

The simplicity of it rattled her.

Because that had never been the bargain before. With anyone.

She’d spent most of her adult life learning to love people quietly. In her head. From a safe distance. From behind the glass wall of sarcasm and wit and I-don’t-actually-need-you bravado. She’d never been good at the part where you say the thing. Out loud. Where you admit to the chaos inside you and risk watching someone recoil from it.

And Rio had just… offered space.

No pressure. No demand.

Just space.

Agatha exhaled slowly, pressing her knuckles to her lips like maybe the pressure could keep the words in.

It didn’t.

“I watched you sleep,” she said.

Rio didn’t blink.

“For forty-seven minutes,” Agatha added, because if she was going down, she might as well be honest.

Still—no reaction.

Just a small head tilt, like Rio was filing the number away.

“And?” she asked.

Agatha blinked. “And… it was a lot.”

Rio’s voice was calm. “A lot in what direction?”

Agatha made a helpless gesture with her free hand. “All of them. Every direction. I just… laid there, watching you, and thinking how stupidly calm you look when you sleep. Like your brain shuts down in the right way. Like nothing in the world is loud enough to follow you into that space.”

Rio considered that. “It’s not.”

Agatha swallowed. “That’s horrifying.”

“It’s peaceful.”

“It’s vulnerable.” Her voice cracked slightly. “And you make it look so easy.”

“I’ve had practice.”

Agatha paused.

And then, quieter: “I haven’t.”

Rio’s fingers flexed gently, grounding her.

“I didn’t used to sleep well next to people,” Agatha went on. “I still don’t, sometimes. But with you… I do. Or I almost do. It’s getting there. And that’s terrifying.”

Rio said nothing.

Just let her keep going.

Agatha stared at the blanket bunched up around their waists, her chest tight and aching. She wasn’t crying. Not yet. But the pressure was there—behind her eyes, in the back of her throat. The feeling of something waiting to fall out.

“I think I might be in love with you,” she whispered.

And there it was.

Dropped like a stone in still water.

It wasn’t planned. Wasn’t rehearsed. Wasn’t even finished forming when she said it.

It just came out.

And she didn’t take it back.

Didn’t panic.

Didn’t fumble for clarification.

She just watched Rio’s face, waiting for it to change.

Rio blinked once. Twice.

Then she said, without hesitation: “That makes sense.”

Agatha let out a shaky breath that might’ve been a laugh.

“You’re not surprised?”

“I’m observant,” Rio said. “You look at me like I’m the last puzzle piece that finally made the picture make sense.”

Agatha stared. “That is… so accurate it should be illegal.”

Rio gave her a slow, sleepy smile.

Then she reached out again.

Wrapped both arms around Agatha, pulling her into her chest with a motion that was soft but certain. Like there was no question this was where she belonged.

Agatha folded into the embrace instantly.

Her face pressed to Rio’s collarbone. Her body curled instinctively toward the warmth, the safety, the steadiness of being held like that. Like it was normal. Like it was allowed.

“You make it better,” she whispered.

She didn’t mean to say it. Not out loud. But it slipped out like breath.

Rio said nothing.

Didn’t ask her to elaborate.

Didn’t analyze it.

She just pulled her tighter.

Fingers gently stroking her back. Breathing steady. Heartbeat calm and consistent beneath Agatha’s ear.

They lay like that for a long time.

No more spiraling. No more noise.

Just stillness.

And for the first time that morning, Agatha stopped counting the minutes.

Because this?

This didn’t need measuring.

It was enough.
