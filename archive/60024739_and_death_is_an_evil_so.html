and death is an evil, so the gods agree

by dumblibramoon

Summary

“Unless you're here to kill me,” Agatha muttered, not looking up from her books, “I really don't care.”

“See, that's the thing.” Rio's voice took on an oddly gleeful tone. “I can't kill anyone right now. I quit.”

That got Agatha's attention. Her head snapped up, eyes narrowing as she took in Rio's appearance. Death looked... different. Still draped in her usual black attire, but there was something more solid about her presence. More... mortal.

“You what?”

“Quit! Resigned. Took early retirement. Put in my two weeks' notice, whatever. Except I didn't actually give notice, I just stopped showing up.” Rio beamed.

In which Death retires.

Agatha was having a fucking terrible week.

She'd lost her magic to Wanda Maximoff of all people, then spent what felt like decades trapped in that suburban sitcom hell, forced to play the role of Agnes with nothing but her own screaming consciousness as company.

And now she was sitting in her kitchen at three in the morning, surrounded by grimoires and revenge plots, when Death itself appeared on her counter.

“So,” Rio said, swinging her legs off the edge of the counter like a child. “Funny story.”

Agatha kept her eyes fixed on the grimoire before her, refusing to give Rio the satisfaction of a reaction. Her fingers tightened on the pages, fighting the urge to check if Rio was holding a scythe behind her back. That was the thing about Death, yeah? -you never knew if she was there to flirt or to reap your soul. Usually both.

The last time Rio had shown up unannounced, Agatha had spent three weeks trying to regrow her left lung. But she had bigger problems than her homicidal ex showing up for their yearly dance of try-to-kill-each-other, fuck-about-it, try-to-kill-each-other-again.

Like how to rebuild centuries of magic without any actual magic. Like how to start over when everything she'd built had crumbled to ash. Like-

“The thing is,” Rio continued, undeterred by Agatha's silence, “I may have done something a teensy bit impulsive.”

“Unless you're here to kill me,” Agatha muttered, not looking up from her books, “I really don't care.”

“See, that's the thing.” Rio's voice took on an oddly gleeful tone. “I can't kill anyone right now. I quit.”

That got Agatha's attention. Her head snapped up, eyes narrowing as she took in Rio's appearance. Death looked... different. Still draped in her usual black attire, but there was something more solid about her presence. More... mortal.

“You what?”

“Quit! Resigned. Took early retirement. Put in my two weeks' notice, whatever. Except I didn't actually give notice, I just stopped showing up.” Rio beamed. “Bet you’ve never seen Death go on strike before.”

Agatha clenched her teeth. “Rio. It’s three in the fucking morning.”

“Is it? Time feels so much more... sequential now. It's fascinating.”

Agatha pinched the bridge of her nose, feeling a headache coming on. “What did you do?”

“I told you. I quit being Death.” Rio hopped off the counter and started rummaging through Agatha's freezer. “Do you not have any ice cream? How do you not have ice cream? This is a crisis, Agatha.”

“The crisis,” Agatha said through gritted teeth, “is that you can't just quit being Death. It's not a job, it's what you are.”

Rio emerged from the freezer with a triumphant “aha!” and a frozen TV dinner. “Not anymore! Now I'm just Rio, your friendly neighborhood ex-Death. Who is also about to try frozen mac and cheese for the first time.” She paused. “Remind me, how do microwaves work?”

As if on cue, Agatha's phone - which hadn't lit up once in the three years she'd been trapped in that sitcom hell - started buzzing. News alerts began flooding her screen. Her eyes widened as she read the headlines.

MIRACLE AT MEMORIAL HOSPITAL: TERMINAL PATIENTS SHOW UNEXPECTED RECOVERY

FIRST TIME FOR EVERYTHING: NO VEHICULAR CRASHES IN A CALENDAR MONTH

DEATH TOLL FROM MAJOR ACCIDENT: ZERO

“Rio,” Agatha said slowly, “what the fuck have you done?”

“Created a little chaos.” Rio was now intensely focused on trying to open the TV dinner package. “Made some waves. Taken a stand. How do humans deal with packaging? This is violence.”

Agatha snatched the package from Rio's hands. “People can't die.”

“I know! Isn't it great?”

“No! This is the opposite of great. This is-” Agatha gestured wildly at her phone, which was still buzzing with alerts. “This is catastrophic.”

Rio shrugged, an infuriatingly casual gesture. “Seems fine to me. No one's dying, the sun is shining-”

“It's the middle of the night.”

“-metaphorically shining, and I'm about to experience processed cheese for the first time.” Right. Death didn’t need to eat. Agatha remembered this now. Rio made grabby hands at the TV dinner. “Come on, Agatha. When's the last time you just... lived a little?”

“When's the last time you completely upended the natural order of things?”

Rio pretended to think about it. “Tuesday? No, wait, that was the thing with the plague rats. Wednesday, definitely Wednesday.”

Agatha stared at Rio, centuries of shared history and barely suppressed rage bubbling beneath her skin.

Here was Death itself - the entity she'd loved and hated and tried to kill more times than she could count - sitting in her kitchen at three in the morning, excited about processed cheese. It was exactly the kind of cosmic joke the universe loved to play on her.

“Alright. Let me make sure I’ve got the full picture, yeah?” she said, each word sharp enough to draw blood. “You abandoned your eternal duty, threw the entire world into chaos, and showed up in my kitchen...” (Eh, Ralph’s kitchen, whatever. Not her problem.) “Because you wanted to use my microwave?”

Rio's eyes glittered with that familiar dangerous mirth, the kind that usually preceded natural disasters.

“Don't be ridiculous. I also want to try pizza. And sleeping. And maybe start a small fire.” She tilted her head, considering. “Actually, a medium fire. Large, at most.”

The headache building behind Agatha's eyes intensified.

She could feel her phone continuing to buzz against the counter, probably more news about the collapse of the natural order.

But Rio just sat there, radiating chaotic delight, as if she hadn't just potentially doomed existence because she was curious about dairy products.

“I knew you were unhinged,” Agatha said, scoffing, “but this is impressive, even for you.”

“Aw, you think I'm impressive.” Rio's grin turned wicked. She lowered her chin, looking up at Agatha through her lashes. “Should I be worried that you're taking the end of death itself so calmly? Usually you'd have tried to stab me at least twice by now.” She wiggled her eyebrows.

Agatha's fingers twitched toward the knife block. “Don't tempt me.”

Rio just smirked, which made Agatha want to stab her even more.

She'd always been like this - infuriatingly untouchable, delighting in Agatha's rage like it was a particularly entertaining show put on just for her.

Before Agatha could decide whether attempted murder was worth the inevitable property damage, her phone buzzed again. This time it wasn't a news alert but a text from an unknown number, the characters writhing and shifting on the screen:

COSMIC ENTITY EMERGENCY MEETING. YOUR HOUSE. 10 MINUTES. BRING DEATH.

“Oh, here come the fun police,” Rio said, reading over Agatha's shoulder. She'd moved without a sound, her breath (since when did Death need to breathe?) tickling Agatha's neck. “Would you believe me if I said I had a prior engagement?”

Agatha turned slowly, finding herself almost nose to nose with Rio. Even mortal, Death's eyes swirled. “You mean your date with processed cheese?”

“It's a very important date.” Rio didn't move back, her proximity a familiar torment. “Besides, cosmic entities are so... dramatic.”

Coming from the woman who once rearranged an entire constellation because Agatha said it looked “tacky,” this was rich.

Before Agatha could point this out, reality rippled.

Sick.

Her kitchen filled with beings of cosmic power. (Disgusting.)

Time appeared as an elderly librarian, complete with a cardigan that seemed to contain actual constellations. Fate showed up in what appeared to be corporate HR attire, clutching a clipboard that probably held the destiny of worlds. Chaos looked like a teenager who'd raided Hot Topic's entire inventory, and various other entities crowded the limited space, their true forms barely contained by unsettlingly human shapes.

Rio, displaying the self-preservation instincts of a meerkat with a death wish, immediately ducked behind Agatha.

“Don't let them take me back,” she stage-whispered against Agatha's ear. “I haven't even tried pizza yet.”

Agatha felt a muscle in her jaw twitch. “Get. Off. Me.”

“Make me.” Rio's arms snaked around Agatha's waist, and she could feel Death's smile against her neck. “Oh. Right. Sorry, sweetheart, you can't. No magic.”

No magic. No anything. She was still in her own personal hell and here was fucking Rio.

Three centuries of history crashed between them like a wave. Rio must have felt Agatha stiffen because her grip loosened slightly, something almost like an apology in the gesture. But before either of them could speak, Time cleared its throat.

“This,” it said in a voice that carried the weight of eons, “is unacceptable.”

“I left a note!” Rio protested, still using Agatha as a human shield. Her fingers pressed into Agatha's hips, a touch so familiar it ached.

Even now, after everything, Agatha's body remembered the shape of Rio's hands, the careful way Death had always held her, like she was something precious and breakable and eternal all at once.

“'Peace out, losers' is not a proper resignation letter,” Fate replied, its pen scratching furiously against the clipboard. Each stroke seemed to alter destiny itself, stars shifting in their courses.

“I added a smiley face!” Rio's chin hooked over Agatha's shoulder, her chest pressed against Agatha's back.

She was warm now, Agatha realized with a start. Rio was actually warm. Death had always run cold before, like marble on a winter morning.

But this - this was human warmth, alive and present and devastating.

“The natural order is collapsing,” Time continued, but Agatha barely heard them. She was too aware of Rio's breath against her neck, of the way Death's immortal hands trembled slightly where they gripped her waist.

Something was wrong. Rio was never uncertain, never vulnerable. She was chaos and endings and terrible jokes about mortality, but she was never, ever afraid.

“Sounds like a you problem.” Rio's voice carried its usual bravado, but Agatha felt the way she pressed closer, seeking refuge or comfort or something else entirely.

Agatha had spent centuries building walls between them, crafting careful barriers from rage and grief and betrayal.

But Rio had always been able to slip through the cracks, to find the spaces between her ribs where Agatha's anger gave way to something softer, something that remembered moonlit nights and laughter and kisses that left ash on her tongue.

“Enough!” Agatha's shout silenced the room. She turned in Rio's arms, dislodging Death's grip but staying close enough to still see the shadows in her eyes, smoke from a funeral pyre. “Why are you really here, Rio?”

For a moment, just a moment, Rio's carefully constructed mask of chaotic glee flickered. Something ancient and raw showed through.

“Would you believe me,” Rio said softly, her smile crooked and honest in a way that made Agatha's chest hurt, “if I said I missed you?”

“Missed me?” Agatha's laugh was sharp enough to cut. “Please, you just fucking saw me. It can’t have been more than a century ago. When you tried to reap my soul. Again.”

“That was work,” Rio protested. Her hands hadn't left Agatha's waist, thumbs tracing absent patterns that felt like sigils against her skin. “This is... personal.”

The cosmic entities watched their exchange like it was the latest episode of their favorite drama. Nosy freaks.

But Agatha barely noticed them, too caught up in the way Rio was looking at her - like she was something vast and terrible and beautiful, like she was something worth upending the universe for.

“Everything's personal with you,” Agatha said, but the words lacked their usual bite. She could feel Rio's heartbeat against her back now, quick and nervous and so damnably human. “You once redirected a meteor because I said I was having a bad day.”

“It was a very small meteor.”

“It was going to hit Chicago.”

“Chicago had it coming.” Rio's smile flickered, that mask of manic glee slipping again. “I watched you, you know. Here. In Westview.”

The name hit like a physical blow. Agatha stiffened, but Rio's grip tightened, keeping her close.

“Every decade,” Death continued, her voice soft and wrecked. “Every episode. And after. When it wasn’t even really you anymore. Every moment you were trapped in that suburban nightmare, playing the nose neighbor with that vapid, plastic smile. Do you know how many times I almost-” She cut herself off, swallowing hard.

Jesus, fuck. Agatha’d lost the Darkhold by then. So Rio had seen her, all that time. Those three years.

“Almost what?” Agatha's voice was barely a whisper.

“Almost broke every rule there is. Almost tore that whole reality apart. Almost killed her for what she did to you.” Rio's laugh was hollow. “But I couldn't. Because I'm Death, and Death has rules.”

She said it like it was a curse, like it was chains wrapped around her throat.

Agatha knew that feeling intimately - the weight of what you are becoming a cage that holds you back from what you want to be.

“Had rules,” Agatha snapped, because she'd be damned if she let Death get away with being dramatic about her own choices. “Past tense. Since you decided to quit your eternal cosmic duty and show up in my kitchen at three in the morning to steal my frozen food.”

“Actually.” Rio's eyes dropped to Agatha's lips, just for a moment. “I quit three weeks ago. The frozen food thing was just an excuse to see you.”

“Three weeks-” Agatha laughed bitterly. “When the spell lifted.”

Rio nodded, and oh, there it was. The truth, written in the vulnerable line of her mouth, in the way her fingers trembled against Agatha's sides.

“I saw what was coming,” Death whispered. “I always see what's coming. And I couldn't... I couldn't bear to-”

“Couldn't what?” Agatha demanded, dread pooling in her stomach. She knew Rio's tells by now - the way her jaw tightened, the slight tremor in her hands, the way her eyes darted away just before delivering catastrophic news. “Rio. What did you see?”

Death had always looked best in shadows. Even now, mortal and warm and terrifyingly vulnerable, Rio carried darkness like a second skin. But the kitchen's fluorescent lights stripped away all mystery, leaving her exposed. Almost human. Scared.

“I saw your future,” Rio said finally. Her fingers curled into the fabric of Agatha's shirt like she was afraid Agatha might disappear. “All of them. Every possible path, every choice, every...” She swallowed hard. “Every death.”

“That's literally your job,” Agatha pointed out, but her voice shook slightly. She'd never seen Rio like this - Death itself, brought this low by something as human and quaint as fear.

“No, you don't understand.” Rio's laugh was bitter. “I see everyone's death. It's... background noise. A constant stream of endings that I've learned to tune out. But yours?” Her hands came up to frame Agatha's face, the touch achingly gentle. “Yours have always screamed the loudest.”

Somewhere behind them, Fate muttered something about “inappropriate workplace relationships.” Time shushed them.

“And what I saw coming...” Rio continued, her thumbs tracing Agatha's cheekbones like she was trying to memorize the shape of her. “Every path, every possibility, they all led to the same place. To me, with your soul in my hands. And I just... I couldn't. Not again. Not after-”

She cut herself off, but Agatha heard the unspoken word anyway. Not after her son. Not after the last time Rio had been forced to choose between duty and whatever twisted, broken thing still spluttered between them. (That thing was not love. Surely, after all this time and all these wounds, not love.)

“So you quit.” Agatha's voice was steady, even as her heart thundered beneath her sternum. “You threw the entire universe into chaos because you didn't want to have to take me.”

“I prefer to think of it as a grand romantic gesture.”

“You're fucking insane,” Agatha breathed, but she found herself leaning closer, drawn in by the gravity of Rio's presence, by the desperation written in every line of her face.

“Probably,” Rio agreed. “But at least I get to lose the rest of my mind with you before the universe implodes.”

“The universe isn't actually going to implode,” Time interjected, sounding rather put out. “It's just going to... destabilize. Catastrophically.”

“Same difference,” Rio said breezily, but her hands were still cradling Agatha's face like she was something precious, worth burning worlds for. It was terrifying.

It was exactly the kind of reckless, universe-endangering chaos that had made Agatha fall in love with her in the first place. Fucking hell, she couldn’t do this again.

“This is ridiculous,” Agatha muttered, fighting the urge to lean into Rio's touch. “You can't just break fundamental cosmic laws because you didn't like what you saw in my future.”

“Watch me.” Rio's grin was sharp enough to cut glass. “I've spent literal eons playing by the rules. Following orders. Being a good little harbinger of mortality. And where did it get me?” She laughed. “Alone in an endless existence, forced to take everything I've ever loved.”

Her voice cracked on the last word, and Agatha felt something in her chest crack with it.

Because that was the thing about Rio - underneath all the chaos and terrible jokes and casual disregard for universal law, there was this. This raw, endless capacity for something that made her do incredibly stupid things like quit being Death itself.

“You've never been alone,” Agatha found herself saying, the words dragged from some deep, carefully guarded part of her soul. “Even when I hated you- especially when I hated you- you were always...” She trailed off, frustrated by the inadequacy of language to capture what Rio was to her. An anchor. A storm. A constant eternally defying constants.

“Always yours?” Rio suggested, and her smile was softer now, tinged with something ancient and aching.

“Always a pain in my ass,” Agatha corrected, but she was leaning closer, pulled by the gravity of Rio's presence, by the warmth of her newly mortal skin, by three centuries of love and hate and everything in between.

Agatha could feel the weight of centuries pressing down on them, every shared moment, every fight, every reconciliation stretched between them.

Rio's hands were still gentle against her face, but there was something desperate in her touch now, something that spoke of eons of loneliness and the terrible, beautiful folly of loving a mortal.

“You know what the worst part was?” Rio's voice was barely a whisper, meant only for Agatha. “Watching you in Westview, trapped in that pastel hellscape? It wasn't seeing you suffer.” Well, no, of course it wasn’t. Freak. “The worst part was watching you play something you could never be. Like some cosmic joke. The most powerful witch I've ever known, reduced to bringing fucking casseroles to potlucks.”

“The casseroles were actually quite good,” Chaos piped up from somewhere behind them.

“Not the time,” Time muttered.

“They were terrible,” Agatha said, ignoring them both. “Everything tasted like mothballs.”

Rio's laugh was wet with unshed tears. “God, I missed you. The real you. Not that suburban Stepford nightmare version. You.” Her thumb traced Agatha's bottom lip, the touch feather-light. “My vicious, brilliant-”

“I'm not yours,” Agatha said automatically, but the words rang hollow even to her own ears.

“No?” Rio's eyebrow arched. “Then why am I the only one you've tried to kill more than once and still kept around?”

“Because you're literally Death. It's not like it stuck.”

“You know what did stick?” Rio's eyes were ancient and young at once, galaxies spinning in their depths. “Every moment with you. Every fight. Every kiss. Every time you looked at me like I was something worth damning the world for.” Her smile turned wry. “Which, speaking of...”

“Don't,” Agatha warned, but she didn't step back from Rio's touch. Maybe she couldn't. Maybe some part of her had always been tethered to Death, even when she'd hated her most. “Don't make this romantic. You literally broke the universe.”

“Technically,” Rio's thumb still traced patterns against Agatha's cheek, “I just quit my job. The universe breaking is more of a side effect. Like mercury in retrograde, but with more existential consequences.”

“That's not better.”

“No?” Rio's smile turned sharp. “Then why haven't you pushed me away yet?”

The question hung between them, heavy with implications. Agatha was acutely aware of every point of contact between them - Rio's hands on her face, their bodies close enough that she could feel the strange new warmth of Death's mortal form, the way the air itself seemed to crackle with possibility.

“Because,” Agatha said slowly, tasting copper as she bit back harsher words, “you're clearly having some sort of cosmic breakdown and I don't trust you not to accidentally end reality if left unsupervised.”

“Liar,” Rio breathed, but it was fond. Tender, even. “You're worried about me.”

“I'm worried about the fundamental laws of existence.” And no longer being able to kill anyone? Boring as hell.

“Sure, but mostly about me.” Rio's eyes glittered with that familiar dangerous mirth, but there was something else there too - something raw and honest that made Agatha's chest ache. “Go ahead, Agatha. Admit it. You still care.”

“I still think you’re infuriating.”

“Not a denial.”

Behind them, Fate cleared their throat. “If we could perhaps focus on the collapse of-”

“Shut up,” Rio snapped, not breaking eye contact.

Time muttered something that sounded suspiciously like “this is why we don't date mortals” but neither of them paid any attention. They were caught in their own gravity now, the rest of the universe reduced to background noise.

“You know what's funny?” Rio's voice was soft, almost wondering. “I've watched stars die and worlds end. But you?” Her laugh was a broken thing. “You're the only soul I couldn't bear to collect. Not again. Not after-”

“Don't.” Agatha's voice cracked. “Don't bring him into this.”

“He's already in this.” Rio's hands trembled against Agatha's skin. “He's in every moment between us. Every time I see your future, I see him too. I see how it broke us. How it's still breaking us.” “You had a choice. I know you had a choice,” Agatha whispered, but the old accusation felt hollow now, worn smooth by centuries of repeating it.

“Did I?” Rio's eyes were ancient, full of shadows Agatha couldn't name. “Death doesn't get choices. Death just is. Or at least...” Her smile turned sharp, brittle. “Death was. Until I decided to quit rather than take another person I loved.”

The words hit Agatha like a physical blow. She stared at Rio, really looking at her for the first time since she'd appeared.

At the way mortality sat on her shoulders like an ill-fitting coat.

At the terrifying, beautiful absurdity of Death itself choosing unemployment over hurting her again.

“You couldn't have just sent a card? Maybe some flowers? Had to go and break the whole concept of mortality instead?”

Rio laughed, the sound caught somewhere between a sob and a joke. “You hate flowers. Last time I brought you roses, you set them on fire. Besides, when have I ever done anything the easy way?”

“Never,” Agatha admitted, leaning into the touch despite herself.

“You love it.” Rio's voice softened. “You love chaos. You always have. It's why you love-” She caught herself, but the unfinished sentence hung between them like a live wire.

Agatha felt something in her chest crack open, raw and aching and true. “You can't do this,” she whispered. “You can't choose me over the universe, Rio. Over your own nature.”

“Watch me.” Rio's grin was sharp as broken glass. “What's Death without a little rebellion? Besides, my nature?” Her laugh was low, dark. “This is my nature. Whatever this is. Everything else is just... paperwork.”

From somewhere behind them came the distinct sound of Fate having an aneurysm.

“The natural order is not paperwork,” Time snapped, but Rio wasn't listening. She was looking at Agatha like she was something vast and terrible and beautiful.

“You know what I saw?” Rio's voice was barely a whisper now. “In every possible future? I saw you die. Over and over and over. Sometimes fighting. Sometimes peaceful. Sometimes-” Her voice cracked. “Sometimes by my own hand. Because that's what Death does. Death takes everything, eventually. Even the things it loves most.”

“Rio-”

“And I thought, you know what? Fuck it.” Rio's smile was beautiful and broken and just a little unhinged. “Fuck destiny. Fuck the natural order. Fuck being Death itself if it means I have to lose you again and again.”

“So you just... quit?” Agatha couldn't quite keep the incredulous laugh from her voice. “Just walked away from being an eternal cosmic force because you didn't like my future?”

“No.” Rio's hands slid down to cup Agatha's jaw, holding her like something precious and dangerous all at once. “I quit because I saw every possible way I could lose you, and I decided I'd rather tear apart reality itself than let any of them happen.”

Agatha felt something inside her break - not the way glass breaks, but the way dawn breaks. Slowly, inevitably, beautiful and terrible all at once.

“You’re impossible,” she breathed, and then she was pulling Rio closer, closing the last distance between them. “You absolute nightmare.”

Rio's lips, soft and yielding now in mortality, met Agatha's with a tentative warmth, as if still unsure they had the right.

Agatha felt raw, on fire, Rio’s mouth no longer the cold, unyielding marble of immortality.

When they broke apart, the kitchen was suspiciously empty of cosmic entities.

“Guess time doesn’t do well with public displays of affection?” Rio asked, sounding delighted.

“Guess not.” Agatha didn't move from the circle of Rio's arms. “You still have to go back, you know. The universe can't actually function without Death.” Rio had never run before. She’d never tried. She had to know she’d never succeed.

“I know.” Rio pressed their foreheads together, her breath ghosting across Agatha's lips. “But maybe... maybe we can find a better way. One that doesn't end with me having to take you.”

“There's always a price for cheating Death,” Agatha reminded her softly.

Rio's laugh was warm against her skin. “Good thing you're sleeping with Death then, isn't it?”

“Bold of you to assume you're getting anywhere near my bed after breaking the fundamental laws of existence.”

“Please. Like that's not the hottest thing I've ever done.”

Agatha looked at her very seriously. “You're the worst thing that's ever happened to me.”

“I know.” Rio's eyes sparkled with that dangerous mirth Agatha had always loved and hated in equal measure. “But admit it - existence would be boring without me.”

No way in hell was Agatha saying I love you, too.

“Existence would be significantly more stable without you.” She tangled her fingers in Rio's shirt, pulling her closer.

Rio's answering smile could have burned Ralph’s house to the ground. “I'll take it.”
